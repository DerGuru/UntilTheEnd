I lifted a bite of not-rice from the cold bowl. It had been days since I last ate; I had avoided counting how many. My body had continued unchanged while you prepared meals at my side. Now I put the food into my mouth.

The not-rice was burned. Amber grains clung among the pale ones, carrying the taste of the pot's bottom. You measured carefully enough. Judging when to move the pot had always been less reliable.

It tasted almost like burned rice.

The grains stuck together, chewy where they had cooked too long without stirring. Smoke ran under the starchy taste. I worked through the mouthful slowly. Most of your cooking fell between what I could eat willingly and what I would ever ask for again.

I chewed.

My teeth broke the clump apart. Each softened grain pressed against my tongue, then came the movement of swallowing. After so many days, the act took attention. I held the bowl steady and waited for the taste to fade.

You said nothing.

You let me finish the mouthful without remarking on it. I could be a woman eating food at a fire for a little while, doing something ordinary enough to pass without a record.

Xiǎohú was eating too. Something small and furred protruded from her mouth, caught while we had been occupied with the notebook. She blinked at me from the fire's edge, then lowered her head to her catch. At least she had supplied her own dinner.

I took a second bite. It had the same burned edge as the first, with the same stubborn clump of grains. Your food was reliably edible. I had rarely finished one of your meals uncertain about who had cooked it.

The fire was almost out. Mountain shadows had merged into continuous darkness; the last gray clung to the ridge. Clouds moved west across it. Again I wondered whether I had changed their course. The question came whenever I looked at the sky now.

I could leave it unanswered for tonight. I looked back at the food in my hands.

Three or four bites were left in the bowl. I had eaten enough to disturb the neat portion you had given me.

I set the bowl down again.

The eggs returned to mind, followed by the dead bird. I remembered the silence around Wei, then another face from the evening when the names had overwhelmed me. Eating had left those memories as clear as before.

Yet the burned taste still coated my mouth. That small failure belonged to your cooking. The pot had sat too long over the hotter part of the fire. You had measured the grain with care, then given it less attention than it needed while it cooked.

I could account for that without examining a feeling or a change in the sky. A man had prepared a meal imperfectly. I had eaten some of it. The simplicity was strange enough that I sat turning it over while you reached for the pot.

Wind came up, brightening the embers. Red light spread across the stones around the fire. Past them, the mountain was dark.

You scrubbed the pot with grass and water, working at the grains stuck to the bottom. Once it was clean, you packed it away. You stirred the embers before unrolling your sleeping mat.

"Tomorrow," you said.

I lay down on the hard ground. Stars appeared through gaps in the clouds. I watched them for a while without trying to decide what had moved the cover aside.

My half-eaten bowl stood next to me. I left it within reach.

I closed my eyes.

For the first time in days, the grass beside me held still through each breath. Its dry tips brushed my hand. The campsite was quiet.

I fell asleep thinking about the not-rice.

You burned the not-rice every time; the consistency was almost admirable. I ate it anyway.
