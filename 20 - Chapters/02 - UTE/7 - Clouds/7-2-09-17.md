The rain stopped, or paused. The fire was going, the pot was on, the rice was on. You were talking. About nothing. About everything that constituted nothing. About the mundane, aggressively ordinary content of a life lived alongside me.

"... needs reorganizing," you mumbled. "The herb pouch is leaking again. The stitching frayed where it bends, right at the fold, which means I need to re-sew the whole seam, and I should use a waxed thread this time, but I don't have waxed thread so I'll have to wax it myself with candle tallow, which means melting the candle, which ..."

You talked while you cooked. The talking was texture. The sound of a human being occupying time with language, filling the silence that would otherwise fill itself with the weight of everything that silence contained. You talked about the herb pouch and the way the rain had smelled different at this altitude.

You were, specifically and deliberately, being annoying.

The tactical annoyance of a man who understood that his companion's interior landscape was a territory of dangerous weight. And that the antidote to dangerous weight was, sometimes, the trivial, the mundane, the stupid. You were filling the space with normalcy the way you fill a hole with gravel — to stabilize.

"... and the rice needs watching because the fire is uneven and the heat distribution is off, which means the left side burns faster than the right, which means I need to rotate the pot every ..."

Quietly. Simply. I reached across the fire — the distance between us, the space that separated my cooking-abstinence from your cooking-monopoly — and I took the wooden spoon from your hand, put it in the pot and stirred.

Rice. In a pot. Over a fire. The simplest mechanical act in the history of human food preparation. The circular motion of a utensil in a container, redistributing heat, preventing adhesion, the physics of cooking reduced to its most fundamental operation. Stir.

I stirred.

You leaned back.

The talking stopped. The tactical annoyance mission completed, its objective achieved not by the words but by the irritation the words produced. The irritation that had mobilized an arm that had been idle, that had prompted a hand to reach, that had resulted in the unremarkable, entirely mundane act of a woman stirring rice.

A spoon in a pot. The smallest possible agency.

I stirred. The rice was fine. Adequate. The fire was uneven, as you had said, and the left side was indeed cooking faster. So the rotation was necessary and the attention to these details was real. Physical. Present. Performing a task that required no conviction, no immortal power, no cosmic architecture. Just a hand, a spoon and the patience to keep moving them until the rice was done.

Steam rose. The fire settled. Xiǎohú appeared from wherever she had been sheltering during the rain and found her evening position, the triangle point, the third corner of whatever shape the three of us made in the world.

You watched me stir. Your face was doing its thing. The non-expression that contained everything, the mask that was transparent if you knew how to read it. I had learned to read it across mountains, a partnership that neither of us had asked for and neither of us would trade.

Relief was on your face. The quiet kind. The deep-structure kind. The relief of a man who had been watching someone he cared about not-participate in life and who was now stirring rice.

I stirred until the rice was done. Served two bowls. Set your near the fire. Held mine.

The steam rose. The evening was cold, the post-rain air carrying the freshness of atmosphere that has been washed. Stars beginning to appear as the clouds thinned, appearing and disappearing, the flickering emergence of light through dissolving cover.

I ate.

Half the bowl. The other half cooled while I sat with it, while the fire settled, while the stars appeared and disappeared behind the thinning clouds. Half a bowl, eaten with the deliberate attention of someone for whom eating was a choice. A small, human, ordinary choice.

The spoon in the pot. The rice in the bowl. 

You watched the steam rise and your silence was the right shape for this moment.
