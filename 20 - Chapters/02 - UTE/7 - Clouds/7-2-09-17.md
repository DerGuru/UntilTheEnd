The rain paused while the rice cooked. You crouched by the fire, talking about our supplies. Water still dripped from the branches around us, but you had found a subject more immediate than anything I might do to the weather.

"... needs reorganizing," you mumbled. "The herb pouch is leaking again. The stitching frayed where it bends, right at the fold. I'll have to resew the whole seam. I should use waxed thread this time, but I haven't got any. I could dress it with candle tallow, which means melting the candle, which..."

You talked as you cooked, moving from the herb pouch to the different smell of rain at this altitude. I didn't have to answer. Your voice filled the pauses in which I would otherwise have returned to our failed attempts. The pouch, at least, was something a needle could mend.

After a while I began to suspect you were being annoying on purpose. You usually noticed when I stopped listening. This evening you plowed on, giving each small inconvenience its full share of the conversation. I had spent the day unable to change myself, and you were making it steadily harder to think about anything except our badly sewn herb pouch.

"... and the rice needs watching because the fire is uneven. The left side burns faster than the right, which means I need to turn the pot every..."

I reached across the fire for the wooden spoon in your hand. You let me take it. I put it into the pot, drawing the rice away from the hotter side.

The spoon scraped along the bottom. Rice loosened under it and shifted toward the cooler side of the pot. I followed the curve of the ceramic, working the grains away from where the flame was highest.

I stirred.

You leaned back.

You stopped talking. The timing did little to clear you of suspicion. You had spent a good while explaining a task that your hand had already been performing. Now my hand was doing it, which apparently removed the need for further instruction.

I kept the spoon.

I stirred. The rice was cooking well enough. You had been right about the fire: heat rose unevenly against the pot. I turned it, then brought the spoon around the bottom again. The task wanted attention, this small thing I had left to you for so long. The grains moving under the wood told me how it was going.

Steam rose as the fire settled. Xiǎohú came out from her shelter against the rain and took her evening place, completing the loose triangle around the fire.

You watched me stir. Your shoulders eased against the ground behind you. Across all these mountains I had learned to read small changes in your face, and this one was clear enough. Your eyes went to my hand on the spoon, then to the fire.

I had spent days letting you do everything while I sat next to you. Now you could leave the pot to me. Neither of us made more of it.

I stirred until the rice was done, then served two bowls. I set yours near the fire. Mine rested in my hands.

Steam rose into the cold evening. The air smelled fresh after the rain. As the clouds thinned, stars appeared in the gaps, disappearing again when another patch passed over them.

I ate.

I ate half the bowl, attending to each mouthful. The other half cooled while I sat with it. Above us more stars showed through the thinning cover. The fire sank lower, its warmth still reaching my hands.

The spoon rested in the pot where I had left it. You watched the steam rise from your bowl. I was content to sit next to you without another word.
