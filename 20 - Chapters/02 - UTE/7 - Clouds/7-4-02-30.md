I sat on the grass with my eyes closed. Stone pressed up through the thin soil. Air filled my lungs and my ribs rose. My body occupied this patch of ground as plainly as anything else in the clearing.

I turned my attention toward that body. The outward changes had become easy to recognize. Here I had to attend to the one thing through which I recognized everything else.

I am mortal.

The three words were clear. I understood them and could hold each in mind as easily as I could hear your voice. I let the sentence stand and kept breathing.

I am mortal.

I tried to settle into the certainty I had found with the water. The sentence should describe the body sitting here. Yet I wanted it to become true so badly that the wanting brought pressure with it, and I was pressing the thought inward again.

Nothing.

The old certainty lay under it. My attention went inward only so far before it met the same solid limit. It had been there so long that I could scarcely imagine a self on either side of it.

I am mortal.

/ɡɵ̆ŋ/. The old word for wall surfaced, rough as stone against stone. The sound fit that same immovable boundary. It was part of how I existed, as settled as the ribs moving with each breath. I had built myself to survive. Now I was asking that shape of existence to admit an end.

I opened my eyes.

"Nothing," I said.

You sat cross-legged next to me with your pen ready. Your gaze searched my face for some sign of what had happened while my eyes were closed.

"What did you feel?" you asked.

"Resistance."

"Where?"

I searched for a place to point to. My chest rose easily; my head was clear. Wherever I turned, the same certainty lay under my attention. The resistance and the person experiencing it would not come apart.

"From me," I said.

I rested my hands on my knees. I had known for millennia that death did not apply to this body. I could speak about mortality, imagine it, and understand its consequences. Calling myself mortal still felt as false as calling myself a stone. I was the source of that certainty.

"From you?" you repeated. 

"From me."

You wrote slowly. The characters grew denser where you set down my answer. I searched for anything more precise to tell you and found nothing.

"Would you try again?" you asked.

"Maybe later or tomorrow. I need to let this sink in for a bit."

I stood. The stream ran along the edge of the clearing; birds called from the trees. Xiǎohú lay with her eyes closed. The ordinary sounds went on around another failed attempt.

I walked to the stream to wash my face. Cold water ran over my cheeks into my cupped hands. I lifted it, held it, and let it go without difficulty.

Behind me, you went on writing. We had found the source of the resistance again. Changing it would mean changing the person who had to do the work. To finish, I would have to let the self that had survived all those years come to an end. I crouched over the water longer than the washing needed.

I dried my face with my sleeve.

Tomorrow.
