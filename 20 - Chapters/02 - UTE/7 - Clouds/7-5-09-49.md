Yun sits on the rock. The ridge where Zhìwēng stood is empty — has been empty long enough for the eye to stop checking.

The sentence is still there. Below the sternum. Above the diaphragm. *Don't be afraid.*

She breathes. The air is the same air it was during the conversation — thin, cold, carrying stone-scent and the ozone-hint of recent rain. But the breathing is different. Less deliberate. The chest expanding because chests expand, the ribs separating because ribs separate. The autonomic reasserting itself over the intentional.

Xiǎohú sits beside her cleaning her paws with the methodical attention that animals apply to grooming. The birds have resumed their territorial declarations in the scrub below.

She speaks.

To the air. To the mountain. Past Xiǎohú, past the birds, to the open sky, to everything and nothing specific.

"I have lived. That was enough."

The words come out. Louder than a thought, quieter than a declaration. The volume of a person testing a sentence's weight in air rather than in mind. The voice is hers. The sentence is Wei's.

The words sound hollow.

She knows this. She hears the hollowness that something, which distinguishes performance from truth, that separates mere saying from meaning it. The words are correct. The grammar is correct. The sentiment is — adjacent to correct. But the sentence is a costume rather than a skin, worn rather than grown.

She speaks Wei's truth about herself. And the truth does not fit. Not yet.

But hollow things can be filled. Performances can become authentic through repetition. The "not yet" implies a "but eventually." A promise disguised as a limitation.

She has said it. Out loud. The words are out. The air is fresh. The cold is real.

She breathes in again. This breath is different from the one before — less deliberate, more automatic, she is settling into the pattern of breathing-because-alive rather than breathing-because-choosing-to. The autonomic system taking over from the conscious one.

Below the rock, the mountain descends. Her eyes follow the trail — barely a trail, more a sequence of possible steps suggested by geology — winds down through scrub and rock toward a distant treeline of the forest. And below the forest — she looks. Squints against the overcast.

Distance. The flattening of terrain that indicates a valley. The green of agriculture — cultivated green, the distinct shade that human intervention produces, the organized green that wild green never achieves. Fields. Cultivation. The evidence of a human presence, of the relationship between people and land that produces food.

Xiǎohú follows her gaze downward. Her eyes tracking the same descent, reading the same landscape, arriving at the same conclusion through a different vocabulary.

She stands on the rock with the hollow sentence still vibrating in her chest and the mountain still falling away beneath her and the evidence of human life visible in the valley below.

She has said it. The words are empty. They will fill.

Or they won't.

Both possibilities are acceptable. Both are — real. The acceptance that comes from exhaustion and honesty and the state of a woman who has tried everything she can try alone and knows that trying alone has reached its limit.

She takes a step. Down. Toward the treeline. Toward the valley. Toward the smoke.

The step is heavy. She feels the stone's response to her weight as if her heavy thoughts carry their own weight with her.

One step. Then another. The descent begins.

The clouds above the peak are — present. Beginning to dissolve.
