The mountain path is steep, grey, empty.

Yun walks. One foot, then the other, the mechanical repetition of a body on inclines — the muscular negotiation between weight and gradient, the knees absorbing, the ankles adjusting, the architecture of locomotion performing its ancient function without consultation.

The clouds sit above the ridgeline. They do not move. Heavy, flat, too dense for the wind to shift, too thick to remember they are vapor.

She is alone.

The silence is different. Bottomless. The working silence of two people walking together has a floor, has the structural support of another person's breathing and footsteps and the knowledge that speech is available even when unused. This silence has no floor. This silence goes all the way down.

Xiǎohú is ahead. A rust-colored shape on the grey stone, moving with the efficient gait of an animal that does not require companionship but tolerates proximity. Her bright eyes catch the light when she turns — brief, warm, the only color on the monochrome trail.

Wind.

The wind is cold. Actually cold — beyond the theoretical cold that her qi usually deflects automatically. This cold is different. This cold is there.

She stops and looks at her hands. The pause of a woman noticing something that should not be possible — a halt in stride, raised hands, the visual inspection of a phenomenon that contradicts everything her qi has been doing for longer than most mountains have been mountains.

The tips of her fingers are blue. The vascular response of blood retreating from extremities to preserve the core, the triage decision that the circulatory system makes when heat is insufficient. Her body is making that decision. Her body never had to made that decision.

For one breath — one single, measurable, countable breath — her qi has not shielded her.

For one breath, she is exposed.

She exhales onto her hands. The warm air meets the cold skin and the warmth is — hers. Produced by her lungs. She rubs her hands together. The friction generates heat. The heat is mechanical. Physical. The friction-warmth of skin against skin, produced entirely by motion.

She rubs her hands together like a human.

The moment is beautiful. And terrifying. The two things are not separate — they occupy the same space, the identical temporal and emotional territory, the beauty and the terror coexisting in the narrow window of a woman discovering that her hands can be cold.

The blue retreats. The qi returns — subtle and automatic. The defense mechanism reasserts itself the way a sleeping guard resumes position after a momentary lapse. The fingertips are warm again. Their color returns. She is protected again, shielded again, immortal again.

But the blue was there.

She looks at her hands again. The blue is gone. The evidence is gone. The fingertips have their ordinary color — the uniform color of her skin that has existed for millennia, static and unmarked, sealed from the environment.

Three seconds of feeling cold. Three seconds of seeing blue. Three seconds of being human.

The path continues upward. Into the mountains. Toward — she does not name the destination. It exists as direction rather than location, as trajectory rather than point. Upward. Into the cold. Into the work.

Xiǎohú waits on a rock. Patient. The animal patience that does not distinguish between delay and purpose, that treats all pauses as equivalent and resumes movement when movement resumes.

She walks. The wind blows. The cold is — ordinary cold now, deflected cold, the environmental temperature that exists outside the qi's perimeter and that does not penetrate.

But it had penetrated.

The clouds above the ridge do not move. Heavy. Grey. Waiting.

She climbs.
