She sits on a rock, legs crossed, the mountain falling away beneath her in the vertiginous cascade of altitude, the world laid out below like something flattened. The memory arrives without permission, coming the way water comes through stone. Finding its own path, following its own schedule, arriving by its own will.

Wei.

His death. But different this time. IT has been looked at so many times the edges have softened and the image has changed from photograph to painting. Less precise, more true.

His face.

Calm. The calm that had no right being there. Wei was loud, stubborn and argumentative. But in his last moments, he had faced dying with a stillness that contradicted everything he'd been alive.

A bird calls. Outside. Real. The piercing note of a mountain bird, pure presence, the acoustic signature of a living thing announcing its existence to the air. She hears it. Each note. Each pause between notes. The frequency and duration and silence that compose the bird's statement, whatever that statement is.

Wei had lived.

The sentence forms not as thought but as presence. Wei had lived. He had stolen food. He had cooked rice — terrible rice, burned rice, rice that was somehow both overcooked and undercooked in the same bowl. He had grinned and argued constantly, instinctively, the way some people breathe.

He had lived.

The statement lands. In her. In the interior space where statements go when they are too important for consciousness and too heavy for thought. The statement settles into the bones. Not comfortably. The bones are not comfortable with anything right now, the bones are still vibrating from the seven seconds and the panic that came with them. But the statement settles.

He had lived. A short life, but a full one. With ups and downs. With every emotion there is. That was enough.

The statements apply to Wei. To the boy who died calm and unimpressed and complete. They do not yet apply to the woman sitting on the rock looking at the mountain, the woman who has lived for millennia and is still learning to be calm and still becoming complete.

But they apply to him.

And the recognition that one human being had lived sufficiently and the death was a conclusion rather than a robbery — is something that sits in the chest below the fear and provides it with context.

The bird calls again. Three notes. A pause. Two notes. The language-without-words that birds have been speaking since before human language existed.

She listens. Really listens. Each note arriving individually, carrying its own frequency, occupying its own moment. The pauses between the notes are as full as the notes themselves. The musical understanding that silence is part of the composition, that the not-sound is part of the sound, that absence is part of presence.

The raw hearing — the unfiltered input that had been overwhelming once, exhausting, too much — is quiet now. Not because the filter returned. Because she stopped fighting the signal. The bird is loud and clear and present and it is not too much. It is enough.

She breathes. The clouds above the peak are moving slowly, the leisurely drift of high-altitude formations. The mountain is there. The world is there. The bird is there. The fear is there. The memory is there. The understanding is there. The truth is there.

Wei's face fades. Gently. The way an image fades when the mind has finished using it. The way clouds fade.

The boy had lived. The boy had died. The living was the important part.

She sits on the rock. The bird calls. The mountain exists. The fear sits in her chest.

It is still there.

But beneath the fear — or beside it, or woven into it — is something small. Warm. Present like a seed before it knows what it will become.
