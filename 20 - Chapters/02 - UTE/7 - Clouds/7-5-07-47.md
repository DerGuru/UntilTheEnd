She sits cross-legged on a rock above the steep slope. Altitude flattens the distant ground, but the drop under her is dizzyingly close. Cold presses through her clothing. With it comes the memory of a weight she once held in her arms.

Wei.

She has returned to his death so often that the edges of the memory have softened. Some details are harder to hold. Others are clear enough that she can look at what she could scarcely bear to consider before.

His face.

His face is calm. She remembers how unexpected that calm was. Wei had been loud, stubborn, and argumentative. In his last moments, the boy who had always found another objection had met death with stillness.

A mountain bird calls near the rock. The note is clear enough to draw her attention from the memory. She listens to its pitch, its duration, and the pause before the next note. Each sound reaches her separately through the cold air.

Wei had lived.

She remembers him stealing food. She remembers his rice: burned grains clumped around others that were still hard, an accomplishment she had never quite understood. He had grinned at her across the bowl. He had argued as readily as other people breathed, finding something to say even when she gave him little encouragement.

He had lived.

She stays with that part of him. Her body still carries the strain of the seven heartbeats, the panic in which they ended. Remembering him alive gives her something to hold against it. The thought is uncomfortable in its own way: it asks her to let his life fill more of the memory than its ending.

He had lived. A short life, but a full one. With ups and downs. With every emotion there is. That was enough.

That is true of Wei. The boy had reached his own end calmly, still himself. She sits on the rock after millennia of living, far from that calm. She cannot yet make the same statement about her own life.

She can make it about his.

She remembers a human being who had lived fully in the time he had. His death can belong to that life without taking the living away. The recognition rests under the fear in her chest, a second thing to feel.

The bird calls again. Three notes, then a pause, then two. She waits to hear what follows.

She follows each note through its ending. The pauses matter. She holds the last sound before the next begins, aware of the whole call because there is space in it.

The clarity had overwhelmed her once. Hearing everything so sharply had been exhausting. The sound is just as clear now, but she has stopped holding it away. She listens to the bird as it is and lets one call finish before another arrives.

She breathes. Clouds move slowly above the peak. The bird is near, and the fear sits in her chest. She attends to the mountain while the memory of Wei stays with her.

His face grows less distinct as the clouds pass. She lets it fade without reaching after it.

The boy had lived. The boy had died. The living was the important part.

She sits on the rock. The bird calls. The mountain exists. The fear sits in her chest.

It is still there.

Beside the fear is something small and warm. She cannot yet say what it will become. She lets it be.
