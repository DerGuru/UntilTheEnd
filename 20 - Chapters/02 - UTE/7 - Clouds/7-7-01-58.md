Morning light comes through the shutters in thin lines, the way it does when the frame doesn't quite fit. Dust turns in the beam — lazy, unhurried, catching the gold as small things catch what larger things miss.

Yun wakes slowly. The ceiling assembles itself above her. Wood, old, a crack running east to west that she has never noticed before. The bed is hard. The blanket smells faintly of cedar, or maybe that is the chest it was stored in. Her body is warm underneath it and does not want to move and that, too, is ordinary. She lets herself lie there.

She breathes. In. Out. The breath goes all the way down.

And something is absent.

Not missing — not taken. Absent the way a sound is absent after it stops, when the room is still ringing with the shape of what was there. The conviction. The deep-structural *Not now* that has been the foundation of everything she is. The refusal that became law, became qi, became the millennia. She had carried it so long that the carrying was invisible, the way you stop noticing the weight of your own bones.

It is not there.

She hadn't felt it dissolve, didn't feel it leave. It is gone the way snow is gone after a warm night — not melted before her eyes, just no longer present. The ground bare where the weight was. Lighter, strange and quiet.

She lies still and waits for the panic.

It does not come. She waits a little longer, like you wait for a knock that was supposed to arrive at noon. One breath. Two. The house stays silent.

That is new. The absence of the conviction should trigger the refusal. The refusal should rebuild the conviction. The whole architecture should reassert itself, the way it always has — every time she has approached this edge, every time she has come close to the threshold, the machinery has caught her. Pulled her back. Rebuilt the wall before she could step through.

The fear is not there either. She can still feel the residue, the faint impression where it sat, like the warmth a cat leaves on a cushion after it moves. But it is not active. It is not fueling. It is not rebuilding anything.

She accepted it. Last night, on the roof, under stars that were her companions for many millennia. She accepted the fear. And the fear, accepted, stopped feeding the conviction. And the conviction, unfed, stopped.

That is all. That is everything.

She lies in the bed and breathes. The breath goes all the way down without catching on anything. The dust keeps turning. But is the crack in the ceiling getting longer? 

Xiǎohú is at the foot of the bed. Watching her with those amber eyes that have watched her through since Yun first recognized her at the edge of a clearing — maybe even longer — and which have never once asked for an explanation. Yun is awake. Yun is different. Xiǎohú notes this the way she notes all things. With patience, without comment.

Yun is different. Like she has been unlocked. A door that has been bolted from inside with the bolt now drawn. The door is still closed but it is no longer held shut. It can open. It will open. And when the time comes, there will be nothing stopping it.

*I am mortal.*

The thought arrives quietly without fanfare or cosmic weight. Just a fact, sitting in her chest beside the breath, the way a stone sits at the bottom of a clear stream — visible, settled, belonging there. She is mortal. The conviction is gone. The body will age. Eventually — not today, not soon, but eventually — the body will do what all bodies do.

She lies in the bed and the morning continues around her. The dust turns. The light moves across the floor in the slow migration that light performs when no one is asking it to hurry. Somewhere outside, a bird starts up — tentative, testing, the first phrase of a song it will spend the rest of the morning perfecting.

She is here. Mortal. Resting. Still a bit tired in the ordinary way that bodies are not fully awake after sleep that was real sleep, deep and dreamless, rather than the imitation that an immortal body performs out of habit.

Xiǎohú yawns. The yawn is enormous — far too large for the animal producing it, all teeth and pink tongue and the theatrical commitment of a creature that takes yawning seriously. Then she stretches, back arching, the morning routine of an animal that may not know what has changed and would not alter her schedule if she did.

Yun gets up.
