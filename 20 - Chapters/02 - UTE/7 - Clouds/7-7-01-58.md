Morning light comes through gaps around the shutters. Dust turns slowly in the thin beams, each particle catching gold before it drifts out of the light.

Yun wakes slowly to the wooden ceiling. A crack runs east to west across boards she has looked at without noticing it. The bed is hard. Her blanket smells of cedar, from the chest where it was stored. She is warm under it and in no hurry to move. She lets herself lie there.

She breathes. In. Out. The breath goes all the way down.

And something is absent.

She checks the place where the old conviction lay. Not now, the refusal under so much of what she is. It became law through millennia of repetition. She had carried it so long that she stopped noticing the effort, until trying to put it down revealed its weight.

It is not there.

She slept through its ending. Snow can vanish during a warm night and leave bare ground where its weight rested. The lightness here is like that, so quiet she keeps probing the space. There was no final effort, no instant she can name as the departure. She went to sleep with the fear allowed beside her. Now she is awake in what followed.

She lies still and waits for the panic.

It does not come. She waits a while longer, expecting the interruption as she would a promised visitor. One breath passes, then a second. The house is silent.

That is new. Each previous opening brought the refusal back. The certainty rebuilt itself before she could get past it, and returned more firmly whenever she approached the edge. She remembers the seven heartbeats, then the pressure that erased the burn. Another breath passes with no such return.

An impression remains where the fear sat, like a cushion that holds warmth after an animal has left it. The old urgency is gone. Only the memory of pressure is left.

Last night she accepted the fear under the stars. She stopped supplying the effort that sustained it. Through the night, the conviction lost what held it in place. The sequence is simple.

That is all. That is everything.

Her breath reaches its full depth. Dust keeps turning in the light. She looks at the crack in the ceiling again, wondering whether it is getting longer.

Xiǎohú lies at the foot of the bed. Yun knows those amber eyes from the edge of a clearing, perhaps from longer ago than she realized at the time. The fox does not move while Yun examines the change in her. There is no question to answer before she can continue.

It is as if an inner bolt has been drawn. The door is still closed, but it can open now. When the time comes, she need no longer hold it shut.

I am mortal.

The thought settles beside her breath, plain as a stone at the bottom of clear water. She is mortal. Her body will age. The ending need not come today, or soon, for the possibility to be true. She can lie under the blanket this morning knowing that a last morning will arrive. That possibility belongs to her now.

Light moves slowly across the floor. Outside, a bird begins a short phrase, pauses, and calls again. Its song comes through the gap by the shutters.

She is here, mortal and resting. Tiredness lingers after the deep sleep, in the warmth under the blanket and the unhurried time it takes to wake fully. For once it asks only for a small allowance. She gives it another breath before moving.

Xiǎohú yawns enormously, showing teeth around a pink tongue. It is far too large a yawn for the animal producing it. Then she stretches until her back arches above the bedding. The morning goes on as before, unchanged by what Yun has discovered.

Yun gets up.
