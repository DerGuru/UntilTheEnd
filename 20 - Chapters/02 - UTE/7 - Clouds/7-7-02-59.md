She rises from the bed and takes a breath longer than she expects. Perhaps two. Her legs bear her weight with an effort she would once have left to qi. She is heavier, her feet pressing into boards that creak. She stands and listens to the ordinary sound of the house holding her.

She bends over the basin by the door. Her reflection has the same proportions, the same features she has worn for millennia. Yet she studies each line as if after an illness, finding details in a face she thought she knew. Nothing visible tells another person what has changed. To her, every part now belongs to a different expectation. It is a human face. It will age.

She goes to the garden.

The seeds she planted many days ago are growing. She kneels by the green shoots. Morning soil is cool under her knees, still damp where the sun has yet to reach it. She has left the seeds to grow in their own time, and the unconscious shaping that accompanied her for so long is absent now.

Soil, water, and sunlight have brought them this far. She lets the modest result be as remarkable as it would be in anyone else's garden.

She touches a leaf. It bends under her finger and springs back when she lets go.

There is another absence. The pressure that emanated from her has stopped. She had grown used to it as to a hum too constant to hear. Now the quiet is distinct. No unconscious adjustment shapes the temperature, the weather, or the small lives nearby.

The garden settles around her, and so do the house and the village beyond the fence, freed from that pressure. Its effects had been subtle: rain held aside, temperatures kept comfortable, and small animals diverted before they reached her. She had lived with those changes so long that she had forgotten them. The difference is as quiet as air entering a room through an opened window. The space around her holds none of the small adjustments she used to maintain.

A small brown bug crosses her hand and continues straight over her wrist into the grass. Its path runs uninterrupted through the place where her qi would once have diverted it.

The bug goes. She likes its indifference.

Memories arrive together: faces, names, places she has stood, and seasons she has seen turn. Thousands of years crowd a single breath. That weight had overwhelmed her before. She remembers kneeling in a river while the current stopped around her, the world bending under what she had struggled to contain.

Nothing bends now. The garden is exactly as it was.

The sensation lasts one breath. Maybe two.

Then the memories settle like snow after a fall. They cover so much of what she can recall, but she can let them rest. The garden has held its shape through their arrival. A face from long ago grows quiet in her as she looks at the small shoots. They belong to her. She belongs to the life that made them. On the cool soil the connection is warmth, like the warmth a house holds after a long day of sun.

Small insects cross her hands. The shoots are at their ordinary stage of growth. Sunlight warms the back of her neck, the same warmth that falls on the plants beside her.

She sits easily and leaves the feeling unexamined. She has tested so many sensations for proof of what they meant. This one needs none. The ground holds her while another bug finds its path across her fingers. She lets it finish crossing before she moves her hand.
