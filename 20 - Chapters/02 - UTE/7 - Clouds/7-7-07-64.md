She stands at the stove watching the pot, patient because she knows what inattention costs. Still, the rice at the bottom turns brown, then dark. The warm smell reaches her. She knows that particular char as she knows a melody she has not heard in years.

Xu Ran's burned rice. She had complained about it through months of mountain walking, then eaten it anyway. He had produced it as reliably as other people breathed. At the end he had made one good meal, leaving her to suspect that the others involved a choice. He understood the process. The required patience had been less appealing.

She has made his mistake. Too little water, the heat too high. The result has the layers she remembers: burned grain under an adequate portion, with a few stubbornly firm grains on top. She has inherited his method more precisely than she intended.

She eats it. The burned part is crunchy. The adequate part is adequate. She is eating Xu Ran's rice in a kitchen he never entered, months after he closed the notebook to walk away.

The memory of him stays with her while she eats. His face across the fire comes back with a fondness softened by time. It warms the meal instead of making the empty place across from her harder to look at. She has let time work on the remembering as it works on other things.

She sets down the empty bowl to scrub the pot. Charcoal loosens from the iron under patient, circular strokes. Her hands go on with the work while she thinks about what she has remembered.

She thinks about collecting.

She can choose which moments to keep. For so long, memories accumulated without that choice, sediment left by a river that never stopped flowing. Millennia arrived whether she wanted them or not. Now she can look at something happening in front of her and decide to remember it.

Late-afternoon light falls through the kitchen window. It catches the dust, warming the color of everything it touches. On the counter the wood grain shows, with small imperfections and a ring left by setting her cup down too often in one place. This one.

The Counter's voice carries faintly from the lane. A number rises, then another as she moves along her work. She sounds as certain of its importance as she did at the garden fence. The same seriousness runs through each change in pitch, the assurance that her task matters without anyone's confirmation. This one.

She remembers the plum blossoms in spring, their light sweetness lasting through a brief stretch of days. She had attended to it more closely because it would pass. This one.

Collecting. She can notice her life as it happens and give each chosen moment enough attention to keep it. This light. This voice. The smell of blossoms she can recall while scrubbing a pot.

Dinner is better than the rice. She makes bean stew with ginger from the tangle at the garden's edge, a patch she has stopped trying to straighten. The result is acceptable. She could offer it to someone without either boasting or apologizing. That middle ground is a small victory she had not known she wanted.

She eats at the table with the candle lit. Outside, the long mountain evening changes slowly from gold to copper, then to a deep blue that lasts only a short while. She watches the color between mouthfuls of warm stew. It will never be quite the same again.

Xiǎohú lies under the table where dropped food sometimes lands. Yun sees her watching each movement of the spoon. Nothing falls. The fox goes unrewarded.

Loneliness has changed so gradually that she notices only now, sitting here. Before, she was too permanent for the passing lives around her, unable to meet people in the same span of time. Now she is between connections. There can be another conversation tomorrow, another meal with someone. The quiet is an interval she can inhabit. The possibility does not need to fill the other chair this evening. The empty chair leaves room, as an opening in a wall lets light through.

She sits alone at the table while the half-eaten stew cools. The candle burns in the quiet house. As the last evening colors fade, she notices the first stars in the window.

The room is available. So is she.

This, she thinks. This one, too.

She collects the moment and adds it to the others.
