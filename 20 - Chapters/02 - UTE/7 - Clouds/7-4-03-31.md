The next day, I sat on the same patch of grass at your suggestion. You wanted the circumstances as close as possible to yesterday's. If something changed, we would have fewer causes to consider. You settled beside me with the notebook open.

I am mortal.

I tried to carry the sentence deeper. I had spent yesterday finding where it stopped. Now I held it more deliberately, keeping my attention on it when the old certainty rose beneath.

I am mortal. I put the weight on the first word, then the second, then the last. Each version lasted a breath before I tried the next.

I was looking for an arrangement I could hold as fact. The words never changed. The meaning shifted depending on whether I placed myself, my existence, or its ending at the center.

Something small happened.

A sound reached me from beyond the limit. It was muffled, too indistinct to identify, like an echo heard through a thick wall. After it faded I held my attention there, trying to reach the space it had come from.

I opened my eyes. The stream ran through the unchanged clearing. No birds called. I could not remember whether they had been calling before I began; all my attention had been inward.

"Something happened," I said.

Your pen began to move while I spoke. You had been watching closely enough to notice when I opened my eyes. Now you looked between my face and the page.

"Describe it," you said.

"An echo. Behind everything. Like a sound on the other side of something thick."

"Duration?"

"Brief. Half a breath."

You wrote in small, precise characters. Next to the duration you noted the conditions: the time of day, my posture, and what you had seen in my face during the attempt. I waited while you finished.

"Could it have been environmental?" you asked. "Something in the clearing? The stream? The birds?"

I looked around the clearing. Water moved in the stream; grass lay near my knees. The leaves of the nearest tree were unnaturally still. Movement had stopped in them, as if air had thickened around the branches during my attempt.

"I don't think so," I said. "Maybe the leaves. But they would have sounded different. This was internal."

You looked at the tree, then added a note.

"Again?" you said.

"Not today."

I did not get up. With the outward changes, the effort lingered in my body afterward. This fatigue reached into the attention I would need to begin again. I could scarcely hold the sentence in mind now, though the echo was still clear.

We ate more of the not-rice. You made it reliably enough that neither of us expected variation. I took each mouthful without tasting it. The bowl warmed my hands as I turned over what I had heard.

"What could that echo mean?" I asked.

"What does an echo require?" you asked.

"Space. On the other side."

"And what does hearing it require?"

"Permeability."

"So we have two findings. There is something on the other side of the wall. And the wall isn't absolute. It's permeable to some degree."

The argument held. Something had reached me from beyond the limit. However faint the echo, I had heard it. Perhaps the boundary had a thin place I had failed to find before. Perhaps the attempts of these last months had begun to change it.

I looked down at the bowl. That boundary was my own creation. The weakness I hoped to discover would belong to me as surely as the resistance did. I would have to let some part of myself yield, then use that yielding to continue. Even following the thought took more attention than I wanted to spend over food.

"Symptom," you said, writing again. "Diagnosis. Treatment." You paused to look at me. "Look at it as any healer would. Except the patient and the disease are the same thing."

"Is that supposed to help?"

"It's supposed to give us a structure. We already have the first two. Treatment—"

"That's the part I need."

"That comes later."

Afternoon light moved across the clearing. Clouds drifted above us. I looked at them briefly, still uncertain whether their movement belonged to the weather.

I washed the bowls in the cold stream. Grain clung to the sides where it had dried. I worked it free with my fingers, then let the current rinse the surfaces clean.

Tomorrow I would try again. I might hear the echo or find only the old resistance. For now I had the half-breath when something had reached me.

I lifted the clean bowls from the water. We had that much more to work with than yesterday.
