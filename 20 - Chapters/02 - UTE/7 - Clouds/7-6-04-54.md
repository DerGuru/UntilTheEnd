The plum tree in the garden is old. Knobbed. The gnarled architecture of a tree that has been growing in the same soil for decades, the trunk thick and twisted, which tells about the resilience and endurance of a plant that has survived seasons that weaker plants did not. The bark is grey-brown. The branches spread in the asymmetric pattern that real growth produces — no two limbs the same length, no angle repeated.

Its blossoms are pink and white. The color-gradient that plum trees produce in the season of the climate — petals that are pink at the edge, white at the center, producing the overall impression of a canopy that is blushing. The scent is — she breathes. Sweet. Light. The particular signature that plum blossoms generate and that humans experience as beauty.

She sits beneath it. Her back against the trunk. The bark rough against her back — through the fabric, through the layers, the tree's surface pressing into her spine with the insistence of something that has been here longer than she has and is not adjusting to accommodate her.

Yun's eyes are open. The meditation — if the word still applies to what she is doing — happens with eyes open. She sits. The garden is there, the village is there, a child is playing somewhere three houses away, and the sound of the child is real, present, part of the meditation rather than separate from it.

She breathes.

The fear is there.

She lets it in.

She opens the door — the interior door that has been held closed for millennia, that was built by a child in a cave, maintained by a woman on a mountain and reinforced by every mechanism of survival that her body could deploy. She opens it.

And it comes through.

It is large. Larger than the room it was kept in. The expansion of a confined thing released into a larger space — filling the garden, the village, the sky. It is everywhere. In her chest. In her throat. In her fingertips that tingle. In her body that is experiencing the complete, unfiltered presence of the thing it has been filtering for millennia.

She drowns.

It is like an ocean. She is in it — no surface, no bottom, no shore — and it feels like the all-encompassing experience of being submerged in something total.

And she breathes.

Drowning and breathing. The impossibility of the combination is the point — the paradox of a woman who is completely submerged in fear, completely alive, the completeness of both states not canceling but coexisting. She drowns. She breathes.

A petal falls from the plum tree. Dislodged by wind — the light gust that moves through the garden, the atmospheric breath that the afternoon produces. The petal descends in a spiral — curved, erratic, a flat light object falling through air. It lands on her hand.

Pink. Light. Warm from the sun.

She looks at *it*.

It screams — has been screaming since she opened the door, the sustained output of a feeling that has been confined, is now free and is using its freedom to express its full volume. Nobody hears the scream. It is interior — cellular, molecular, the frequency that every part of her body generates when the survival mechanism registers that the gates are open.

She listens.

The way you listen to rain. Receiving it. Letting each drop arrive and pass. Listening. The passive reception of a sound that exists and will continue without your participation.

She listens to it the way she listens to rain.

And it gets quieter.

Still present. Still audible. But quieter. The gradual diminishment that occurs when the listener stops resisting and the sender discovers that resistance was part of its fuel. Without the fuel the sound still continues. But softer. Less. The volume dropping not because the source is weaker but because the amplifier has been removed.

She sits under the plum tree with a petal on her hand, her fear in her chest and it is quieter than it was a minute ago. The quiet is — not peace. Not yet. But the space in which peace might grow, the cleared ground that precedes planting, the silence that precedes sound.

A bird sings. 

She sits. She lets the fear go.

The petal lifts from her hand — wind again. The petal joins the air. Joins the wind. 

She watches it go.

The fear is still there. In the chest. In the garden.

But it is quieter.

A second petal lands on her hand.
