Yun settles under the old plum tree in the garden. Its thick trunk has twisted through decades of growth in the same soil. Gray-brown bark rises in knobs under her hand. Branches spread overhead at uneven angles, each a different length. She follows one limb to the blossoms at its end.

The petals are pink at the edges, fading to white at the center. Together they put a blush across the canopy. Their scent is light, sweet enough to follow without being overwhelmed. It lingers through the next breath.

She rests her back against the trunk. Rough bark presses through her clothing. The tree has held this ground far longer than her brief stay in the village. She shifts until its shape stops digging into her.

Her eyes are open. If this is still meditation, it includes the garden. A child plays three houses away, close enough for the sounds to carry over the fence. She lets them into her attention alongside the blossoms.

She breathes.

The fear is there.

She lets it in.

She lets the old barrier open. A child in a cave had made it; the woman on the mountain had kept reinforcing it with every attempt to survive. She feels its edges as she lets what lies behind them reach her.

And it comes through.

The fear is larger than the space that held it. It fills the garden, the village, and the sky beyond. It sits in her chest, then her throat. Her fingertips tingle. For once she lets the full sensation reach her without shutting off the part that is hardest to bear.

She drowns.

She is in an ocean of it, too deep to find a surface. It extends past anywhere she can turn her attention, and there is nothing else. The garden is still before her eyes, but fear runs through every part of the looking. She cannot find an edge. She stays against the bark while it fills the space she has opened.

And she breathes.

Drowning and breathing. The fear occupies everything, and her lungs still take in air. She is alive inside it. Another breath reaches the same depth as the last, though the sensation of submersion holds.

A light gust shakes a petal loose. It spirals down from the plum tree, turning unevenly. Yun follows its curve until it lands on her hand.

Pink. Light. Warm from the sun.

She looks at the petal as the fear rises.

It is a scream she cannot release through her throat. The same urgency fills every part of her, unbroken since she let the feeling through. She sits breathing quietly, and the pressure inside her is loud enough to fill the garden.

She listens.

She listens as she would to rain. One drop arrives, then another. Each sound can come without her stopping it or hurrying it along. Listening gives her something to do that leaves the feeling intact. She lets the next surge arrive before thinking about what follows.

She gives the fear that same attention.

And it gets quieter.

The feeling is as clear as before, but its urgency diminishes. She has stopped bracing against each wave, and the bracing had added its own strain. She lets a breath pass without preparing for the next surge.

The petal lies on her hand. Fear sits in her chest, quieter than when the petal fell. In that small easing is room for bark against her back and sunlight on her fingers. Peace could grow in a space like that.

A bird sings. 

She sits. She lets the fear go.

Wind lifts the petal from her hand. It turns in the air before drifting out of reach.

She watches it go.

The fear is still there. In the chest. In the garden.

But it is quieter.

A second petal lands on her hand.
