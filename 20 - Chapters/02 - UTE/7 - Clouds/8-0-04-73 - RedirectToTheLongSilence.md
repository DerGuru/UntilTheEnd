If you read Stones and Bones, you know how Yun's/Mei's story starts.

You've seen the girl who pressed her palm to warm earth and felt something without a name. You've seen the tribe, the blood, the throne she built because it was the only shape strength came in. You've watched her win.

Winning was not the end. It wasn't even the beginning of the real story.

Before Yun. Before the numbness had a reason. Before forever meant anything but "not yet."

There was a woman who walked away from everything she'd ruled. A world with no edge she could find. A warmth she didn't expect to find twice — in someone else's hands, at someone else's fire. And after that, a quiet that crept in as gradually as a room going dark, so gradually she never caught the moment the sun was gone.

She thought she was getting stronger. She didn't know what the strength would cost her — or who.

**"Until The End: Origins — The Long Silence"**

*What do you become, when nothing can take you and no one gets to stay?*

*Everything, for a while. And then, for a very long while, less and less.*

---

This is not *Stones and Bones*. The blood is mostly over; what's left is quieter and somehow harder to survive. And this is not *Until The End* either — that peace is still tens of thousands of years away, and she has almost everything left to lose before she earns it. The Long Silence is a part in between: a woman with more time than the world has patience, and fewer reasons every year to use it well.

The quiet is not peace. It's what's left when everything else finally stops arriving.
