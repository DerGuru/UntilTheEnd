For a few heartbeats, she becomes mortal.

Suddenly, there is time. Pure. Immediate. Her body that had lived so long in stasis now experiences it in full. Sseconds are not just passing, they are happening. The flow of time is no longer ambient, it is pervasive. Not just there, moving. Not just a concept, reality.

Her body becomes grounded.

The floor presses up. The cold presses in. She is *here* — in the cave, on the stone, in the body that sits on the body of the mountain. Present the way stones are present.

The fire — the small cave fire, the orange combustion that has been providing light and warmth and the regular crackle that has been counting time — jumps.

A leaf. Fallen from somewhere — blown in, perhaps, by the intermittent wind that pushes through the cave mouth. A dry leaf, sitting on her lap, on the grey fabric of her clothing. The leaf touches the fire's zone of influence. The heat reaches it. The leaf burns.

The burn transfers.

A spot on the fabric. Black. The charcoal marking of organic material combusted at the point of contact with textile — the tiny, measurable, visible evidence on her clothing. A burn mark. On her clothing.

And beneath it — through the fabric, against the skin — a small red dot. The size of a millet grain. The heat that should not have reached her. Did.

She stares at it.

Normally — for millennia, for the entire cataloged duration of her existence — the qi had shielded her. Automatically. Not as conscious decision but as fundamental operating parameter. The background process that protects the body from environmental damage, so deep, so automatic, so structurally integrated that her flesh might as well be stone.

Yet, the red point is there. On her thigh. Through the fabric. The evidence that for one heartbeat — for the exact duration of one cardiac cycle, one contraction, one pump — the protection lapsed.

She counts.

One. Two. Three.

She has counted before. Heartbeats. Intervals. Other people's time. But not this — not seconds of her own mortality measured in real-time. This counting has weight. Each number is a second she could have been hurt. Each number is evidence.

Four. Five.

The red dot remains. Red. Definite. The evidence.

The feeling is — the word forms as sensation rather than thought, as the bodily recognition of something the mind has only now caught up to.

FINALLY.

The feeling of the word. The somatic experience of arriving after a journey too long for the destination to be celebrated. Finally. The word that has been waiting for this moment to exist. The word that has been waiting for this moment to be true.

Six. Seven.

The heartbeat passes. The moment closes.

The qi returns — she feels it gathering at the edges of the hesitation, the defense mechanism recognizing the breach and mobilizing its response. But for seven seconds — for seven counted, witnessed, experienced seconds — the red dot exists and time flows through her. She is mortal. The mortality might not have been conscious but is happening anyway and —

The water drips. The fire crackles. The stone is cold. 

Already seven seconds of mortality in a life measured in millennia.

She does not look away from the red dot. She watches it the way she has watched everything important — without blinking, without moving, with the visual hunger of someone who has waited long enough that the waiting has become indistinguishable from the wanting.

The red dot. Red on skin. Proof.
