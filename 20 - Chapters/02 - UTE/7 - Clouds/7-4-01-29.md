The stream at the edge of the clearing ran clear over flat stones, its sound thin in the early light. Water slid past while you settled where you could see me.

"Are you ready?" you asked.

I was accustomed to having an answer when someone asked that. For so long, experience had supplied what I needed before a difficulty could become serious. Now I sat listening to the stream without knowing whether I could do what we had prepared for.

I began the demonstration anyway. You could watch what happened while my attention was occupied with making it happen. Afterward we would have your account as well as mine. That had become useful enough for me to want a witness.

I attended to the stream where it ran between mossy rocks. A ribbon of water rose from it, curving through the air. The flow continued along that arc wherever I knew it belonged. I held it without strain. Barely a score of days had brought me this far. After millennia of old skills, I had found something new to learn, and the speed of it was hard to enjoy without unease.

I returned the water to the stream. Its flow joined the current without disturbing the surface.

A closed bud stood on the bush nearest my right hand. I knew it open, and it opened. When I knew it closed, the petals folded again. I held a particular shade of pink in mind, somewhere between rose and dawn. The color spread across the petals.

I turned to the sunlight entering the clearing. It bent where I knew it would. The tree beside you stood still while its shadow moved left. The new angle of light fell across the ground.

The air warmed. I kept the change small enough for you to take without discomfort. Then I cooled it before letting the warmth return. I judged each alteration as it happened, stopping where I meant to.

Xiǎohú lifted off the ground. Her amber eyes widened as her paws left the grass. I set her down gently. She shook herself, then moved farther from me. I could have reached her there just as easily, but I left her resting place alone.

You watched the last change before nodding.

"You're ready," you said.

"I'm not."

The answer came easily this time. I had demonstrated everything we had practiced, but the next step still lay ahead.

"Why do you think so?"

I let three breaths pass, then a fourth. The morning air lay still around us. I attended to it closely enough to know I was leaving it alone. That bought me time to find the answer.

"Because I'm afraid."

I had named unease before, even fear. This admission reached the part I had avoided naming for so long. I sat with my hands in my lap, waiting to hear what you would make of it.

The temperature dropped around us. A breeze that had begun among the leaves ceased. The change touched my face as the words ended.

You waited. Your pen lay still, leaving me time to sit with what I had said. I listened to the stream.

"Good," you said at last, with a nod.

"How is that good?"

"Anyone who has no fear has nothing to lose." You set down your pen to look directly at me. "You're afraid because you need this to work, and because it might. That's a beginning."

I looked down. I could bend water, grow flowers, shift light, warm air, and lift objects. Each change had become precise enough for you to watch without flinching. My own hands were as they had been before the demonstration.

Turning that power inward was the task still ahead of me. I had wanted it for so long. Now that I could begin to see how it might happen, I hesitated.

You had called the fear a beginning. I looked at the pen beside your knee, then back at your face. I could not believe the answer, but I believed you meant it.
