She stands at the mouth of a cave. She recognizes the place. It feels like — not return, arrival. She has been here before, many times, across millennia, but she has never been here as this person. She has never been here old, white-haired, mortal. She has never been here as someone who chose to come. Every other time, the cave was a place she passed through or a place she fled to. This time, she walked here on old knees, up a mountain, because she wanted to stay.

The cave has not changed.

The world has changed. Empires had risen, collapsed, been forgotten and replaced by empires that did not know they were repeating a pattern. Languages had been born and died. Have left ghosts in the mouths of languages that came after. The surface of the planet has been rewritten a thousand times — forests becoming fields becoming cities becoming ruins becoming forests again, the patient cycle of a world that never finishes revising itself.

The cave has not changed.

The stone walls are the same. Grey, dense, the geological stubbornness of a formation that was already old when Yun was young. The entrance is still in the same shape — irregular, narrower at the top, wider at the base, the organic geometry that water and time carved into the rock face before either of them had a name.

She enters.

The dimness is the same. The light recedes behind her, the visible world contracting to the irregular rectangle of the entrance, the interior assembling itself from shadow into presence the way a room assembles when your eyes adjust. The air is cool. The subterranean temperature that caves maintain regardless of the season above — their own quiet climate, indifferent to the weather's drama.

Water drips. Different water, same drip. The same acoustic signature — plip, pause, plip — that she remembers from the beginning, when she was small enough that the cave seemed large. The drip was the only sound. She has heard it across millennia. Has heard it in memory when she was a thousand li away. Has carried the sound of it the way you carry the sound of a voice you loved — not in the ears, but somewhere deeper, somewhere the body stores things it refuses to forget.

She sits down on the stone. The same position a child chose because it was far enough from the entrance to be warm, close enough to see light. The stone is cool under her and her bones feel it more than they used to. She adjusts, finds a suitable position and settles.

She remembers her time as a child in this cave. Small. On this stone. The child was afraid. The child was alone. The child refused to die. Not as a thought or a decision — as a refusal, bone-deep, wordless, becoming the shape of what she was.

Now she is old. Warm. Peaceful. A woman in this cave. On the same stone. Hearing the same dripping water. Seeing the same grey walls in the same light. But different from the child in every way that matters. The child refused. The woman accepted. The distance between those two things is measured in many millennia and in the lives she had lived and lost and carried.

Xiǎohú settles beside her. The warm weight of the animal against her hip, the familiar pressure that has been there for years now. Bright eyes catching the entrance-light, reflecting it back in amber. Xiǎohú does not know what this place is. Xiǎohú does not care. She is warm and the stone is cool and the woman is here, and that is sufficient.

Through the entrance Yun sees clouds. They are moving across a sky that in the blue of late afternoon in mountains. The clouds are drifting. The slow transit of high-altitude formations of a world that is always in motion even when it looks still.

Yun. 云. Cloud. Her name. 

She chose it — or maybe she didn't. Sometimes a name chooses you, which is to say they arrive and you recognize them. Clouds form and move and dissolve. The dissolving is not death. The dissolving is transition — water becoming vapor becoming cloud becoming rain becoming water, the cycle that does not end because ending is not what cycles do.

She watches the clouds through the entrance. Xiǎohú watches with her. Amber eyes, bright, steady — the patient gaze of an animal that watches without judgment, is present without condition.

She is tired. A good tiredness — the tiredness of an old woman who has climbed a mountain for days and is sitting on a stone at the end of a long day, a long journey, a long life. 

She sits in the cave. Her cave. The first cave. The last cave. The same cave.

The clouds move outside the entrance. The water drips its patient rhythm. Xiǎohú breathes beside her, the small, warm bellows of an animal at rest.

She breathes.

She is home.
