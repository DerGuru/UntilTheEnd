She rests at the mouth of the cave, old knees still carrying the ache of the climb. She recognizes the entrance. Across millennia she has passed through this place or fled toward it, bringing another part of her life to the same stone. This time she arrives white-haired and mortal. She has walked here because she wants to stay.

The cave has not changed.

Outside it, empires have risen, collapsed, and been forgotten. Others have repeated their patterns without knowing the people who came before them. Languages have left fragments in the tongues that followed them, old sounds surviving after their meanings changed. She has watched forests become fields, cities rise over fields, and trees grow through the ruins. She could cross a valley she once knew and find the river elsewhere, the settlement buried, the name of the place forgotten.

The cave has not changed.

Gray walls enclose the same space. The stone was already old when she was young. Above her, the entrance narrows irregularly before widening at the base. Water shaped those edges long before she had names for either the place or the force that formed it.

She enters.

Light recedes behind her until the entrance frames an irregular patch of sky. As her eyes adjust, the interior grows distinct in the shadow. Cool air touches her face. The cave holds its own temperature, sheltered from the season outside. She remembers that separate quiet from childhood, the small climate of stone under the changing weather. She can leave the exposed slope behind and still see its light.

Water drips in the old rhythm: plip, a pause, and another plip. She remembers it from the beginning, when her small body made the cave seem large. At times it had been the only sound. She has heard it in memory a thousand li away, carrying it as she carries a beloved voice. The water itself has passed through the mountain countless times since then. The sound has kept its shape. Now it reaches her directly from the stone, close enough for her to wait between one drop and the next.

She sits where the child once chose to sit, far enough inside for shelter but close enough to see light. Her bones feel the cool stone more sharply now. She adjusts, finds a position, and settles.

She remembers the small body curled on this stone. She had been afraid and alone, holding to survival before she could have explained the need. That wordless refusal became the shape in which she lived.

Now she is old, at ease inside the same walls. The child refused an ending; the woman has accepted one. Between those two responses lie millennia, lives she has lost, and lives she still carries. She hears the same water and remembers the distance she has traveled. The light falls on the same gray surfaces. She has brought a different life back to them, old enough to rest where the child once curled against the cold.

Xiǎohú settles against her hip. The weight has accompanied her for years. Light from the entrance catches the amber eyes as the fox turns toward Yun. Warmth reaches Yun through her clothing, enough to make the cool stone comfortable.

Clouds cross the late-afternoon blue outside. She watches their slow movement above the mountains. Even where the ridge seems motionless, wind continues over it.

Yun. 云. Cloud. Her name.

She remembers a cloud, and then the name. It is as much found as chosen. Vapor gathers into cloud, falls as rain, and rises again from water. She watches a shape dissolve above the ridge, leaving room for the blue beyond it.

Xiǎohú turns toward the entrance too, its light reflected in the steady amber eyes. The fox rests against her hip as she has in so many other places. Yun lets the pressure stay, comforted by a presence she does not need to explain the cave to.

She is tired in a way she can welcome. An old woman has climbed for days to reach this stone. She can rest at the end of the day, the journey, and the long life that brought her here.

She sits in the cave. Her cave. The first cave. The last cave. The same cave.

Clouds move outside the entrance. Water drips on in its steady rhythm. Against her hip, Xiǎohú's flank rises with each quiet breath.

She breathes.

She is home.
