Rain came overnight, persistent enough to keep the cave's drip in my thoughts. I lay awake, holding it away from us. Outside our shelter, water ran down the mountain.

By morning, the world was washed. The trail steamed where the early sun hit wet stone. You packed the camp in the wordless routine we'd developed: bedrolls, cook pot, and notebook wrapped in oilcloth. I scattered the fire's ashes. We walked higher. The trail thinned between exposed rocks, with only scattered scrub growing along it. Above us, the sky had cleared.

You hadn't spoken since we'd started. For a quarter of a watch there were only our footsteps, our breathing, and the creak of pack straps. I began to wonder whether you were waiting for me to speak. Then you shifted the weight on your shoulders, eyes on the trail.

"Learn it as a tool," you said. "Then put it down."

I stopped.

"What?"

"You're trying to force a door that opens inward," you said. "Every time you push, it closes harder. So stop pushing."

"And do what?"

"Learn how it works first. You can warp reality. You've proven that. But it's uncontrolled. It happens defensively. What if you controlled it?"

"I've controlled it. At the river. The sect—"

"That was reactive. I need them gone, they're gone. What I mean is practiced control. Deliberate work, like a craftsman's. Small things. Then bigger things. Then..." You waited, glancing back over your shoulder.

"Then myself."

"Then yourself."

I considered as the trail climbed. Xiǎohú was ahead, as always, placing her paws easily between rocks that forced us to shorten our steps.

"Since when are you that clever?" I said.

"I wasn't considered one of the best cultivators of my sect for no reason. Body tempering only gets you so far."

I almost smiled.

For two days I had been running headfirst into something I had built myself. You were suggesting I examine it first. I could learn where the weight rested, which parts supported others, and what happened when I moved one small piece. Once I understood that, I might take it apart without bringing everything down around us.

Craftsmanship.

"How?" I asked.

"Start small. You bent a tree without meaning to. Bend one on purpose. Understand how it works from the inside. Watch what you do when it happens. Where the conviction sits. What it feels like. How the qi responds. Map yourself."

You were right. I looked down at the wet trail, hunting for a response that would let me admit it with some dignity. Gratitude would have been appropriate. I settled for a question.

"You've been thinking about this," I said.

"I've been thinking about you," you said. And then, before the sentence could acquire weight, "In a clinical sense. Observational."

"Of course."

"I'm a scholar."

"Of course."

We walked. High, thin clouds had formed from the remnants of yesterday's cover. Their edges changed above the ridge as the wind carried them. One narrowed until blue showed through its center. I was starting to envy how easily it changed.

The steps were clear but the path between them was not. I stood at the beginning of it with a fox and a scholar.

"We need to find a place," I said. "Somewhere stable. Where I can practice without—" I gestured at the barren rock around us. "Without this. I need things to work with. Trees. Water. Something that responds."

You studied the ridge ahead, then the trail we'd come up. Below, the treeline made a dark band of green across the slope.

"So we go back down," you said. "There was a plateau. Half a watch below the treeline, maybe a full watch. Flat ground. A stream."

"You noticed that while walking?"

"I notice everything while walking. It's a curse."

The corner of your mouth moved. "A plateau with trees and a stream. And enough distance from anything you could accidentally flatten."

"That's the idea."

We turned around on the narrow trail. You shifted your pack for the descent. I followed, scanning for the plateau below the trees.
