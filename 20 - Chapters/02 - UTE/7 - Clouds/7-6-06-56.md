After dark, she climbs the wooden ladder against the back wall. The rungs are worn smooth by previous climbers, who presumably had their own reasons for wanting to be above their ceilings.

The roof is flat. Stone tiles hold the afternoon's warmth and give it back to her palms.

She lies down facing the sky.

Stars fill the clear sky, so many that the constellations she knows nearly disappear among them. She lets her eyes drift past the patterns to the sheer extent overhead.

She looks at the stars, but her thoughts are elsewhere.

Fearless in order to accept the fear. Xu Ran's words return with the memory of steam rising from a pot of not-rice. They had sounded absurd then. They sound less absurd here.

The fear of death built her. Not now. The refusal reaches back past the words she has for it, into the child who needed to survive another night. It became conviction, then law. Her qi, her renewal, and the resistance she keeps meeting all rest on that beginning. She has repeated it through every threat to her life.

Fear became the source of her immortality. A girl who did not understand what she was doing built the foundation; a woman who did not understand what she had done kept adding to it. Eventually that certainty could change the world around her.

The tiles warm her back. Xu Ran had given her a description she can almost follow now. If she could allow the fear itself to remain, she might stop reinforcing what it built. The foundation could ease through something quieter than another attempt to tear it out.

A shooting star.

A bright line crosses the dark. A fragment that has traveled many millions of li burns through the sky in three heartbeats. She follows it until the trail fades and the stars return.

Wei would have said: "Make a wish."

And Yun would have said nothing.

He would have grinned at her silence. She can picture it. He would keep talking, and she would let him.

She lies under the stars with Wei's face in memory, the calm face at the end of his life. Even there he was unimpressed, as fully himself as when he argued over rice.

He had been fearless.

He had known fear. She remembers him on his knees in the dirt, blood on his lip. "I'm going to die." His voice had been too flat to sound brave. She had seen what frightened him, then watched him make decisions that fear could not dictate.

His calm at the end belonged to those choices. He had spent his short life refusing to be governed by what frightened him. The practice had reached deeply enough that she had mistaken its result for an absence of fear.

He could accept because he had practiced refusing to run.

She has answered fear differently. For millennia she built walls, cut herself off, and made herself unreachable. The feeling sat behind those defenses, as powerful as at the beginning. She had rarely let herself meet it directly.

But she can stop?

Every wall she built began with the certainty that something was dangerous. Each time she suppressed the feeling, she confirmed that she could not bear contact with it. She had kept the protection firm because she was still afraid.

Here the fear is present. She can acknowledge it, allow it, and survive the contact. It has not ended her on this rooftop. She can lie under the stars with the feeling beside her breath. Perhaps she has been able to for far longer than she let herself know.

The stars turn. The slow rotation that is actually the earth spinning beneath them.

She breathes through the quiet, the fear in her chest.

She can stop being afraid of the ending. What follows, what happens to the conviction when its foundation shifts, is beyond this small decision. She need not dictate the result as well. The protection may change once she stops sustaining the need for it.

The stars turn. The roof is warm. The village sleeps below.

She lies on her back with the fear. Its weight is familiar, and the roof holds her up. For now she leaves the feeling where it is instead of building another barrier between it and herself.

She can be with it as she is with the weather, with the weight of her body, with the ordinary conditions of being alive.

Or not.
