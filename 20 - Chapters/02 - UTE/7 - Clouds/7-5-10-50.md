The path leads down. She descends with it. Slowly.

The scrub gives way to single trees. The trees multiply into forest. The canopy closes overhead, transforms the landscape from open to enclosed, from sky-visible to sky-implied, from the exposed geography of the summit to the sheltered geography of the descent.

She feels the weight of her legs. Not because she is tired — she does not tire, not in the functional sense, the qi maintaining the muscles and the joints regardless of distance or duration. She feels the weight because she is allowing the weight. A small permission. Conscious. The deliberate un-deflection of a force that has been deflected automatically for millennia.

The weight is — grounding.

Xiǎohú moves through the underbrush with the efficiency of an animal in its element, the terrain-matching that smaller mammals achieve, body low, movement economical, bright eyes in the green shadow. She knows where she is going. Animals always know where they are going.

The fear is there.

Named and visible. In her chest, below sternum, above diaphragm. She walks with it the way she walks with the weight — acknowledging, allowing, neither fighting nor following.

It has not been defeated. It cannot be defeated. This is her understanding now. Fear is something you see. And then you live with.

A stone shifts under her foot. The looseness of mountain trail that makes descents treacherous and keeps her attentive to each step. She adjusts. The ankle compensates, the ancient instinct that converts loss of balance into continuation of movement.

Through the trees, below the forest, smoke becomes visible.

It rises in columns of white-grey. The color of wood-smoke — cooking fires, heating fires, the small contained fires that people maintain. The smoke rises vertically in the still air and then disperses at altitude, spreading, becoming clouds in the sky. Clouds from below.

Yun arrives at the village.

It is small. Much like Wei's village had been what felt a lifetime ago. A mortal's lifetime. There are some houses — she can see rooflines through the thinning trees. Angular. Orderly. The organization of structures that reflects the organization of lives, of a species that cannot help but keep organizing.

A market place forms in the center. Or the suggestion of one — an open space between structures where the ground is more worn than the surrounding ground, where the earth is compacted by the repeated passage of feet. The evidence of commerce is recorded in the soil.

People bustle around.

She can hear them before she sees them. Voices at varying distances, the conversational murmur that groups produce. The sound that humanity makes when humanity is not performing for an audience but simply — existing.

She stops at the forest's edge. The village is in front of her. Xiǎohú sits beside her, eyes on the village, ears tilted forward, assessing the settlement.

There is no desire. Neither a push nor a pull of need.

But the next step is in there. Among the smoke and the voices and the organized structures of ordinary lives, of ordinary people, who are mortal and live with their mortality. Daily. Unremarkably. The way you have weather.

She takes the step.

The treeline opens. The path widens. The village assembles itself from impression into detail — walls, doors, windows, the material vocabulary of human construction. Close enough now to smell individual cooking, to see the laundry. Close enough to hear the individual voices separating from the communal murmur into distinct conversations, distinct lives.

She walks toward it.

Above her, the clouds thin. For one moment the sky opens. Blue. The particular deep-altitude blue that exists only where the air is thin enough to stop pretending.

Then the clouds close again. But the blue was there.

She walks toward the village.
