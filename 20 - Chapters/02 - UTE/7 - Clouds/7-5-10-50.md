She follows the path down slowly. Single trees rise among the scrub, growing closer together as she descends. Then the canopy closes overhead. Sky shows between branches where the ridge was open, and the air is sheltered against her face.

Her legs are heavy. Qi sustains them through the descent, maintaining the joints as it has for so long. She lets the weight be, inside that protection. It is a small permission: a sensation to attend to without making the effort vanish at once.

Each step settles her more firmly against the ground.

Xiǎohú slips through the underbrush ahead. Her body stays low under the branches, each movement carrying her cleanly through a gap. Bright eyes catch the green shadow when she turns back to Yun, then goes on.

The fear is there.

It sits under her sternum. She walks with it as she walks with the weight of her legs, allowing the sensation without taking direction from it.

She had tried to defeat it. Now it is something to live with, one part of herself that goes along with the next step.

A loose stone shifts under her foot. She adjusts her ankle, setting her weight on firmer ground before taking the next step. The descent holds her attention on the ground.

Through the trees, below the forest, smoke rises.

White-gray columns rise from the small fires people keep for warmth and cooking. In the still air, the smoke climbs straight before spreading higher up. It thins into cloudlike patches above the trees. Clouds from below.

Yun arrives at the village.

The village is small, much like Wei's was what feels like a lifetime ago. A mortal's lifetime. Rooflines show through the thinning trees, their angles close together around the open ground. She begins to tell where one household ends and another begins.

An open space lies near the center. The earth there is more worn than the ground around it, compacted by repeated passage. It has the shape of a marketplace, somewhere people gather often enough to leave that difference in the soil.

People bustle around.

Voices reach her before she can tell the people speaking. They overlap at different distances, ordinary conversations going on out of sight. Someone finishes a sentence; another voice answers farther off.

She stops at the forest's edge. Xiǎohú sits next to her with ears tilted toward the voices. They look down at the village together. Smoke rises past the nearest roof. Someone prepares food; another person crosses the open ground. Those small arrangements go on without waiting for her.

Little pulls her toward it. She can choose the next step anyway.

It lies among the smoke, the voices, and the ordinary arrangements of lives that will end. Those people live with mortality each day. She has reached the place where she might learn to do the same.

She takes the step.

The path widens beyond the treeline. She makes out walls, doors, and windows now. Cooking smells separate as she approaches; laundry hangs where she saw only rooflines. The voices resolve into conversations, each close enough to follow if she chooses.

She walks toward it.

Above her, the clouds thin enough to reveal a patch of deep blue between their moving edges.

Then the clouds close again. But the blue was there.

She walks toward the village.
