I was pushing again, hands against the stone. An eighth attempt, then a ninth. Repetition had settled into a rhythm when the cold under my fingers brought something up from much deeper.

The memory was old.

It reached past the shaping hands, past anything I had told you so far. I left my hands where they were while the image sharpened.

I remembered a cave. Stone enclosed a narrow space, cold even where my body touched it. The walls drew closer as I recalled their shape. In that memory my fingers were small enough to fit into a crack matching the one under my present hand.

No one else was there.

"Where are you?" you asked.

Your voice reached me through the recollection. Spring air moved across my face on the mountainside while I tried to understand the small body I remembered. A child? I had lived so long that even recognizing myself took effort. Those fingers belonged to the beginning of me.

"Nowhere," I said.

My eyes were wet at the corners. I tightened my jaw, but my hands reached past the stone for something I remembered touching. Your eyes dropped to them.

"Describe nowhere," you said.

You had given me something I could do. If you had asked how I felt, I could have sent you away with an answer that told you nothing. Rough stone was easier. I began there.

"It's cold," I said. "And tight. Rough stone."

"Where?"

"I'm in a cave. Small. Underground, I think. There's very little light. What comes in is gray."

"When do you think this happened?"

"I'm not sure. I'm smaller than now. I must be a child. So the beginning? The very beginning. My beginning? I don't know how long ago that was. But it feels like—" I stopped, unable to continue.

You stopped writing in the middle of a stroke. The pen rested on the paper, darkening the unfinished mark. Then you lifted it and met my eyes.

"Your beginning?"

"I think so."

The images were fragmentary but clear. After so many attempts I could reach past some of the resistance. It still pressed against me, but I could hold the memory long enough to describe it, which had been impossible before.

Cold stone lay under those small fingers. I remembered drawing my knees close to keep what warmth I had. My breath sounded loud against the walls. Past it came water gurgling, then the faint movements of my body on the floor. Each sound ended in that close space.

"I was alone," I said. "There was a cave and I was in it. And I was alone."

You wrote in careful strokes. Usually your pen hurried to keep up with me. This time you gave each word room, looking up between phrases to make sure I was still with the memory.

"How old were you in the memory?" you asked.

"Young. I don't know an age. Just the body. Small. Cold. The hands were smaller than mine."

"What else?"

"Water. The sound of it was constant. Like the cave's heartbeat."

The image grew fainter as I spoke. I tried to hold the sound of water longer, but the wind across the ridge was clearer now.

I opened my eyes to the mountain. You sat next to me. Xiǎohú watched from the far side of the scattered stones.

"That's where it started," I said.

"A cave."

"A cave."

You glanced at me, then down at the page where you had stopped mid-stroke.

You wrote.

I sat in the mountain air with the metallic taste on my tongue. The cold of the cave still lay in those smaller hands.

The clouds moved overhead.

I had been a child in a cave, alone, at the beginning of everything.
