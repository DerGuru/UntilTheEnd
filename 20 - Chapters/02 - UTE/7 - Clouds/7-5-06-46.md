Yun sits on the stone floor, which holds her with the indifferent support that stone provides to everything placed upon it.

Her hands rest on her knees. Open. Upward. The posture of not-gripping, the physical configuration of palms that have decided — or have been too exhausted to decide otherwise — to stop holding.

The dark is thorough. Cave-dark — deeper than night, which the sky salts with stars and the horizon outlines with silhouette. The visual silence of a space that light does not reach.

She sits in the dark because the dark is where she is.

A sound emerged. Deeper than the dripping, lower, the acoustic signature of stone settling. The geological micro-movement that mountains produce over centuries, compressed into an audible instant. A click. A shift. The cave adjusting its relationship with the earth.

She breathes. The breath cloud is visible to no one, audible to no one.

The fear is still there.

She examines it. From the inside. The fear has texture. It felt smooth in places — the worn areas where she's handled it without knowing. Like a patina that develops on frequently touched surfaces. It was rough in others — the raw edges, where the fear meets the rest of her without transition, the abrupt boundaries between afraid and everything else.

The fear has volume. Almost like a presence that occupies space in her chest, that displaces other things, that takes up room. 

She sits with it.

The way you sit with weather. The way you sit with a landscape. The way you sit with something that is going to be there regardless of your position on the matter and therefore requires cohabitation rather than resolution.

A gust of wind enters through the cave mouth. Cold. The atmospheric breath of the mountain entering the mountain's lungs. The fire's remains stir. Ash lifts and settles.

Different. She is different from yesterday. It was invisible yesterday. Today it is visible. It was unnamed yesterday. Today it is named. It was abstract yesterday. Today it is almost palpable. An emotion that has been living in her body for millennia and has only now been given form.

The form does not help. But having one changes the relationship. The difference much like being lost and being lost-with-a-map. The map does not make the terrain passable, but the map makes the terrain identifiable, makes it known. And knowledge is the ground on which the next step will be taken. Whatever that step is.

Yun stands.

She walks to the cave mouth. Morning light arrives — the diffuse grey of an overcast sky filtering into the cave entrance, the illumination of a world that is lit but muted, present but overcast.

The stone beneath her feet is rough. She feels the texture through her feet — the geological braille of a cave floor, its composition readable through the feet walking on it.

She stands at the mouth and takes a look outward. The mountain is there. The clouds are there. The geography of a high-altitude landscape that does not care about her fear or her understanding of it.

The wind touches her face. Cold. Real. Felt.

She stands at the threshold between dark and light, between inside and outside, between the place where the truth was felt and the place where the truth must be lived.

Xiǎohú emerges beside her. Amber eyes blinking at the grey light. She shakes herself and looks up at Yun.

She stands. She breathes. The air tastes of rain.

Nobody tells her what to do next.

She has to decide that on her own. 

Like it's always been. 

Yun takes a step.
