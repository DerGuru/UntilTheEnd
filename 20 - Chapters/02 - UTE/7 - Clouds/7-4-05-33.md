The evening routine. Your back to me, shirt pulled up to the shoulders, the vertebrae visible in the firelight like a string of pale stones. The meridians ran familiar under my attention — open, flowing, the channels I had mapped so many times they were more familiar than geography.

I worked without thinking. The qi moved through me and into the pathways and the pathways responded and the body beneath my hands was warm and alive and functioning within the parameters that a mortal body functioned within. Adequate. Stable. The slow maintenance that kept the channels clear and the circulation smooth and the damage from the broken core from spreading further than it already had.

The core.

I checked it out of habit. The way you checked a wound you'd been monitoring — the peripheral glance, the diagnostic sweep, the quick assessment that confirmed what you already knew: still broken, still fractured, still the damage that my proximity had caused years ago when my power had been unconscious and my presence had been poison.

I stopped.

The fracture was smaller.

Not subtly. Not the imperceptible shift that optimism could fabricate from insufficient data. Smaller. Measurably. The edges that had been ragged were smooth now. New tissue — if tissue was even the word for the substance that cores were made of — had grown across the gap. Scar tissue. Repair tissue. The biological vocabulary didn't fit, but the phenomenon was unmistakable: the core was healing.

I held my breath. Checked again. More carefully this time, the diagnostic attention narrowed to a point, the full weight of my perception focused on the fracture line that I had monitored for years and found unchanged, unchanged, unchanged — until now.

Smaller. By a third, maybe more. The edges knitting. The gap filling.

I hadn't done this. I had never worked on the core. Never touched it, never directed qi at it, never attempted the repair that would have required a precision I didn't trust myself to have. The core was his and the damage was mine and the repair was — whose?

Mine.

The realization arrived quietly. The power that had broken this core — the unconscious, uncontrolled, proximity-based radiation of a being whose conviction warped reality without consent — that same power was healing it. The same mechanism. The same source. The same involuntary influence that had cracked it was now closing the crack.

Because something had changed. In me. The direction of the influence had shifted — not from destructive to constructive, not that simple — but from unconscious and harmful to unconscious and... restorative. The months of work. The bricks. The training. The slow, painful recalibration of a power that had spent millennia operating without oversight. Something had shifted. Not everything. Not enough. But enough that the passive effect of my presence on his body had reversed its polarity.

I was healing him. By being near him.

You didn't notice. Your breathing was even, the steady rhythm of a man who had been through this routine enough times that it no longer required alertness. The channels were open. The qi was flowing. From your perspective: maintenance. Routine. Tuesday.

I finished the routine. Set your shirt down. Sat back.

"Anything?" you asked. The standard question. The post-maintenance check-in that you always asked and I always answered.

"Nothing new," I said.

The lie was small. The lie was necessary. The lie was — tactical. Because if the core was healing at this rate, it needed days. A week, maybe. A week of proximity, of nearness, of the passive influence that was doing what active intention couldn't. A week of me being close enough for the power to complete what it had started.

A week of keeping you here.

You turned. Pulled the shirt down. Sat against your boulder and reached for the notebook and the pen and the posture of evening that constituted your end-of-day ritual: documentation, review, the scholar's habit of processing the day through the filter of writing.

I needed a reason.

A reason for you to stay. To not suggest moving, not propose the next leg of travel, not begin the conversation about returning that had been circling the edges of our exchanges for days. You had work. You had a life below this mountain. You had obligations and connections and the entire apparatus of a mortal existence that didn't pause because an immortal woman needed proximity time.

A reason.

The fire crackled. The stars were beginning their nightly emergence. The air was cooling in the way mountain air cooled after dark — rapidly, thoroughly, the temperature dropping like a stone through water.

I looked at you. Writing. Your face in the warm light of the fire, the concentration, the pen moving, the man who had spent months documenting my power and my training and my slow unraveling of a prison I'd built millennia ago. The man who recorded everything and understood most of it and asked the questions that mattered.

The man whose core was healing because I was near him. Who didn't know it. Who would leave if I didn't give him a reason to stay.

"I want to tell you something," I said.

You looked up.

"Tomorrow. Not tonight. Tomorrow. I want to tell you — about the beginning. The real beginning. All of it."

The pen stopped. Your expression shifted — the recognition of something offered that hadn't been offered before. The complete history. The unredacted version. The thing I had parceled out in fragments and allusions and careful selections across months of conversation.

"All of it?" you said.

"All of it."

You nodded. Once. The nod that contained volumes — the agreement, the commitment, the understanding that this was both gift and obligation, that receiving a story was a form of contract with the teller.

"Tomorrow," you said.

Tomorrow. And the day after. And the day after that. As many days as the story needed, as many evenings as the telling required, as many nights of proximity as the core needed to close its fracture.

The reason was real. I did want to tell you. I owed you the truth, the complete truth, the unbroken narrative that would fill your notebook and give you the record you had been assembling piece by piece. The reason was genuine.

It was also a strategy.

I lay awake that night. The fire died. The stars rotated. Your breathing deepened into sleep, the even rhythm of a man who didn't know his body was repairing itself, who didn't know that the woman beside him was the medicine, who didn't know that proximity was healing what proximity had broken.

For the first time in millennia, my presence had healed something.

Not destroyed. Not broken. Not erased.

Healed.

I lay in the dark with that knowledge and it was warm and it was terrifying and it was the closest thing to hope I had felt since the cave.
