You sat with your back to me, your shirt pulled up to the shoulders. Firelight picked out your vertebrae, a string of pale stones under the skin. The meridians lay open and flowing under my attention, channels I had mapped so often that I knew them better than the country we traveled.

I worked without thinking. Qi moved through me into the pathways, and they answered under my hands. Your body was warm and alive, its ordinary functions steady. Adequate. Stable. I kept the channels clear so the circulation could pass around the broken core. That slow maintenance had kept the damage from spreading farther.

The core.

I checked it out of habit, like a wound I had been monitoring. A quick sweep should have confirmed what I already knew: the fracture sat where I had caused it years ago, stopping your ascension before it could destroy a village.

I stopped.

The fracture was smaller.

The difference was small enough that I distrusted it. I examined the ragged edges again. They were smoothing. Qi had accumulated along the fracture lines, dense layers filling the gaps as sediment fills a riverbed. Slowly. Persistently. I traced the new deposits along a break I knew well. The core was healing.

I held my breath and checked again, narrowing my attention to a point, the full weight of my perception on the fracture I had monitored for months. Every time, I had found it unchanged. Or so I had thought. Each change must have been so minute that I had mistaken it for what was already there.

Perhaps a fifth had healed, maybe less. The edges were knitting. The gap was filling.

I had confined my work to the surrounding channels. I had left the core untouched, avoiding a repair that would require a precision I did not trust myself to have. Each evening I had stopped at the same boundary.

I followed the qi flowing into the core. My proximity carried an abundance of excess qi. It always had. That passive flood had pushed Wei into growth he could not sustain and forced other cultivators past their limits. Here the qi entered the core without leaving it. It accumulated, building layer upon layer along the breaks. I had intended none of this, yet the repair went on under my hands. A wound could heal by itself when the conditions allowed it. Those conditions were at work in you.

The difference between you and the others was the broken core itself: a place where the qi could go. It crystallized in the cracks like mineral deposits in stone. Layer by layer, day by day. The clearer I kept your channels, the more efficiently the excess reached the fracture. The more reached it, the more it filled.

I had been delivering the medicine every evening for months. The routine I performed out of habit, out of care, and out of the simple need to keep your meridians open had been preparing a repair I did not know was possible.

I was healing you, just by being near you.

Your breathing stayed even. You had been through the routine often enough to relax under my hands. The channels were open. Qi flowed as it always did, giving you no reason to suspect anything had changed. Another evening's maintenance.

I finished the work.

"Anything?" you asked. The standard question, the post-maintenance check-in you always asked and I always answered.

"I'm not sure, but it looks like something is happening to your core," I said. "It's healing."

You turned slowly, giving the words time to reach you. You pulled your shirt down and sat to face me. I had all your attention now, though your breathing was as measured as it had been under my hands.

"Healing?" you repeated, testing the word before accepting it.

"The fracture. It's smaller. Qi has been accumulating along the cracks. Crystallizing. Filling them." I paused. "I think it's been happening for months. I just didn't see it until now."

"How much?"

"A fifth. Maybe less."

You were quiet. The fire crackled. Your hand moved toward the pen, then stopped short of it. For a while you looked at me without putting the discovery on paper.

"The maintenance," you said. "The meridian work." You had reached the same conclusion I had.

"It clears the path. The excess qi from my proximity reaches the core." I looked at the fire. "The same thing that pushed Wei too fast. That overwhelmed others. It's depositing in the cracks. Because the cracks are there to fill."

"A broken vessel collecting what would have burst an intact one."

"Yes."

You nodded slowly. Then you reached for the notebook. Small, precise characters filled the page as you recorded the discovery. You left room below them for what we had yet to learn.

"How long?" you asked without looking up. "To completion."

"I don't know. This is the first time I saw it." I paused. "I'll watch it. Over the next days. Maybe we can extrapolate from there."

You nodded. The pen moved again, adding a note with a question mark next to it. You had a place for things we did not yet know.

"You didn't know?" you asked. Not accusation. Verification.

"I didn't know."

The fire crackled as stars emerged. The air cooled as mountain air cooled after dark: rapidly, thoroughly.

I lay awake that night. Your breathing deepened into sleep, even and slow. You knew now that your body was repairing itself and that the woman next to you was the medicine.

For the first time in millennia, I could look at what my presence had done without finding damage at the end of it. I had examined the fracture twice. Under my hands, it had been healing.

I lay in the dark with that knowledge. Your breathing went on next to me, warm life I had helped instead of harmed. I wanted the repair to continue. The wanting frightened me, but I held on to it. It was the closest thing to hope I had.
