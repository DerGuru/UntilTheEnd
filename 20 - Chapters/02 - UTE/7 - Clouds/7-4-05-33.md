The evening routine. Your back to me, shirt pulled up to the shoulders, the vertebrae visible in the firelight like a string of pale stones. The meridians ran familiar under my attention — open, flowing, the channels I had mapped so many times they were more familiar than geography.

I worked without thinking. The qi moved through me and into the pathways. The pathways responded and the body beneath my hands was warm and alive. Functioning within parameters that a mortal body functioned within. Adequate. Stable. The slow maintenance that kept the channels clear and the circulation smooth and the damage from the broken core from spreading further than it already had.

The core.

I checked it out of habit. The way you checked a wound you'd been monitoring — the peripheral glance, the diagnostic sweep, the quick assessment that's supposed to confirm what you already knew. Still broken, still fractured, still the damage that I had caused years ago when I prevented your ascension to kill a village.

I stopped.

The fracture was smaller.

Not just the imperceptible shift that optimism could fabricate from insufficient data. Small but measurable. The edges that had been ragged were smoothing. Qi had accumulated along the fracture lines — dense, layered, filling the gaps the way sediment fills a riverbed. Slowly. Persistently. The core was healing.

I held my breath. Checked again. More carefully this time, the diagnostic attention narrowed to a point, the full weight of my perception focused on the fracture line that I had monitored for months now and found unchanged every time. Or so I had thought. It must have changed in such a minute way, I hadn't recognized it as such — until now.

The changes were small. Healed by a fifth, maybe less. But the edges were knitting. The gap filling.

I hadn't done this. I had never worked on the core. Never touched it, never directed qi at it, never attempted the repair that would have required a precision I didn't trust myself to have. 

I was focusing all my attention to the core and the qi that was flowing into it. My proximity — the passive flood that had pushed Wei into too-fast growth, that had forced unnamed cultivators past their limits — carried excess qi in abundance. It always had. The qi was flowing to the core, entering it but never left. It was accumulating. Building. Layering. The healing process that I hadn't intended but that was happening anyway, the way a wound heals on its own when the conditions are right and the body can do what it does without interference.

The realization arrived like a revelation. The difference between you and all the others was that you had a broken core, a place where the qi could go. It crystallized. Settled into the cracks like mineral deposits in stone. Layer by layer. Day by day. The clearer I kept your channels, the more efficiently the excess reached the fracture. The more it reached, the more it filled.

I had been delivering the medicine every evening for months. The maintenance routine — the one I performed out of habit, out of care, out of the simple need to keep your meridians open — had been creating the conditions for repair without my knowing it. 

I was healing you, just by being near you.

You didn't notice. Your breathing was even, the steady rhythm of a man who had been through this routine enough times that it no longer required alertness. The channels were open. The qi was flowing. From your perspective it was maintenance. Routine. Tuesday.

I finished the it.

"Anything?" you asked. The standard question. The post-maintenance check-in that you always asked and I always answered.

"I'm not sure, but it looks like something is happening to your core," I said. "It's healing."

You turned. The movement was slow — not the startled pivot of surprise but the controlled rotation of a man who had heard something that he couldn't believe and that required his full attention. You pulled the shirt down. Sat facing me.

"Healing?" you repeated. The word placed between us like an object to be examined.

"The fracture. It's smaller. Qi has been accumulating along the cracks — crystallizing. Filling them." I paused. "I think it's been happening for months. I just didn't see it until now."

"How much?"

"A fifth. Maybe less."

You were quiet. The fire crackled. Your hand moved to the pen, then stopped — the aborted reflex of a man who wanted to document but understood that this moment required presence rather than record.

"The maintenance," you said. "The meridian work." You came to the same conclusion I had come to.

"Clears the path. The excess qi from my proximity reaches the core." I looked at the fire. "The same thing that pushed Wei too fast. That overwhelmed others. It's — depositing. In the cracks. Because the cracks are there to fill."

"A broken vessel collecting what would have burst an intact one."

"Yes."

You nodded. The nod that processed rather than approved — the internal rearrangement of a model encountering new data. You reached for the notebook. You wrote. The characters small, precise, the density of a man recording a discovery.

"How long?" you asked without looking up. "To completion."

"I don't know. This is the first time I saw it." I paused. "I'll watch it. Over the next days. Maybe we can extrapolate from there."

You nodded. The pen moved again — a note, a question mark, the scholar's shorthand for *insufficient data, observe.*

"You didn't know?" you asked. Not accusation. Verification.

"I didn't know."

The fire crackled. The stars emerged. The air cooled as mountain air cooled after dark — rapidly, thoroughly.

I lay awake that night. Your breathing deepened into sleep, the even rhythm of a man who now knew his body was repairing itself, who now knew the woman beside him was the medicine.

For the first time in millennia, my presence had healed something.

Not destroyed. Not broken. Not erased.

Healed.

I lay in the dark with that knowledge and it was warm and it was terrifying and it was the closest thing to hope I could have.
