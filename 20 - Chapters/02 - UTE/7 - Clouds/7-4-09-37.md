Morning light reached the clearing as I settled on the grass. Stone pressed up through it. Above us, the ridge brightened as gradually as it had on so many mornings. We had another day for the work.

I closed my eyes and turned my attention inward. The old limit waited under every thought I could put into words. I approached the conviction the child in the cave had formed and tried to let it dissolve.

I understood everything.

Fear had crystallized into belief. Belief had become reality. The wall was part of me, strengthened by every attempt to fight it. I understood why surrender mattered, why knowing the answer differed from making it part of myself. I could even follow the paradox of becoming fearless enough to accept fear. Each explanation fitted the others.

Nothing changed.

The structure was as clear as a wall held up to a lamp. Every brick showed. I could describe the mortar, the foundation, and the choices a frightened child had made in that dark cave. Understanding gave me names for all of it without loosening a single part.

I could have explained it to someone else.

I could not dissolve it.

When I opened my eyes, you were watching from three steps away. You had left that distance for the exercise, close enough to observe without occupying the space in which I worked.

"Nothing?" you said.

"I understand everything."

"And?"

"Nothing."

You looked at me for a long time. It was the pause that came before your most important sentences. I kept my eyes on you and offered no further explanation.

"If understanding were enough," you said, "you'd already be mortal."

Wind shifted above us. A leaf spiraled down onto your notebook. You picked it up to set it aside.

You had given me gentler versions of that answer before. This one left little room to pretend I needed only a clearer account of what was happening.

I had understood since the river, since the discovery that I had been doing it myself. I knew the trap. I knew I had to believe differently, to hold differently, and to let that difference reach deeper than thought. Months of understanding had left me sitting here, still immortal.

Each step was clear in my head. Changing the certainty beneath them was as distant as a shore I could see but not reach.

"I know," I said.

"Then stop knowing. Start being."

I could turn that into another instruction to follow. I could try, bringing the same effort to it as before. Even deciding not to try became another way of seeking a result. I looked at my hands, still arranged carefully on my knees. How was I to begin without making the beginning an act of will?

You stood and walked to the stream. When you reached the cold water, I turned back to the clearing.

The explanation was complete. So was the resistance. I saw what should allow a change, and nothing changed. Describing the distance between knowing and becoming had done little to carry me across it. I had become good at identifying the shore. From where I sat, the water was just as wide.

Xiǎohú settled next to me, rust-colored fur against gray stone. Her warmth reached my leg. She lay there while I breathed.

The grass was still around us. I controlled the outward changes now, letting things run in their ordinary course. That mastery made the remaining failure sharper. So much of the world could change under my attention. I was as I had always been.

Below the ridge, water ran over stones, and you moved at the edge of it.

Start being.

The instruction outlasted the conversation. It held the part of the journey still before me, the unfinished work after millennia of living. I let it be, without arranging another exercise around it.

Then I closed my eyes again. I could sit here longer. I could let the words settle.
