The fire had burned low while I held the bowl of not-rice. Warmth lingered in my palms. I had yet to eat. Naming the repetition had done little to stop the thoughts from returning.

You sat across from me with your empty bowl next to you. You had set it down carefully, within reach for washing later. Even tired, you put your things where your hand could find them.

An ember collapsed in the fire. The light shifted across your face, leaving half of it in shadow. The features I knew looked strange from where I sat.

"I was thinking about the crater," I said.

I had meant to speak about the nest again. Instead I found myself recalling another fire, another place where you had written while I tried to understand what had happened to me.

You turned toward me and waited while I found the next words.

"The basalt," I said. "The boundary. The thing that said *not yet.* The crack. We've talked about it."

"Yes."

"You documented it. Your notebook. Stone, crack, no force, no sound."

"Yes."

"You wrote something else that day."

A small piece of charred wood fell against the ember. It settled in the pause.

Your hands had gone still. Usually in the evening you rolled the pen between your fingers or eased the joints of your hands after walking. Now they rested where I could see them beside your knees.

"I saw it," I said. "In the tea house. The zigzag map. When you opened the notebook to show me the convergence. The page opened before the map."

You were quiet.

"Two words and a date. Your handwriting, but not your handwriting. Pressed harder. Characters too close."

The fire moved again. Xiǎohú was a shape at the edge of the light, amber eyes half-closed.

"*Approached,*" I said. "*Refused.*"

You exhaled slowly. I had seen those words months ago. I waited for you to explain them.

"A sect scout," you said.

"At the crater. While I was on the basalt."

"Yes."

"What did they want?"

"Position. Route. Patterns. They already had information about you. What they wanted was your exact location."

"My location."

"Your route ahead. Where you'd be, when. The prediction, not the record."

"And you said no."

"I said no."

You said it evenly, as plain an account as the two words on the page. I looked at your still hands.

"Why?"

You picked up a twig, turned it between your fingers, and set it down. I remembered a pebble at the stream, months ago on the far side of a mountain range. The delegation had left that night. You had occupied your hands in much the same way while deciding what to tell me.

"Because I wanted answers," you said. "Not a head on a pike."

"Pragmatic."

"It was pragmatic."

"Was?"

The fire was low now. Red light faded across your hands. Above us, the mountain cut a starless shape against the dark sky.

"Later the reason changed," you said. "The crack in the stone. Your face afterward. You were frightened of something you couldn't name. You looked as though you didn't know what you were. And I thought—"

You stopped, looking down at the twig. I let the pause stand.

"I thought that scholars don't send scouts to buy loyalty. People who do that are not studying. They are acquiring."

"You could have told me."

"Yes."

"Instead you wrote it down and turned the page?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

You shifted the twig until it lay along the stones around the fire. Then you took your hand away.

"Because," you said, "if I told you, the choice would become yours. Yours to evaluate, to weigh, and to carry. You were already carrying—"

You gestured past the fire, toward the dark trail. We had spent days speaking of the nest, the fog, and the losses I had begun to remember. I knew what you meant without asking you to name them again.

"You were carrying enough."

The last flame subsided. Embers lit the small space between us while everything past it disappeared into darkness. I sat with what you had told me, turning the old glimpse of your notebook into a choice you had made.

I looked at the bowl in my hands. The not-rice had cooled. The steam was gone.

"And instead you carried it alone," I said.

"That was the point."

"The point of what?"

You looked directly at me. You had made that choice months ago, then traveled beside me without bringing it up. Your hands were still again. You had finished arranging what you could tell me.

"Staying," you said.

I set the bowl down between my feet. The word hung in the quiet.

You reached for the notebook. I knew the gesture well by then. Once you had found words for something, you usually needed to see them on the page.

"Write it down," I said.

You opened the notebook to the old page. There were the two words again: *Approached. Refused.* The strokes pressed deep into the paper, their spacing cramped.

"All of it," I said. "The approach. The offer. The refusal. Both reasons, the pragmatic one and the one that came after."

You wrote. The handwriting was steady this time, the letters measured, the spacing even. The script was yours again.

The account grew beneath the old entry. It took more than two words this time.

Only a few embers remained. Their red glow reflected in Xiǎohú's eyes, two amber points at the edge of the darkness.

You wrote while I picked up the cold bowl. The mountain was quiet around us. Your pen moved across the paper, setting down a choice you had expected to keep to yourself.
