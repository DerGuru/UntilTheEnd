The fire was low. The not-rice was still in the bowl. The bowl was in my hands.
I didn't eat. The loop was still there — the circle, the inventory, the perpetual motion of guilt that you had named and that naming had not dissolved. The rice was warm against my palms. The warmth was real but the warmth was not enough.

You sat across the fire. Your bowl set empty aside with the casual precision that characterized your relationship to objects — everything placed, nothing thrown, the spatial discipline of a man whose mind organized by habit.

The fire settled. An ember collapsed, the collapse redistributed the heat, the light shifted. For a moment your face was different — half-lit, the shadows falling on the side I didn't usually see, the geometry of firelight making a stranger out of a familiar man.

"I was thinking about the crater," I said.

The words arrived unprepared, unbidden, from somewhere below the systematic disclosure that had characterized the last days of telling. They came from the place where words came from, when the unconscious mind was running the actual conversation.

You looked at me with complete attention, the total allocation of observational resources. The scholar recognizing that the source had found new material.

"The basalt," I said. "The boundary. The thing that said *not yet.* The crack. We've talked about it."

"Yes."

"You documented it. Your notebook. Stone, crack, no force, no sound."

"Yes."

"You wrote something else that day."

The fire moved. The same ember, still settling, still redistributing. The sound of wood adjusting to its own combustion, the small structural failures that fire produced in matter.

Your hands were still. They had been moving — the habitual fidgeting that accompanied evenings, the pen-rolling, the knuckle-cracking, the physical restlessness of a body that had been walking all day and hadn't yet found its resting configuration. The hands stopped.

"I saw it," I said. "In the tea house. The zigzag map. When you opened the notebook to show me the convergence. The page opened before the map."

You were quiet.

"Two words and a date. Your handwriting, but not your handwriting. Pressed harder. Characters too close."

The fire moved again. Xiǎohú was a shape at the edge of the light, amber eyes half-closed.

"*Approached,*" I said. "*Refused.*"

You exhaled. The mechanical release of air that the body performed when a held tension was removed. The sound was small. The sound contained months.

"A sect scout," you said.

"At the crater. While I was on the basalt."

"Yes."

"What did they want?"

"Position. Route. Patterns. They already had information about you. What they wanted was coordinates."

"My location."

"Your trajectory. Where you'd be, when. The prediction, not the record."

"And you said no."

"I said no."

The words were flat. Your register of factual disclosure — bare, compressed, stripped of architecture. The words were the same shape as the two words in the notebook. 

"Why?"

You picked up a twig. Turned it in your fingers. Set it down. The hands buying the mind time. The same gesture you'd made with the pebble at the stream, months and a mountain range ago, the night the delegation had come and gone. The physical grammar of a man negotiating between what you knew and what you were willing to say.

"Because I wanted answers," you said. "Not a head on a pike."

"Pragmatic."

"It was pragmatic."

"Was?"

The fire was very low. The light was red and diminishing. The mountain above us was a darkness against a darker sky, the absence of stars where stone occupied space.

"Later the reason changed," you said. "The crack in the stone. Your face after. The expression of a woman who was frightened of something she couldn't name. The expression that said: *I don't know what I am.* And I thought —"

You stopped. A man arriving at the truth of your own motivation and finding it less articulable than he'd expected.

"I thought that scholars don't send scouts to buy loyalty. People who do that are not studying. They are acquiring."

"You could have told me."

"Yes."

"Instead you wrote it down and turned the page?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

You set the twig down. Aligned with the stones around the fire. The precision that served as architecture when language was still under construction.

"Because," you said. "If I told you, the choice would become yours — yours to evaluate, yours to weigh, yours to carry. And you were already carrying —"

You gestured. At everything. The mountain, the fog, the trail, the nest, the eggs, the thousand names, the inventory of destruction that we'd been walking through for days. The gesture encompassed the weight that I was already bearing and the judgment that adding to it would have been — what? Cruel? Unnecessary?

"You were carrying enough."

The fire settled to its final configuration. The embers glowed. The darkness was complete everywhere except the small circle where two people sat with the residue of a months-old secret that had been opened, examined and found to be exactly the shape they'd both expected.

I looked at the bowl in my hands. The not-rice had cooled. The steam was gone, the warmth diminished.

"And instead you carried it alone," I said.

"That was the point."

"The point of what?"

You looked at me. The direct gaze. Your face was transparent if you knew how to read it. And what was on it was simpler than pride, simpler than modesty. The face of a man who had made a decision a long time ago, had continued making it every day since and had never wanted the decision to be seen.

"Staying," you said.

The word sat in the fire-lit air. I set the bowl down. 

You reached for your notebook. The gesture was habitual — the automatic reaching of a man whose response to experience was documentation, whose processing occurred through pen and ink, whose understanding was built character by character on pages that accumulated into a record.

"Write it down," I said.

You opened the notebook. Found the page — the old page, the one with the two words. I saw them again. *Approached. Refused.* The handwriting still different. Still pressed too hard, still too close, still the script of a man writing under pressure.

"All of it," I said. "The approach. The offer. The refusal. Both reasons — the pragmatic one and the one that came after."

You wrote. The pen moved. The handwriting was steady this time. The letters were measured. The spacing was even. The script was yours again.

I watched you. The man who wrote everything down. More than three words now.

The fire was gone. Only a few embers remained. Xiǎohú's eyes reflected the red, two amber points in the darkness, watching with patience.

You wrote. I picked up the cold bowl. The mountain was quiet around us, the geological patience of stone that had been here before us and would be here after, the mineral indifference that placed our conversation in the correct temporal context. Brief, mortal, and happening anyway.
