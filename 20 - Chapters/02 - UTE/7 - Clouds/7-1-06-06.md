Two mountain deer grazed on sparse scrub a hundred paces from our camp. I was approaching when both raised their heads. Their whole bodies flinched. Then they ran, breaking into a ragged gallop before I had taken another step toward them.

The birds fell quiet next. Their calls had carried through the trees all morning, overlapping from branch to branch. Now they called only from farther off. As I walked, the quiet traveled with me. A bird ahead would stop, and another would call behind us.

Xiǎohú trotted at my side, amber eyes forward. Whatever the other animals sensed barely touched her stride. Around me, for sixty paces, the small movements in the trees had ceased. Beyond that, the deer were still running.

Plants responded more slowly. By the second afternoon, the scrub grass around our camp was leaning toward me. Blades tilted inward on every side, their stems bending from the crevices. I moved to see them from another angle. They still leaned my way, though the birds had gone quiet.

I didn't know what to make of that. Neither did you, when I pointed it out.

You were cooking rice again, with bitter herbs and dried meat. The bag was getting lighter but still held enough for us. A modest fire burned under the pot. I watched you make something whose outcome we could usually rely on.

You watched the bubbles rise through the rice. Then the pot cracked. It was a traveling merchant's pot, light enough for a scholar's pack. A fracture appeared on the side facing me, thin as a thread from rim to base. Water seeped through it, hissing where it met the embers.

"I am currently losing my rice," you said, "because your qi is cracking the pot."

Half-cooked rice lay in the pot, its grains emerging as the water level fell.

I blinked.

"It's still doing it," you said. You sounded unalarmed, though your dinner was running into the fire. "Your qi. I can feel it. Not like before, not the discharge. More like... pressure. Ambient. Constant. Three paces in every direction, roughly. Everything within three paces is being compressed. Slightly."

My hands rested open on my knees. I had been sitting quietly, directing no qi at anything. Water still hissed against the embers. Past the fire, the grass leaned toward me.

"I'm sorry," I said.

It was an inadequate apology for the rice, let alone the rest. I looked past the damaged pot toward the trees where the deer had fled. Both certainties were still in me: I could not die, and I wanted to. Sitting still had done little to ease the strain between them.

The damage spread past my skin. Ceramic cracked within three paces, and animals fled from much farther. I could draw no boundary around it, no line where I knew I stopped affecting things. You were holding bowls next to a leaking pot because I had tried to change myself.

You salvaged the rice, spooning it into bowls before the last water escaped. Half-cooked, but edible. You ate. I looked at my bowl.

The bitter herbs rose from it without making me want a mouthful. I had never needed the food. After three days of pushing against myself, I had nearly forgotten why I ate it anyway. The bowl sat untouched while you worked through yours.

"Eat," you said.

"I'm not hungry."

"Eat anyway."

You pushed the bowl toward me. You knew perfectly well hunger had little to do with it. You left it within reach.

I didn't eat.

I sat at the fire while you ate. Xiǎohú had found a spot at the edge of the disturbed ground and slept there, her flank rising steadily. Between us lay the rice, cooling in its bowl.

I stayed.

Thick clouds moved across the stars. They hung low over the mountains, heavy with moisture. As they passed, one patch of stars appeared while another went dark. The clear spaces grew smaller.

You washed the pot. The crack was still there, a fine line the length of the ceramic.

"I can seal that with clay," you said.

You would. The pot would hold water again. The morning would come. The practice would resume.

I sat. The rice cooled in the bowl against my palms. Above us, the clouds covered the last of the stars and the dark was complete.
