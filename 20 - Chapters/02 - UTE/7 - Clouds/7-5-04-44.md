Panic rises.

Her body is seizing. The hands clenching — fists forming automatically, fingers curling inward, nails digging into palms with the pressure that produces crescents in the skin. The jaw locks. The teeth meet with a click that echoes in the cave.

The qi slams back. HARD. 

The qi crashes into place like a door slammed by a hurricane, the violent restoration of a condition that has been momentarily interrupted and will not be interrupted again. The cells stop hesitating. They grip. The mechanism that has maintained immortality for millennia returns to operation with the ferocity of a force that has been threatened and will now overcompensate.

The red dot heals.

She watches it. Red becomes pink. Pink becomes skin. The mark is gone.

Seven seconds of evidence, erased in two. The physical proof of mortality eliminated by the reflex of survival. The stone tablet of her achievement wiped clean by the hand that built the wall.

She breathes heavy. The breathing of someone in the aftermath of danger — the overcompensated gulping, the chest expanding beyond comfort because every instinct has sent the warning signal and the lungs are responding with everything they have.

Sweat forms on her forehead. On her palms — the palms that the nails just marked, the crescents already healing, the skin already returning to its unmarked state. The moisture of fear-response to what the it interprets as mortal threat and has deployed every countermeasure it possesses.

Her pulse drums in her ears. The auditory intrusion of a heartbeat so strong it becomes sound, the heart hammering against the chest wall with the urgency of an organ that believes that its existence is threatened.

The fire burns. The same fire. The same cave. The same dripping water. Everything identical except the woman sitting in the cave, shakes. Adrenaline still rushing through her veins.

The survival reflex. 

The one that predates thought, predates language, predates everything except her most fundamental imperative. CONTINUE. The reflex that has been running — uninterrupted, unquestioned, unchallenged — for millennia. The reflex that built the wall. The reflex that is the wall. The ancient belief that converts the threat of cessation into the absolute command to persist.

She slowly unclenches her hands. The fingers straightening one by one. Releasing muscles that have been holding with everything they have. The palms are smooth. The crescent marks are gone. Her qi has already repaired the damage.

Seven seconds of proof. It feels like a hallucination and the whole experience feels like something that happened to someone else.

But it didn't happen to someone else.

It happened to her. In this cave. By this fire.

The burn mark is gone. The evidence is erased. The fabric is smooth and grey and unmarked and immortal.

But she knows.

She knows it was there. She counted. Seven real seconds in which mortality was not a concept but a condition.

The water drips. The cave echoes. The fire crackles.

She sits in the aftermath of panic. She is still afraid. The wall is still there. The reflex is still operational. The survival mechanism that a child built in a cave millennia ago is still functioning, still vigilant, still stronger than intention, still deeper than understanding.

Her hands tremble. She watches them tremble. She lets them.

Outside the cave, the wind changes direction. The sound shifts — a different whistle, a different pressure, the atmospheric signature of weather deciding to become different weather. The fire leans toward the cave mouth. 

She is still sitting.

She is still here.

The burn mark is gone.

But it was real.
