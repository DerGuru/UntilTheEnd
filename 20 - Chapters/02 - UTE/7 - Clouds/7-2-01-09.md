We were back below the tree line, back into the thicker green, the return to altitude where things grew. The direction I was pulled towards was still up, higher into the stones and rubble, but currently my priorities sat with controlling what had been unconscious or reactive so far. The clearing was a flat space between boulders, sheltered by pines on three sides, open enough that the morning light reached the ground. You'd arranged three things in front of me with the care of a man setting up an experiment: a wooden bowl of water, the cooking pot with a pine seedling sprouting from packed earth inside it and a candle, unlit.

"Three mediums," you said. "Not just stones this time."

I sat in front of the bowl. Water. Clear, still, reflecting the overcast sky above — a ceiling of thin cloud, grey-white, the diffused light that came when the sun was present but not visible. The surface was motionless — the motionlessness of water in a windless place, the equilibrium that physics produced when no force acted upon liquid.

First attempt.

I willed the water to move. Concentrated. Focused the directed intention that combat and cultivation had trained me to deploy — the laser-focus of a will aimed at an outcome. Move. The water should shift. The water should —

Nothing.

The water remained still, reflective and undisturbed. My will hit its surface and slid off the way words had slid off my conviction. Present, acknowledged, irrelevant. The water didn't care about my will. The water was water. Willing it to change was like willing the sky to be green — the instruction had no channel, no mechanism, no connection between intention and reality.

Second attempt.

Not will. Something else. Something I'd learned at the river, with the sash and the open hands. Not the grip but the absence of grip. Not the push but the state. I looked at the water and I — didn't try. I believed. The water flows there. Not should flow, not will flow. Flows. Present tense. Statement of fact. The water flows there because it does, because it has, because the world is arranged in a way that produces this flow and the flow is natural and the naturalness is the point.

The water moved.

Not spectacularly. It shifted — the surface tilting slightly, as if the bowl had been angled, even though the bowl was level. The water moved toward the edge I'd indicated. Slowly. Quietly. As if it had always been moving there and I had only just noticed.

"That doesn't make sense," you said.

"Exactly."

The half-smile happened before I could prevent it. Because you were right — it didn't make sense. Will produced nothing. Belief produced water that moved on its own. The mechanism was absurd, irrational, the logic that philosophers would have spent centuries arguing about and physicists would have denied entirely. It made no sense.

Yet it worked.

You were writing. The pen capturing data while the data was fresh — direction, volume, duration, the observations of a man treating the impossible as a research project. Quantifiable impossibility. Your specialty.

"Again," you said. "But — please explain what you did differently."

I considered. The distinction was — difficult to articulate, in the way that the difference between remembering and imagining is difficult to articulate. Both involve mental images. Both produce internal experience. But one is retrieval and the other is construction and the difference between them is the difference between finding something and making something.

"Will is pressure," I said. "Applied from outside. Forcing the water to comply. It doesn't work because the water isn't subject to my will. I'm not its authority."

You nodded. Writing.

"Belief is — state. Not action. I'm not making the water move. I'm — knowing that it moves. The way I know gravity exists. I don't will gravity. I don't force gravity. I know gravity and gravity is. The water moving is — is."

"You're describing faith," you said.

"No. Faith implies uncertainty. This is — certainty. The bone-deep certainty that this is how the world is. Not how I want it to be. Not how it is supposed to be. How it actually is. Even if it currently is obviously not."

You looked at the water. The bowl. The slight asymmetry of the surface that indicated the water had, indeed, moved.

"The difference between forcing and being," you said. "The one enforces. The other accepts."

You wrote that down. Underlined it.

The candle was next. The plant was next. The days ahead were next.

I looked at the water. It had settled back to level — the belief hadn't sustained, the certainty having the lifespan of a held breath rather than a permanent state. The water was water again. Still. Patient. Ready to move whenever the next conviction arrived.

Xiǎohú was watching from her boulder. Amber eyes, evaluative, the patient witness to a morning's first lesson. She blinked once. Looked away. Above her, the clouds had thinned enough to admit a single bar of direct sun, which crossed the clearing and vanished.

Training had begun.
