We were back below the tree line, surrounded by thicker green. The stones and rubble higher up still pulled at me, but first I needed to control what had so far happened in reflex. Our clearing lay flat between boulders, sheltered by pines on three sides. Morning light reached the ground through the open side. You had arranged three things before me: a wooden bowl of water, our cooking pot holding a pine seedling in packed earth, and an unlit candle.

"Three mediums," you said. "Not just stones this time."

I sat in front of the bowl. Its clear water reflected the gray-white cloud above us. The sun lit the clearing through that thin cover, leaving little shadow on the ground. I waited until the water lay perfectly still.

First attempt.

I willed the water to move. Combat and cultivation had taught me to narrow my attention until only the desired outcome remained. I concentrated on one edge of the bowl, pressing my intention toward it. The water should shift there. I held the thought until the effort tightened my jaw.

Nothing.

The water lay still, reflecting the pale sky. My effort had left as little trace on it as the words about mortality had left within me. I could direct ordinary qi at the bowl, of course, but that was not what I was learning. Between my intention and the unchanged water lay no connection.

Second attempt.

I let the effort ease. My hands brought back the sash at the river, the moment I had held them open. I let the recollection settle. The water flows there. I held it as something already happening, as ordinary as the stream beyond the clearing. I knew the direction it flowed.

The water moved.

Its surface tilted. The bowl sat level on the ground, yet the water crept toward the edge I had chosen. The reflected cloud stretched across it. The movement was quiet enough that, had you looked elsewhere, you might have missed it.

"That doesn't make sense," you said.

"Exactly."

I almost smiled. Will had produced nothing, and certainty had moved the water. A philosopher could have spent a lifetime objecting to that distinction. I had trouble explaining it even with the tilted surface in front of us. Your bewilderment was, for once, comforting.

Yet it worked.

You were writing, looking from the water to the page. You recorded its direction, how much had moved, and how long the movement lasted. Presented with something impossible, you were finding the parts you could measure.

"Again," you said. "But please explain what you did differently."

I considered while the water settled. Remembering and imagining can produce equally clear pictures, yet they feel different to the person doing them. What I was trying to describe was as plain to me as that distinction, and as hard to put into words.

"Will is pressure," I said. "Applied from outside. Forcing the water to comply. It doesn't work because the water isn't subject to my will. I'm not its authority."

You nodded as you wrote.

"Belief is a state. I'm not making the water move. I'm knowing that it moves. As I know a stone will fall if I drop it. I don't have to force the falling. It happens. The water moving is as certain as that."

"You're describing faith," you said.

"No. Faith implies uncertainty. This is certainty. The bone-deep certainty that this is how the world is. Not how I want it to be. Not how it is supposed to be. How it actually is. Even if it currently is obviously not."

You looked at the water, at the bowl, at the slight tilt of the surface that showed the water had, indeed, moved.

"The difference between forcing and being," you said. "The one enforces. The other accepts."

You wrote that down, then underlined it.

The candle sat next to the plant. We had enough to work on for the days ahead.

The water had settled level again. I had held the certainty for little more than a breath, and now the reflected sky lay flat across the bowl. I would have to find that state again for the next attempt.

Xiǎohú watched from her boulder. She blinked once, then looked away. Above her, the clouds thinned enough to admit a single bar of direct sun. It crossed the clearing before the light softened again.

Training had begun.
