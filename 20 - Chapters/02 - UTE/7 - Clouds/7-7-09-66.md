After the others leave, she sorts her possessions. Years in the village have given her little to arrange: a few clothes, a bowl, a cup, and a pot.

She places what she will leave behind on the table, clean enough for whoever comes next. It looks like a setting for someone she has yet to meet. She adjusts the cup twice before taking her hand away.

The neighbor appears in the doorway before Yun reaches it. Of course she does. She notices departures as some people notice an approaching rain, finding signs before anyone else has thought to look.

"I'm going," Yun says.

"Where?"

"Home."

"But you have a home."

She gestures toward the room, then the garden with its bare late-autumn plum tree. Beyond the fence lie rooftops, smoke, and the lane where the Counter walks. Yun follows the gesture.

"A different one."

The neighbor studies her for a long time. They have shared rice, watched each other's gardens, and learned more through proximity than either deliberately disclosed. Living close enough to hear another person's kettle leaves little need for formal confidences. Yun lets her finish looking.

"Don't get lost."

It is as close to goodbye as the neighbor will come. Affection delivered as instruction.

She closes the door behind her. The plum tree stands bare, its bark gray. It will bud again when the season turns, whoever occupies the house. She looks at it, grateful to leave one farewell uncomplicated. She has sat under those branches through fear, blossoms, and the slow change of seasons. The tree can go on with its cycle after her place under it is empty.

The path out of the village climbs. She follows it slowly, placing her feet where her stiffening knees can bear the incline.

"Will you come back?"

She stops and turns.

The Counter stands on the path behind her. Her face has the same seriousness she brings to wrinkles, lanterns, and the steps between houses. She waits for an answer.

"No," Yun says gently.

"But you'll remember me," the Counter says. Not a question.

They look at each other for a breath, perhaps two. Yun keeps the moment: the Counter on the path, her face serious with the certainty that she will be remembered. This one. The last from the village.

"Yes," Yun says. "I'll remember everything."

The Counter nods, then turns back toward the village. She counts her steps as she goes. The numbers grow quieter along the path until Yun can no longer make them out.

Yun watches her go before turning uphill again.

Her knees ache on the steep path. Xiǎohú walks at her side, matching her pace as she did before they reached the village. Yun glances at the amber eyes, then at the next stretch of ground.

Walking brings the notebook to mind: worn leather, stained pages, and dots where characters should have been. A man had recorded what she told him because he thought it worth keeping. He had taken it under his arm to walk away without looking back.

She does not look back.

The path narrows as the village recedes below her. Stone replaces packed earth underfoot. She adjusts her pace to the rougher surface. Coming or going, the incline is the same. It gives her something immediate to attend to while the houses grow smaller behind her.

She climbs on old knees, white hair moving against her shoulders, every step an effort. This is what she has come to do.

Clouds dissolve above her. Afternoon light breaks through in columns of gold, brightening the slope before another patch of cover passes. It reaches her white hair, her lined face, and the path ahead.

She climbs toward the light.
