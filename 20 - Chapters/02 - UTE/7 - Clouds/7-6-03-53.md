Yun stands behind the house, looking at the overgrown ground. Weeds fill every handspan of neglected soil. Among them lie stones, debris, and the remains of whatever the previous tenant grew. Years without tending have blurred the old beds.

She borrows a shovel from the landlord to begin digging. Rust marks the iron near the wooden handle. The grip is smooth from use. Damp earth turns under the blade, exposing a darker underside. It is cool when she reaches down to break a clod.

She makes approximate rows. She knows the principle, though she has practiced it less recently than many other things. Seeds need room between them. She judges the distance as best she can with the edge of the shovel. The rows will do.

She opens the small paper packet she bought at the market two days ago. What will grow from it, she has yet to establish. With her power she could identify each seed, follow its growth, and know the harvest more precisely than any farmer. She could bring that harvest here in a blink.

She doesn't.

She places the seeds in the rows one by one. Her fingers open a space in the soil, set a seed in it, and cover it again. The gesture is older than the civilizations she remembers. Once the earth lies over each seed, there is little more for her hand to do.

Planting. Waiting. Letting.

That sequence has become a habit in other parts of her life too. She leaves the thought alone rather than making another lesson from it.

"... Water?"

A voice draws her attention to the low fence. Three boards mark the boundary of the next garden, doing little to prevent anyone crossing it. A thin man stands there. Work has left sharp angles in his shoulders, wrists, and jaw; he looks as though he eats less than his labor requires.

"My well runs slow this time of year," he says. He gestures toward a plot smaller than hers, better tended but dry. Pale soil shows around plants whose leaves have begun to droop.

She nods. "Take what you need."

He does. The well by her house serves this end of the village; it has enough to spare. He fills a bucket to carry back to his plot. Water darkens the pale soil where he pours it.

"Thank you," he says.

The exchange is small enough to finish with those words. She has water; he needs it. He can carry the bucket himself. She has helped someone without changing the ground under him or supplying a force he cannot bear. She returns to the soil while he tends his plants.

Her knees are damp from kneeling. Earth lies under her nails. Above her, clouds pass across the sun. Children, roosters, and the thin man's bucket clank and call beyond the fence. The village goes on while her seeds lie covered in the ground.

She leaves the seeds to grow at their own pace. The garden will produce what it will.

Stepping back takes restraint. She could so easily go on working past what the shovel can do. One action would follow the last without a pause. Waiting leaves the rows exactly as they are. She lets the soil lie still, trusting that growth can happen without her setting its timing.

Xiǎohú watches from the doorstep, her eyes following the shovel as Yun lays it down. The shovel lies by the unfinished garden. For today, it has done enough.

She washes her hands at the well. Cold water loosens the earth under her nails, leaving a few stubborn traces she works free with her fingertips.

She dries her hands on her clothes.

The seeds are in the ground.

Enough for today.
