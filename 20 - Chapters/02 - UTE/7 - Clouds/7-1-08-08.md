The fire was down to orange-gray embers. Heat still reached my knees, though the light had faded from the stones around us. The low clouds that had been gathering all evening now covered the stars.

I sat with my hands on my knees, letting each breath come. Four days of failed attempts had left me too tired to arrange another. My body felt as though I had run for many li. We had walked only ordinary distances. The effort had all happened here, sitting next to you.

I had not eaten.

You had placed the bowl within my reach. Steam rose from the rice, carrying the scent of herbs with a faint char from the bottom crust. Even your careful cooking left a little of that. None of it made me lift my hands from my knees.

I was too exhausted to want food. Eating meant reaching for the bowl, taking the chopsticks, and attending to something other than the strain in me. I watched the steam instead.

I stayed.

I stayed at the fire next to you. My rice stood untouched. At the edge of the dark, embers glinted in Xiǎohú's amber eyes, two small points above the outline of her body. Clouds hid whatever moved above us.

I stayed because I wanted to leave.

Yesterday, or any of the days before it, I would have withdrawn into the dark. I knew how easy the first few steps would be. The fire would recede behind me, and I could stop wondering what you saw when you looked at me. Solitude asked little of me after so much practice.

Tonight I sat on the stone. The impulse to leave still came. I was too tired to follow it. Time passed, then a little more, with you eating next to me.

The smallest possible progress.

You ate slowly. Between mouthfuls your chopsticks hovered above the bowl. You swallowed, and the bowl scraped softly as you gathered another portion. Those small sounds reminded me I was sitting with someone.

You left my bowl alone. Steam rose thinner as the rice cooled. You had already offered it; now you let me decide when to reach. If I left it until morning, you would have a cold breakfast. The thought did not make me move.

I looked up. The sky was dark from ridge to ridge, without a star showing. Earlier the cloud cover had been gathering in plain view. Now I could only picture its movement, following the wind against my face.

Clouds.

They had dissolved above us during the day. The shapes looked solid from the ground, yet a change in the wind could draw them apart. Up close there would be only damp air. Even the heavy cover above us was changing while I sat unable to see it.

Yun meant cloud.

It was the name I had given a young boy. I had looked up when he asked, finding a word in something that would change before he could ask again. I had carried that word ever since. Under the hidden sky, I thought of how much effort I spent staying the same.

Maybe the name was the first clue.

I let the thought sit while the rice cooled. For once I considered something without demanding a use for it.

The embers settled. You finished eating, set your bowl down, and leaned back to look at the covered sky. Your shoulder was near mine. You let the silence continue.

Xiǎohú's breathing was steady. Her flank rose softly at the edge of the firelight. After a while I was listening to it.

A long time passed.

I reached for the rice. The bowl was cool now, its surface dry where steam had dampened the rim. I lifted it into my hands. The small weight settled against my palms, plain ceramic holding enough rice for one person. You had finished yours long ago.

I did not eat.

I held the bowl. My fingers curved under it, taking the weight without squeezing. The embers still warmed my knees. You were still next to me. The stone was hard under me, the ceramic cool, my breath slow.

I thought of tomorrow's fire. There would be rice again, and you stirring it. A fox-spirit would find her place at the edge of the light.

I could sit here again.
