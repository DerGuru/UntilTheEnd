By morning, gold light reaches through the entrance. It crosses the floor, rises along the walls, and falls on Yun where she rests against the stone. Her hands lie in her lap. White hair spreads across her shoulders, catching the light at its edges.

She breathes.

The breath is slow and takes no effort. It enters, then leaves, while the light crosses the floor.

The quiet recalls another silence, in a forest so long ago that it feels like two millennia. She had walked among trees holding herself apart from the life around her. Little reached her then. She passed through places without letting anyone near.

This silence is full. Full of everything she has lived through since then.

Water marks the time in this first cave. Plip, a pause, and another plip. The sound reaches her as it reached the child who sat here afraid of dying. Cool walls surround the same sheltered space. So much has happened outside them while the water went on falling.

A young boy's grin returns with the sound. Distance has softened the memory without taking its warmth. She remembers the boy, the young man he became, and the warmth that outlasted losing him. She lets his face stay without describing it.

The smell of burned rice follows. A notebook closes in memory. She hears footsteps moving away, the three-step distance widening along a mountain path. He had left a space by the fire and carried his life elsewhere. She had let him take her words with him.

A number sounds from a remembered lane. The Counter has found something else worth numbering, as she found the lanterns worth checking by the well.

A door opens in memory before Yun can reach it. Her neighbor stands there with another instruction, affection made practical. "Don't get lost." The words leave no room for argument.

A voice rises beyond all reasonable necessity. She remembers tomatoes yielding under her fingers while Loudcumber gives advice to the whole market. He had never required proof that his listeners were hard of hearing.

An old man's face returns. He is older than most she has known, though younger than she is, still untired of the world. Someone she had once been sent to kill has become a presence she can meet without explaining herself. He asked the question she needed.

Warmth rests against her hip here in the cave. Xiǎohú's amber eyes are open, turned toward the entrance. Forest shows below the sky. Morning goes on there as it has through all the mornings she remembers, touching leaves she can see from where she sits.

Xiǎohú breathes. Yun breathes.

Her hands are still in her lap. She has finished the journey that brought her here. She gave the story to someone who could carry it. Now she sits in the quiet after the telling, with little left that needs to become words. What she remembers can stay as warmth, as a face, and as the sound of someone calling a number down a lane.

Water drips. Plip. Pause. Plip.

She breathes.

Slower.

The space between inhalation and exhalation lengthens. She has done what she came to do. She rests in the quiet as she rested in the house after her visitors left, listening to the sounds that stayed. The stone supports her back through the next slow breath.

Steady golden light touches the floor in a place she remembers from many mornings. She follows its edge along the stone.

She hears a warbler, then two sparrows. Their calls overlap, each voice still distinct. She has listened to such birds for so many years that she remembers when their phrases had fewer notes. The songs have changed and stayed the same. She follows the one nearest the entrance until another begins.

Wind rustles the leaves outside. It reaches into the cave to touch her face, cooler than the air near the light. It has passed over her face at every age since childhood here, and it reaches the old woman now as lightly as it reached the girl.

She breathes.

Slower.

Xiǎohú lies against her hip, amber eyes steady, reflecting the light. The fox's warmth reaches Yun through her clothes. She has known that small weight through travel, through the village, and through the climb that brought them here.

The light changes. The birds continue. The wind continues. The water drips.

She breathes.

And the space between the breaths grows.

Her chest rises more slowly. The pause after each movement leaves room for another drip of water, another sound from the trees. She rests through the interval before the next breath comes.

Her eyes stay open to the light. The entrance frames forest and sky. Clouds form above the ridge, move with the wind, and thin into blue. She has watched that cycle for millennia. A shape can disappear while water continues through another part of its journey. Here, at the end of her own passage, she belongs to the world that goes on.

Yun. 云. Cloud.

She breathes out.

A breath enters.

This one stays. Her chest settles into stillness under the white hair. The pause lengthens while water keeps falling at the back of the cave.

The light through the entrance does not change.

The birds continue singing. Wind moves through the trees. Water falls in the cave: plip, a pause, and another plip. It sounded at her beginning. It sounds here at the end, and goes on past the last breath.

The forest is green outside. Insects hum under the rustle of leaves. Small movements stir the branches where the birds call. Life fills the space she spent so long observing from a distance.

Clouds thin across the blue sky. Farther along the ridge, another gathers.

Xiǎohú lies against her. Amber eyes stay open to the entrance, the small body pressed to a cooling hip. The fox has held that place through the last slow breaths. She holds it now, her flank rising against the still fabric.

Light crosses the floor to reach the far wall. Stone surrounds the woman inside the mountain. Outside, the slope descends toward the forest, then toward the places where other people are beginning their morning. The cave sits inside that greater landscape, a small shelter where she came to rest.

A woman in a cave. White hair across her shoulders. Still hands. Open eyes in the light.

The world continues. She had feared its continuation without her, had held herself apart from it for so long. At the end she allows it. Past the entrance, the wind carries another patch of cloud across the blue.

The tiredness is gone.
