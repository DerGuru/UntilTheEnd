The morning was colder than the last. Frost crusted the scrub grass and cracked under your feet as you moved around the campsite. You lifted the loose pages from beside the notebook.

Something had changed between us. We had shared this place, the work, and the hope of finding what came next. Now the work we could do together was nearly done, and we moved around each other quietly because of it.

You were packing.

Usually that meant preparing for another day's walk. This morning you sorted what you would carry through a longer absence. You looked over each sheet before adding it to the stack.

Notes had piled up beyond what the notebook could hold. You squared the pages, aligned their edges, and placed them in the case. I remembered the evenings that had produced those extra sheets, when the story outgrew the room you had left for it.

Then you picked up the notebook itself. I had seen the worn leather so often that I had stopped looking. Now every stain along the pages showed, along with the strain in the binding and the places your hands had darkened the cover.

It was full, stained, and irreplaceable.

You tucked it under your arm as you reached for the pack. The life in it was mine; the record was your work. You held it close. Your own life was in those pages too.

"I can't teach you what you need to learn," you said.

You knelt over the pack, face angled away, and I saw only part of your expression as you arranged what went inside.

"Nobody can."

"I know," I said.

"The last piece belongs to you. I'm a distraction."

I looked at the notebook under your arm. After everything we had done, you had found so small a word for yourself.

"You're not a distraction."

You turned toward me. Your mouth moved toward a smile, but your eyes stayed serious.

"Yes, I am. The best one you ever had."

The joke was easy to hear. So was what made it necessary. You had learned to give me both in one sentence and let me pick which to answer.

I had no answer before you went back to packing.

I sat by the low fire. I could have asked for another day, could have found a reason we needed more time. I followed each possibility far enough to see what I was doing. Everything I might say was either too small for what you had given me or large enough to become a demand. Your hands finished another part of the packing. I let them.

Xiǎohú watched the papers go into the case. Her amber eyes followed your hand, then the notebook under your arm.

By evening the preparations were finished. The fire dwindled. We left the pot where it was instead of cooking another meal.

You sat down near me in the dark. We had filled so many evenings with questions, then with the story they opened. Now you settled beside the packed things, and I stayed quiet with you.
