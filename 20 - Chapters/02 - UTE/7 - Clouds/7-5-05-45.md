The stone wall of the cave glistens. Condensation catching firelight, the mineral sweat of subterranean rock, each droplet a tiny lens reflecting the orange of the dying fire.

She does not move.

After the panic — after the slamming door of qi, after the trembling hands, after the qi's violent reassertion of its oldest protocol — she sits on the cold floor in the half-dark of dying embers.

Water drips. The periodic measure of time that does not care about her.

Her hands are damp. The salt-water evidence of a body that believed it was dying and deployed every countermeasure in its inventory. She wipes them on her robe. Left hand. Right hand. Left hand. The methodical repetition of a gesture that serves no functional purpose but provides the tactile anchor of hands touching fabric, skin touching cloth.

The truth sits in the cave with her. 

She is afraid to die.

Afraid to die the way a child is afraid of the dark.

The comparison surfaces. A child. In the dark. Afraid. The original configuration — the template from which everything else was built. A child in a cave, small and cold and surrounded by the evidence of death and so afraid that the fear became architecture, became structure, became the wall that now resists her with the impenetrability of a conviction held since consciousness began.

Fear. Pure and primitive fear. Deeper than everything she constructed over millennia — the mechanism, the curse, the metaphysical anomaly, the divine punishment. Beneath all of them lay fear. The fear that a small animal feels when the predator approaches, when the body says run and the running becomes the organism's primary function. And her function became permanent.

Her immortality is based on fear.

Every manifestation of it. The qi that shields automatically. The cells that replicate perfectly. The self that persists endlessly. All of it — every layer, every mechanism, every invisible process that maintains her existence against the natural order — is based on fear. Expressed as conviction. Expressed as power. Expressed as the reality-warping capacity of a woman who believed so hard she wouldn't die that the universe complied.

The fire is down to embers. The orange is fading. The cold is arriving — real cold, cave cold, the temperature of stone that has never been warm and will never be warm.

She feels it.

On her skin. Through her clothing. Into the muscles. The qi is shielding. The reflex is back in full operation. But the shielding is partial. Porous. A small percentage of the cold is penetrating. A fraction of the temperature is reaching her.

The panic loosened something.

Something peripheral — like a secondary defense, a satellite structure orbiting the primary mechanism. Something is slightly less rigid than it was an hour ago. The seven seconds created a crack. The crack healed. But the healing is not perfect. The wall remembers being breached the way a bone remembers being broken. Structurally restored but experientially marked.

She wipes her hands again. Left. Right. Left.

The cave is dark. The fire is embers. The water drips.

She sits in the dark with fear. The fear is hers and that is the point. It is not imposed. It is hers. Created by her. Maintained by her. Powered by the engine of a belief so old it predates everything else she is.

Fearless in order to accept the fear. The paradox that sounded absurd over a pot of not-rice. That sounds less absurd now. In the dark. With the fear sitting where it sits.

The path is clear now, lined up like stones on a path. Understanding is the head. Admission is the mouth. Feeling is the bones.

She has reached the bones.

The question remains. Enormous. Simple. Impossible. Present.

How does a woman die when her body screams to live?

Nobody answers. Nobody is here to answer. The cave. The embers. The dripping water. Xiǎohú, a warm shape against her leg, eyes closed, breathing.

The question hangs in the dark.

She sits. She feels. She is afraid.

The embers darken.
