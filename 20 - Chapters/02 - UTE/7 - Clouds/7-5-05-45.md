Condensation glistens on the cave wall. Droplets catch the orange of the dying fire, bright against the damp stone. Yun watches one lengthen before it runs into the darkness below.

She does not move.

Her hands still tremble after the panic. She rests them near her knees while the renewed pressure of qi settles through her body. In the half-dark, the cold of the floor is back.

Water drips at the back of the cave, steadily enough for her to wait between the sounds.

Her hands are damp with sweat. She wipes the left on her robe, then the right, then the left again. The cloth has already taken most of the moisture. She repeats the movement for the texture under her fingers, following skin against cloth until her attention settles.

She is afraid to die. After all her declarations, the fear still tightens her breath in the dark. It is as immediate as a child's.

The cold brings back the first cave. She remembers the small body curled there, surrounded by a world in which things died. She had been afraid enough to make another possibility her certainty. The resistance she feels now began in that dark place and has held through every life since.

She has called her immortality a curse. At other times she has looked for punishment or some force acting upon her. Under those explanations, the same fear has run on. She feels it in the urge to preserve her warmth, to make herself small, and to stay alive. A moment's need has lasted through millennia.

Her immortality is based on fear.

The shielding qi returned in response to it. So did the renewal that erased the red mark. She looks at the healed place on her thigh, following the connection she could once only describe. Fear became conviction, then power, then the reality in which she continued. Every layer of protection rests on that certainty.

The orange fades as the last wood settles into embers. Cold spreads from the floor into her clothing. The damp stone farther back is never touched by the fire's warmth.

She feels it.

The cold comes through the protection. Qi lies around her as before, yet some of the chill still reaches her skin. She attends to that small discomfort. The shielding has returned less completely than she expected.

The panic loosened something.

Something at the edge of the protection is less rigid than it was half a watch ago. The seven heartbeats ended. The burn healed. Still, the cold reaches her through a place that had been closed before. A bone can mend while retaining the trace of a break. She stays with this small difference instead of testing how far it extends.

She wipes her hands again. Left. Right. Left.

The cave is dark. The fire is embers. The water drips.

She sits with the fear in the dark. It belongs to her as surely as the hands she wipes against her robe. She has sustained it for so long that its hold is older than everything else she can name about herself.

She remembers the words spoken over a pot of not-rice: fearless enough to accept the fear. They had sounded absurd across the fire. Here, feeling the pressure they described, she can almost follow what he meant.

She understood the fear, then named it aloud. Now she has lived through its return and felt her fingers clench as qi restored what she was trying to relinquish.

The question is as simple as it is hard to answer.

How does a woman die when her body screams to live?

Xiǎohú breathes against her leg, eyes closed, a small warmth next to the cold stone. Water drips while Yun sits with a question only she can answer.

The question hangs in the dark.

She sits. She feels. She is afraid.

The embers darken.
