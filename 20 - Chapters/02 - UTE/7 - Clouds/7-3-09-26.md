Trail dust had settled into the creases of my boots, a fine gray layer that aged them. It shifted with each step. For two days I had returned to the same thoughts, beginning with the nest until every other loss followed it.

You stopped beside me before I had put that repetition into words.

"Stop," you said.

Clouds hid the afternoon sun. I no longer knew whether to call them weather; the same uncertainty followed everything around me. Ahead of us, Xiǎohú continued along the gray trail, a rust-colored shape among the rocks.

"Stop what?" I said.

"That leads nowhere."

"What does?"

"The thing you're doing. The loop. I can see it. You've been walking the same thought for two days. You start with the nest. Then Wei. Then every other loss. You arrive at guilt, return to the nest, and start again."

I turned to you. I had wanted privacy for those thoughts, though I had given you ample chance to see where they led.

"How would you know?" I said.

The challenge sat plain in my voice. You had been beside me through both days. I had said enough aloud for you to follow the rest, or so I assumed. I expected you to remind me of that.

"Because I think that way," you said.

You said it as plainly as you had told me to stop. I looked up from the dust on my boots.

"Guilt as a circle," you said. "I know that pattern. The loop. Event, cause, and self. Then it starts again. You keep repeating it without arriving anywhere. It doesn't resolve. It just—"

Your hand described a circle in the air. You let it fall to your side.

"You?" I said.

"Yes."

"About what?"

You paused longer than you usually did over a question. I waited. Your hand lay still beside your pack.

"That doesn't matter right now," you said. "What matters is that guilt goes in circles. It's movement disguised as progress. Energy without work. Heat without light."

"So what do you do?" I asked.

You met my eyes. I had learned to expect an answer I might dislike when you looked that directly at me.

"I cook rice," you said.

You set down your pack to make a fire. Once it caught, you put the pot over it. You measured water, then the wild grain you had found that morning. It was close enough to rice to cook much the same way. You attended to each part of the preparation, leaving me to watch from the trail.

I watched.

"That's it?" I said.

"That's it."

"You cook rice."

"Yes."

"And the guilt?"

"It stays. But it stays smaller. Contained. The rice doesn't fix anything. The rice is rice. But making it requires attention. And attention can only be in one place at a time. If mine is on the rice, it's not on the loop."

Heat rose under the pot. The water warmed gradually, small movements beginning near the side closest to the flame. You watched for them before adding the grain.

You stirred the grain through the water. Your gaze never left the pot. Whatever had brought you to this habit, for now the food had your attention.

I sat down without being asked. Warmth reached me from the fire while the not-rice cooked. I was still thinking about the nest, but your hand moving the spoon now sat between one thought and the next.

The eggs were as clear as before. So was the silence around Wei, then the names that had followed it. I still moved from one loss to another without reaching an end.

Steam rose from the pot. You shifted it over the fire, checking the heat. Food needed a place to cook, fuel under it, and someone there when the water began to bubble. Your hands gave me something present to follow.

You had learned this through something of your own. I wondered how often you had sat over a pot while an old regret returned, how many meals it had taken to discover that the work helped. You had offered me the part I could use. The rest was yours to tell when you chose.

I let you keep it. You had done the same for me beside enough fires.

When the not-rice was ready, you divided it between two bowls. You gave each of us the same amount, as you usually did.

"Here," you said.

You held out the steaming bowl. The smell was close to rice, with the wild grain under it. I had seen it go into the water. Now it was food you were offering me.

I took the bowl.

The same thoughts continued. I held the warm bowl while you picked up your spoon, giving me another thing to attend to.
