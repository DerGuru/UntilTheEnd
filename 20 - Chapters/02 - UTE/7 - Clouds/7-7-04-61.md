On the second day of the village's seven-day round, she bends over the basin to wash her face. It is market day, when she goes to the stalls to let Loudcumber tell her which vegetables she needs. There is little other reason to remember the date. Yet it sticks, placing the morning within the ordinary rhythm she has begun to keep.

The basin stands on the wooden table. Mirrors are a luxury the house lacks; cool water serves instead. Her reflection ripples, then steadies. She studies the face she has worn for millennia, every proportion known.

A fine wrinkle lies at the corner of her left eye. One. She leans closer to the water. A friend might call it a laugh line, if she had friends who said such things. If she laughed enough to make the explanation plausible.

She traces it with a fingertip. The skin has a texture there, like the trace of a fold in paper smoothed flat. Time has begun drawing something new across a face she thought she knew completely.

Fascinating is the nearest word she can find. She has seen wrinkles on so many faces. Finding one here is as different as touching snow after only hearing about it. Her fingertip returns to the crease and confirms that it belongs to the skin. She can hardly leave it alone.

"So that's what it feels like," she says to the reflection. Her voice sounds small in the house that now contains her, the wrinkle, and the morning.

The scratch comes that afternoon while she pulls weeds under the plum tree. Pink-and-white blossoms spread overhead, their sweetness light on the air. A thorn catches the back of her hand as she reaches into a tangled patch. A thin red line appears, small enough for most gardeners to forget by dinner.

Blood beads along the line. For millennia her qi would have sealed such a scratch as soon as it formed. Now there is time to examine it. The injury will scab, then heal over the coming days. The slow repair can happen while she does something else. It will still be there when she looks again. She is learning to expect that too, the ordinary persistence of a mark that needs time.

The smallest changes are extraordinary. She has lived with the same face, the same skin, and the same tireless body through the rise of whole civilizations. A wrinkle gives her something new to trace. A scratch lasts long enough to watch it change. She glances at the red line once more and returns to the weeds.

A voice calls from the fence. The neighbor holds out a jar of something pickled at full arm's length, leaving no room between the gift and its acceptance.

"For you. You're too thin."

Yun takes the jar. The neighbor examines her face quickly, thoroughly, and without a trace of apology.

"You have wrinkles," the neighbor says.

"One."

"Wrinkles come from laughing."

"I don't laugh," she says.

The neighbor gives her a long look.

"You should start," the neighbor says. She turns to leave, then stops. "Come eat with me tomorrow. You look like you need a proper meal."

She leaves before Yun can offer an answer. The neighbor has a habit of saying exactly what she means, then removing herself before anyone can complicate it with discussion.

The scratch has begun to scab, a small dark line at the start of the repair.

She returns to the basin and traces the wrinkle once more.

Her mouth lifts in the reflection, the beginning of a smile, close enough to recognize. The neighbor has invited her for a proper meal. For once, she has something simple to look forward to.

Tomorrow, perhaps.
