We had returned to higher elevations. Following the direction I felt drawn to. The last place of our return was already behind us. The trail was narrower now, the last suggestion of maintained path giving way to animal tracks and geological accident — disappeared and reappeared in the white, the intermittent visibility that fog produced, the world flickering between present and absent with each shift of the moisture.

The fog was mine. I didn't know that yet — not at the start of the morning, not in the first minutes of walking when the mist sat low and thick in the valley like something poured. I thought it was weather. Mountain weather, the kind that arrived without consultation and departed without announcement, the atmospheric caprice of high altitudes where the difference between cloud and fog was a matter of elevation.

I stopped for no apparent reason. There was no external trigger. The legs ceasing their mechanical repetition without instruction from the mind, the halt arriving from somewhere below conscious decision.

"What are you thinking about?" you said.

You were beside me and you could see it. You could see everything about me now. Like a doctor could read a body after years of practice. The micro-expression, the halted stride, the posture of a woman standing in fog and thinking about a dead boy.

"Someone who was better than me," I said.

"That's most people."

The delivery. Flat. Perfect. Your intonation that placed the line exactly on the border between provocation and comfort, the surgical deployment of humor that served as defibrillator for a heart that was threatening to stop.

Almost a smile. The muscles around my mouth attempted the gesture — the upward pull, the contraction — and achieved perhaps sixty percent of the intended result before the weight of the memory pulled them back.

Around us, the fog thickened.

The immediate, responsive thickening of a medium responding to an emotion. The fog closed. The visibility that had been ten meters became five, became three, the white pressing in from all sides with the intimacy of a world wrapping itself around a feeling it was compelled to express.

Xiǎohú was a shape in the white — amber points at about knee height, moving, the only color in a colorless world.

I didn't notice the fog was mine. Not yet. For now, the fog was weather, the weather was cooperating with my mood and the cooperation was coincidence.

Of course it was.

We walked. The fog walked with us. The mountain continued its patient ascent beneath our feet, invisible now, present only as incline, texture and the effort of bodies climbing through impedance.

You said nothing more.
