We climbed again, toward the place that still pulled at me. The spot where we had turned back lay below us now. Our path narrowed into animal tracks between rocks, showing briefly through the fog before another fold of white covered it.

The fog was mine. At the start of the morning, with damp air on my face, I took it for weather. Mist lay thick in the valley. We had climbed through cloud before; this was another stretch where we would have to watch our footing until it cleared.

I stopped on the narrow track. I had been thinking about Wei. For a few breaths I stood looking into the white past the next rock, not moving toward it.

"What are you thinking about?" you said.

You stopped next to me. After so much traveling together, you noticed when my stride changed. Your eyes were on my face while I looked past the rock.

"Someone who was better than me," I said.

"That's most people."

You said it flatly, leaving me to decide whether to take offense. I turned toward you. It was a remarkably poor moment to insult someone who could rearrange the mountain, which you knew perfectly well.

My mouth lifted before I thought of the boy again. For that brief moment you had made me attend to the living person next to me. You waited out the pause.

Around us, the fog thickened.

It closed around us as the memory returned. Ten paces of track were visible, then five, then three. White filled the space between the rocks, leaving only their nearest edges.

Xiǎohú moved through the white at knee height. Her amber eyes were the only color past you.

Dampness beaded on my face, and I didn't recognize what I was doing. The weather happened to have thickened while I thought of him. I let myself accept the coincidence.

Of course it was.

We walked on. Fog pressed close. The rough track climbed under my feet, though I could scarcely see where it led. You matched my pace through the white.

You said nothing more.
