Not enough. The wave was ahead of me. Already past the trench. Already south. Half a heartbeat from the barriers. One from the village. Three from the slope where Wei stood with the villagers, a bundle at his feet and his eyes pointed in the direction I'd gone.

It was not enough to kill anymore, but enough to break. Enough to crush the grain stores that stood between the village and winter. Enough to poison the well permanently, for the people who were living there at least. Enough to turn a community that had survived through stubbornness into a place where the numbers didn't work anymore.

Not enough.

I didn't think about it. That's the truth, and it matters here. Thousands of years of existence had produced a mind that thought about everything, that analyzed, calculated, weighed, measured, and rationalized every action or inaction with exhaustive thoroughness. Too much time, too much evidence, and too little willingness to act without certainty.

I didn't think about it.

My hand came up.

Open. Palm forward. Fingers spread. The gesture I hadn't needed in three thousand years. The bones singing. The muscles still remembering.

Four words came to me with a resolution that transcended thought. My mind went clear and cold, accepting what was coming.

Then so be it.

The wave was less than a heartbeat from the barrier wall. The distance between catastrophe and my people had shrunk to nothing. My hand was up. My palm was forward. Decisions required deliberation. These four words had come without it.

This required nothing except the thing I'd spent thousands of years burying and these past days digging back up.

**Then so be it.**
