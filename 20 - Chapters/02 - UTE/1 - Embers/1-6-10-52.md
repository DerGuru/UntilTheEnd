Dawn came in the usual colors, dark fading to gray, the first thin line of gold on the eastern ridge. The wrongness was in the air. The weight of it. Along with the light came pressure, vibration, and a dense expectancy that made me brace for impact.

I stood at the river. The trench was full enough. The widened channel was flowing, brown, warm, and shallow, but flowing. The stones I'd stacked along the edges held their positions. The qi-channel intersection was exactly where my calculations had placed it: the point where Xu Ran's discharge would meet the water, the physics would do its work, and the village would survive. The people on the southern slope. The boy sleeping on bare ground. The mother with cracked hands.

Probably. The margin wasn't comfortable. But it existed and margins were all I had.

The qi-column brightened. One final compression. It hummed in my teeth, in the bones of my face, and in the cartilage between my vertebrae. Xu Ran's spiral hit its structural limit. The layers of compressed qi, each one packed tighter than the last, reached the density at which containment equaled capacity. The only directions left were inward, into failure, or outward.

He went outward.

The first release came at the moment the sun crested the ridge. Poetic coincidence, or not. Cultivators often timed their transitions to natural cycles, aligning their pressure points with the earth's rotation, leveraging the planet's own qi-flow to supplement what they were forcing into place. Smart. Disciplined. Exactly the strategic thinking that would make Xu Ran formidable and that currently made him catastrophic.

The shockwave expanded from his position like a sphere of compressed air, invisible, vast, and carrying the accumulated energy of many days of hunting. Consolidation had forced that energy into a shape that was still unstable. The pressure hit the river before the sound did. The water surged upstream first, pushed backward by the pressure, piling against the channel walls in a wave that shouldn't have existed in a drought-reduced river. Then the absorption began. The water caught the energy, held it, and dispersed it through its volume like a sponge catching thrown water.

The channel held.

The widened section, my hundred and twenty paces, caught the remainder. Clay, stone, and qi I'd promised myself I wouldn't use. The qi-energy that the natural channel couldn't absorb flowed into the trench, hit the stacked walls, and was redirected into the downstream pool, where the volume was sufficient to disperse it without further propagation.

The residual pressure passed through the ground under me, a weakened vibration carrying three-tenths of the original force. It would reach the village in four heartbeats.

I counted.

One. The vibration crossed the river bed.

Two. It entered the valley floor, passing through the qi-channels I'd redirected many days ago.

Three. It hit the stone barriers on the eastern ridge. The barriers deflected most of it. The second wall, the one I'd rebuilt after the hunting explosion, lost its top row again. Stones tumbled. The deflection angle narrowed.

Four. The residual wave reached the village.

I couldn't see it from here, but the faint, distant tremor reached me as the attenuated pressure touched the buildings I'd repaired, the fences Wei had rebuilt, and the wall that had already fallen once. The vibration was minor. Less than the hunting explosion. Less than the tremors we'd endured for so many days. Within the margin.

A tile falling. Maybe two. A window cracking. Something wooden shifting.

The walls stood. The buildings held. The people lived.

The margin held.

The shockwave passed. The river settled. The qi-column tightened again. The compressed energy held its coiled shape, taut as a held breath.

Xu Ran had not finished.

The valley was still here.

I sat on the bank. The river murmured. The dawn continued its progression over tribulations, thresholds, and the small, desperate engineering of a woman who'd dug a trench to save a village that didn't know it needed saving.

My hands were in the water. The current was warm, still warm, still carrying the residual qi from the absorption, the energy dissipating into heat that made the shallow flow feel like bathwater. My palms were smooth against the riverbed stones. No blisters. No calluses. No evidence.

I allowed myself thirty heartbeats.

Then I stood up. I dried my hands on my clothes and walked south.

They would want to know. They would want to go home.
