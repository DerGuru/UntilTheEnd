The forest was dying around me. Its slow decline through the drought, the dead branches, and the retreat of life from the valley's periphery had accelerated into something immediate. The trees closest to Xu Ran's position were stripped, bark peeled away by qi-pressure, the exposed wood underneath bleached white, the grain visible like bone under skin. Further out, the trees were still standing but emptied, even their twigs gone, their branches bare. Each one a skeleton of its former architecture, alive half a watch ago.

The river was ahead. My river. My trench. A hundred and twenty paces. Clay and stone that I'd dug in the dark, part by hand, part by something I was still pretending was just efficiency. Widened, reinforced, and aligned with the physics of qi-absorption. I'd been studying those physics since before the trees I was walking through had been seeds.

The trench was still intact. The stones held. The water flowed, barely, but flowing. The qi-flow I'd redirected was still channeling through the widened section, the water absorbing residual energy.

I knelt at the riverbank and checked the stones.

The ground was fractured. Deep, structural fissures running through the forest floor like the crazing on old pottery. Qi leaked from the cracks in visible wisps, pale and faintly luminescent, the excess energy that Xu Ran's consolidation was producing faster than the earth could absorb.

I walked through this. Toward the center.

The air was thick enough to taste. Metallic, electric, with an undertone of something organic, the smell of qi at concentrations that dissolved wood and stone into their smallest parts. Structures stable for centuries were breaking down around me. Ozone. Iron. The faint, sweet decay of wood crumbling at an accelerated rate.

Normal humans would be dead by now. The qi-density at this distance, two hundred paces from the epicenter, was sufficient to disrupt everything living. The body would fail under its interference. Death would feel like falling asleep: a gradual dimming, a loss of coordination, and a slide into unconsciousness before the heart stopped. Quick. Painless, probably. Not a bad way to go, if you had to.

The pressure spiked.

For half a heartbeat the air tightened like the water around you when you dive too fast. My skin prickled. The river ahead slapped once against its own bank, a sharp sound.

Then it eased back to its steady, murderous weight. Never gone.

I kept walking.

One hundred paces. The trees had disintegrated, their structure unable to withstand the qi-saturation. Where ancient oaks had stood, there were circles of gray dust on the forest floor. Centuries of growth, reduced to powder. The ground beneath was smooth, glassy, the clay fused by qi-pressure into something that resembled ceramic. Dark, reflective, and warm under my feet.

Behind me. Above me. Around me.

Xu Ran's presence.

He was close, fifty paces. His qi-spiral registered with the same granularity as the stone under my fingers. Tight. Dense. Layered. The final configuration: everything packed, everything compressed, the structure complete. The containment held. The threshold approached with the mathematical inevitability of a countdown that had started many days ago and was now in its final digits.

The tribulation.

It would begin soon. A few hundred heartbeats. A handful, maybe. The qi-column was pulsing, a pale gold pillar rising from the dead forest like something sacred. The light caught the dust of disintegrated trees and turned it into a shimmering curtain. Warm and soft, a radiance painters spend lifetimes trying to capture. Beautiful, and surrounded by a hundred paces of ash that had been forest this morning.

I took a deep breath and nodded once, punctuation between one task and the next.

The next task was waiting.

The epicenter was ahead. I stood on the glazed ground above the river. The trench behind me. The village to the west, a collection of empty buildings and a southern slope where fifty-four people were safe. Among them, one twelve-year-old boy.

Hopefully safe enough.

I waited. The air was still. The ground hummed. The sky was wrong, too bright, too saturated, painful to look at.

The countdown was nearly done.

It sang in my bones, the old frequency that had been part of me since my first memories and would be part of me after the last. I knew what was coming. I always knew.

I stood still. My hands were at my sides. My feet were on the glazed earth. My breathing was even.

Like standing on a hilltop, watching.

But this time, I was standing here. In the valley. In the path. In the direct trajectory of something that was about to happen.

For the first time in a long time, I was not observing.

I was here.
