The boar's tusks caught the light as its head came up, aimed at the boy's chest. He threw himself sideways, twisting as far as the trapped ankle allowed. The tusks scraped the earth where his chest had been, gouging twin furrows in the dirt. The boar's momentum carried it forward. Its shoulder struck the root holding the boy's ankle hard enough to snap it. He rolled free, scrambled onto his knees, and crawled, because standing took time he didn't have.

The boar turned with lumbering force, a beast too large for its frame. Its hooves churned the ground as its hindquarters swung wide, smashing through a sapling that had taken thirty years to grow. The sapling went over with hardly a sound, its roots too shallow to hold it upright.

The boy made it to his feet. He was limping; the ankle that had been caught was already swelling, the skin flushed red even through the dirt. He backed up, hands out in front of him as if his palms could stop a laden cart's weight of qi-hardened muscle. His eyes were locked on the beast. His chest heaved.

"*Please.*"

That one was quieter, addressed to anything that might hear him. The sky, the trees, and whatever power a child believed in when the world narrowed to an animal bearing down on him. His eyes returned to the tusks.

The boar pawed the ground. Its breath came in thick snorts that misted in the morning air, warmer than it should have been, heated by the qi burning through its body. Its eyes fixed on the boy again.

I watched.

I know how that sounds. I remember the smell of the snapped sapling. Believe me, it sounds worse from the inside.

It charged.

This time, there was no room to dodge. The boy was backed against a moss-covered boulder twice his height. He pressed himself flat against the stone. His fingers clawed at the rock behind him, searching for a handhold or a crack. Centuries of rain and root pressure had worn it smooth as far as he could reach.

The boar closed the distance in two strides.

The boy did something unexpected. He stopped trying to climb. Facing the beast, he picked up a rock from the ground, a small one barely the size of his fist, still crusted with dirt.

He threw it.

It hit the boar on the snout, right between the nostrils. Stone struck cartilage with a dull thok. The boar flinched, its head jerking aside. At this tier, a mortal's throw would barely have hurt it, but the sudden impact had interrupted its charge. The flinch bought a heartbeat. Maybe two.

The boy turned to me.

He had exhausted the scream. Dirt, tears, and snot streaked his face. His hands were shaking so badly that the second rock he'd picked up rattled against his palm.

"Are you just going to *watch*?!"

His voice cracked on the last word.

The boar recovered. It shook its head and snorted, this time releasing a pulse of qi that flattened the grass in a circle around its hooves. The air thickened. Pressure tightened across my skin, sharper than the displaced air of its charge.

The boy staggered. His knees buckled under the qi pressure. He didn't have the cultivation to resist even a tier-two beast's passive aura. His nose began to bleed, a thin red line tracking from his left nostril to his lip.

He wiped it on his sleeve without looking away from the beast.

"I'm going to die," he said, and the words came out too flat to be brave. "And you're going to—" He swallowed. "You're going to watch."

The boar lowered its head.

I took stock of the boy: the blood on his lip, the rock in his shaking hand, the ankle already turning purple, the basket overturned twenty paces behind the beast with its cargo of poisonous mushrooms scattered across the dirt.

He was going to die collecting the wrong mushrooms for someone waiting at home. A small death for a large reason. By morning, the forest would sound much as it had before he entered it.

The boar charged.

I moved.
