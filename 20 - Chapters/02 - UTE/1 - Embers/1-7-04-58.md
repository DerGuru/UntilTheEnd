Xu Ran's core sealed with a pulse that rang in the marrow of every bone. What had begun days ago, the compression, the tribulation, and the initial release that had tested my trench, reached its conclusion with the elegance of something done correctly by someone talented enough to deserve the result. The remaining spiral collapsed inward. The last layers folded. The qi compressed into a single point of density that flickered, held, and then solidified.

The breakthrough had settled. The core was whole.

The boy had done it. The young man who'd been hunting spirit beasts, draining the valley, and sitting in a cave compressing power with single-minded focus on his own ascension had ascended. His new core burned in his chest like a small sun, stable, brilliant, and radiating power that would make him a significant figure in whatever sect or faction or territory he chose to enter.

Good for him.

The shockwave built in the fraction of a heartbeat between the core's final seal and expulsion. The structure locked, and everything it couldn't contain was pushed outward.

All of it.

All at once.

A sphere of pure qi-energy expanding from Xu Ran's position at a speed that made sound look leisurely, carrying the accumulated waste of many days of consolidation and the specific violence of a structure rearranging itself at a fundamental level.

The shockwave hit me first.

It moved through my body like wind through an open window, present, impotent, and barely noticed. The qi-energy at this concentration was a catastrophe for everything in its path except me. For me, it was weather. Warm weather, noted and dismissed.

I didn't move. The wave passed through me, around me, and continued south.

It hit the last standing trees. The remaining skeletons, the bare, stripped trunks that had survived the preparation phase, snapped at every point simultaneously. They fragmented where they stood. Splinters. Dust. The wood went from solid to powder between one heartbeat and the next.

It hit the ground. The glazed surface cracked wider, deep, branching fractures radiating outward from the epicenter like the pattern on a shattered mirror. The earth itself groaned, a sound too low to hear clearly but heavy in my chest as the rock fractured under me.

It hit the river.

My river. My trench. My hundred and twenty paces. Clay, stone, and compromise.

The water caught it. The widened channel received the leading edge and did what water does with energy: absorbed it. The surface erupted. Steam. All at once, violently. The qi-energy was too concentrated for gradual absorption; the water took it instantly, exceeded its thermal capacity, and converted to vapor with a violence that threw a wall of steam a hundred paces into the air.

The stones held. The stacked granite, each piece chosen for density, each angle calculated for deflection, caught the excess energy and redirected it. Some of it. The front row burst. Three stones fractured, the granite unable to absorb the qi-load, splitting along crystal planes with sharp, percussive cracks. But the second row held. The third row held. The angles I'd calculated deflected the redirected energy into the downstream pool, where the volume absorbed it with a surge that raised the water level by a pace and sent waves crashing against the banks.

The trench held.

One third of the shockwave. Absorbed. Dispersed. Gone.

But two thirds remained. The portion that the water and the stones couldn't catch, the overwhelming majority, rolled onward. South. Toward the village. Toward the southern slope. Toward the fifty-four people three li away, the twelve-year-old boy among them. They were directly in the path of a force that would crack foundations, collapse roofs, shatter the grain stores, and contaminate the well that Mrs. Liu had dug deeper with her practical, indomitable hands.

Two thirds.

The wave rolled on. Three li. Five heartbeats. My barriers, the stone walls on the eastern ridge, would catch some. The redirected qi-channels would absorb some. But even combined, the reduction would bring the wave down to two-fifths of its original force.

Two-fifths of the original force, hitting an unprotected village.

Not lethal. Probably. The evacuation had moved everyone to the southern slope, behind the ridge. The ridge itself would block part of the pressure. The buildings would take the damage instead of the people.

But. The buildings were homes and the homes held stores. The stores held the grain, the dried fish, and the filtered water that kept the village's people from starvation. Starvation was slower than a shockwave. It was just as final.

Two-fifths.

Two heartbeats. The wave was past the trench. It moved through the dead zone, crossed the fused ground, and entered the valley where the living trees had stood before everything living fled.

Two heartbeats until the barriers. Three until the village. Five until the southern slope.

Not enough.

The words arrived with the simplicity of arithmetic. The trench, the barriers, the redirections, everything I'd built by effort and the deliberate, principled refusal to use what I was: not enough.

The wave rolled south. The valley trembled in advance of its arrival.

One heartbeat.

Not enough.
