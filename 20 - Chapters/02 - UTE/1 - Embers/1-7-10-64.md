Three words, low in my throat as I knelt over him. I couldn't remember when I'd last used that register. Below diction and above vibration, a quality of authority that made the people nearest me draw back. I hadn't raised my voice. I hadn't needed to.

They moved. All of them. Elder Li released Wei's head, lowering it to the ground with one hand while pulling back with the other. His mother lifted her hands from Wei's face. She didn't leave reach of her unconscious child, but she made space. A pace. Enough.

I settled my weight on my knees.

His face was slack and gave me little reassurance. His mind was elsewhere, deep in unconsciousness. What remained was breathing, heartbeat, and the slow, grinding work of repair.

Under his skin, the light pulsed. Irregular, too fast, each pulse a small discharge of excess qi leaking through the channel walls and radiating outward through tissue still adapting to the energy. The channels were closed; the safety mechanism had worked, shutting intake before rupture. But they were still loaded. Full to capacity with qi they hadn't been designed to hold, straining at their walls, the pressure producing the glow as any overcharged body produces waste heat.

I placed my hands on his chest.

"What are you doing?" His mother's voice. Close. Ragged.

"Helping." One word. It was all I could spare.

"Will he—"

"Quiet. Please."

She went quiet. The *please* did it. I hadn't used the word in centuries.

Both hands, flat, palms down, over the place where the primary meridian originated, where the dantian would form once he was old enough and sufficiently trained, where the central hub of the qi-circulatory network gathered its energy before distributing it to the periphery.

I closed my eyes.

And I reached in.

Deeper than touch. Deeper than the qi-sense that healers use to map damage and identify blockages. What I did was simpler, more dangerous, and more intimate than any of those approximations.

His channels lay open to me, each one: the primary, the secondaries, and the tertiary network. Built by my instruction, shaped by his practice, strengthened by months of careful, gradual development. Strained. Overcharged. But intact.

The pressure was uneven. The primary channel bore the most; it had absorbed the bulk of the qi-flood and hadn't had time to spread the excess before shutdown. The secondaries were loaded but manageable. The tertiaries, the fine network, were hot, inflamed, the tissue around them irritated by energy levels they'd never been exposed to.

I opened the primary. Slowly. A fraction at a time, releasing the trapped pressure. Too fast and the channel walls would rupture. Too slow and the trapped energy would convert to heat, burning him from the inside.

Qi leaked out, a thin stream, controlled and directed. I guided it downward, through my own hands, through me, and into the ground. I absorbed it as the river had absorbed the shockwave: completely, without resistance, without reaching my limit.

The primary channel's pressure dropped. The glow along his sternum faded from blue-white to blue. Then the secondaries began to drain, the paired channels running from heart to hands, following the path of least resistance like water on a slope. The glow along his arms dimmed. The channels relaxed.

The tertiaries followed. Hundreds of them, thousands, a web that mapped every nerve and capillary. Each one overcharged. Each one draining into the central pathway, through me, and into the earth. I just needed to keep the primary open, the flow steady, and the ground under my hands from saturating.

The ground absorbed the energy. There was room.

Six hundred heartbeats. Nine hundred.

"His face," his mother whispered. "The light is fading. Is that—"

"Good," I said. "It's good."

She pressed both hands over her mouth. She held the sound in. She held everything in.

His breathing changed. The shallow, rapid pattern slowed. It deepened. The intervals stretched. One, two, three. One, two, three. The third-step pattern reasserting itself, the body's trained rhythm emerging as the overload eased.

The glow faded. A faint luminescence remained, a residual charge that would take watches or days to fully dissipate. But the active radiation had stopped. The channels were open, clear, and functional.

Different.

The capacity was larger than yesterday. The qi-flood had done what controlled training would have done over months: it had stressed the channels to their limit and the channels had responded by restructuring, like muscle building density under strain or bone adding mass under impact.

He was at the third step now. Maybe higher. But through catastrophe. Through the uncontrolled intake of dispersed qi that I had put into the atmosphere to save a village. My doing. My consequence. Linked by a chain of causation that started with a raised hand and ended with a twelve-year-old boy glowing on the ground.

His face relaxed. The vacancy softened into something more recognizable. Sleep. Real sleep. The body's natural recovery state, the deep, restorative work of repair, integration, and preparation for waking. His features settled into the arrangement that twelve-year-old faces make when the day's demands have been removed and the architecture of childhood reasserts itself.

I lifted my hands from his chest and set them in my lap.

He would live. He would wake up several watches from now, maybe tomorrow, once his body had finished its reconstruction. Once his mind had processed whatever it had seen in the moment when everything opened and the world was made of light.

He would be different.

"He'll wake?" Elder Li. Standing behind the others, hands clasped, out of practical contributions and left with only the question.

"Yes."

"And then?"

I didn't answer. *And then* was a door I hadn't opened yet. The hinges were rusted. The room behind it was dark.

My hands were steady. Warm. Too smooth, the skin too unblemished for an old woman who collected herbs, knew things about stones, and lived at the edge of a forest.

And everyone was watching.
