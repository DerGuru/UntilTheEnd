You meditated at night, sitting upright on your bedroll under the trees. That in itself was ordinary. Cultivators meditated as farmers planted or scholars read, the professional activity that defined the category. What was unusual was the volume.

It began with breathing: deep, rhythmic inhalation and exhalation that directed qi through the pathways. Most cultivators performed it in silence, in the private architecture of their cultivation.

You breathed loudly.

You put deliberate effort into the volume. Each inhalation was audible at ten paces. Each exhalation even more so. You hissed air through pursed lips, directing the sound outward across the clearing.

Then the mantras.

You half-voiced them, making the space between speech and thought audible. Words, or approximations of words. Syllables from the old water-traditions, the ones that predated the organized sects. I caught fragments. *hàiyuán*, *chénduǐ*. Return-to-source. Sink-to-bottom. You chanted them loudly and consistently.

The mantras had the bare contour of a tune. Their rise and fall suggested you had decided at some point that chanting was the form, non-negotiable.

You hummed between mantras. The humming bridged one set of syllables to the next, a continuous output that filled the intervals and ensured none was silent.

No interval was silent.

I stood at my standard distance. Brown grass. Stars. The conditions of every night since I entered the forest. I had found the hut, walked, and stopped. But now there was sound. Your meditation filled the shared space, continuous, unavoidable.

It was irritating. I had to attend closely to recognize that, because the disconnection normally filtered such categorizations.

Irritating was a category. Irritating required preference: silence over noise, one state over another. I want this to be different. The disconnection should have prevented irritating. Yet it didn't.

"Can you do that quieter?"

The words came out despite the disconnection. Five words. A request. An irritation had found a passage through, like water finding a crack in a dam I had trusted to hold.

You stopped. Mid-mantra. Mid-hum. The silence that followed was absolute. The cessation was as audible as the sound had been.

"No," you said.

"It's not a request."

"Noted." You straightened your spine by an increment that said the conversation was being tolerated rather than engaged. "Still a no."

You resumed. The humming. The mantras. The breathing. Your nightly noise, a meditation that sounded like an argument with silence and was, apparently, an immovable feature of your presence.

I stood. In the noise.

The noise was deliberate. Standing there, I began to suspect that you needed to meditate loudly. Perhaps silence was what you feared. Perhaps silence was forgetting. The broken core, the compulsive notation, and the loud meditation began to look like parts of one behavior: the continuous production of evidence that you existed. You recorded the world. Did the recording make you real?

Silence led to disappearance. Noise made survival possible.

I thought you were afraid of the thing I was becoming.

The observation reached me coldly, from behind glass. It was the first I had made about you that reached past your annoying habits to what they might mean.

You were broken differently. You fought subtraction with addition: more sound, more notes, and the continuous documentation of everything you perceived. Perception was ephemeral. The ephemeral was the enemy, and the enemy was silence.

We were the same problem expressed in opposite directions. I was silence that produced death. You were noise that fought forgetting.

You continued. The mantras rose and fell. The humming bridged the gaps. Your breathing filled the space between the humming and the mantras until every gap was closed.

I was enduring.

You had made me say five words. You had made me experience irritation. You had made me endure. In the hierarchy of reconnection, if such a hierarchy existed, these were small movements. Negligible.

But the temperature was rising.

You would not know this. Or you would know it eventually from your notebook, from the data you accumulated: my word-count, my proximity, and my responses over time.

You meditated loudly until the stars shifted to the positions that indicated midnight. Then you stopped and slept. The sleeping was silent. Your waking was designed to prevent silence, yet your sleeping was the silence you feared. But sleeping was temporary. Waking was long. Waking was loud.

I stood. In the returned silence. In the contrast.

The silence was different after noise. The rhythm of your mantras still ran through it. Each gap fell where I had come to expect another note. Even with you asleep, I stood waiting for the next one. The clearing had been this quiet before you came. Now every missing sound had a place: the breath through your lips, the murmured syllable, and the hum that should have followed.

Silence with a you-shaped hole in it.

I stood until morning. Eyes open. The qi cycling in my chest hummed on, an octave below your meditation, a counterpoint no one heard because no one was supposed to.

Morning came. You woke. You opened the notebook.

"You're still standing," you said. Not a question. An entry being verified against last night's data.

"You're still writing," I said.

You considered this. The brush paused over the page while you decided whether a counter-observation merited documentation.

"That is not equivalent," you said.

"No," I said. "Yours is louder."

You wrote.
