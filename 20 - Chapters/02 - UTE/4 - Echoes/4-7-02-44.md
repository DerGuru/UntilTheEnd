You lasted two watches.

Two watches of walking, slow and careful, each step a negotiation between forward motion and the broken infrastructure that permitted it. Then you stopped. Your body made the decision and informed you afterward. You sat on a root, your breathing went shallow, and your right hand pressed against your ribs, which meant the end of today's travel.

We were in the forest again, real forest with standing trees, intact canopy, and filtered light. The destroyed clearing was long behind us. The air smelled of leaf litter and damp bark instead of shattered wood and exposed earth.

I built the fire. You sat against the root, watching the canopy and breathing.

Your notebook was in my pack. I took it out and held it where you could see it.

"Can I read this? All of it?" I asked.

Your gaze went from the notebook to me and back. Calculation moved behind your eyes, someone deciding how much to permit.

"Yes," you said.

I sat on a root across from you and opened it. Your handwriting was miniature and dense, the script of someone who wrote faster than neatness permitted.

The first entry: *Day 1. Found her. Oak tree. 10 paces. No response. Qi-radius constant, approximately 3 paces. No food consumed. No sleep observed. No directional preference. Subject appears functional but unmotivated.*

Subject. You had called me subject.

I turned pages. Day by day. Word counts. Qi-measurements. Directional data. Everything quantified. Everything preserved.

I stopped at an entry. Read it twice.

"The path," I said. "A path through undergrowth. With a cairn."

"You were frustrated," you said. "The stream crossing. You were irritated about being slowed. Moments later, a path appeared through dense undergrowth that should have required a detour. Old-looking. Worn edges. Compressed earth. And a cairn: three stones, mossy, years of growth. None of it had been there a short while before."

I turned more pages. Stopped again.

"I fell?" I asked.

You looked up. Something shifted in your expression.

"You wrote about it. That I fell and something caught me."

"A cliff," you said slowly, watching my face. "You walked off the edge. Something arrested the fall at the last moment. Something like a qi-cushion, but not from your meridian system. From deeper."

I did not remember the cliff, the fall, the arrest, or the height. Your words describing it were words about someone else.

"We talked about this," you said. "At length. The cliff was why you started the poison experiment. It was the first evidence of the substrate, your word. The fall was the beginning of everything that followed."

The beginning. I studied the entry, your miniature script documenting an event that had set the course we were still on. I could not reach any of it: the experience, the conversation, the decision it had produced.

"I don't remember any of it," I said.

You said nothing, but your eyes changed. The clinical detachment receded, replaced by something I would not name.

I read. Turned. Read.

The observations accumulated. Your careful, hedged language. *Consistent with internal origin. Consistent with defensive response. Consistent with pattern-suppression.* You never wrote conclusions, only observations, the discipline of someone who understood the difference.

"Something comes from inside me," I said, reading your words rather than my own. "Below the meridians. Below the cultivated structure."

"Yes."

"And something doesn't want me to understand it."

"That's what the data suggests." You shifted against the root. The ribs. "The headaches correlate with cognitive progress. The memory gaps correlate with insight. The erasures correlate with pattern recognition. It's defensive."

Erasures. The word sat in your handwriting, and the gaps in my own memory sat where the things you described should have been. The cliff. The path. The mechanisms. All documented in your hand because my mind could not hold them.

"After the poison," I said, turning to the last entries, the pages where your handwriting stopped. "There's nothing here."

You were quiet. A different quiet than before.

"The shockwave knocked me unconscious," you said. "I woke up only the next evening. You were sitting by the fire. And to my question what had happened, you had only one word." A pause. "Me."

I remembered that. But between the poison and the word lay a gap, watches I had lived through and that had been taken.

Not nothing, then. A sensation, the strange, slow receding of the morning after. A room emptying while I stood in it. Something had been there and then it wasn't. I remembered the *shape* of the loss but not what had been lost.

"Whatever I understood after the poison is gone," I said. It was no question. I had written nothing down. The notebook had been in the debris, and I had found no paper and pen to record my own mind before the erasure took it.

"What else is gone?" I said.

You were quiet, choosing words.

"I don't know," you said. "I documented what I observed. But I wasn't always present. And the things that happen inside your mind—" You paused. "You don't share those."

True. I did not.

"I can tell you what you said and did," you said. "I can tell you the qi-readings and the behavioral shifts. What you thought, what you actually understood before the erasures: that's not in the notebook. That was yours. I couldn't record what I couldn't see, hear, or detect otherwise."

"How much else have I lost?" I said.

The pages held your miniature script and months of data. Everything you could see, preserved. Everything I could not hold, gone.

But reading your words, the observations, the patterns, the hedged conclusions, produced no headache. No pressure behind the eyes. No blur. I was reading your understanding, and whatever operated inside me did not recognize external input as a threat.

I could not think the patterns into existence. But I could read them.

I closed the notebook. Held it in both hands. The cracked binding, the loose pages, the weight of someone else's documentation of my life.

"I'd like to keep reading," I said. "Later."

You nodded, a man who had written a notebook for exactly this purpose and had waited for exactly this moment.

The light filtered through the canopy. You closed your eyes. The ribs. The exhaustion. Within a short while, you were asleep.

I sat with the notebook in my lap, reading and rereading as the filtered light shifted toward evening. Your breathing was steady. The forest was quiet.

When the light began to dim, I set the notebook aside and built the fire. The evening routine: wood, flame, heated water, the small domestic repetitions that structured time. I cooked the rice and set your portion near the fire to stay warm. You woke when the smell reached you, the slow, reluctant waking of a body that wanted more sleep and was denied it by hunger.

You ate, slowly, one-handed, the ribs dictating the terms. I did not eat. We did not speak. The conversation from the afternoon was enough. More than enough.

Evening. I placed my hand on your chest and sent the threads through the cleared paths. This time you did not flinch. The qi settled into the tissue around the ribs, warming, reducing. Afterward you breathed easier.

The night was cold again, open forest, clear sky through the gaps in the canopy. I fed qi into the fire as I had the night before. The warmth wrapped the camp. You slept. I sat with the notebook, the fire, and the knowledge that I was less than I had been. The evidence was in my hands.
