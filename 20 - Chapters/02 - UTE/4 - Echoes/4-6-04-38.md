I tore my sleeve.

The motion was automatic. Thousands of years of automatic, the practiced mechanics of someone who had dressed wounds in wars, in villages, in the repetitive emergencies that immortality presented. I tore the sleeve at the seam, folded it, and pressed it against the laceration on your temple.

Blood. Your blood. On my hands. On the fabric. On the ground. Around us, the dust was still settling. Fine particles drifting through shafts of light that had no business being this beautiful.

Your breathing was shallow and wet, the wetness blood in the airways. Your ribs were broken because of me.

/ʔh:/. The sound arrived in the old tongue, the language I'd helped shape when words were still forming, a linguistic fossil from a period that memory had not erased because the sound was attached to muscle memory, and muscle memory was deeper than thought. /ʔh:/. Pressure. The first word for pressing something closed.

I pressed the fabric against the wound and the wound against the skull. My hand against the fabric. Pressure through medium.

I could have healed this with qi. But your core was broken. Your meridian structure was fractured, incomplete, the channels that should have carried qi damaged in ways that made them unpredictable. Threading qi into a broken meridian system would be pressure into a cracked pipe. The energy would go where the cracks directed it, not where I intended. I could accelerate the wrong thing. I could rupture what remained.

I had healed Wei with qi. It had saved him and made everything worse. I had no intention of doing that again, not with you, not when the risk was this high and qi this unreliable. Your channels were rubble.

Later, maybe. Small amounts. Carefully. When you were stable and the risk was manageable. Not now, with my hands still shaking and your body broken in ways I could not fully assess while you were unconscious. Now it was sleeves, pressure, fire, and water. The tools of people who did not have qi. The tools that worked regardless.

I needed water to clean the wounds. The stream was two hundred paces west. I needed fire too. You would go into shock if you were not already, and your core temperature would drop. The water had to be boiled, or I'd risk infection. I needed fire and I needed water. I needed to be in two places, and I could only be in one. The ground was still warm but cooling fast. I had to move.

Fire first.

There were no insects. The clearing was silent in a way clearings never are: no hum, no buzz, no small wings. Everything that could flee had fled. Everything that could not was gone.

I let go of the pressure bandage, which stayed in place because I had positioned your head so gravity kept force on the wound. Wood was everywhere. The shockwave had produced plenty, the cracked and fallen trees providing fuel in abundance. I gathered it, arranged it, and ignited it with some qi. Out of reflex I looked for your pack. It had been blown ten paces farther away, but it was intact.

Fire.

The flames took. The warmth began, irregular and smoky from fresh-broken wood, but warm. I moved you closer, careful of the ribs and the arm.

The grass had survived. Between the fissures, in patches, the blades lay flat but green. The shockwave had passed over it as wind passes over wheat, pressing it down without destroying it. The grass would straighten in a day. The trees would not.

With the fire going, I needed water.

I rushed the two hundred paces. An immortal body had its uses: speed without strain, distance without fatigue. At the stream the light broke on the water's surface in shifting patterns, white fragments on green current. A fish rose and snapped at a water strider. The small violence of ordinary hunger. The stream was untouched by the clearing. It went on.

I filled your water-skin and returned as fast as I went.

I cleaned the wounds. The temple laceration was deep enough for concern, shallow enough that the skull was intact. The blood was already slowing. The pressure had worked, the clotting had begun, the processes mortal bodies used when damaged were functioning.

Your arm was dislocated, not broken. I braced your shoulder, took the arm, and pulled, using the rotational force relocation required, the technique that would have been painful but necessary.

The joint seated. The pop. The sound of bone finding socket.

You did not wake. The concussion held you under.

A bird called, distant, from the zone where the trees had only bent, where the canopy was torn but the branches held. One bird. The first sound from outside since the blast. It called twice and stopped.

I sat back on the churned ground, in the circle of destruction, the fire between me and the unconscious man whose ribs were broken by my protection.

My hands were still shaking. The tremor had nothing to do with temperature, fatigue, or effort. I knew what it was.

My hands were shaking because of what I had done.

I had nearly killed you. My continued existence, the thing the thing-below-qi maintained at any cost, had treated the nearest human being as irrelevant collateral. The shockwave had not discriminated.

My shaking hands. You, unconscious. The devastation all around us.

And I realized two things, simultaneously. Neither arrived as thought. Both arrived as weight.

What kept me alive did not feel like an enemy. It knew where to push. It knew when to wake. It knew the architecture of my body as only something that *lived* there could. The thing below the qi, the thing that woke, warmed, protected, and destroyed, did not feel foreign. It felt like mine, the way a heartbeat felt like mine even though I had never told it to beat.

But it had not protected you. The shockwave had expanded outward from me. Through me and past me. And everything in its path was acceptable damage.

I was the danger. I had always been the danger.

"/ø ʔm̩/," I said.

The old tongue again. The fossil language. The sounds the deeper memory attached to the deeper understanding. I am doing this. Present tense, ongoing. Every moment of my continued existence was a moment of potential lethality to anyone in proximity.

Inward. The direction arrived without my permission, from the cliff, from the bearing set before I understood what it pointed toward. Inward. The answer was inside. The danger was inside. The protection was inside. Everything was inside.

And inside was me.

I sat by the fire and waited. Your breathing continued. The wet sound diminished over the watches as the blood cleared from your airways. The ribs would take weeks. Mortal bodies healed at mortal speed.

At the far edge of the destruction, where the fallen trunks met the standing wounded, small branches still carried leaves. Green, intact, sheltered by the geometry of collapse: a branch angled just so, a trunk that had taken the force, the accidental architecture of survival. Ten paces closer, the branches were stripped bare. The line was sharp. Leaves. No leaves. The blast had drawn a border, and the border was exact.

I waited. My hands shook. The fire burned the shattered wood. The sky moved and changed over the small catastrophe on the ground below.

You were alive. I had done this.

Both facts were true at once, and I had to hold them both.
