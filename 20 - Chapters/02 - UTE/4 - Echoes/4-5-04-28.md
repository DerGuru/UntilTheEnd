We headed north, stopping at a stream a watch's walk from the crater. After we drank, you ate some dried fruit. You sat on a rock with the notebook open on your knee.

I rested on another rock while you wrote. For a while I had nothing to examine or consider. Water ran past us in the channel it had worn over centuries, and I drew no conclusion from it.

I stood.

"South," I said.

You looked up from the notebook. Your pen stayed above the page longer than it usually did between observations. You were waiting for me to add something.

"South," you repeated.

"There's a volcanic site. A crater that became a lake. Temples on the eastern shore." The details came easily. "The qi deep beneath it should be strong. The basalt may have preserved something from the original event."

You went on looking at me. Usually you would have written down those details before asking your next question. Now the pen hung motionless between your fingers.

"Yun."

"What?"

You held the look for two heartbeats. Three. The stream filled the silence between us.

"Nothing," you said. You closed the notebook, stood, and shouldered your pack. Apparently you would learn more by following than by arguing with me at the stream.

We walked south.

I knew the trail, though I could not have said why. A bend took us left before an outcrop. Farther on, the ground descended through a grove where loam gave way to volcanic ash. I recognized each turn without being able to recall walking here before.

You walked fifteen paces behind me, the gap stretching as I quickened my pace. Your breathing grew heavier on the inclines. Your pen scratched across the page as you walked. Usually you waited until we stopped to write; whatever you were recording now was too urgent to leave.

Sulfur reached me first, too faint for most people to taste. Then incense drifted from ahead, carried by wind moving across water.

We crested the ridge above a bowl of dark water, fifty paces across. Rain and groundwater had filled it over four hundred years. Erosion rounded its rim under the new growth. Small temples stood on the eastern shore, with stone foundations, wooden roofs, and copper ornaments green with age. Pilgrims followed the path below us. Incense smoke drifted low over the lake.

I stopped.

I knew this place. Its rim curved exactly as I remembered. The water had the right color; the temple roofs caught the afternoon light at an angle I recognized. I stood at the top of the path, unable to take another step.

I pictured the shore below without descending to it. Yet I could not place the memory that supplied those details. I searched for the occasion when I had stood here.

You reached me, breathing hard. Your gaze moved over the crater, the lake, and the temples. You found nothing new in the view.

"Why are we here?" I asked.

Your pen stopped again. I had listened to its scratching all along the path. Now you held it motionless above the open notebook.

You looked at me for a long time. Something in your face had changed. Standing there, I could not read it; remembering your still hand now, I think you had already understood how little you could do to help.

"You don't know," you said.

Not a question.

"I know this place," I said. "I can see that I know it. The temples. The copper. The basalt at the waterline. I know the western shore is rockier. No path, no pilgrims. I know the sulfur smell doesn't fully dissipate from volcanic ground. I know all of these things. But I don't know—"

I stopped. The sentence had no ending.

"We were here this morning," you said. You spoke carefully, watching my face between the words.

"This morning."

"Before we walked to the stream. You went down to the basalt. You touched the stone. You pulled your hand back."

You held the notebook where I could read it. Your tiny handwriting filled the page under today's date. You had named this place, then described what I had done here.

*"Subject touched basalt at waterline. She withdrew her hand immediately, apparently involuntarily. She made three attempts. On the third, she held contact for five heartbeats. Visible tremor. Subject tested stones along western shore for longer than a quarter watch."*

One entry. Of me. My morning. The one I had no memory of.

"I didn't do that," I said.

Your records were careful. I had seen you take measurements, correct entries, and fill the smallest spaces between observations. I had no reason to mistrust what you wrote.

But I did not remember.

I searched for the first touch of basalt. I remembered its cold from other visits long ago, but nothing from this morning. Each detail you gave me reached the same empty place.

You pointed to a later entry under the same date.

*"Subject placed her hand on basalt at crater rim. Stone cracked in a single line, approximately a handspan and a half. No visible force or audible sound. Subject visibly disturbed. She stated: 'I don't know.'"*

"The crack is still there," you said. "In the stone. You can go look at it."

I looked toward the stone you indicated on the rim. The crack was too small to see from where we stood, so I went down. There it was, the length your notebook gave, clean across the basalt.

I stood over proof of a morning I could not remember.

Not now.

The refusal came again. I remembered the stream, the dried fruit, and your notebook on your knee. I remembered standing to say we should go south. Everything between our first arrival at the crater and that rest was missing.

Whatever had removed it could reach inside me without disturbing the memories around it.

"Something is fighting me," I said.

You wrote quickly. This conversation could disappear too. I held each word in mind as your pen set it down.

The thought was dizzying.

"How many times have we been here?"

"This is the second time."

You held the notebook open for me. I had to consult your record to learn where I had been. Whatever I remembered on my own could no longer be trusted to contain the whole day.

I read the entries again. You had watched a woman touch those stones. You had heard what she said afterward.

I did not recognize her.

I looked from the page to my hand. It had rested on that stone; you had watched it tremble. I accepted the evidence, yet the woman you described was as strange to me as someone I had never met.

Your pen rested between two entries. I should have taken up one of my own then. I left the record to you, which cost me later.

"We keep going," I said. "You keep writing. Make sure we reach the next place. Write down everything. Every detail. Every word. Every sensation. I want it all in the notebook."

You wrote that down too.
