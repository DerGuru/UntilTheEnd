My hands shook above the gray rock while I counted my pulse. The shivering lasted a thousand and twenty heartbeats. I returned to the count whenever a thought fell apart before I could finish it.

The next beat was always something I could follow. My body shook while the numbers piled up.

The rock was cold under me. I had sat down at some point. Or collapsed. The distinction required analysis, and the part of me that analyzed was shaking too hard to function.

My hands were the worst. I held them out and watched the tremor move through fingers I could not keep still. They had stayed steady through battles, storms, and centuries when I cared little what I did with them. Now they shook because something had caught me at the bottom of a cliff.

Something had caught me.

Cold spread through my joints as I considered it. The rock was chilled by the mountain air, but this reached deeper. I tried to think past it, and the same thought returned.

Something had caught me.

The warmth had come from inside my chest. It had slowed the fall until my feet settled onto this stone, then withdrawn. The last moment of suspension above the ground was still vivid. Whatever had held me there had completed its work before I understood what it was doing.

My jaw clenched. The teeth clicked anyway.

Under my qi, the stirring had quieted. Something still sat in the place the warmth had come from, capable of doing the same thing again. I had no part in that readiness, though it lay inside my own chest.

I pressed my palms flat against the rock, cold stone with rough grain. When I pressed harder, the shaking eased.

The thought circled back.

Something inside me had decided I would not die.

I tried to stand and my knees buckled. I sat back down.

A bird flew overhead. Small. Dark against the blue sky. Its shadow crossed the rock and was gone.

The cold receded in phases, ice thinning in the joints as my blood carried warmth back out. My fingers grew warm. The shivering in my hands slowed, until at last only the fingertips trembled. Then nothing.

My body settled back into its rhythm. I could hold my fingers still again.

I had survived. And I had watched.

Pressure had built against my heart. Warmth had traveled outward along channels I knew, then beyond them, and I had followed it through my body while it slowed the fall. It felt older than the qi whose movements I had spent millennia learning.

Looking at my hands, I could scarcely claim what they had survived.

Something residing in me had acted without my direction. It used my channels, occupied my body, and changed the outcome when I stepped off a cliff. Its presence was clear to me, and out of reach.

My hands started shaking again.

I pressed them against the rock and held them there. The grain bit into the palms. Sensation. Concrete.

How long had I carried it? I pressed harder against the rock, trying to recall that first stirring beneath the qi. It was older than my core, older than anything I had practiced or been taught. Everything I knew how to use rested on it. I had lived with it long enough to mistake its work for my own nature.

Wind stirred dust across the gray stone. Insects sounded again. Farther off, settling rock ticked.

My palms stayed flat against the stone. Between uneven breaths, I found a new description for what I had always called immortality. I wasn't really immortal.

I was inhabited.

Or so it looked, sitting under that cliff. Something chose its own moment to intervene. It had done so through fire, blades, and drowning, preserving this body each time. I had always been left alive afterward, as surely as I was alive now.

I breathed in the dry mineral dust. My breath shook as I let it out.

I had jumped. Something beneath everything I knew how to control had prevented me from reaching the rock at speed. My choice had changed nothing about the result. The cold it had left still sat in my joints.

With my hands pressed against the ground, I turned my attention to that quiet place inside.

I wanted to understand.

The low humming ran through my breastbone again. The fall had given me a place to direct my attention, and even after the warmth withdrew I could follow where it had been.

I wanted to understand what I was.

My stomach tightened. I held the question instead of waiting for it to pass. After the long walk through the forest, that alone was new. I had let so much drift out of reach; now I needed to follow this one thing until I understood it.

Qi continued circulating through its channels. I listened more closely. Those channels might serve something deeper than I had imagined when I learned to use them.

I stood.

This time the knees held.

I rose because I had something to do. Before the fall I had moved whenever staying still became too much trouble. Now I looked up the cliff with the question still clear in me.

The gray stone rose toward the distant blue sky. Cold rock lay under my feet. I looked from one to the other, carrying the question.
