The cultivators came from the east in the early morning mist. Three of them. Visibility in the lower forest had dropped to forty paces, a tactical condition cultivators exploited.

You sensed them too. Your broken core, with its quarter-tone-flat resonance, still detected cultivation signatures. Even a cracked bell rang. You woke at once, your alertness calibrated to threat.

"Three," you said. "Southeast. Moving in formation."

A formation meant intent. These people had calculated angles and spacing. They had discussed lines of approach. They had decided I was worth a formation.

My weight shifted onto my forward foot. Through the disconnection came the same impulse that had moved the body toward the wolf-beast in the clearing. It worked below wanting, below the deliberate thought I could barely sustain. It had a direction.

Search.

I turned toward them.

Toward. The body had been facing north. Now it faced southeast, into the mist where the three signatures approached in their calculated formation.

Halfway through the turn, I caught what I was doing. Another crack formed in the glass, a splinter of recognition. I could have stopped turning. I chose to finish. Something had reached through the disconnection.

Ahead, the space between the trees held three signatures resolving from abstraction into presence.

"What are you doing?"

The question arrived from behind me, from ten paces.

"Warming up."

I walked toward them.

Branches scraped my sleeves as I stopped picking a path around the roots. The body moved and the direction was toward.

They emerged from the mist at twenty paces. Three cultivators. Sect uniforms in the coloring and cut of the organization that had sent scouts to the settlement, the same one that existed in my history as: enemy.

They saw me. The lead cultivator, middle-aged, with the build of a combat specialist, registered my approach with visible surprise. Decades of this, yet he checked his stride when he saw which direction I was going. They had expected to find me. They had not expected me to find them.

I walked toward them as if I'd been expecting them. My shoulders dropped. The release spread across my back, as if I'd set down something heavy I'd carried too long.

The lead cultivator drew his weapon. A sword. Standard issue, mid-level. He drew it with the fluidity of long practice.

The other two flanked in standard formation, spaced to maximize coverage and minimize mutual interference.

Two breaths.

I closed the distance in the first. One accelerating step carried me fifteen paces in the interval between one heartbeat and the next.

The lead cultivator's sword came up. The correct response, the one his training was designed to produce. The sword was between us, the correct tactical position for a sword.

I went through it.

Through the defense. Through the guard. Through the architecture decades of training had installed. My hand closed on his wrist. I twisted.

*Crack.*

Short, dry, like a branch. The wrist went. The sword went. He made the involuntary sound pain produced.

The second cultivator was already moving. The flanking attack from the left, coordinated and rehearsed. My attention was not directional. It was the passive awareness of a body that had done this for longer than their sect had existed.

One finger on his lower dantian, the knot where the meridians met, the weak point. I knew it as intimately as the smell of damp wood in our fire, from repetition over millennia. The finger pressed. The qi behind it pressed.

A hiss, quiet, like air escaping something sealed. The dantian buckled under the pressure. The qi stopped flowing through it.

He collapsed from the sudden absence of qi-flow.

The third made the correct assessment: two allies down in less time than it took to identify the threat. He was younger, with a newly sewn field insignia. He ran.

I let him.

Engagement had exhausted me already. The stimulus had been received; the receipt was complete.

I stood over two cultivators. One clutched his wrist, guarding a joint that no longer functioned. The other lay on the ground, breathing, conscious, and unable to access his cultivation. Both still alive. Killing required an additional action and additional actions required additional motivation.

The forest was quiet. The mist thinned.

I turned back. Toward camp. Toward the embers and the researcher with the notebook.

The nothing came back. It filled the space the stimulus had left. Completely. The fight was over. The silence returned.

Nothing had changed. Two people had been broken and a third had fled. The noise had happened; now it was gone. I had turned to greet danger like an old acquaintance, searching for something I still could not find. The searching was all I had.

You stood at the camp's edge. Notebook open. You had been ten paces behind me when I turned; you had watched the entire thing from there.

You wrote.

Your hand was steady. Your face said your understanding of the subject had just expanded by a factor you had not anticipated. You wrote three pages.

You did not ask if I was alright.

You already knew the answer.
