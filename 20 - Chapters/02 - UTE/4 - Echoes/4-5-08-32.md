For three days you wrote about everything around us. The terrain, the weather, and my headaches filled more pages. The ache behind my eyes had become constant. You recorded the food I left untouched, my open eyes at night, and the distance I put between us.

As we gained altitude, you noted the changing kinds of mushroom. Even a bird that followed us for half a day earned an entry before it flew elsewhere.

You did not write about the smile or talk about it.

Each time your pen moved, the omission was there. You usually recorded everything, small details with the same care as the things that frightened us. You had left that one expression out, and it was hard to ignore while you recorded the rest so carefully.

On the third morning you woke to find me by the old fire, coaxing its embers with a few branches. Birds began calling through the trees. Light reached the clearing in separate patches under the canopy.

I had sat there all night as the fire died. After its last flame sank, I stared at the dark space above the embers. Now the new branches began to smoke.

The smoke brought back a war from another era. It had spread across a whole region, lasting long enough for children to grow into its fighting men. I had fought in it as I fought in so many others, certain of winning.

I remembered the battlefield afterward. Smoke rose between scattered bodies. Iron, burned earth, and the sweetness left by a great discharge of qi filled the air. Many people had stood there alive before I arrived.

I had laughed at how easily it was finished. I remembered the sound, full enough to move my whole chest. Everyone who had stood before me lay on the ground. Looking over the field, I had found that amusing.

A branch hissed over the embers. I recognized the laugh as mine despite all the years between that field and this fire.

The smile in the clearing had begun with the same warmth. I had felt that old pleasure before I attended to what I was doing. Centuries separated the two fights. At the embers, I found little difference in what I had enjoyed about them.

Another fight could bring the same pleasure. After enough of them, I might laugh openly over what I had done. I had chosen that before. The new branches caught. I considered how easily I could choose it again.

A decision that I would not make again.

You stirred, cleared your throat, and reached for the notebook after a few sleepy heartbeats. Of course you did.

Your eyes went from the kindling fire to me. You did not ask what I'd been thinking about. You had learned which silences to leave alone.

I rebuilt the fire while you prepared water for tea. Your attention wandered from the pot more than once. When it was ready, you handed me a cup. I held its warmth between my palms without drinking.

"No more fighting," I said.

You watched me over your own cup. Steam rose between us.

"The clearing," you said.

"The clearing. And before. And before that."

You waited with your cup raised, leaving room for me to continue.

"I liked the fight," I said. "That feeling is old. Older than the crater. I spent centuries on battlefields because I enjoyed being there."

You drank your tea while I gripped my cup with both hands.

"The smile," you said.

"The smile." I nodded.

"I understand."

You set down the cup, opened the notebook, and began writing on a blank page.

My words appeared in your tiny script. At last the smile had entered the record. You still knew nothing of the particular battlefield or the laugh I remembered. I had given you the decision that followed them, and that much you could preserve.

"No more fighting," you read back. "Subject's decision. Self-imposed after recognition of recurrent enjoyment of violence."

"You make it sound clinical."

"I make everything sound clinical. That's the point." You looked up. "The point is that the clinical version exists when the emotional version becomes unreliable."

By the time we packed, the tea had gone cold in the cup I still held. The new talisman weighed against my robe as we set off.

I carried the decision with me. For now I could hold to it.
