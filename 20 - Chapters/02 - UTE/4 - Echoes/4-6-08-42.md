You woke in the late afternoon.

Your breathing shifted first, a deeper intake, immediately punished by the ribs, immediately abandoned. Then a sound, the one a body made when it returned to a world it had not agreed to leave. Your fingers moved against the pack beneath your shoulder. Your eyes opened.

The left eye was swollen half shut from the temple laceration. The right eye was clear and already doing what your eyes always did: surveying, noting, assembling.

You saw the fallen trees. The fissured ground. The lean-to of broken trunks above you, casting the shade I had put you in. The fire, low, at the edge of the clearing.

You saw me.

I sat next to you. My hands were clean. I had washed them in the stream watches ago, but my clothes still carried the stains. Brown on the sleeve where the fabric had been torn for your bandage. Brown on the hem, the knee. The scab on your temple was dark and tight. Your arm, reset and swollen, lay against your body in the position that cost the least pain.

You tried to sit up. The ribs stopped you. You lay back slowly, with control, negotiating with damage.

"Don't," I said, quietly. It was a request.

You turned to me, the right eye steady, the left one trying. Even now, injured, waking into a world you did not yet understand, you were already reading.

Your hand went to your hip, where the notebook should have been. Your fingers found nothing, and a flicker crossed your face, the brief alarm of a man separated from his instrument.

It lay beside me. I had found it in the debris, battered, the binding cracked, but intact. I had kept it out of your line of sight. You needed water and rest before you needed documentation.

"Notebook," you said. Your voice was rough and dry, a throat that had not had water since before the experiment.

I handed you the water-skin. "Later."

You drank in small sips, because the ribs would not tolerate a full swallow. You handed it back. Your gaze went to my clothes, the stains, the fire, the destruction, a narrative assembled from evidence. You built it piece by piece, from the outside in.

"How long?" you asked.

"Since yesterday. It's afternoon."

You processed the lost time. The discomfort that crossed your face was a documenter's, a man who had missed the event he had come to document.

"What happened?"

"The poison was neutralized. Before it reached lethal concentration. Something in me neutralized it."

You watched my face rather than my words, reading what it carried instead of what my mouth delivered.

"And then?"

"The neutralization released a shockwave. Qi-pressure." I nodded at the fallen trees. "You were at ten paces."

You turned to the trees, then to your own body. The connection formed behind your eyes: the evidence, then the conclusion.

You were quiet for a long time. The fire crackled softly. A bird called from the surviving trees, the same species that had been colonizing the wreckage all day. The late-afternoon light was lengthening, the shadows of the fallen trunks stretching across the fissured ground.

"What was that?" you asked, quiet and steady, a man who needed the phenomenon named.

Your pale face. The scab on the temple. The swollen arm. The ribs you were breathing around. Everything I had done to you was written on your body.

"Me."

One word. The smallest true answer.

You held my gaze. Five heartbeats. Ten. Long enough to weigh the word against everything you knew, the shockwave, the protection, the poison's failure, the centuries. Long enough to find that it fit.

You leaned back slowly, the ribs complaining, and closed your eyes. You breathed only as far as the damage allowed.

"You," you said slowly. It came out as an echo.

You did not ask for more.

The fire settled. The bird called once more and stopped. The light moved.

You breathed. I sat. The word lay between us.

You took that word with you. You never returned it and never asked for a longer one.
