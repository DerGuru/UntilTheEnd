I sat on a boulder near the cliff's base. The place where I had landed was too charged to stay in. From this other rock I looked back at it and reconstructed the fall, the intervention, and the landing.

I began with the warmth. It had started below the core, deeper than the place cultivation theory described. I rested a hand against my chest and tried to tell the remembered pressure from the qi moving through it.

It had spread through the meridians, then along other paths. Those deeper routes were older than the ones I knew. Perhaps my cultivation rested on channels I had never learned to use. The warmth had moved through them easily enough.

I tried to name it. After so many days of simply noticing things, the effort itself took some getting used to. I sat on the boulder and went back over each stage.

Qi had circulated on while the warmth rose beneath it. Spirit belonged to the same practice, dependent on the structures I understood. Perhaps the soul came closer. I held each word against the pressure I remembered. None fitted what had happened in my chest.

It had felt like me.

I looked down at the hand resting against my ribs. I had lived in this body long before I learned a name for what moved through it. That old intimacy was in the thing I had felt, however little else I recognized.

It felt like what I was before I was what I am.

I could get no closer in words. The stone under me had a texture I could describe easily. This other sensation slipped each description the moment I tried to hold it still.

The pressure against my heart had been as inevitable as the fall itself, a hand pressing from within. Whatever caused it had kept my body intact. When the rock should have broken me, that pressure had increased until I descended safely instead. The cloth had loosened against my chest as I slowed.

This was as close to an explanation as I could get from inside the experience.

I could describe what I had felt without knowing how to repeat it. A swordsman can make a perfect cut and fail to explain it to anyone else. I had less than that: one experience I had never intended to produce. Understanding it would have to begin with something I could test.

I stood again.

I knew where to look now. Somewhere below the qi I could direct lay something I could scarcely reach. The fall had made it stir. Other occasions when I had survived might have left something to follow too.

I had helped shape words long before the first sects existed. Standing by the boulder, I still had none for this. I would have to attend to what happened inside my body without expecting a name to explain it.

The question had changed.

Something inside me had prevented my death. That much I knew. I needed to understand what could act through my body without my permission.

What am I?

The shift was not from despair to hope. There was no light. There was a question. Questions did not illuminate. They pointed.

I looked up the cliff and considered the climb. I could follow the question without believing I would like the answer.

I straightened my shoulders. My jaw unclenched as I drew a steadier breath. After days of walking wherever the ground allowed, I was preparing to go somewhere for a reason. My hands were steady enough to climb.

I did not want to live. But I wanted to know what I was.

Beside that boulder, I had found the right question. I had no idea then how much its answer would change.

The low humming went on under my ribs. Whatever had intervened was quiet again. I listened to it as I crossed from the boulder to the base of the cliff, and I meant to go on listening even when it gave me nothing new.

I began to climb.
