The evening came gently. The light shifted from the long afternoon into the warm tones that preceded dusk: amber filtering through the bare trunks at the destruction's edge, the shadows of the fallen trees stretching until they merged with each other and with the general dimming.

We did not speak. The silence between us was occupied. You lay under the lean-to, breathing your careful breaths and watching the light change. I tended the fire and added wood, the cycle from the night before resuming.

I heated water. You drank in small sips, the ribs still governing everything: depth of breath, angle of swallow, the geometry of existing inside a damaged frame. I waited until you had finished.

"I'm going to feed you some qi, to help with the ribs," I said. "Like last night. You were asleep for those."

The right eye was steady, the left still swollen half shut. You nodded.

I placed my hand on your chest, over the ribs. You flinched, from the anticipation rather than the touch. I sent the thread, thin, along the primary meridian. The qi settled into the tissue around the ribs, warm and gentle. Your breathing lost its sharp punishment and softened as the inflammation released its grip by a small degree. Your face registered it.

I sent a second thread, deeper, along the secondary meridian toward the shoulder. The cleared paths from earlier that day accepted it easily, and the sediment I had removed in the morning was still gone. But the channel itself was narrower here, more scarred, with residual buildup from the old damage, the years since the tribulation. I pressed against a constriction, gently. The silt shifted. The channel widened.

You went still. A twitch in your shoulder, a channel remembering what flow felt like. Something opening. Your eyes found mine.

"That's not just the ribs," you said.

I held the thread steady but said nothing.

"You're clearing the channels." You said it flatly, like a man who had spent his life studying qi and knew what channel work felt like even through a broken system. Your hand moved to your chest, near mine but not touching it, the instinct of a former cultivator sensing qi move inside himself for the first time in a long time.

"The old damage left residue," I said. "The healing threads work better through clean paths."

You were quiet. Then:

"The channels. Not the core."

"Not the core."

A pause. The fire crackled. An ember popped and sent a small shower of sparks upward into the deepening sky.

"Could you?" you asked. "The core."

I withdrew my hand and sat back. I thought about the collapsed structure at the center of your system: the sharp-edged wreckage, the instability, the places where any intervention would cascade into secondary collapses.

"Not yet," I said. "Maybe."

You accepted that the way you accepted data, without emotion or argument. Filed. Stored. Available for future reference. Maybe was not no. Maybe was a door that was closed but not locked.

I continued the channel work. Three more paths, slow and careful. You lay still and said nothing, but your face showed that the feeling was strange. Strange and half remembered. Qi moved through channels that had been inert for a long time. Not the full flow of an active cultivator, nothing close to that. A trickle through ruins. But present.

When I finished, I sat back. Your breathing was a fraction easier, the channels carrying the healing qi more efficiently through the cleared paths.

"Thank you," you said.

I said nothing. I had broken your system. Cleaning the debris was the minimum.

Dusk settled. The amber light dimmed through gray into the blue-dark of early evening. The fire became the dominant light again, a warm circle against the cooling air with the two of us inside it.

The night sounds began gradually, the way the light had left. The crickets came first, resuming from the night before, their numbers growing. Then the deeper sounds. A frog from the direction of the stream, the low rhythmic call that meant standing water nearby. An owl, the first, a single low note from far enough away that the shockwave had left its territory intact. It called, waited, and called again. The ancient pattern. Question. Silence. Question.

Something moved in the undergrowth past the fallen trunks. Small, ground-level, navigating the debris field: a mouse or a vole. Nocturnal scavengers working the new terrain. The shockwave had made a habitat for the small things too. Overturned earth meant exposed roots, exposed roots meant insects, insects meant the things that ate them.

You slept. The transition was brief, the exhaustion of a damaged body pulling you under within moments of closing your eyes. Your breathing found its pattern, shallow and steady, the ribs permitting this much and no more.

I fed the fire. The night was colder than the last. The first night's ground heat from the shockwave had dissipated, and the open clearing offered nothing against the sky. No canopy. No windbreak. The cold settled from above, as it does on cloudless nights when heat radiates upward and nothing sends it back. Your blanket was insufficient. You curled tighter, the ribs protesting the contraction.

I fed qi into the fire, into the wood, the embers, and the air around the flames. A thin, steady current warmed the circle by a notch or two. The fire burned no brighter, but the heat carried further, wrapped around the lean-to, and settled against the blanket.

The owl called again, closer, over the open ground where devastation had made good hunting. A bat crossed the firelight, a quick dark shape, silent, pursuing something too small to see.

The night deepened. The stars showed through the open sky above the clearing, unfiltered, a clear field slowly rotating. The cold pressed down from that vastness, the fire pressed back, and between them was a small warm space where you breathed.

I sat with the stillness. Fed the fire. Checked on you. The cycle of care that had become the rhythm of my night. Somewhere in the watches between midnight and dawn, the frog stopped calling and a different bird began, a nightjar, its churring repetition filling the dark. It was close, in one of the fallen trunks, perhaps. Nesting in the wreckage already. Two days, and life was moving in.

Dawn arrived without announcement. The same slow dilution: dark thinning to gray, gray accepting color. Dew had formed on everything, the fallen trunks, the grass between the fissures, the fabric of your blanket, and the wood by the fire. Wet. Clean. The world washed overnight.

You coughed before you woke. A deep, wet sound, immediately punished by the ribs. Your body curled in the protective fold, involuntary, the architecture of a torso defending itself. Three coughs. Each produced a grimace. Each came regardless of the grimace.

The coughing settled. You straightened slowly, incrementally, the negotiation with pain that had become your method of achieving vertical. You sat up. The blanket fell from your shoulders. The bruise around your left eye had advanced to purple-yellow, the color progression that marked two days of healing. Your right eye was clear.

I handed you the water-skin. You drank in small sips. Careful. The ribs.

Your gaze went around the clearing, the fallen trees, the fire, and me, sitting where I had sat for two nights.

You said nothing about the destruction or the night.

You reached for your pack. Found the rice. Looked at it. Put it down.

"Not yet," you said.

The ribs. Cooking required motion. Motion required breath.

I took the rice. I took the pot. I built the cooking fire from the embers and the broken wood around us.

I cooked.

The practiced motions of food preparation. Water. Rice. Heat. Duration. A sequence stored in a memory the erasures did not reach, because cooking threatened nothing.

You watched from the ground, your right eye tracking my motions. Without documenting. Your notebook was still in my pack; you had not asked for it, and I had not offered.

I served the rice. Placed the bowl next to you and moved back to my place by the fire.

You ate slowly, each breath between bites calculated for the ribs.

We did not speak.

After the eating, after the fire burned lower, after the morning progressed from gray to the diffused warmth of a clouded sun, you stood. You swayed and caught yourself, but you stood.

"We should move," you said.

You were right. Staying in the circle of destruction was not sustainable. The location was evidence, and evidence attracted attention.

"I can stabilize you," I said. "Qi. You wouldn't have to walk."

You met my eyes. You understood exactly what was being offered and had already decided.

"No. I want to— I need to walk."

You picked up your pack, slowly, the ribs dictating the arc of the motion, and put it on. The left strap first, then the right, each a controlled negotiation with damaged infrastructure.

You walked slowly, because your body allowed nothing faster. I moved at your speed. The matching was automatic, an adjustment I did not name and you did not ask about.

You noticed. The sideways glance. The assessment. My pace locked to yours.

You said nothing.

We moved. You coughed occasionally. I waited when you paused. We moved.
