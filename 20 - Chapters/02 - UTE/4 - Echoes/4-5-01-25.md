We followed the path down from the cliffs, toward places I remembered surviving. I pictured the old sites clearly enough to choose a route between them. At each of them, something had intervened when I should have died.

I had survived them long before I thought to ask what lay under my qi. Now the cliff had given me a reason to return. The ground changed under my feet as I chose which place to reach first.

"What are we looking for?" you asked, on the second day of the search.

"Traces."

You wrote it down in the notebook you carried open as we walked.

"Where?"

"Places I've been before. Old ones."

"How many?"

"Several."

Your pen paused, then continued.

"What are you looking for there?"

"Signs. That something intervened."

"How long ago?"

Wind from the east, carrying dust and the smell of dry grass. I walked a few steps before answering.

"The oldest has been many millennia. I don't remember exactly."

You wrote that down without comment. Your grip on the pen changed.

"What will we find?"

The path narrowed between stone ridges.

"I don't know," I said. "Hopefully answers."

"How will you know?"

"We'll see."

You wrote that down too.

I quickened my pace.

Your broken core made the quicker pace harder to sustain. Sometimes the gap stretched to twelve paces, sometimes fifteen. Your breathing changed. You walked on without complaint.

We descended through the foothills into the river provinces. Fields spread across the lowlands below us, their boundaries laid down over centuries of cultivation. Roads replaced paths. Settlements appeared in the distance as we followed the ridges.

I kept us clear of the settlements. After so long alone, that was still the easier route. People meant questions, provisions meant bargaining, and either could delay us.

"We could resupply," you said. You were looking at a village below the ridge. Smoke from cook-fires. The sound of a market bell.

"Fine. I'll wait here. You go down and get what we need."

You adjusted your pack straps without another word.

I waited on the ridge while you went down. You returned with dried meat, rice, and a new waterskin before half a watch had passed.

We took a pass road worn smooth by commerce. It led aside from our route, but you could move faster on it than across open ground.

Near a small marketplace I saw someone.

A man walked alone in the opposite direction. His clothing was ordinary. Under his unhurried stride, heavenly qi ran so dense and clean that attending to it took effort. His masking surpassed that of most Immortals I had encountered. I recognized the fusion of soul with flesh, complete enough to leave no seam between them. I had seen that achievement once before. His qi held a narrow, upright shape with an edge I remembered.

Judgement's Gale. I had heard the name.

He passed among the people near the marketplace. I had chosen once to leave him alone, and I saw no reason to change that now.

You walked behind me. The road continued south, with our first destination three days ahead.
