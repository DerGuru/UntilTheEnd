You cooked over a small fire, turning the vegetables before they caught. Any traveler learned enough cooking to survive. You cooked well, with the competence of practice beyond necessity. Food was your closest approximation of control in an uncontrollable world.

Rice. Vegetables foraged from the surrounding forest. You knew your botany: what was edible, what would kill you, and what was merely tolerable. You had found some of the desirable things, too. And you carried ginger as other travelers carried weapons or talismans, a root in a pouch, wrapped in cloth.

The ginger. Mei had used ginger. The woman by the river, eighty centuries ago, the crooked finger and the patient cooking. Different ginger, different century, and the same smell. The connection fired before I could stop it: hot, clean, and gone.

The fire was small. Efficient. You tended it knowing exactly how heat spread under a pot. You took the result seriously. Your single pot sat on stones arranged to direct heat upward, its scratched, dented surface worn by months of daily use.

The smell reached me at eight paces. Before the radius. Inside the green zone. Rice steam, ginger, and heated vegetables. The combination was: food.

I did not eat.

I sat.

This was new. Usually I sat, stood, and walked in patterns the body determined without input from the will. Now I was sitting near a cooking fire with a cooking person. I folded my legs into a posture from other centuries, when I had sat near fires as food was prepared. The habit was older than this absence of hunger. The will was not consulted.

I sat six paces from the fire. At the edge of the transition zone. The gradient: green grass (your side), yellow grass (between), brown grass (mine). Six paces of ecological commentary on my proximity.

You noticed but did not look up. You had learned not to acknowledge behavioral changes in the subject, because acknowledgment could disrupt behavior, and behavior was data. I sat.

You stirred the pot and added some salt, from a smaller pouch. The salt dissolved. The steam changed quality. The smell deepened.

"You're staring."

Your voice was level, directed at the pot, a report made without eye contact. You knew direct address could end the interaction.

I was staring. My eyes were open and had no directive to look elsewhere. The fire was in front of me. The pot was on the fire. You were next to the pot.

"I'm evaluating," I said.

The words surprised me. Evaluating was accurate, or as accurate as any word could be for what the disconnected mind did when it observed a process without having a stake in its outcome. Yet I had said it aloud. I had not spoken voluntarily since the cultivators.

"And?" you said. Still to the pot.

Silence.

You waited. Three heartbeats. Five. The interval after a semi-question closed naturally when no response came.

You served yourself a bowl as scratched and dented as the pot, matching wear from months of being carried together. You ate with focused efficiency, treating meals as scheduled events rather than optional activities.

You served only yourself. No bowl sat in the transition zone, no expectant offering I would be obliged to refuse. You cooked for yourself. You ate for yourself. I recognized the respect in that without being able to appreciate it.

You were eating dinner. I could sit through it without being asked to come back to life.

That separated you from people-who-tried-to-help and placed you among people-who-existed-nearby. The first category produced the anxiety of obligation, the awareness that someone was investing effort that would be wasted. The second produced nothing. You eating dinner at a fire I sat near was like rain falling on a roof I happened to be under. Coincidental proximity. No transaction.

Something small behind my sternum relaxed a fraction. A variation barely large enough to notice. The difference between a frozen pond and the first softening under its ice. Technically warmer. Technically changed. Still impassable to anything hoping to surface.

But the measurement existed.

You finished. You cleaned the bowl with grass and sand, the field method of a traveler with no access to soap. You packed the pot, rewrapped the ginger pouch, and returned it to its place in your pack. You let the fire diminish. The routine never varied, and its structure kept the evenings from becoming silence.

I sat.

I knew you would write this. I knew the notebook would receive the datum: subject sat near cooking fire. Duration: a little over a thousand heartbeats. Distance: six paces. Food consumed: none.

But I sat.

You wrote it. The pen in the firelight. The miniature letters.

I sat until the fire was embers. Then I stood. Then I walked to my standard distance at the clearing edge, the brown-grass zone moving with me. It was as far away as the geography of camp allowed.

You did not watch me go. Or you watched from the corner of your eye, because some data was best collected indirectly. You watched or you didn't.

I almost almost-smiled.

A twitch at the corner of my mouth, suppressed before you could see it. There was something to suppress. Your comment had met my response; we had sat near each other through a meal I didn't eat. Somewhere behind my ribs, that had produced an impulse I might have called humor in another century.

Now it reached me as an echo. The vibration of a string that had been played in a different room.

But the string existed.
