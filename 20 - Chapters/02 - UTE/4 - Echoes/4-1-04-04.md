By the time I reached the clearing, stars showed above the trees. I had walked through several changes of light since the hut. Dusk cooled my skin; morning warmed it again. I noticed both without finding much reason to stop at either.

A great tree had fallen here decades ago. Grass and low shrubs filled the space it had left. Its massive stump was still there, softened by rot. Insects had burrowed into the wood, leaving small openings under the fungi along its sides.

The insects withdrew as I approached. By the time I stopped at the stump, its nearest burrows were quiet. Grass browned around my feet. Beyond three paces it was still green.

I stood at the stump with my eyes open. Over the clearing the stars shone as densely as they did high in the mountains. A poet might have found something to do with that sky. I looked up because nothing stood between it and me.

The humming was there too.

It was almost too low to hear. Inside my chest, qi circulated along old paths without any effort from me. I had given it no task. Still it moved, enough power to shake the clearing passing quietly through my body.

It had been there in the hut, perhaps earlier. At the emptied stump I could finally attend to it. The vibration ran under each breath, keeping me alive with its usual thoroughness.

The spirit-beast arrived at the clearing's edge.

This one belonged to a different species. Dark fur covered its wolf-sized body. Its qi-armor was thinner than the first beast's, probably because it was younger. Beasts of this strength usually lived at the edges of territories occupied by larger ones.

Its eyes were pale and reflective in the starlight. They found me across the clearing and held.

The beast stopped precisely where green grass met brown. It reached one paw forward. Before the paw touched the ground, it flinched back. The rest of its body was on the green side.

It lowered the paw next to the other. Its pale eyes held on me across the grass.

I stared back.

For some time neither of us moved. Around my feet lay dead grass. The stump had gone quiet. There was ample warning between the beast and me, even for something that could not see the qi escaping my skin.

The beast's ears flattened. Its body lowered.

I looked at my hand. Dried blood from the first beast flaked between my knuckles. I had drunk from the stream without thinking to scrub it away.

I walked toward it.

For days I had passed fruit without reaching for it. I had left the hut's hearth cold. Yet now I took a step toward those pale eyes, needing the creature to stay.

I wanted it close enough to respond to me. Something alive within reach, even for a moment. Its chest moved with each breath. Mine moved too.

I crossed the dead circle toward its edge. The beast backed away one step, then another, its pale eyes fixed on mine.

I stopped.

It stopped.

Six paces separated us, twice the radius. The beast crouched low, its muscles taut under the fur. In the cold air each breath made a small cloud before its muzzle. One dissolved, then another.

I stood. Green grass began to brown around me, joining the patch I had left at the stump. The boundary had followed my few steps toward the beast. It would follow any closer step as well.

The beast held for a dozen breaths. Then it crouched lower, turned, and moved into the tree line at a steady pace. I did not follow.

Dark fur merged with the spaces between the trees. Its pale eyes disappeared last.

I looked down at the grass. The boundary was plain on the ground, with my feet on the brown side. I could have crossed it again. The beast would only have had to retreat farther.

I kept looking toward the place where its eyes had disappeared. The small need was still there, however little else reached me. I had let it leave. I still wanted something to be there.

The humming in my chest continued. Low. Constant. The sound I made just by being.

I stood in the clearing until morning. The stars wheeled overhead, my eyes open the whole time. I remember how far they traveled across that patch of sky. I had scarcely moved under them.

Light reached the clearing. The air warmed on my skin. I stood a while longer with the low vibration in my chest. Then I turned toward the path. Staying had given me nothing more.

I walked.

The radius walked with me. Three paces. Three paces between me and everything living.

Behind me was the clearing. The dead circle. The stump that no longer housed insects.

Ahead lay more forest. Small movements sounded past the trees, safely outside my reach.

The humming continued. Low. Below everything.

I walked into the part that was still alive.
