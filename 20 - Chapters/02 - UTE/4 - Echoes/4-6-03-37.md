Below the qi. Below the meridians, already consumed. The Shadow-Eater worked through the dantian’s outer structure with the efficiency of a swarm of ants stripping a carcass. The meridians had died on schedule. And below the entire cultivated structure that the sects taught, that the scrolls documented and the cultivators understood. Below all of that.

Something woke up.

It came the way a vibration comes before you hear the sound, something under the skin, ahead of thought. It woke in the place where there should have been nothing, because cultivation had no name for anything below its own foundation.

But Something was there. Awake.

The cold stopped.

Forty-seven heartbeats in, the Shadow-Eater's progression halted. The compound did not slow. It stopped. Completely, instantaneously, as if a wall had been raised between the compound and the rest of my qi-structure.

And then the cold reversed.

Warmth flooded my body from below, from the place where the unnamed thing I was calling substrate for now was waking. It was unlike qi, unlike meridian-heat. Older. It had presence. This was the warmth of something not just alive but aware, something that had been there below while the cultivators built their structures above it, and had been waiting.

The warmth pushed outward through the dantian, through the primary channels, heat spreading on into the secondary channels and the periphery. I began to sweat, and steam rose from my body. The Shadow-Eater was being consumed by the thing it had been eating, destroyed with the same systematic efficiency it had employed.

Fifty heartbeats. Fifty-two. The dissolution reversed. The channels the Shadow-Eater had already consumed were being rebuilt. Healing would imply damage and repair. This was reconstruction, as if the original architecture were being reinstantiated from a template that existed below the visible structure.

I observed the reversal, the reconstruction, a process that should have been impossible because Yinshi was documented as irreversible.

Fifty-four heartbeats.

The thing below was fully awake. The warmth was a presence, the quality of something aware and responding, engaged in something that required attention.

Something was deliberately protecting me.

Fifty-five heartbeats. The warmth intensified, past reconstruction, past neutralization of the compound. The warmth became pressure. The pressure became overflow. The thing below was no longer just responding to the threat. It was reacting out of all proportion. The threat was neutralized, but the reaction continued. Escalating.

The pressure built.

It pressed in my chest, my skull, the joints, the cartilage, the junctions where qi met flesh. Whatever protected me had no proportional response. It had all or nothing, and it had chosen all. Energy pushed through the pores of my skin. I was radiating, and the pressure was still building.

"Move," I yelled at you.

You looked up. Panic filled your eyes. You had prepared for the poison but not for the response.

The pressure broke.

From everywhere. From the skin. The entire surface of me became the emission point.

A wall of force hit the world.

The first ring of trees, the old growth at the clearing's edge thirty paces from where I stood, did not fall. They ceased to exist as trees. The trunks shattered, the way ceramic shatters, the way bone shatters under force that exceeds the material's capacity to be a material. Bark, heartwood, and sapwood became fragments, debris, particulate that hung in the air and turned sunlight brown. Thirty paces of standing forest reduced to splinters in the time it took a heartbeat to complete.

Past that, at forty paces, fifty, sixty, seventy, the trees fell. The force was less but still absolute. Mature trunks cracked at the base in the slow-motion topple of things that had grown for decades meeting an argument they could not survive. They fell outward, radially, a circle of devastation expanding from me.

The ground split from where I stood to where the last tree fell. One continuous fracture network, surface cracks like a spider's web radiating from my feet through soil and stone: seventy paces of earth that had been solid, fissured now. The cracks were narrow but deep enough to swallow grass.

Further out, at a hundred paces, a hundred and twenty, the trees bent. Cracked but held. The force was insufficient to topple them but enough to break branches, strip leaves, and snap the upper canopy. They would survive as wounded things, listing at angles, their crowns torn open to a sky they had not seen unfiltered in generations.

Dust. Dirt, bark, torn leaves, all of it erupted outward and upward. Visibility dropped. The light went from clearing-brightness to gray-brown murk.

The wave hit you. It lifted you, a pressure wave meeting a body too light to anchor and too close to deflect. You went backward. Your notebook left your hand, your pen left the other, and your body separated from the ground, no longer controlling its own trajectory.

You hit a tree, the one tree that had not fallen because it stood at the wave's edge, where the force was enough to crack but not topple. You hit it with your back. The sound was wrong. A body hitting wood at speed. The sound of ribs cracking, of a spine breaking. The sound of a body that was no longer whole.

You fell to the ground in front of the tree. You were no longer conscious.

You lay still.

I stood in the center. Devastation all around me. Trees down. Ground fissured. Dust settling. And at the edge, against a cracked trunk, lay you, face down and motionless.

Alive or dead. I did not know.

The warmth was gone. The thing below had done its work and retreated. I had to pay the price.

The cold arrived and my whole body shook. The tremor was violent, a full-body convulsion, the muscles firing without coordination. My teeth hit each other. My vision narrowed to a tunnel with dust at its edges.

Then my body gave out. The signal from brain to muscle arrived, but the muscle declined. I went down face first. The impact was distant, the way pain is distant when the body has already left the conversation. I lay on the cracked earth, cheek against the dirt, arms at my sides, fingers curled inward, the hands of a body that had forgotten it had hands.

The ground was warm, heated by the blast, the fissured earth radiating stored energy the way stone radiated fire-heat long after the fire had gone. The warmth entered through chest, stomach, thighs, palms. The ground was feeding me back what the substrate had taken.

I could not move. For a long stretch, maybe more, I lay on the warm dirt and shook. The cold was absolute and left no room for thought. I existed only as a body, doing something that resembled being alive without my participation.

My mind cleared and I saw you.

Through the dust, through the tunnel, through the crack-pattern bisecting my visual field: you. A shape against a tree. Face down. Not moving. A hundred paces away. My qi senses were muddled by the residue of the explosion, and I did not know if you were alive or dead. I could not tell if you were breathing, if your heart was beating, if your qi was present.

So I started crawling. In a very wide definition of crawling.

The first movement was nothing. A twitch of the shoulder. The arm pulling, the elbow dragging, the body advancing by finger-widths. I could not lift my torso. My forehead scraped the dirt. The fissures caught my clothes, my fingers, the fabric of everything. Each pull produced a distance too small to measure and a cost too large to justify.

I pulled anyway.

The ground helped. The warmth bled through wherever my body pressed against the earth and accumulated, slow but real. By ten paces, ten paces that had taken a few hundred heartbeats, the shaking had changed. Still present, still violent, but the muscles were beginning to remember what they were there for. No coordination yet, no real control, only the rudimentary capacity to contract in roughly the right sequence.

I crawled. Badly. It would have embarrassed an infant: elbows and knees dragging a center of gravity that kept listing left. My vision had widened from the tunnel to something approximating a field, though the field was smeared. The dust was settling. The light was returning. And the ground was warm, warm, warm, the only kindness the clearing had left to offer.

Halfway. Fifty paces. Five hundred heartbeats, maybe more. The shaking had shifted from convulsion to tremor. My arms could push. My legs could almost kick. I was still profoundly, structurally cold, but the cold had lost its totality. It was no longer everything. It was merely most things.

The last thirty paces were the ugliest. I could see you clearly now: the angle of your body against the cracked trunk, the unnatural position of your left arm, the dark patch under your head that was blood or shadow and was probably blood. I moved faster. Not fast. Faster than the finger-widths of the beginning. The desperate, graceless forward haul of something with somewhere to be that could almost get there.

I reached you.

My hands were still shaking when I put one on your shoulder. The fingers did not cooperate, so I pressed my whole palm against you, the fine motor control dissolved somewhere back in the first twenty paces. I turned you carefully, as someone turns a body when they suspect most of the things inside it are broken.

You were breathing.

Shallow. Wet. The sound of air moving through something partly blocked, blood in the airways, probably. A laceration on your temple, deep, the skin split to the bone. Blood in your hair, on your face, on the ground under you. Your left arm was dislocated or fractured; the angle said both were possible. Two ribs, at minimum, from the sound of the impact. A ninety-pace flight ended by a tree trunk did not leave a body intact.

But you were breathing. The chest moved. The air went in and came out, even if the coming-out was wet. Continuous.

You were alive.

For now.
