Morning light came through a gap in the canopy, the light that finds open ground wherever trees have fallen. The clearing was sixty paces across. Flat. Compacted earth, sparse grass. Good visibility. Good footing. Somewhere in the canopy a bird sang its repetitive, two-note territorial call, a small sound in a clearing where something was about to happen.

I had arrived before dawn. The phial had needed warmth to liquefy and I had carried it against my skin through the night, but the compound still moved sluggishly when I tilted it. I sat on a fallen trunk at the clearing's edge and waited for the sun to do its work. Dew on the grass. The smell of wet earth and last night's cold. A spider had strung a web between two stalks of grass, small, precise architecture that would stand until the world found a reason to disagree.

The phial warmed. The compound thinned. The spider finished a radial thread.

The sun climbed. The dew burned off in patches, first the exposed grass, then the shadowed edges, the clearing drying from center outward like a slow exhalation. The bird changed its song, adding a third note, lower.

I sat with the phial in my lap and let the clearing exist. Light moved across the grass. An ant carried something pale along the edge of the fallen trunk, methodical and unhurried, the commute of a body that had work to do and no opinion about its cosmic significance. The air smelled of resin, distance, and the particular cleanness of mountain mornings, before the day warmed enough for the forest floor to start composting audibly.

It was a good clearing, quiet where it mattered. Nothing had happened here for a long time and nothing was expected to.

I stood and walked to the center. The phial was in my hand, ceramic, warm, the wax seal intact, the weight of two mouthfuls. Ninety heartbeats to dissolve immortality.

I heard you before I saw you. Footsteps through the undergrowth, neither cautious nor quiet. You had woken to an empty camp, checked your pack, found the absence, and understood.

You emerged from the tree line, notebook in one hand, pen in the other. Your eyes found the phial at once.

You stopped at ten paces. You had calculated the distance yourself: the same one you always kept, the observational distance. Even furious, you defaulted to method.

"You said you'd tell me," you said.

I had. And I hadn't.

Your posture was wrong. You were standing where you did not want to stand, arriving too late to prevent what you had come to prevent. Your weight was forward, ready to intervene, as if the ten paces were a leash and you were testing its length.

"Ten paces," I said. "And your notebook."

Your eyes went to the phial, then the clearing, then the far tree line, fifty paces from where you stood. You raised the pen slightly. The gesture said you were there, you were recording, and you had not agreed to this.

I broke the seal. The wax crumbled. The phial was open.

Absorption at fifteen heartbeats. Onset at thirty. Death at ninety. I knew the numbers.

I drank.

One motion: the phial to my lips, the tilt. The liquid, thin as water and the temperature of the morning, entered my mouth. I swallowed. The ceramic was empty.

The clearing held its features: the light, the trees at thirty paces, you at ten, the empty phial in my hand.

I waited.

The cliff had been instantaneous: the fall was the threat, the response was the stop. The poison was internal, through the blood instead of through gravity.

I waited.

Five heartbeats. Nothing. The liquid sat in my stomach. Someone had put real craftsmanship into this.

Ten heartbeats.

Your pen moved. You were writing.

Fifteen heartbeats. The absorption should be complete.

Twenty heartbeats.

The onset was not what I expected. The old records had described burning, the meridians responding to the compound's approach as nerve endings respond to acid. Progressive. Peripheral to central.

It was coldness.

The cold of absence. Something was being eaten away. The Shadow-Eater was eating.

My peripheral channels went, the minor meridians in my fingers and toes, one by one. Absence. Coldness.

Twenty-five heartbeats. You held your pen at the ready, waiting for the next data point. The next observable change.

"Fingers," I said. "The peripheral channels are dissolving."

You wrote.

"It feels cold. Like a void where the channels used to be. Not pain. Just nothing."

Thirty heartbeats.

"Secondary channels."

The compound was progressing inward. On schedule. I observed myself die from the edges, layer by layer, channel by channel.

It was cold. So cold.

The poison hit the primary channels.

Thirty-five heartbeats. The large meridians. The load-bearing structure.

The sensation of becoming less.

"Primary channels," I said.

Forty heartbeats.

"The dantian."

The compound reached my core.

"Forty-five," you said. Your voice was wrong.

The Shadow-Eater ate.

And then.
