The clarity lasted a watch and a half.

A watch and a half in which the double realization (it comes from me, it protects only me) was sharp. A watch and a half in which the understanding became structural, a grasp of how the thing-below-qi operated.

Then the edges softened.

The understanding dimmed like a light on a slow mechanism. I knew I had realized something and I knew it mattered, but the details blurred. At first I had understood exactly how the protection worked. Then I understood it approximately. Half a watch later, I understood that it worked. The how was gone. Another half watch and I understood something about protection. The what was uncertain.

I was losing something and I was not sure what, which was probably the point.

The erasure was selective and progressive. It worked on whatever part of the understanding the thing inside me identified as a threat. The conclusion, that it came from me and protects only me, was being degraded from precision to blur. Degraded, not destroyed. Previous erasures had been complete, but this one was partial, as if the substrate were working against something that resisted.

The voice. The *not yet* voice. The other tenant.

I did not hear it again as words, but the resistance was its signature. The something inside me erased and the voice resisted. A tug-of-war between the thing that hid and the thing that kept.

By late morning, only a direction was left. Inward. The compass bearing the cliff had set and the poison experiment had confirmed. The erasure could not delete that completely, because the direction existed at the level where the voice operated, and there the voice held at least equal ground. The contest was ongoing.

The sun came close to the zenith and there was nothing left to stop it. The original tree line was gone, reduced to splinters and debris. Past that, the felled trunks lay radially outward for another forty paces. Past those, the trees still standing were stripped bare: trunks intact, canopies torn away, naked wood offering no shade at all. The entire basin was open sky now. The sun hit the fissured ground without filter and the temperature climbed.

You were lying in direct light, no canopy and no standing shade for seventy paces. The felled trunks cast shadows, but low and narrow, and by noon even those would shrink to nothing.

I scanned the wreckage. One of the larger trunks had fallen against another at an angle, two trees toppled into each other, their combined weight making a lean-to of bark and broken wood. The wedge of shade under it was large enough for a man and would last a few watches as the sun tracked overhead.

I moved you there, carefully, on a thin layer of qi shaped into a cushion between you and the ground. It spread your weight, kept the ribs from shifting, and absorbed the small jolts dragging would have caused. I guided you across the fissured earth without your body touching a single crack edge. Your head ended up out of the sun, the shoulder raised on a folded section of your pack, the ribs supported by the ground's angle.

You made a compressed sound when I moved you, pain arriving and being refused entry into full consciousness. Your face tightened around the eyes and jaw, a grimace that lasted two heartbeats and dissolved back into the slack of sleep. Sleep was where the body repaired itself best, where the ribs could begin knitting without being asked to breathe deeply or hold weight.

I placed my hand on your chest. The midday dose. The qi thread, thin and careful, ran through the primary meridian, and the tissue around the ribs took it. The inflammation had gone down since the night. The swelling around the shoulder was less. Marginal improvements, but present.

I withdrew and considered.

The channels themselves. Your meridian system was wreckage and had been since I interrupted your tribulation. My fault. My intervention, my timing, my decision, and the consequence was yours to carry. A shattered core, collapsed junctions, secondary pathways scarred over in places and open in others. The qi I had threaded through them all night had followed the paths that still existed, narrow and unpredictable but functional enough for the small doses.

But some of those paths were partially blocked, choked with scar tissue from the damage I had caused, like riverbeds with sediment. I could clear some of it. It was the least I could do. I had broken this system. Rebuilding it, restoring the channels to their original capacity, reconnecting junctions, reopening the core, was beyond what I would attempt. Not here, not yet. Not on an unconscious man whose system was already stressed by broken ribs and a concussion. The risk of destabilizing what was left was too high. Clearing was different: removing the sediment, widening the existing paths by a fraction.

I owed you that much.

I sent a thread along the primary meridian, slower than the healing doses. It deposited no qi into the tissue but moved through the channel itself, feeling the walls, the constrictions, the places where the residue had narrowed the path. I found a blockage, a thickening rather than a full collapse, scar tissue halving the channel's diameter. I pressed the qi against it, gently, as water presses against silt. The residue shifted a fraction. The channel widened by a margin too small to see but large enough to feel.

I moved to the next constriction. And the next. I worked slowly, like someone cleaning an old irrigation ditch section by section, until it was enough for now. More would stress a system already strained. The cleared paths would make the healing doses more efficient: less resistance, better distribution, more qi reaching the tissue that needed it.

I did not touch the core. It sat at the center of the wreckage like a collapsed building, unstable, sharp-edged, and liable to fall further at the first intervention. Whatever had broken your core had done so thoroughly. Repairing it would take time, and very precise work after all your channels were mended. Not here. Not now. Perhaps not ever. The risk was your life, and the margin was thin.

I sat back.

The sun moved. The shade from the fallen trunk shifted as midday approached its peak. Daytime insects had replaced the night's crickets, the ones that worked in heat and light. Flies found the dried blood on the discarded bandage fabric. The birds had multiplied, the morning scout joined by others. Territorial calls. A woodpecker worked one of the damaged trunks, finding the exposed wood under torn bark. The efficient exploitation of catastrophe. Where trees fell, insects colonized. Where insects colonized, birds followed. The destruction was already becoming a habitat.

The air smelled different than it had at dawn. The night-cooled resin scent had given way to something warmer: heated sap from the broken trunks, the sharp sweetness of freshly exposed heartwood drying in the sun, the mineral smell of fissured earth warming. The clearing was composting in real time. The forest was beginning to digest what I had done to it.

You murmured something. I turned. Your lips moved, a fragment of a word or several, compressed by the shallow breathing your ribs imposed. Unintelligible. Your hand twitched against your pack, fingers curling and releasing, the involuntary movements of a sleep that was not entirely peaceful.

In the shade, the scab on your temple was darkening, drying, contracting as the tissue beneath it healed. The bruising around the left eye had developed fully, dark purple edged with green, the color progression that said the blood was being reabsorbed. Ugly. Functional. Normal.

Your breathing caught. A deeper intake, immediate punishment from the ribs, immediate retreat to the shallow rhythm. Your face tightened again. Two heartbeats. Gone. The ribs reminding the body of the rules, even in sleep.

It was good that you slept.

Something in me is alive. Something in me wants me alive. Something in me is willing to destroy everything near me to achieve that objective. But it let me remember the consequence. The broken ribs, the blood, the shockwave. It left the guilt intact.

Why?

Because guilt reinforced isolation. It *wanted* me to remember the destruction because the memory served its purpose: keep me isolated, keep me away, keep the protection's radius clear.

The counter-manipulation was elegant. It erased the understanding while preserving the fear. I knew I was dangerous but could not remember exactly WHY. I could not reach the understanding that would let me *manage* the danger. I could only feel it.

A cage made of my own feelings, maintained by the thing inside me.

I sat on a fallen tree and held this realization as long as I could: the selective erasure was itself strategic. Whatever did this was curating, not deleting. The thing inside me was intelligent, or at minimum purposeful.

This one degraded too, within a short while. The thing inside me erased my understanding that it erased strategically. What was left after all the degradation was a feeling, held at the level where the voice operated, where the direction lived, where the erasure could not fully reach.

It did not feel like an enemy.

The feeling was simple, the residue the filtering left behind. It explained no mechanism and identified no thing. It said only that the thing was native: something that had always been there, would always be there, and knew me from inside.

At the edge of the wreckage, you stirred. Your eyes opened and found me.

"You're here," you said.

"Yes." Still here. Both of us. The thing that lived in me and me.

You closed your eyes again. The breathing steadied. Sleep, not unconsciousness.
