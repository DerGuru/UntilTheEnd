Cold basalt lay under my feet at the crater's western rim. I closed my eyes and reached for the warmth from the cliff. Something had stirred under my qi there, pressing against my heart from within.

I quieted everything I could and attended to that remembered sensation. It was hard to tell from the ordinary movement through my channels. I listened to my breathing until the qi under it came clear.

The low humming continued. Qi circulated as steadily as it had through the forest, sustaining itself without my help. I let that hum pass and searched under it for what I had felt before.

I concentrated past the channels cultivation had formed. Pressure grew as I held my attention there, as though I were descending through water. The deeper I pushed, the harder the effort became to sustain.

I passed the channels, the core, and the structures I had developed over millennia. Somewhere past them lay the sensation I was trying to recover.

And there I found a boundary.

My attention faltered there. Crossing it was as hard as holding the instant between waking and sleep. I could approach it clearly, but the distinction dissolved as soon as I tried to pass.

I pushed.

The resistance tightened. At the cliff something had stirred; here I got no farther. I knew I should leave this alone.

Not now.

I pushed harder.

My qi surged to its full strength. I had called for no such increase.

I touched the stone.

I pressed harder against the surface and held the contact. A crack appeared under my palm. It lengthened slowly along a grain I could not see, reaching a handspan, then a handspan and a half.

The stone divided cleanly. The ground did not tremble. Across the lake the temples made their small sounds; the splitting basalt made none.

Silence.

I stood with my hand extended toward the new line. Basalt thousands of years old had split while I touched it. I had directed no force into the rock. The narrow crack lay exactly under my palm.

I looked from my hand to the fissure. The refusal I had felt inside me now had an edge I could see. Whatever had done this had confined itself to one narrow line. That precision made it hard to step close again.

You stood just above me on the rim. You had seen it too.

"What—" You stopped. "What was that?"

I looked at the crack. Clean. Impossible.

"I don't know," I said.

You came down carefully to kneel by the crack. Your finger followed it along the surface. Then you looked back at my hand, brow drawn tight.

You reached for your notebook to record what you had seen: the split stone, my resting hand, and the silence in which it had happened.

"Don't do that again," you said.

You said this without looking up.

"I wasn't trying to," I said.

"Which part?"

"Any of it."

You looked up at me before accepting that answer.

You closed the notebook. Neither of us spoke.

Then you said, "What are we doing now?"

"We move to the next one."

You closed the notebook over something else I would learn much later. Someone had approached you on the way to the crater, while I was walking ahead. You had refused them. Standing at the split basalt, I knew nothing of it.

And you had said nothing to me about it.

We moved on.
