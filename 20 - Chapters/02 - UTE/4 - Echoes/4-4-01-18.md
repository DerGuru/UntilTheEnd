The path had brought us back into the mountains. Over several days, the lowlands rose into folds, hills, and finally a ridge. We had followed that ridge for two days. Trees still grew at this height, though the horizon opened above them. On either side the ground fell away.

A fault had sheared the face clean. The path ended at a rim, with no ledge between it and the ground far below.

Flat gray rock lay at the bottom. From this height its roughness had disappeared. Nothing on it would break a fall before the falling body broke.

I followed the last stretch of path until I stood at the edge.

The front third of each foot hung past the stone. I balanced without effort. I had stood on fortress walls, canyon rims, and temple rooftops in dynasties whose names I had forgotten. The small adjustments that kept my weight behind my toes came without thought. I could have stood there indefinitely.

Wind had pressed against us for days at this height, hardest over the last stretch where the ground narrowed. My clothes had tugged at my legs as I walked onto the exposed stone.

The wind had stopped.

It ceased all at once. My clothes hung still against my legs.

Silence.

Even beyond the circle I carried, the air was quiet. Insects, birds, and the faint hiss of moving air had gone together. My breathing filled the silence, each breath so distinct it might have belonged to someone standing close beside me.

I looked down and came to the conclusion that this was a long way to fall.

You had dropped behind on the switchbacks. I heard none of your movements.

I looked down again. The rock lay gray under the open sky, very far from the narrow strip that held my feet. Soon I would learn how much could happen in that distance.

I shifted my weight to my left foot, lifted my right, and put it half a pace forward.

A step. Except beneath the step was nothing.

You came around the switchback behind me and into my field of vision, shock plain in your eyes.

I was still attending to that step beyond the edge. No farewell had formed. Two syllables had, an experiment's description.

Let's see.
