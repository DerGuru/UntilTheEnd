We were in the room. The narrow bed, the straw mattress, the rough blanket. The river sounded through the thin walls. The light through the window had shifted from amber to gray. Somewhere downstairs, a door closed. A child's muffled voice said something and was answered by a woman's laugh.

"The answer is inside me. So I have to go in."

You were sitting on the bed, back against the wall. Your arm was improving. The bruising around your eye had gone the greenish-brown of late-stage resolution. Your ribs were unchanged, but the bed had helped. You looked less like someone held together by discipline alone.

"Sounds unpleasant," you said.

You strapped on your pack one-handed, the ribs precluding two-handed motion. You adjusted your weight, stood up, and moved to my side.

The river flowed below the window, catching the last light in shifting fragments. A dog barked twice from one of the houses and stopped. The smell of woodsmoke drifted in: someone else's fire, someone else's evening meal, the ordinary rhythms of a place that did not know us and would not remember us.

"Forward," you said.

I moved.

We went downstairs, walked out, and left the settlement soon after sunrise. The bridge creaked under our weight. The woman was not hanging laundry yet, too early, the mist still sitting on the river like gauze. A rooster called from behind one of the houses. The sound followed us into the trees, and then the trees swallowed it.

You followed, limping, your breathing calibrated for the ribs. They had improved, but there was still a way to go. I carried your pack. You held your notebook against your chest.

The path climbed through birch and pine. The ground was drier here: sandy soil, scattered needles, the crunch of it under our feet replacing the soft give of forest floor. A jay announced us from three trees ahead, hopping from branch to branch, staying just out of reach. It followed us for an eighth of a watch before dropping back.

We stopped twice, and you leaned against things, a boulder, a fallen trunk. I waited. You straightened. We continued.

By midday the pines had thinned and the landscape opened: a ridge, a valley below, a long view the forested hills rarely permitted. Grass moved in the wind. The sky was high and pale, and the air carried the smell of warm stone. In the distance, south and east, the terrain changed color, darker, rougher, the volcanic geology beginning to assert itself. We stood there taking it all in.

We moved back toward the old crater, where lava had cooled into basalt and I had laid my hand against the stone. I had pulled it back before I understood why. The place where whatever lived inside had first stirred. The place where I would begin the inward turn.

You meditated that evening. On the ground next to the fire, in the lotus position modified for broken ribs. The air smelled different here: drier, mineral, the pine resin giving way to something older. A moth came to the flame, a smaller one than the copper-winged kind from the outcrop, pale, orbiting in slow loops.

I sat next to you, legs crossed, facing the fire. A cricket started somewhere in the grass behind us. Then stopped.

You finished and opened your eyes. The faintest trace of a smile crossed your face.

The moth had landed on a stone near the embers. Wings folded. Still. The valley below us was dark, but the ridge held the last light, a thin line of amber along the rock, fading as we watched.

The fire burned.
