The gray arrived slowly. The transition from firelight to dawn was a dilution, not a switch: the warm orange thinning into something cooler and paler, the shadows losing their depth degree by degree. The fire had burned to embers. I did not add wood. The morning would be warm enough.

You were still sleeping. The last qi thread had settled half a watch ago. Your breathing was steady, the deep, regular rhythm of a body that had decided to live and was doing the work of living without consulting the mind. I had left the laceration to air. The scab was forming well, dark and tight, the skin around it bruised but intact.

I sat where I had sat all night, next to you. The destroyed forest was acquiring the clarity of daylight, which turned devastation from atmospheric to precise. In firelight, the fallen trees had been shapes. In morning light, they were evidence.

The thought arrived as dawn had arrived, gradually, without announcement. A drift. The analysis from the night watches had wound down, the data points sorted, the questions defined but unanswered. In the quiet that followed, in the space where the analytical mind released its grip, something else surfaced.

I should leave.

The calculation was simple. You were injured. I was dangerous. The shockwave had demonstrated, in broken ribs and blood, what proximity to me cost. Being near anyone was lethal to them. The pattern demanded distance. Maximum distance. The only responsible conclusion.

I sat with the calculation. It was correct. Every variable supported it.

And then:

*Not yet.*

Two words. They arrived from somewhere below the calculation, below the analysis, below the part of me that had spent the night sorting data points and feeding qi through broken channels.

*Not yet.*

I sat very still.

The words had come from me. Or had they? That was the problem. They had come from me. They had not come from me. Both were true.

I listened, as you listen after a sound in an empty room, breath held, body held, waiting for it again.

Nothing.

The embers ticked. Your breathing continued. A single bird note came from somewhere in the wounded canopy.

I was listening, really listening, and what reached me was the shape of a voice, an impression, the way a hand leaves warmth on a surface after it lifts. Something had pressed two words into the space where my thoughts lived and then withdrawn. The words were still warm.

The familiarity was the part that tightened my skin. The words felt like my own breathing, like the heartbeat I never directed. Present. Intimate. Belonging to me as things belong to a body, without asking permission.

I had never heard this voice. I had always heard it. The contradiction did not resolve.

Who spoke?

I did not ask the question aloud. I sat with it: the gray dawn, the embers, the sleeping man beside me, the question living in the same space as the two words that had arrived without origin.

Something inside me had an opinion about whether I should leave. Something inside me knew words.

I checked the laceration. The scab held. The mechanical motions of care had carried me through the night, and I performed them as usual, but the normalcy was thin.

You would wake soon, your breathing said, and I would not mention this. I did not know what *it* was. What would I say? That something inside me disagreed with my decision to leave? That the disagreement arrived as two words in a voice I recognized without ever having heard?

I tended the fire. I added a branch from the wreckage. The flame took.

The morning progressed: light increasing, temperature rising, the sounds of a forest adjusting to what the shockwave had done. Birds arrived. Living things showed the resilience they always did when their world was broken.

The fire burned. The man breathed. And somewhere inside me, in the space where the two words had arrived and withdrawn, something waited. Patient. Familiar. Unspeaking.

I tended the fire. I checked the scab, the shoulder, the ribs. And I waited for you to wake.
