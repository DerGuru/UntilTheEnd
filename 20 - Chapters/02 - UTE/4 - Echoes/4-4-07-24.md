You picked up your bowl, washed it, and set it by the pack. When you began to meditate, your voice was louder than usual. The mantras came faster, with insistent humming between them that filled each pause in the clearing.

I sat across the fire, listening. Your voice rose, then fell through the sequence you repeated every forty heartbeats. I had heard it often enough to know where you would take a deeper breath, and how the humming would quicken before the next mantra.

The immortal who didn't care had accidentally memorized a stranger's meditation schedule. This was either the most pathetic thing that had happened in my existence or the second most pathetic, the first being that I noticed I had done it.

I waited for the change I expected. It came.

Through all those days of attending to nothing, I had learned this about you. I could listen to a single breath and know what would follow. For a little while, the next moment held something I could count on.

The night air cooled my back while the fire warmed my front. I followed another sequence through to its end.

Eventually you slept. The clearing grew quiet enough that I could hear the place your voice had occupied.

I never told you I had memorized it. You may have noticed anyway. I carried the sequence with me while you slept.

I had learned it simply by letting you stay close. That small habit could become something more. I looked at the fire without pursuing the thought.

I did not pull on it.

I sat by the fire, listening to your quieter sleeping breath.

The humming under my own ribs went on too.

In the morning we left the cliff behind. The vertical stone receded as we walked away. You followed closer than ten paces now. Green grass lay between us wherever we stopped.

But the movement was different.

I had a direction.

At a fork I stopped to look down both paths. The left descended toward the lowlands, and past them lay the regions where I would begin looking for old sites. I chose that branch.

I had weighed the routes before moving. The small pause mattered. I could name what I wanted from the journey and choose a path that would take us there. After so long, that was work enough.

In the forest I had followed whichever path offered easiest passage, and I could have walked on that way forever without deciding where to go. Now I checked the left-hand track's direction against the place I remembered.

You wrote as we moved. The pen scratched behind me. Even my stopping to choose between two paths had earned an entry.

You did not comment. You followed left.

The path descended through trees toward gentler slopes. Other feet had worn it smooth, and the mountain terrain opened gradually toward the lowlands.

Fine gray dust clung to my robes from the cliff's base. I had brushed them when I landed, but sitting on the rock had marked them again. This time I left the dust alone. It was the only trace I carried of where the long fall had ended.

I would investigate.

I would find the places where I had survived before. Old falls, old wounds, and occasions when I should have died might have left something behind. I could compare them with what had happened at the cliff.

The low humming went with me under my ribs, as constant as breathing. Now I listened for any change in it while we walked.

What am I? I returned to the question as the path descended, my attention on the place in my chest where the pressure had gathered.

You walked behind me, notebook in hand, pen moving. Your breathing carried the quickened rate that altitude produced in a cultivator with a broken core.

Anyone seeing us from a distance might have taken us for pilgrims, two travelers descending from a high place.

The cliff grew more distant with each bend. Ahead lay the first part of the journey I had chosen. You walked close enough now that your uneven breathing carried over the scratch of your pen.

I followed the downhill path with dust still on my robes. Under my ribs, the humming went on.
