The headache arrived without notice, and it arrived at full volume: a nail driven behind the eyes, instantaneous. I stopped walking. I had not stopped walking for anything in weeks, whether sleep, food, or terrain. The body that had survived volcanic eruptions and the cliff, defeated by its own skull.

I pressed my palms against my eyes. Thirty heartbeats. Then the pain diminished. It did not vanish; it shifted from nail to bruise, acute to ambient, a background ache that said: *I was here. I can return.*

"What is it?" you asked from behind me, at your usual distance.

"Nothing."

You wrote it down. Both the question and the nothing. You did not challenge the nothing because you had learned that challenging produced less data than observing.

The second headache came half a watch later with a different texture: weight, as if someone had placed a stone on the inside of my forehead and the stone was growing. Same location, same duration. Thirty heartbeats of the world narrowing to a point behind my eyes, then the slow expansion back to normal. The residual ache layered over the first one's residue.

By the third occurrence I had the pattern.

The headaches followed position, cognitive rather than geographic. The pain came when the mind moved toward the substrate. When my attention turned inward, toward the foundation, toward the territory the investigation targeted, the skull responded with force calibrated to stop the thought.

The substrate's second line of defense.

The first line was the memory erasure: passive, retrospective, cleaning the record after the event so I could not build on what I'd found. The headaches were the second line: active, preemptive, pain deployed in real time at the moment my attention approached the boundary. Your notebook preserved what the erasure removed. The passive defense had failed because an external record existed, and the substrate had escalated to something the notebook couldn't counteract.

The substrate wanted me to stop looking. The headache was proof that the investigation was working.

The fourth occurrence came in the afternoon. I had been pushing deliberately, directing attention inward along the path the cliff had illuminated and testing the boundary like a fence for weak points. The pain arrived faster, and this time it was heat: the inside of my skull held over a flame, the bone warming, the pain liquid and spreading in directions pain shouldn't spread.

Forty heartbeats. Ten longer than the others.

I stopped again. My foot missed the path, coordination disrupted by pain the body had never been designed to process. Palms on eyes. Waiting for it to pass.

You were at my side, close, concern overriding your distance protocol.

"That's the fourth today," you said.

I couldn't respond. The pain still occupied the space where language lived, and my mouth would not form words while the skull was on fire.

It diminished. The residual ache settled alongside the others: three layers now, each a reminder that the substrate could produce this at will and that the will was not mine.

I straightened. The world was the world again: trees, path, gradient, you.

"It happens when I think inward," I said. "Toward the substrate. Toward the boundary. The pain starts. The harder I push, the longer it lasts."

You wrote this down. Fast. The pen moving before the sentence was finished.

"The memory erasure happened after," I said. "This happens during. It's defending in real-time now."

You looked up, assembling a framework from pieces you had been collecting separately: the forgotten morning, the repeated words, the headaches. They fit.

"Escalation," you said.

"Yes."

"First it erased. Now it hurts. Because the erasing stopped working."

"Because you started writing it down."

The sentence landed between us. Your notebook had forced the substrate to change strategy. Your documentation had broken its first defense. It had adapted.

You looked at the notebook in your hand. It had been your project, then my lifeline, and now it was a threat significant enough to provoke a biological escalation in something older than either of us understood.

You wrote that down too, then added a note I would not see until much later.

The note said: *Protection pattern identified. Memory erasure (passive) + headache (active). Self-protection escalating. Subject getting closer.*
