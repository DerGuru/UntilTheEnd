Rain filled the air of the foothills, a continuous drizzle that existed as a state rather than an event. Summer brought vertical downpours; winter drove them sideways. This rain hung around us. It collected in puddles, ran through channels, and soaked the earth until every step was heavier than the last.

You were wet.

Your pack had darkened with moisture. Your hair lay plastered to your head. Your outer robe clung heavy with saturated fabric. You had accepted wetness as a condition of existence and moved with resigned efficiency. The acceptance did not stop the low, continuous muttering.

You cursed the rain. You cursed the path. You cursed the mountain clay that stuck to your boots until you could barely lift them. You cursed with your usual methodical persistence, as a scholar might annotate a text he disapproved of.

Your inventory of grievances was impressive. Allegedly the clay was a personal vendetta. The rain was biased. Your left boot had betrayed you.

I almost said something. I didn't.

I was dry.

My qi deflected rain without direction from me. The droplets reached the three-pace radius and curved. I had done nothing to arrange this, any more than you had arranged to be soaked.

You had tracked the rain's behavior near me for two days, a phenomenon most observers would dismiss. This one was harder to dismiss.

"It bends around you," you said, between curses.

"Hm?"

"The rain. It bends. Like—" You searched for the comparison. "Like water flowing around a stone in a river. You're not wet."

"I am not."

"I'm soaked."

"I can see that."

"So you just get a private pocket of dry air," you said. You lifted a hand toward the space near me and watched the droplets curve away. "Convenient."

"It's three paces."

"Three paces," you repeated, as if the number could be negotiated by repetition. "Do you realize how unfair that is?"

"Don't step closer," I said.

You paused at the edge in my voice, your hand still extended.

"Stop testing it. You'll drown in rain."

You took half a step closer, experimentally.

The rain still avoided me.

It did not avoid you.

You stared at the invisible boundary where my qi treated water like an obstacle. "It stops exactly there," you said. Then, with the stubbornness that governed your entire personality, you added: "I'm going to write that down."

"Write it down," I said. "And keep moving."

I moved in whatever direction lay in front of me, following the easiest footing without choosing a destination. You chose the path, adjusting the trajectory. "Left," you would say. "There's a bridge that way." I would go left, toward the bridge. In the absence of preference, your direction was as valid as any other.

You supplied choices. I supplied motion. It was easier than pretending I still had my own.

We moved through the rain for days. The foothills blurred into one another in the gray light between mountain forest and lowland plains. We met the geography by passing through it, noting its features without caring where they led.

I could manage that much: movement toward something, away from something else. You followed because that was your project. Neither of us stopped for a view.

At each nightfall you made camp by the standard procedure. You sheltered the tinder from the rain and eventually kindled a fire with the stubbornness you brought to all tasks. You cooked with difficulty; even the ginger smelled faint in the damp air. You opened your notebook, protected from the rain inside your robe, and wrote your miniature letters, marks of ink against forgetting.

I sat six paces away, the distance that had become the arrangement at cooking time: near the fire, near the food, near without being in. The rain bent around me and not around you. I had stopped finding cosmic editorial comments meaningful several millennia ago.

You ate. I did not. You wrote. I did not.

"Does it do that to smoke too?" you asked. You held your hands over the fire, palms up, as if the question could be tested by warmth alone.

"Probably," I said. "It bends what it can."

"Day twenty-eight," you said, closing the notebook. "And you've passed thirty-three words today already. That's a new record."

You were counting. Of course you were counting. The words were data points, a rising trend from zero in the first seven days to more than thirty-three on day twenty-eight. The trend was your evidence. Your reason for being here.

I did not tell you that thirty-three was, by certain historical standards, a deluge.

The rain continued. The fire hissed. You curled onto your side, protecting the notebook with your body, and slept.

I sat. Dry. In rain that was everywhere except near me.

The world was gray. It had been gray since the departure and would be until something changed. I couldn't see that change from here. This was the only position available.

Gray was the color of functioning without living. I knew it as surely as I knew the rain bent around me. I had thousands of years of evidence, centuries in which the body walked, the mind observed, and the heart beat. Together they kept me in existence. Another drop curved past my sleeve.

I had done this before. I would do it again. The gray would end and return, and knowing that made the repetition cruel. Immortality had many structural disadvantages. Recurrence was the one no one mentioned.

Two people in the rain. One counting words. One producing few of them.

Morning would come. We would move. You would curse. I would be dry. The arrangement would hold until it changed.

Not now.
