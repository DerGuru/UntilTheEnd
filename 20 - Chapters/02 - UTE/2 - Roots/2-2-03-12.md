Before dawn, I sat by the remains of our fire with three qi-signatures at the edge of my awareness. They were northeast this time, evenly spaced. Each swept a separate stretch of ground toward the same point. Two were Qi Gathering. The leader's irregular pulse suggested a recent advance into Core Formation, still unsettled. Their triangle was closing.

The speed was wrong.

They moved methodically, pausing over residual qi-traces before covering the next stretch. In twenty-one days of patient searching, they had narrowed an area of five hundred li to forty.

My false trail should have sent them southeast. I had practiced that skill for millennia; the traces I'd planted matched those of someone passing through. These searchers had found something else to follow. Around me, the world's natural qi dipped. However carefully I muted my presence, I still consumed it. They were following those depleted patches.

I had judged that few organizations possessed searchers skilled enough to follow such a faint absence. Reasonable. These three were proving how little that mattered.

Wei slept close by. His chest rose and fell in the regular pattern he'd practiced so often.

"Wei."

He woke instantly, his eyes on me. In the village, he'd taken longer to rouse. Now he was reaching for his bundle before I spoke again.

"We need to move."

He didn't ask why. In a few dozen breaths he had packed his bundle, knife, and water. Forty-two days without a fixed camp had made him efficient. He checked the ground where he'd slept, then stood ready to walk.

We were moving before the first light cracked the eastern ridge.

I set a faster pace than usual, though within what he could survive. He jogged on downhill stretches and strengthened his steps with qi on the climbs.

He kept up. Qi strengthened his legs, steadied his joints, and sustained him in the thin air. For someone with so little training, he was remarkably efficient. I checked each step for a falter.

By noon, the signatures had fallen behind us. They went on closing their search pattern while the gap between us widened.

I stopped on a ridge and looked back, extending my awareness.

Three points moved methodically through the ground fifteen li south.

"Who are they?" Wei had read my pause correctly.

"Scouts."

"Whose?"

"Does it matter?"

He considered. "No."

We went north onto exposed ridgelines above the trees. Wind scraped the stone clean. Even the scouts would hesitate to follow us here, where only creatures accustomed to the cold could endure.

Like us. Probably.
