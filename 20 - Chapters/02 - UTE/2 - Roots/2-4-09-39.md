Before dawn, I rose from the cold ashes. Wei slept a few paces away. The lowland chill would have made an ordinary traveler reach for a robe.

I walked east. Away from the camp. Away from him.

I would return before he woke. First I needed to test distance. If my nearness fed the stream, moving away should reduce it.

I counted my paces, my qi-sense extended behind me. His core, breathing, and warm, oversaturated field all came through clearly.

One hundred paces. The stream remained at full density.

Two hundred paces. It was unchanged.

Three hundred paces. A rise hid the camp from sight. The stream thinned.

Five hundred paces. It was thinner again. The farther I walked, the more the flow diminished.

Eight hundred paces. The stream carried about a tenth of its former strength.

One thousand paces. Two li.

Almost nothing remained.

Distance worked.

Two li between us would slow his growth, giving his core time to consolidate.

Two li, with people hunting us. I would have to leave a thirteen-year-old alone. I could cross the gap in heartbeats if I risked tearing through the intervening ground. A careful return would take much longer.

I turned back.

At six hundred paces, the stream strengthened. At four hundred, it carried half its usual flow. At two hundred, the full amount. By one hundred, it had grown stronger than before. More qi flowed into Wei as I approached, making up for what my absence had withheld.

When I reached camp, he was receiving about a fifth more than before I'd left. The brief reduction was already being undone. Distance delayed the flow, only for it to return more strongly.

Wei stirred, still half-asleep.

"You went somewhere."

"Stretching my legs."

He opened one eye and fixed it on my face.

"Your qi feels different."

I went still. "Different how?"

"Stronger. Like you... opened something. Or stopped holding something."

He read the change in my energy as easily as an expression. He didn't question how he knew.

"Go back to sleep."

"Yun."

"What."

"Whatever it was. It didn't work, did it."

For three heartbeats, I had no answer. Half-asleep, he had felt my failure through the qi entering his channels.

"Go back to sleep, Wei."

He closed his eye and lay still, whether asleep or pretending.

The stream continued more strongly than before. Hungry was the word that came to me.

You'd recognize that word.

I watched the current, wondering whether it could follow a will of its own. It moved as if directed, yet every attempt I made to direct it failed.

I sat with Wei until morning.
