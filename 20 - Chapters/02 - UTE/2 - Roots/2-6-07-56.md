The stairs creaked under me in the quiet inn. It was late enough that even the drinkers had surrendered. I climbed one step at a time, hearing Xu Ran's question again.

"What are you?"

Always the same words from different mouths across different centuries. The dry smell of the old wood brought back a library, its shelves crowded with scrolls. So long ago that even the language he'd written in had died.

He was a philosopher. A condition he'd developed rather than a title he'd chosen. A mind that couldn't stop asking, in institutions that couldn't stop answering.

I remembered him thin, worn down by thought. He sat surrounded by scrolls. Thousands. Every attempt humanity had made to articulate the nature of reality in a language we had invented for it.

He'd looked up and seen me without flinching. He had no categories left. He'd dissolved them systematically, deliberately. He sat in the wreckage, serenely uncomfortable, having torn down his own shelter because it was inaccurate.

"What are you?" His first sentence. No greeting.

I sat down among his scrolls and answered.

I told him pieces of truth about time, qi, and the structure of the world as I perceived it. His scrolls were written by people inside the painting, and I was someone who could see the frame.

He listened. Days. Then tens of days.

He understood. That was the problem. Other minds filtered what I said, their limitations protecting them. He didn't filter.

The first month, he was brilliant.

The second month, he was quiet.

The third month, he was still.

The fourth month, he was empty.

He breathed. He ate when food was placed in front of him. But the light behind his eyes was gone. He had stopped asking questions. Everything else had stopped with them.

I left. He sat on among his scrolls, existing, the one verb left when all the others had been removed.

He got his answers. Afterward, I wished he hadn't asked.

My hand tightened on the stair rail. That I was old would have been the survivable answer.

I reached the top of the stairs.

Xu Ran had asked the same question. And I had given him the survivable answer.

Wei was sleeping. His core glowed faintly, warm through the door.

I opened the door and sat.

The stream flowed.

The inn settled. The night continued.
