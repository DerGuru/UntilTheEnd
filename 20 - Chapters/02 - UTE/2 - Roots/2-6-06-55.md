He found me behind the inn that night. Drainage ran along the narrow stone-walled alley. Past it, people spoke in voices low enough to keep strangers from listening. The moon was half.

I'd known he would come. All evening, his attention had returned to the place where my qi should have been. During dinner, his gaze had found me and held. The damage let him sense what his intact perception would have filtered.

Four paces. Close enough to talk. Far enough to run, though running was optimistic given his body and mine.

He stood upright on purpose, and it cost him. The statement: I am not kneeling.

"I know what you are."

"Do you."

"The woman who stopped the sky. The one I've been—" He swallowed. "—looking for."

I said nothing.

"What are you?"

Wind. The half-moon traced its path.

I studied him, the young man who had tried to ascend, been struck down, and spent months paying for me with pieces of himself. He stood here with a cracked core and steady hands.

He didn't kneel. His legs trembled with the effort, but he stood, because standing was the last thing he had.

"Old."

The wind shifted. My shoulders dropped a fraction, a release I hadn't permitted in decades.

Xu Ran waited. Long enough for the voices beyond the wall to fade.

"Why?" he asked.

"Why what?"

"Why didn't you kill me?"

"Why would I?"

"I threatened your village."

"It wasn't my village."

But it was.

His gaze said he'd heard the lie and wouldn't challenge it.

"I'll find you again," he said.

"Many have said that."

"I'm not one of them."

He turned and walked away, unsteady now. He'd spent his last reserves standing upright. At the corner he turned out of sight.

I stood alone in the half-dark with the question still in the air. I'd answered honestly, though I doubt he understood the implications.

The honesty hadn't helped.

I went back inside.
