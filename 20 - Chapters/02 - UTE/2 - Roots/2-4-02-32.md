The next morning, Wei practiced his stabilization exercises by the river. He breathed, circulated, and settled his qi. The new three-step pattern should have reduced pressure.

I sat with my feet in the water, extending my perception past him into the air. I checked the currents as a matter of routine.

Then I examined the space between us more closely. A faint current ran through it, where most cultivators would have sensed only air.

My qi flowed toward him. I wasn't directing it, but the current continued steadily from my body to his.

It looked like sunlight through glass.

The stream pulsed in his rhythm. When his core drew inward, my qi flowed to meet it. As his channels opened, they filled with my strength.

His hands shook.

The shaking was deeper than yesterday's fine tremor. His right hand spasmed mid-form, its fingers locking open. The stabilization pattern collapsed. Qi spiked through his meridians, sending a visible pulse across the water a pace from shore.

Wei caught himself and reset his stance. He began the form again from the second position.

His hands shook again. Worse.

"Stop," I said.

He set his jaw. Forcing his breathing back into rhythm, he pushed through the third transition.

"Wei. Stop."

He stopped, opened his eyes, and stared at his hands. His fingers were still locked at wrong angles. Tendons stood out under the skin.

"That's new." He studied the locked fingers.

"How long?"

"The shaking?" He flexed his fingers until they closed. "You know my hands were shaking since we started. It got worse on the mountain with the moths, but not like this."

I pulled my feet from the water and stood. I crossed to him, taking his right hand without asking. His meridians held three times the qi they could safely carry at this stage.

I recognized my own qi in them.

Oh.

All this time I had studied his talent, trying to account for how far it had carried him. My qi had been flowing into him the entire time.

Forty-seven parts in a hundred. The number from the dark had a cause now. I was supplying the qi that would drive his core to fracture.

I'm breaking him.

I dropped his hand.

"What?" he said.

"Your channels are overpressurized."

"I know. The exercises are supposed to—"

"The exercises aren't enough."

The river ran by us. Wei studied my face, as he had since our first days on the road, keeping my answers for questions he would ask later.

"Something changed," he said. "Between yesterday and now."

"Nothing changed."

"You grabbed my hand like something was wrong."

I had. After all those years of keeping my distance, I had crossed to him without pausing.

"Sit," I said. "I need to recalibrate the exercises."

He sat cross-legged with his hands on his knees, waiting.

I knelt opposite him and extended my perception into his meridians. Small pressure fractures were forming along the tertiary channels. My qi had forced them wider than his body could reinforce.

I tried to draw the stream back, contracting my qi-field as I did when passing through populated places. That had always dimmed my presence.

Nothing happened.

The stream continued steadily from me to him, unchanged by the effort.

I compressed my qi-field harder, sealed its boundaries, and cut the flow.

For three heartbeats, it worked.

Then his core pulled and the stream resumed more strongly. Holding it back only increased the pressure.

"Yun?" Wei's eyes were still closed. "Your qi just did something."

"Hold still."

"It spiked. Then it settled. What are you—"

"Hold. Still."

He held still. I sat back on my heels, taking an ordinary breath where he could see it.

My own qi flowed from my body into his. I had tried twice to stop it.

"New exercises," I said. "Starting tomorrow. Pressure-relief focus. Two sessions instead of three."

"Fewer sessions?" He opened his eyes. "My progress—"

"Your progress will adjust."

He gave me the evaluating look. "You're scared."

"I don't get scared."

"You're sitting differently."

I had set myself on my heels exactly as usual. Still, he could see a tension I hadn't concealed. The qi damaging his channels had sharpened his perception enough to read me.

"Eat," I said. "Then we walk. No more training for today."

"Yun—"

"That's not a suggestion."

He went slowly, looking back twice.

I walked to the treeline and stood with my back to the river.

Behind me, his active qi-cycle slowed to its resting rhythm. My energy still flowed into him. Each inward pull from his core drew more. It went on while he trained, rested, or slept. Reducing his exercises would leave that flow untouched.

The willows moved over the river. In the sunlight past their shade, Wei ate cold rice. My qi fed his channels while I stood among the trees.
