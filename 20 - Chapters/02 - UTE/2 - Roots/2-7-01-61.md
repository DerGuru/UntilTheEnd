Autumn was here. The leaves turned red, gold, and the brown that followed. The air cooled and carried the smell of things ending on schedule.

Wei stood on the road. Pack on his back. Face tilted up. Eyes closed.

"Autumn," he said.

I looked at the leaves and didn't see them.

We walked. The road was wider here, a well-maintained route through the northwest pass. Trees on both sides. Color everywhere, bright enough to look like celebration before the leaves fell. Cart tracks in the mud from a merchant caravan, the ruts still holding rainwater.

"How far to the next town?" Wei asked.

"Two days. Maybe less, if the road holds."

He nodded, then kicked a stone. It skipped ahead and vanished into the grass.

He had bound cloth over herbs on his left forearm, tying the bandage himself. Alone. In the dark. Before he thought I was awake.

I saw the bandage right after he'd tied it and said nothing. I waited for him to mention it.

He didn't.

He walked, bright despite the season, smiling at the leaves. He held his left arm at an angle, favoring the wound.

A creek ran alongside the road for half a li, clear and shallow over pale stones. Wei crouched at its edge and splashed water on his face. He wiped it with his good arm.

"Cold," he said. Grinning.

"It's getting late in the year."

"Still cold." He shook his hand dry and stood. Droplets caught the light.

The road climbed gradually, then steepened. The trees thinned as cedar gave way to birch, white trunks against the hillside. Yellow leaves drifted across the trail. We passed a stone marker, the characters worn smooth by centuries of weather. Something about a temple. Or a toll. The distinction had blurred.

"Your arm," I said, a watch and a half into the walk.

He glanced at it. "Caught it on a branch. Last night."

A lie. Small. Clinical. Delivered in the tone I recognized because I had invented it.

"Let me look at it."

"It's fine."

"Wei."

"It's fine, Yun."

I didn't push.

A fat gray bird burst from the underbrush ahead, wings beating hard. Wei flinched, then laughed at himself.

"Thought it was a spirit beast," he said.

We walked. The road curved toward the distant gray mountains, ridges that had outlasted civilizations. Wind carried pine resin and wet earth.

Wei walked ahead of me. By three steps. Then five. Then seven.

The distance grew.
