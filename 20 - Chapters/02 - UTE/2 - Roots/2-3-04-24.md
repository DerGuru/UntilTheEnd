A young cultivator came south along the narrow path while we walked north. He was lean, in his early twenties, with the frame that cheap cultivation and cheaper meals produce. His robes were patched. His sword was blunt. His signature was an ordinary low-tier Qi Gathering one as he approached.

He felt Wei before he saw him.

His stride slowed, his head turned, and his face changed. He stared toward the qi Wei was shedding so freely.

He stopped in front of a thirteen-year-old whose qi read as middle-phase Foundation Establishment. Wei's channels were more sophisticated than most Core Formation cultivators achieved in a decade.

His mouth opened, closed, and opened again.

"Hey. You. Boy."

Wei stopped to look at him. After months of my scrutiny, he was comfortable returning a stranger's stare.

"How old are you?"

"Thirteen."

The cultivator frowned. His eyes moved over Wei's face, then down to his hands, as if searching for a reason to doubt him.

"Thirteen. And you're—that's Foundation Establishment. Middle phase."

Wei waited. He didn't grasp how unusual his advancement was. To him, this was another day on the road.

"That qi-flow pattern. That's impossible for his age." The cultivator spoke more quietly now, watching Wei's hands. "Who trained you?"

His eyes flicked to me, three steps behind Wei. My qi-signature gave him nothing to read. He passed over me as readily as he might a mortal woman carrying someone else's pack, returning his attention to the boy.

He did not take me seriously. They rarely did.

"Keep walking," I said.

The cultivator flinched and turned toward me. This time he looked properly at my face.

"Wait. Please forgive my ignorance. I've never seen—"

"Keep walking."

Wei glanced at me. I walked. He followed. The cultivator stood in the path behind us, his mouth open, his attention locked on Wei's retreating back.

He would tell everyone he met. Each retelling would add detail while losing accuracy. The story would pass from cultivator to cultivator, beyond any chance of containing it.

Wei's own advancement made him visible now. Other cultivators could read it in the qi he shed. I could blur our trail, but I couldn't make him contain that flow. He needed three more cultivation stages before he could suppress it himself.

Too fast.
