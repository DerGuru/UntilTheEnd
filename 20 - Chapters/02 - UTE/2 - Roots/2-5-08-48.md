I heard him leave on the twelfth night. His blanket shifted. He eased his weight off one foot and exhaled with all the care a thirteen-year-old could manage. He was quiet, but I could hear a moth's heartbeat at forty paces.

Wind had shifted since dusk. Colder now, carrying pine resin and the mineral edge of distant snow.

Twenty paces east. The Wei-defined radius of privacy. He'd chosen this distance before. He was consistent.

He trained alone. The stabilization exercises. My exercises. He performed them perfectly. His qi circulated smoothly, balanced through his channels, his core stable. Every movement carried the precision of a practitioner who had passed learning and arrived at owning.

His qi-pattern was clean. Cleaner than when I supervised. At twenty paces, the stream was weaker. His channels were running mostly on their own capacity.

He trained better without me.

Was he better off without me?

A third of a watch. Then he stopped and didn't come back. He sat cross-legged, facing east, hands on his knees.

Someone deciding something sits like that.

Then he walked back and lay down. He faced the forest as he slept.

His breathing was calm. His core was steady. The tremor in his hands, present even in sleep, had eased by a fraction.

The crack was quiet. But measurable.

/xʀ̩p̚↓/. The old tongue's word for it. The sound of tearing, spoken in a language I'd helped shape when words were still simple, before the sects gave everything formal names. A jagged tear through something I had thought would hold.

The fire was embers. The forest was the forest.

The problem was me.

You'd have told me sooner. Or maybe not. You had your own version of staying too long.
