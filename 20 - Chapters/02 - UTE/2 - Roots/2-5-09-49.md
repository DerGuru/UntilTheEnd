Before dawn on the fourteenth day, two signatures slid across my senses beyond the forest. South-southeast, maybe thirty li out. They moved with a tracker's purpose. One was Iron Lotus. The other I didn't recognize, which was worse. Known threats have limits. With strangers, I couldn't guess what they could bring.

They weren't close, but they were following our route and holding to it.

I woke Wei.

"We run."

He studied my face.

"How many?"

"Two. Far, but not far enough."

His jaw set. He didn't argue.

He ran.

We moved north at the maximum sustained pace his body could maintain without qi-assistance. I didn't allow qi-output. Qi meant a signature, and a signature meant detection. The next pursuers would come prepared for what Bear had reported.

Through forest that thinned as altitude dropped. Through valleys that smelled like rain. Through geography I knew from centuries ago that had changed in the slow, indifferent way land changes.

Wei's breathing changed during the second half-watch. Shorter. Sharper. His controlled rhythm broke into uneven gasps. The stubbornness-muscle in his jaw locked.

He didn't stop.

A watch and a half. Ridge, descent, valley, stream crossing, and ascent. The geological rhythm of northern travel.

He forced his breathing under control, each breath carefully drawn through pain.

Two watches and a half.

His face was white. He had been forcing himself onward long past exhaustion.

"Stop."

He stopped at once. He'd been waiting for the command, running on spent legs for the last half-watch. His knees buckled. He caught himself and stood there, breathing.

"Sit."

He sat on a rock, hands on his knees, chest heaving.

I checked his channels with my hands held just above his chest. The stream surged at my approach. My fingers trembled.

His qi-pattern was ragged. Overtaxed. Two watches and a half of sustained physical output without qi-assistance had depleted reserves that a thirteen-year-old body couldn't replace through breathing alone.

I fed a thread of healing qi into his channels to stabilize them. The cycle that was treatment and damage simultaneously.

He endured it. Before, he'd have leaned into my hands with the trust of a boy who believed completely. Now he sat still.

The qi settled. His breathing slowed. I withdrew.

"The next ones will be stronger," I said.

He nodded without speaking.

The afternoon light came through the canopy in fragments. Somewhere east, smoke rose from a settlement or a camp, too faint for Wei to see. People. A direction we could take when we had to stop.

He shouldered his pack.

We walked. North. Into whatever north contained.

The exhaustion would pass. The walking wouldn't. The lie wouldn't. The crack wouldn't.
