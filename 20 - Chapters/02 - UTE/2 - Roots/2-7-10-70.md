At dawn, Wei woke gasping, his core visible through his shirt. The glow was no longer limited to sleep. This was the first of three surges over three days. The interval was shrinking.

He dampened it. In three breaths, his core was locked down and his signature reduced. He sat breathing hard, hands between his knees. His tremor was expanding into both forearms. It was continuous.

"I'm fine," he said.

"I didn't ask."

"I know. I'm telling you anyway."

On the second day, at midday, we were walking. His step faltered without warning. He caught himself and stood still until his balance returned. A thin line of red ran from his left nostril to his lips. He wiped it away with the back of his hand.

It worsened on the third day. The surge became continuous. His core vibrated constantly, the hum present all day.

Stability count: back to three.

Three out of ten. Where ten was healthy and one was Xu Ran.

He laughed.

At a bird. A small brown mountain bird collided with a pine branch. It overcorrected and spiraled improbably onto a rock, where it folded its wings neatly.

Wei laughed. A genuine sound I hadn't heard in many days.

"You're staring," he said.

"I was watching the bird."

"The bird left."

"Then I was watching where the bird was."

He gave me a look. Amused. Allowing it.

His laugh suddenly sounded like the thin chime of glass.

"We're going deeper into the range," I said.

"We're already in the mountains."

"Higher. Deeper."

"Why?"

"Because nobody is out there."

"And if I—" He stopped. The same broken sentence. The same unfinished thought. "If I—"

"Then we're alone."

"That's what you said last time."

"The answer hasn't changed."

He held my gaze. Long. Then he shouldered his pack.

"Okay," he said.

We walked into deeper ranges where the air was thin and the rock was bare. Wei set the pace, faster than yesterday, as if the decision had loosened something. Or as if something behind us was gaining.

Wind from ahead. Cold. Carrying nothing.

Three. Out of ten. And falling. But walking.
