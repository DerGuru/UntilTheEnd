The road ended. What we'd been calling a trail narrowed to a goat path, then to a line, and finally to nothing. Rock, grass, and sky were all that remained.

Wei stood at the boundary between trail and not-trail. Pack on his back. His hair moving in wind that tasted of altitude and emptiness.

He looked at the mountains.

I looked at the mountains.

"Mountains again," he said.

"Mountains," I said.

Behind us: everything we'd left. A village. Three sects at an inn. A legend spreading south. A boy with a broken core who had said *I'll find you again.* A flower that had bloomed and died in ten heartbeats.

Behind us: the world.

Ahead: mountains. The space between the ridges dwarfed everything in it. The geographical equivalent of silence.

Wei walked forward. Past the end of the trail. Onto rock that had never been walked on in any pattern that constituted a path.

He walked ahead of me.

This was new. The distance had been growing for many days. But the position was intentional. He was leading.

I am going. You can follow.

Something shifted behind my eyes, in my sternum. Like déjà vu, but heavier, a vertigo that left me standing. The taste of metal, brief and sourceless. A wrongness from directions that shouldn't exist.

I let it pass. It passed.

I followed.

His back was straight, his pack adjusted, and his hands were trembling. Both. The tremor showed from behind, his fingers shaking even as they hung loose at his sides.

Three out of ten. And falling.

I followed him into the mountains.

We walked. No dialogue. No plan. One ahead, one behind, steps falling into shared rhythm without discussion.

The mountains rose around us. Past the ordered slopes lay rock, sky, and thin air. His breathing carried its cost.

Wei didn't look back.

I followed.

One step. Then the next.

The mountains.

The sky.

The boy ahead.

The being behind.

I followed.
