The small, dirty inn stood where three trails met. Travelers carried news through its doors in conversation. By the time a fact left again, it might be a legend. I needed one.

Scouts were looking for us on behalf of sects whose curiosity would eventually become dangerous. Distance hadn't stopped the flow into Wei. Neither had suppression. I could still decide where our pursuers looked. While I sought a way to help him, I needed to send them elsewhere.

The innkeeper was fat, talkative, and indiscreet. He received gossip as eagerly as he passed it on, without considering its value.

I approached the counter while Wei waited outside. For once he'd accepted my suggestion. Maybe he'd heard something in my voice. Or he preferred fresh air to the inn's smell of fermented cabbage.

"Tea," I said.

The innkeeper poured. Brown. Lukewarm.

"Traveling?"

"Passing through."

"From where?"

"East." A lie. We'd come from the north.

"Heading?"

"South." A lie. We were heading west.

He leaned over the counter. "I heard something interesting the other day. A reliable merchant told me. He's been running the river route for years."

I said nothing. He filled the space.

"He said there's a woman moving through the southern provinces. Unusual. Old, but not old-looking. You know what I mean?"

I sipped the tea. It was as bad as expected.

"Cultivator?" I made it sound idle.

"She must be. The things he described..." He made a vague, expansive gesture. "He said she stopped a tribulation. With one hand."

"Stories grow."

"This one has legs."

I set down the cup and turned as if to leave. Then I paused, letting him see me reconsider.

"I've heard that story. Different version. They say she went south. Over the Jade Pass. Heading toward the coast."

His eyes widened. He leaned farther across the counter.

"The coast? That's specific."

"Travelers talk."

"They do, they do."

I left with the taste of bad tea in my mouth. I had given him a story about myself to repeat. However useful the lie, I disliked hearing my own deeds become gossip in my voice.

I hated every word of it.

Wei sat on a wall outside, carving steadily enough to hide his tremor.

He looked up. "What'd you tell him?"

"Nothing. Eat."

I tossed him a rice cake from a cart at the crossroads. He ate without pressing the question.

Half a watch later, we followed the western trail past the crossroads.

I stopped and crouched to examine the ground.

A jagged scar ran about a pace through the rock. It cut deep into the grain. Qi expelled violently from a body that couldn't contain it left marks like that.

I placed my palm on the scar and read the trace.

I knew the signature from the tribulation, though it had changed. Its old pattern still showed under the ragged edges. It was weaker now, damaged by something its owner couldn't control.

Xu Ran.

The trace was many days old. He must have been standing here when his core spasmed. Its crack was widening. His cultivation was degrading in the residue, his overstrained foundation slowly coming apart.

He was looking for me. And he was running out of time.

I erased the scar with a flick of qi, smoothing the rock as the residue dissolved. Our pursuers could learn too much from it.

Wei hadn't noticed. He was ahead, walking, his back to me.

I followed.

At nightfall we made camp.

Wei carved by the fire, shaping a small bird. It was still rough, but its intent was clear. His hands shook as he worked.

My qi flowed into him at full strength.

"What's that one?" I wanted to hear him speak after the long silence.

He held it up and turned it. "It's a bird."

"Doesn't look like a bird."

"It will." He went back to work as if the matter were settled. Another shaving curled from the wing.

I settled across from him. After testing two li of distance, I had returned. Tonight I would stay close enough to speak again.

I had calculated what leaving would cost him. Staying meant guarding him from the world while my own qi damaged his channels. His knife scraped through the choice I kept making.

The fire burned. Wei carved. His hands shook. The bird took shape.

The stream flowed between us. Tomorrow I would try again to stop it.

The bird had wings. Small, uneven, and unmistakably a bird.
