On the fourth day of our descent, soft ground replaced the stony foothill trails. We had reached river country. Long willow branches hung over the banks, their tips moving above water broken bright by sunlight. The air was warm.

A good day for stopping by a river. For once, we could choose a place simply because it was pleasant.

Wei fished.

He'd already failed twice. The first attempt had produced nothing. The second produced a broken line and a vocabulary that suggested his father's influence more than his mother's. Now he gripped the stick with grim determination. He had caught fish before; apparently that made failure less acceptable.

His stick, string, and bent-needle hook were crude, though better than the equipment he'd last succeeded with. His technique was still awful. He stood ankle-deep in the shallows, gripping too tightly. Qi leaked into the water, scattering fish within ten paces.

I sat on the bank with my feet in the water. Usually I kept clear of things I had no reason to touch. Today I'd lowered my feet into the current simply because I wanted to. I left the reason at that.

Clear, cool water ran over my ankles, dividing around them as it did around the stones.

"HA!"

Wei pulled. The stick bent as the string went taut. He leaned back, nearly fell, and caught himself with a qi-pulse through his heel. Riverbed mud burst away beneath his foot.

From the water came a fish.

Small. It fit in his palm with room to spare.

"That's a fish." He held it up, dripping water down his sleeve.

"That's an argument against fishing."

"It's a START."

He held it higher. The small silver fish thrashed in his grip. Wei grinned at me as if it could feed a village. It was scarcely worth cooking. He had every right to that grin.

I almost smiled. The almost was enough.

He waded out and sat beside me. He came within two paces now without asking. I made room for his wet sleeve.

He studied the fish in his palm. His grin softened at how little of his hand it covered. Even he conceded that it was small.

Then his hands trembled.

The shudder ran from his wrists to his fingertips, the same vibration I'd seen in the mountains. He lowered the fish quickly into the water. It darted away in a flash of silver.

"Too small. It wouldn't have made a meal for both of us."

I said nothing. His hands were shaking too hard to hold a fish the size of his thumb. The shaking was getting worse.

The river moved. The willows swayed.

I left my feet in the water, the cool current passing over them. For this afternoon, I wanted to stay on a pleasant bank watching a boy fail at fishing. I could do that much.

Under his skin, qi pulsed more strongly than yesterday.

Ripples spread where he'd released the fish.

Wei waded back in without bait. He stood in the current with his hands underwater, hiding the tremor.

Tomorrow I would teach him the Reed Stilling exercise. It would give his hands useful work while I looked for a lasting remedy.

The afternoon passed with the current and I let it go.
