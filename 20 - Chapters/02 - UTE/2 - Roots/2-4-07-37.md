On the second day after my failed attempt, Wei walked close to me. He glanced at my face. The hills had flattened into abandoned farmland, with fields gone to seed past half-collapsed stone walls. Here and there a building's foundation showed through the grass.

He had walked this close before, when he'd noticed something he couldn't yet ask about.

He stopped.

He cut his cultivation cycle short in mid-breath. A vertical line formed between his eyebrows.

He turned his hands over, palm up, then palm down.

Then he looked at me.

"Yun?"

"What?"

He opened his mouth, closed it, and tried again.

"Nothing."

He turned back toward the path and resumed walking.

I let him.

The road bent south around a collapsed grain shed. Roof beams jutted from the wreckage. Wei kicked a shard of roof tile, and it skittered across the packed dirt.

"Was this a village?"

"Once."

"What happened?"

"People left. Buildings didn't."

He nodded once, filing it away for later.

That afternoon we reached a cold stream running between the abandoned fields. Wei knelt, drank, and washed his face.

"My hands feel different."

I waited.

"Heavier. Like they're... full of something."

"Your channels are developing."

"Is that what it is?"

"That's what it is."

His channels were developing with every pulse of qi I fed them. I left that part unspoken.

I filled the waterskin while he sat on the bank, flexing his fingers. The water was cold enough to numb them. It came from deep rock rather than surface runoff.

"Can you feel yours?" he asked. "Your channels."

"Not the way you mean."

"What way, then?"

"The way you feel your skeleton. You know it's there. You don't think about it."

He frowned. "That sounds boring."

"It is."

By the time the light turned amber, we'd found flat ground sheltered between two low walls. Wei gathered wood without being asked. Once we stopped, he checked the camp's edges, prepared the fire, and saw to the water. He knew what to do.

I set wards. He arranged kindling. Neither of us spoke for a while.

"Yun?"

"Yes?"

"You look tired."

"I don't get tired."

"You look tired."

I almost laughed. The fire caught. Wei fed it a branch, then another. Sparks rose and vanished against the darkening sky.

"The hands thing," he said. "It's not bad, is it?"

"No."

"Just different."

"Just different."

He lay back against his pack and watched the sky lose its color, one shade at a time.

The vertical line between his eyebrows faded. He held his hands where he could see them, near the fire.
