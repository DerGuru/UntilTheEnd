We made camp that night on a ledge above a mist-filled valley. Blue and gray slopes faded below us as Wei set his pack against a rock.

He was exhausted from the climb, with little strength left in either his muscles or his qi. He'd eaten his rice, which was improving. The burned layer was thinner now, barely a crust. He sat against the rock with his eyes half-closed.

His hands trembled openly in his lap. The stick he usually carved was still in his pack. He glanced toward it once and didn't get up.

"My fingers are buzzing."

The tremor was fine and rapid, even in the firelight. Excess qi spilled through his hands.

"They'll settle."

"They've been buzzing since the ledge." He held them up, studying them against the fire's light. "It's not bad. Like... holding a jar of bees. Tiny bees."

"Don't circulate. Let the excess bleed off naturally."

He dropped his hands, flexed them, and closed his eyes.

"Is this what it's like for everyone? The first year?"

"No."

He opened one eye. "Worse or better?"

"Different."

"You know that's not an answer."

"I know."

The left corner of his mouth twitched upward, then settled. He was too tired for a full smile.

Cold wind moved across the ridge, carrying the smell of ice from higher peaks. Far below, water ran through stone.

I was adding a branch to the fire when a moth landed on Wei's wrist.

Another followed, then three more settled along his forearm. Their wings pulsed slowly together. I recognized the rhythm. It matched his qi-circulation exactly.

Wei opened his eyes and looked down at his arm. "Huh."

"Don't move."

"I wasn't planning to." He watched the moths, holding his arm where he could see their wings. He looked more interested than alarmed. "Are they eating my qi?"

"Drinking. There's a difference." I counted seven now. Eight. Their bodies glowed blue under their wings as they took in qi. I had seen that glow near sect compounds, where qi thickened the air. At this altitude, they had gathered around a boy in his first year. "You're leaking."

"I'm *what?*"

"Leaking. Your channels aren't containing everything. Excess is bleeding through your skin." I flicked the nearest moth off his arm. It spiraled into the dark. "They can taste it."

"Cool."

The other moths lifted from his arm. Their blue lights dwindled into the valley. Wei leaned forward to watch them go.

"You could have let them stay."

"They were accelerating your bleed rate."

"They were pretty."

I didn't answer that. He wasn't wrong.

His qi pulsed again, warm against my awareness. Under his skin it pressed outward, brighter with each circulation. His channels held the pressure. They had so little room left.

It was beautiful. So were the blue lights disappearing below our ledge. The last of them faded. I turned back to his thin wrists.

His advancement was still accelerating. Changes that had taken dozens of days now took only a few. Without knowing why, I couldn't tell him what would happen next.

"Yun."

His voice blurred with approaching sleep.

"What."

"Am I doing something wrong?"

The fire cracked. A spark rose, drifted, and died.

"No."

"Then why do you watch me like that? When you think I'm asleep?"

I turned toward the mist-filled valley, away from the boy who noticed too much.

"Go to sleep."

He slept. His breathing settled into the third-step pattern, his hands finally still on his knees.

By the dying fire I checked his channels as I did every night. I measured their width against the flow, then examined the density of his core. I tested how close it was to instability. Everything had advanced too quickly.

Tonight I found something new. His channels had widened three degrees since that morning, all in a single day.

The moths had gathered in the qi he was shedding. Other things could follow it too.

I finished checking him and sat listening for anything else approaching through the valley.

Nothing yet.
