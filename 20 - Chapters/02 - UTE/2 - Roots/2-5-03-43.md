Bear's hand was still rising when Wei moved. The third gesture, the third demonstration, and another calculated increase in pressure from a man accustomed to wearing resistance down.

He never found the threshold.

Wei jumped.

Months of training vanished. The footwork I'd taught him was gone. He jumped like a child, with everything, all at once, without a thought for the consequences.

His qi exploded.

The power I'd fed him detonated. His entire output, amplified by my stream, concentrated into his fist. He drove it toward Bear's chest with the speed of free fall. Millennia of second-hand power channeled through thirteen years of bone, muscle, and will.

Contact.

His fist hit Bear's sternum. A thirteen-year-old Foundation Establishment cultivator striking a Dao-Seeking elder.

Bear went back. One step. His rear foot ground a furrow in the packed earth. His qi-field destabilized for a fraction of a heartbeat, a fraction that, in combat between cultivators, was an eternity. His eyes widened at the impossibility. The blow hadn't hurt him. But it had *moved* him.

Stillness.

Bear struck back with the reflexive violence of a Dao-Seeking cultivator who had been touched by an insect.

A wall of energy. Wei flew. Ten paces. One moment at Bear's chest, the next airborne.

He hit a tree. The sound: *crk*. Several muffled cracks ran together. Ribs. Maybe his arm.

Blood. From his mouth. Bright, oxygenated red from internal sources.

His core flared. Visible through his clothing, through the blood. Beautiful and terrifying, the edge of tolerance, tipping.

Forty-seven parts in a hundred screamed in my head. His current saturation was almost there.

Not yet. Close.

I moved.

A breath.

A single exhalation, the simplest function of a body. I'd been breathing for longer than most civilizations lasted, and not one breath had been notable.

This one was.

The breath carried a fraction. A whisper of the total. A single drop with the ocean's salinity, too little to measure and enough to show what the whole contained.

Qi-pressure bent the trees in still air. The ground cracked; deep fractures opened through the bedrock under us.

Bear knelt.

His legs buckled under the pressure. His knees struck the earth hard enough to be heard across the clearing. He strained to rise, but the force held him down.

His qi-field folded inward, compressed from a room-filling presence to a fraction, packed tight inside his body.

A sound.

Glass. Breaking. Less than a whisper. Something fracturing at a level of reality that shouldn't produce sound and did anyway. Nobody else heard it.

I heard it.

I filed it and let it go.

"Leave," I said.

Bear left, stumbling at first. He looked back once, still wide-eyed.

I went to Wei.

He looked up. Blood on his chin. In his teeth. And a grin, impossible, irrational. A boy broken against a tree by a force he couldn't comprehend, and his first reaction was to grin.

"I hit him."

I knelt next to him, a hand on his chest. Two ribs cracked, one bruised. Left arm, deep tissue. Nothing that wouldn't heal with qi and time.

His core pulsed against my palm. Too fast. Too hot. It was still running at its limit, and the fuel keeping it there flowed from my hand into his chest in the same gesture that was healing his bones.

Healing him and breaking him. Same hand.

"I hit him." Quieter. The grin softened. Pain, pride, confusion, and trust, all at once.

"You did."

"He was strong."

"Yes."

"Stronger than me."

"Yes."

"But I hit him. And he moved."

"Yes."

He closed his eyes, still grinning through the pain, and let me work. His body, his core, his safety, all handed to the woman whose proximity was the thing putting him in danger.

He lay still under my hand.

You knew that. You used to say it differently. Something about faith and fists.
