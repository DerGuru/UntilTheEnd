The door scraped open across the stone floor. A young man stood in the entrance. Eighteen, nineteen. Thin, then thinner than thin, hollow-cheeked and sunken-eyed. He looked like he'd been burning himself for fuel because nothing else was left.

His clothes had been expensive once. Layered, embroidered sect clothing, investment as much as identity. Now they were torn and stained, the quality surviving only in the cut.

His walk was unsteady. He paused between steps, gathering the strength to take another.

The gaps in his qi were plain. His core was damaged, degraded, and running at a fraction of its former capacity. It was cracked. The distinction mattered. Shattered cores died. Cracked cores leaked.

Xu Ran.

The genius who had tried to ascend. He had triggered a tribulation, bringing the sky down on a village where a boy and a woman lived. His core had cracked.

The merchant's *broken core, traveling this road*. I'd known the name. I hadn't expected to see what was left of the face.

I went still under the surface, my attention narrowing to a sharpness I hadn't needed in many days.

Wei turned toward me, close enough to catch the change in my qi.

"Who's that?" Quiet. Under the room noise.

I didn't answer.

Xu Ran crossed to the counter and spoke to Pig Nose. The gesture was universal: a hand extended, a request for shelter.

He placed something on the counter. Coin-sized, but it was a core fragment. A piece of himself, physically extracted. Crystallized qi, dark-gold, compressed into solid matter. Pig Nose turned it over uncertainly, then took it anyway.

Xu Ran received a bowl. He sat with his back to the wall and ate mechanically, his eyes elsewhere.

"Yun." Wei, again. Closer. "You know him?"

"Eat your noodles."

He ate and watched me watch, reading every flicker on my face, every piece of the puzzle I refused to assemble.

We sat eight paces from a man with a cracked core, paying for soup with pieces of himself.
