Well past midnight, I sat in the dark beside a dead fire, making the same calculations I made every night. By morning I would find another reason to set them aside.

Wei had left the camp. His weight shifted off the bedroll, his footsteps careful, and I tracked his qi-signature northeast through the trees. I let him go. He wanted to train alone, away from my corrections or the small "hm" with which I measured his progress.

I understood. That didn't make me comfortable.

He stopped in a flat clearing two hundred paces northeast. He'd chosen well. He sat, breathed, and circulated his qi. His pattern settled into place. He followed it so easily now that a mistake would have taken effort.

Then he pushed.

He drew in more qi, driving it faster through his channels. In a single breath, he exceeded every limit I'd given him. For so many days I had told him to slow down. Now, alone in the clearing, he was testing what else he could do.

The answer came as an explosion.

His channels couldn't hold the pressure. Qi burst from his whole body, striking the ground, air, and trees within three paces of him.

Earth tore upward. Soil scattered with stones and broken roots. A young pine, two arm-widths thick, toppled out of the ground. Wei stood in a shallow crater at the center of the damage.

Silence.

I was there in a heartbeat. Dirt, leaves, and fine dust covered him.

He was grinning.

"Happens to everyone."

It did not happen to everyone.

His hands shook badly. He reached for his carving stick and began to whittle. Fast, precise strokes drew the excess qi into the work. While the blade moved, he could hide the tremor.

He'd set himself a rule: three days without an outburst before he counted himself stable. He would have to start again.

"How much of that did you see?"

"All of it."

"Were you going to stop me?"

"Would you have let me?"

He considered that and carved another piece away. "No."

"Then no."

The crater was three paces of churned ground. Anyone with qi-perception within twenty li could have felt the outburst.

Wei's grin faded. Between cuts, he opened his fingers and closed them again, testing his control.

At the edge of the torn earth, I remembered another young cultivator. He'd been brilliant, as intent on his progress as Wei was on the stick in his hands. His talent had carried him forward so quickly that everyone praised him. Nobody questioned the pace.

I'd seen him among his fellow students. His training had belonged to someone else.

His core had glowed through his skin and clothes, far brighter than Wei's faint shimmer. It generated more energy than he could regulate.

Wei's breathing hitched. A single missed beat in the third-step pattern, then it resumed.

The memory didn't stop.

The glow lasted three days. His footsteps left qi-traces on the ground; the air around him vibrated. His fellow students began keeping their distance even before anyone warned them.

On the fourth day, the glow stopped.

On the fifth day, they found his body.

His core had collapsed inward, consuming itself. His body looked intact from outside. Inside, nothing answered.

I hadn't intervened. He wasn't mine.

A stone shifted in the crater. Wei sat three paces from the hole he'd made.

You would have. He wasn't yours either and you would have.

That boy's core had glowed for three days. On the fourth, it was cold.

Wei carved on with trembling hands. His core vibrated beneath the steady hum I monitored nightly. Its light was still hidden under his skin, but growing brighter.

I had built Wei's foundation myself. I knew what could happen to a hasty one; thousands of years had given me ample time to learn. I held to that thought while his knife scraped down the stick.

I stepped into the clearing.

"Sleep."

He looked from me to the crater, then down at his hands. They were beginning to still.

"It really does happen to everyone."

"Sleep."

He slept, or pretended to. I sat by the crater and told myself the measures were acceptable.

They were not.

In the morning we would move faster. Some things I couldn't teach him out here, and I would need materials before his condition worsened.
