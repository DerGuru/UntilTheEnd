Cold morning mist hung at waist height along the mountain path. The trees' lower trunks faded into it. Generations of feet had worn this narrow trail before loose stones and undergrowth began covering it again.

Wei walked two steps ahead. His pace was steady; qi moved through his channels in time with his stride. He was beginning to manage the flow that had spilled from him on the first day. A root still caught his foot occasionally, or a loose stone shifted under him, but he recovered faster.

He'd stopped talking. His jaw was set, his muscles tight, his eyes forward. At each rough patch he chose his next foothold with care and went on.

He didn't look back.

I walked behind him, placing my feet soundlessly out of habit. Millennia of movement had made that much ordinary. I hardly needed to look down to avoid the twigs that snapped under his soles.

A bird called. Wei turned his head quickly. The left corner of his mouth lifted.

We were walking away from everything he'd known, his bundle still bouncing against his back, and a bird could make him look up from the path.

A stream crossed the path. Wei jumped. Qi strengthened his legs as he pushed off, carrying him three paces where he would once have managed one. He landed, caught himself, and kept walking.

"You could just step through it," he said without turning.

"Where's the fun in that?"

He glanced back. Half a grin. "You don't have fun."

"I have fun. You just can't tell."

"Nobody can tell."

He walked on. The grin stayed as he turned back to the path.

"Yun."

"What."

"I'm hungry."

"You ate a watch ago."

"A rice ball isn't food. It's a suggestion of food." He ducked under a low branch without breaking stride. "When we find a town, I want dumplings."

"We're not finding a town."

"Then I want dumplings in a forest."

I didn't answer. His half-grin returned. Silence had always given him altogether too much encouragement.

I stepped through the stream. Water rose around my ankles and soaked the hem of my robe. The cold went unnoticed. I had long been able to let sensations pass.

The path climbed through thinning mist. Pines replaced the broadleaf trees. Their needles lay thick over the higher stretches of trail.

Wei was ahead. Two steps. Always two.

He didn't know where we were going. After last night's answers, he hadn't asked again. He went north because I kept him going north. At a fork, I indicated the higher track; where the trail faded, a word turned him toward the next climb. He managed to follow me while walking ahead.

His thin shoulders sat easier now, his hands hanging loose at his sides, the tremor gone and the fingers still. Temporarily. I knew that. He was beginning to know it too.

The mountain rose ahead of us. Through the thinning forest, a great stretch of clear blue sky opened above. The air grew thinner as we climbed toward it.

Then I felt it.

A faint qi-signature moved south-southeast of us, at the edge of detection. It was too weak to threaten me. Its movements were deliberate, quartering the ground instead of following a route. Someone was searching.

A second signature joined the first. It was weaker but moved faster, flanking the first.

I stopped walking.

Wei made it three steps before he turned. "What?"

"Nothing." I adjusted our bearing west. "Walk faster."

He didn't move. His eyes narrowed in the look that usually preceded an argument. "You stopped. You never stop."

"And now I'm walking again."

"You're also lying."

Thirteen years old and already better at reading me than cultivators ten times his age.

"There are people behind us," I said. "Far away. They're looking for something. Probably not us."

"Probably?"

"Walk faster."

He walked faster.

I brushed away our qi trail. The minor technique took little more effort than smoothing footprints from sand. Wei's channels flickered as the suppression touched them.

"That felt weird," he said without slowing.

"Don't think about it."

The signatures faded as we crested the ridge. They were still searching, but the old stone between us scattered their qi until I could barely distinguish one from the other. It would blur our traces too. Good enough.

Wei kept his eyes on the descent. I followed without looking back. Enough came through the stone to tell me what was behind us.

We went down the path, faster now.
