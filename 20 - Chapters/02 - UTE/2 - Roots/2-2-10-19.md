The rain hadn't let up when we climbed the next day. Mud clung to our soles. I carried the memory of the scholar through each wet stretch of path, unable to leave it at last night's fire.

Wei glanced back at me every few steps. After months of traveling together, he'd learned how I held myself. He could tell when my attention wandered without using any qi at all. I found his accuracy impressive. I also wished he would stop looking.

He was quiet for a while.

"Your pace is different today."

I didn't respond.

"Slower. And you keep looking south."

"I'm not looking south."

"You're looking at something that's south of where we are. Same thing."

He waited after that, giving me time to answer if I chose. He had learned which silences to leave alone. Usually he dealt with them by staying nearby, quiet enough for me to pretend he hadn't noticed.

He moved farther ahead on the path. He still glanced back to check that I was following.

I hated it.

He was learning me in small, imperfect pieces. One day he would carry all of them into a grave. The space he had left between us was there for my sake, and I knew it.

He might have sixty years, seventy, or longer if cultivation extended his life. Eventually he would die. He would take what he knew of my silences, the small signs of my discomfort, and the tilt of my head when a memory caught me. Someone would have to learn it all again.

You know some of those signs now too. I remember rain running into the open hand at my side.

Every time I let someone close, I accepted that end. They would know me for a while. After they died, there would be nobody to notice those small things until I allowed another person to begin.

"Do you want to talk about it?"

"No."

"Okay." A pause. "But you should know that your right hand has been clenched for a watch."

I looked down. He was right. I opened it. The nail marks were white crescents against the palm.

"Noted."

"Just saying."

He faced forward again, stepping around a muddy patch. He had said what he wanted me to hear and left it there.

He was changing as we traveled. The stronger channels were easy to see, but there was this patience too. I had given him so many evasions. He was learning to work around them.

The scholar had changed while knowing me too. I studied Wei's narrow back, trying to hold on to the differences between them. Wei still asked questions he expected me to answer. So far, he hadn't held his questions or his impatience at a respectful distance.

We walked through the rain. I kept my right hand open.
