On our last night on the mountain, we camped where the trees thinned around a small natural clearing. Roots edged the open ground. Wei unrolled his bed under the open sky.

He lay with his eyes closed, too tired to move but slow to settle. His breathing was even. His core still vibrated.

The irregular pulse that began with his outburst had not gone away.

Forty-seven parts in a hundred.

Anything else would do. For three days I had reached for something else and ended up at that number.

I sat cross-legged three paces away, close enough to intervene. I could still pretend I had chosen the spot for another reason.

His core pulsed once, then again. An extra beat interrupted the rhythm after each circulation.

When he drifted into sleep, I stood and walked over. Exhaustion held him too deeply to wake. His channels kept circulating qi while he slept, with nobody directing the flow. Pressure built in them.

I knelt and placed my hand on his forehead. The skin was warm. The heat of a body generating more energy than it consumed.

His face was still round despite the cold rations and the climb. In sleep his mouth relaxed. With my hand on his forehead, he was the boy who used to follow me through the village.

His qi met my palm.

A gentle pulse pressed against my hand. It strengthened where our qi touched, flowing toward my palm as readily as water downhill. I held still as the flow increased.

I let it. Three heartbeats.

In those three heartbeats, his core stabilized. Its vibration smoothed into a regular rhythm. I waited through the next pulse; the extra beat was gone.

I pulled my hand back.

The steadier rhythm held after I broke contact. I couldn't tell how long it would last.

I stepped back. His core glowed faintly through skin and fabric, warm as late-afternoon light.

Deadly.

I stared at that faint glow and finally admitted what I had set aside for three days.

I drew my hand into my sleeve. For three heartbeats, contact with my qi had steadied his channels. The warmth still lay on my palm.

Darkness deepened until only the stars shone above the faint glow of the boy beside me.

Wei stirred, waking just enough to turn his face toward me.

"Yun?"

"I'm here."

"...warm."

He slept again. His core glowed softly. His breathing settled.

I sat until dawn.

In the morning we descended into the foothills. I listened for the irregular beat to return.
