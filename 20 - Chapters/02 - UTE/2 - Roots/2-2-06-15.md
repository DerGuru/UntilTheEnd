Wei was still at my side on the forest path when I took hold of him. I compressed the distance ahead into a single motion. On its other side was another mountain ridge.

One step.

The world blurred. Wei blinked at the shorter trees around us. We had left the forest path, the clearing, and the three scouts thirty li behind. Up here the air was colder. A heartbeat earlier, his feet had been on ground far below this ridge.

Wei buckled.

He braced his hands on his knees, swallowing hard. His face went pale, then green. The rapid change would almost have been interesting in someone else. He worked his jaw sideways against the pressure in his ears. He clapped his hands over them, swallowing again. The small tremor he'd carried since the village spread through his whole body.

He breathed hard, too fast to hold a count. The sudden displacement had disrupted his circulation. Qi surged unevenly through his channels like water in a shaken bottle. Each attempt to steady it sent another tremor through him.

"How—"

"Don't ask."

"How did—"

"Don't. Ask."

His mouth opened, then closed. Gradually his breath found the third-step pattern we'd practiced so often. His color returned in stages, from green through gray to something approaching human. His ears popped. He swallowed, winced, and swallowed again.

"...okay."

He said it quietly from his crouch, his face still the color of old paper. His hands gripped his knees to stop them shaking. I had heard that word often enough from him. This time I wished he'd argued.

The ridge belonged to a different range. Its rock was older; short, hardy trees grew in the exposed soil. Below us lay an unfamiliar valley. The scouts' signatures had vanished from my awareness. At thirty li, they were beyond sensing us too.

Our trail ended thirty li away. The scouts would have to regroup there, trying to explain how their target had disappeared between one step and the next. Their jade tablet could scarcely prepare them for that.

The step had cost me nothing, no more than a breath. I stood steady on the ridge while Wei waited out the shaking.

That ease was the problem. I could carry him across thirty li without pausing to consider what the journey would do to him. He was crouched at my feet, still struggling to recover.

Wei sat on the ridge and looked down into the valley. His circulation had settled. His hands were steady now.

He was adapting to the altitude, the cold, and a displacement that should have ruptured his channels. Thirty li in one step, with me treating it as an ordinary part of the journey. He sat there, breathing evenly.

You would have walked those thirty li, feeling every one of them. Watching Wei on the cold stone, I barely considered that sort of effort. But I'm getting ahead of myself.

"You could have done that anytime?"

"Yes."

"You could have done that when the scouts appeared?"

"Yes."

"You waited."

I held his gaze.

"I wanted to see the tablet."

He considered that. "What did you see?"

"They are organized. They have resources. They have information. Someone gathers their reports and compares them. Someone decides how to act on them. Then someone checks the results." I turned toward the ridge we'd left. "Strength is only part of it. They're methodical."

He nodded slowly. He glanced in the same direction, toward the scouts we could no longer see. Three men carrying orders from people far past that ridge.

"That's worse than strong enemies."

"Yes."

He stood. His knees held. He looked north and started walking.

I followed.

Later, I pieced together what happened behind us from intercepted records. The three scouts regrouped in an empty clearing thirty li away. They sent a sealed qi-message reporting that the target had vanished. It reached a sect hall in a city Wei had never heard of. A functionary read the message and added it to the records.

The record grew while we walked north over the cold stone.
