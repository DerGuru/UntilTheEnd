We stopped in a forest clearing where a gap in the canopy let sunlight onto level earth. I had chosen it for a meal. The people who reached it while we ate had likely made a more careful choice.

Wei's rice had improved. The edges still burned, but the center was cooked now. The herbs had progressed from medicinal to almost flavoring. He sat cross-legged on a fallen log, his bowl balanced on one knee. He used his chopsticks carefully despite his hunger.

Three figures stepped from the trees.

They came forward in a practiced triangle, their leader a half step ahead of the other two. They had watched us from the treeline before choosing this moment. Their steps matched.

The leader was thin, with a narrow face above narrow shoulders. His plain robes hung neatly from them. His Qi Gathering signature was controlled, ordinary for his level. His hands were long-fingered and steady, occupied with a jade tablet. He presented it with a magistrate's gravity.

They knelt.

All three lowered a knee, pressed a fist to the ground, and bowed their heads. They completed the movement together, without looking at one another.

"Honored Elder. The Jade Path Sect requests an audience."

Wei stopped chewing. Rice hung from his chopstick. He stared.

Three bowed heads, and we hadn't even finished lunch.

The leader raised the tablet. Needle was the name that came to me. His thin face suited it, and so did his precise voice. Every movement was correct enough to be irritating.

I read it.

The inscription was meticulous. A steady hand had carved information nobody should have been able to compile. It listed my height, my build, and the approximate age I appeared to be. It noted the absence of a readable qi-signature. My last confirmed location was Dong valley, three months earlier.

And at the bottom, in smaller characters:

*Documented action: dissolution of a Core Formation Tribulation through mental exertion alone.*

They'd made a record of me. Each small character represented someone watching, asking questions, or comparing reports. The stone held the results for whoever would decide what to do next.

The tablet was worse than an army. An army meant someone had decided. A catalog meant someone was still deciding.

Wei swallowed his rice. He looked from the tablet to the three kneeling figures, then to me.

"Why are you kneeling?"

Silence. Needle's mouth tightened. He looked up at the thirteen-year-old with rice on his chin, then back at me. Whatever response he'd prepared, this had disrupted it.

The corner of my mouth twitched. I held it still. Centuries of practice should have made that easier, but Wei kept finding fresh ways to test it.

The flankers exchanged glances. Needle recovered.

"Honored Elder, please—"

I stood up. Wei stood up. His rice bowl, abandoned on the log, tilted and dripped broth onto the bark.

I turned and walked toward the trees.

"Honored Elder, the Jade Path Sect means no—"

I was already between the trunks, with Wei behind me. Needle called after us. His voice grew fainter as we walked, less composed with each unanswered request.

We walked. Running would have given the encounter more urgency than it deserved.
