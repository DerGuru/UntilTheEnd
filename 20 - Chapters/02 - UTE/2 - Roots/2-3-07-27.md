I spent the morning returning displaced earth to the crater. By noon, the clearing lay level again. I reset the uprooted pine; it would die anyway, but an upright tree was less conspicuous than a fallen one. Then I smoothed away the qi-residue.

The work was as easy as sweeping a floor. It was also tedious. Each of his outbursts left more to clean up.

Wei woke stiff. He sat up, winced, and began examining his injuries. He was becoming practiced at this part of the morning.

Bruises marked his forearms. Skin had torn from his palms where the qi vented. A blue-black mark spread across his ribcage, where the force of his own discharge had struck him.

None of it was serious. All of it was there.

"Let me see."

He held out his hands obediently, watching my face for the correction he expected.

I held his wrists. The channels beneath them were raw and swollen. He'd worked them too hard to recover easily. His palms were warm with the qi moving through the damage to repair it.

I fed a thread of qi into his channels, just enough to quicken the healing without disrupting it. They took it in immediately.

"Those are just—"

"Hold still."

He held still while I worked for a few dozen breaths. The bruises remained, but the channels sealed. I checked the tiny tears one by one until they had all mended.

I let go of his wrists.

"Starting today. Stabilization exercises."

He looked at me.

"Qi-steps in sand. Slow circulation. Breathing counts extended to six and six."

"In sand? You heard the cultivator on the road. I am at Foundation Establishment middle phase—"

"Slow."

"But I CAN do more—"

"You bleed."

The skin of his torn palms was fresh and pink. He turned one hand over, then the other, tracing the bruises with his eyes.

"...okay."

His mouth tightened. He could do extraordinary things now; he'd proved that in front of me. I kept telling him to slow down. He flexed his newly healed hands as if testing which evidence to trust.

I understood his frustration. To answer it, I would have to explain the irregular flicker in his core. The vibration was worsening. I hadn't identified its cause, though a suspicion was taking shape. I kept my eyes on his hands instead of testing it.

"Sand," I said. "Tomorrow."

He nodded without enthusiasm. At least he had agreed to the exercise. Tomorrow I would find out whether he meant it.

For now, he studied his palms.
