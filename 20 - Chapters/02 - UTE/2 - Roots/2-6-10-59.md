Before noon, Xu Ran packed to leave. If that was the word for three items and the absence of a fourth. A bedroll repaired more often than replaced. A waterskin. A small pouch that clinked with core fragments, the currency of the broken, accepted at a fraction of their value.

Wei watched from the window. I watched from the doorway.

"The road north forks at the river," I said. Merely information, I told myself.

"I know." Xu Ran adjusted his pack. "I came from the north."

"And you're going back?"

"I'm going forward. The direction is incidental."

His shoulders were different. Three days ago they had carried the forward lean of someone still searching. Now they were level. Whatever had driven him across months of wrong roads and the wreckage of his own core was still unanswered. But he'd stood in front of the thing he'd come looking for. It hadn't welcomed him, and he was still standing.

He turned at the gate. He looked at me, then past me, at the window where Wei's face was half-visible behind a shutter, cover that only works if the observer cooperates.

He held my gaze. Three heartbeats. Four.

"You never told me your name," he said. "Your real one."

"No."

"I'll get it. Eventually."

He walked down the path, past the gate, and through the first stand of trees. His back was straight, straighter than a broken core justified. He had lost everything except the angle at which he faced the world.

Then he was out of sight. I tracked him further through qi: a dot of movement, a pulse of energy, until I decided to leave him be.

I stood in the courtyard. Birds darted at one another along the fence. Wind shifted across the yard.

And then the world changed.

The air became heavier without moving. The birdsong stopped. Every call cut off in the same instant.

I turned.

He was there.

He stood at the edge of the courtyard where the stable met the fence. A moment ago, I'd looked straight past that space. Now I understood how carefully he'd kept himself from being noticed.

Old. Old as rivers, moving on the surface, still in the depths. His face was lines and patience. His robe was simple. Gray or brown or the color between that doesn't have a name.

His qi.

He aimed it at me. A lantern can light a room or a face. He was lighting mine. Only mine. The grass stood upright. The water in the trough lay still. Nothing in the courtyard changed except the space between him and me.

I have felt qi for longer than the concept has had a name. I have felt the qi of emperors and ascendants, of beings who shook mountains or chose to leave them standing.

This was ocean, down to the lightless depths. The weight of water that had been water longer than continents had been solid. He held all that pressure on a line no wider than my shoulders.

Immortal. At least. Probably beyond, into territory where cultivation stages became names too small for what they described.

He raised two fingers in greeting. The old way: equals meeting on a road, acknowledging each other's existence with minimal formality. A greeting that hadn't been used in centuries.

He smiled with an easy warmth, as though he knew me already.

I knew his shape, his pattern, though I couldn't place a memory beside it. A melody heard in a different key, centuries apart. The same melody.

He opened his mouth. The beginning of a question that had been composed over years, or decades, or a span that makes decades irrelevant.

"Not now," I said.

He closed his mouth, saving the question for later. His smile deepened. He had received exactly the answer he'd expected and found it sufficient.

He lowered his hand, nodded once, and turned. He was gone before I saw him take a step.

The birds resumed. The wind continued. The courtyard was a courtyard again.

I stood. My hands were still, and the stillness was a decision.

"That was—" I stopped.

I left the thought unfinished. Any name I gave what I'd felt would have reduced it to something far smaller.

I went inside. Wei was at the table, drawing something in the dust with his finger.

"You were standing outside a long time," he said.

"Watching the road."

"See anything?"

"No," I said.

Another lie. Added to the ledger.

"Let's go," I said.

We went.
