We had put a good stretch of forest between us and the clearing when Wei asked about the tablet. I kept us at a brisk pace he could sustain. Overhead, the canopy had thickened again. Broken patches of sunlight warmed the shaded ground.

"They had your description."

"Yes."

"On jade. Not paper. Jade lasts."

"Yes."

"Someone spent months collecting information about you. Maybe years."

I let him continue. He was putting what he'd seen together, trying each observation before reaching a conclusion.

A branch cracked overhead. We both looked up. Wind, weight, gravity. Nothing. We walked on.

"They'll send more."

"Yes."

"After these three report."

"Yes."

For a while, he walked in silence. He held the third-step breathing pattern, timing each exhale to his footfall.

"The tablet said 'dissolution of a Core Formation Tribulation through mental exertion alone.'"

I missed a step.

"You read the tablet."

"I was sitting three paces away."

"You read classical Jade Path script at three paces."

He paused. "I... yes. Is that unusual?"

It was. Jade Path script used compact cultivator notation. Its small characters carried technical meanings in archaic grammar. A literate Foundation Establishment cultivator would need to hold that tablet to read it. A thirteen-year-old village boy with four months of training should not have been able to read it at all.

A stone turned under my foot. I corrected without looking.

His channels had changed. Now his perception was changing too, allowing him to see small details at a distance. But he'd also understood the words. These changes were coming all at once, without time for him to adjust between them.

"It's fine," I said.

He nodded, though he watched me for another few steps. He had begun returning to answers like that later, when I'd hoped the subject was settled.

We walked deeper into the forest. The scouts' signatures dimmed behind us, spreading apart as the gap grew too wide for them to maintain their formation.

They would regroup and report. Someone would add our direction of travel to the tablet. They would record my companion too, a boy with qi-signatures that didn't match his apparent age.

Somewhere ahead, a stream ran thin and distant through rock.

I'd been invisible for millennia. That was over.
