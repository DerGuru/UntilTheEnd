For three days, we walked under tall conifers along animal trails. The ground was too steep for anyone to profit from logging it, too remote to haul the wood away. Dense canopy shaded the clean streams.

Wei practiced the slow pattern I'd given him, the one built around qi-steps in sand. He spoke little during the exercises. Every correction brought a tight movement of his jaw.

He drew faint qi-lines in the frozen ground, holding back most of his strength. His core hummed under the restraint. His channels ran full, and he released just enough qi to trace each pattern.

"Again."

He redrew the pattern more slowly. Excess qi spilled into the left line, making it wobble.

"The left line drifts."

"I know."

"Then fix it."

"I'm *trying*."

He was. His core supplied more qi as he concentrated harder. The increased flow made his control worse. His effort was feeding the problem.

"Stop."

He stopped with his shoulders rigid, his jaw set.

"Eat."

"I'm not hungry."

"Eat anyway."

He ate cold rice and dried meat. He took a smaller portion again. We had enough for more, but he left it in the pack.

We spent the afternoon walking northeast, deeper into country absent from every map I knew. I'd seen most of them. The forest thickened around older trees. Little light reached the ground under those broad crowns.

Wei walked ahead, choosing the path with growing confidence. I let him. His choices were safe enough. He needed to practice making them without waiting for a word from me.

He turned. "How far to the next town?"

"Two days. Maybe three."

"We need salt."

"I know."

"And needle and thread. My pack's coming apart."

"I know."

He turned back toward the path. He had been keeping track of our supplies, finding things he could decide for himself. I was content to let him tell me about the salt.

Later, I sat watch. The scouts were behind us; nothing nearby could threaten me. Still, I stared out through the trees. If I lay down, I would have only the number to think about.

Forty-seven.

It was as precise as when it first came. Memories usually blurred. This one held fixed, certain as a measurement.

Wei shifted in his sleep. His core gave one hard pulse, stronger than the small movement of his shoulders suggested.

Stars showed between the branches. You always found more comfort in them than I did.

At first light, Wei woke and asked, "Where next?" I told him northeast. He began packing without asking why.
