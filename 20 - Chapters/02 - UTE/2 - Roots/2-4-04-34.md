Three days later, we walked through low, forested hills. The gentle path needed little attention. Wei's voice filled the quiet between the trees.

He talked about the road, the fish he would catch at the next river, and a merchant who'd tried to sell him glass as jade. This time the fish would be a real one, he assured me, large enough to eat. Then he showed me a stone shaped like a cat. He understood my silences well enough. He talked anyway.

"—and the cat-stone has these little lines that look like whiskers, see—"

I barely looked at his stone. My qi flowed toward him as he walked. Watching that stream, I remembered a garden in the southwest, at a sect whose name I'd stopped saying. Old cherry trees grew there, planted by people who had considered beauty part of their cultivation.

A girl of seventeen had meditated under the largest tree. Her qi had been pure, still unshaped by any particular path.

I'd stayed one day, then three, then seven. Soon I returned regularly. I had told myself the garden was pleasant. Resting under those trees had been easy. I had also liked watching her practice.

She had advanced from Foundation to Core Formation in six months, passing through its phases in another year. Each breakthrough followed close behind the last. Her sectmates had called her a genius. Her elders had called her blessed. With cherry blossoms on my shoulders, I had recognized the pace.

I'd seen it in others scattered across the centuries. Someone near me would grow too fast. Observers would praise their talent, certain that great things lay ahead. I knew how often that promise had ended badly.

Gravel shifted under my foot as the trail bent. Wei held his cat-shaped stone out toward me.

At the time, I had put the girl's progress down to her own potential. I had thought my presence in that garden incidental.

The sect had named her the Jade Phoenix. Elders had knelt before her.

Wei was still explaining the little lines on his stone. I heard him, though fallen blossoms were clearer to me than the object in his hand.

In three years near me, she had made thirty years of ordinary progress.

In the fourth spring, when the cherries bloomed again, she had reached for the last breakthrough. Her core was fuller than her body could sustain.

Her core had broken with a quiet sigh. She sank to her knees beneath the cherry tree. Pink blossoms settled on her white clothes.

I'd been three steps away. I reached out, sending qi through the space where her core had been.

She had survived with her cultivation gone. All that strength, all those years spent learning to use it, had ended beneath the tree.

Now my strength flowed into Wei. I had given her more than she could carry too.

"—Yun? Yun. You stopped." Wei's voice cut through.

I had stopped in the middle of the trail. Wei had lowered the stone to look at me.

"I'm fine."

"You were—your eyes went weird."

"I'm fine, Wei."

He watched me a moment longer, plainly unconvinced. Then he put the stone away.

I started walking again. He followed. I tried to let the image of pink blossoms on white cloth fade.

The stream flowed on.
