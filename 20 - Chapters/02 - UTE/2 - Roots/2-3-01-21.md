I climbed above Wei on a narrow mountain trail, looking for the next foothold through the mist. Three qi-signatures had appeared at our previous campsite yesterday morning. A Qi Gathering searcher could track us indefinitely on flat ground. Climbing would drain their energy. Every pause they took would give us more time.

Path was a charitable word for this strip of exposed stone and twisted roots. Game animals had worn it into the cliff face. Its angle made each step awkward. Above the treeline, mist hid the holds until I was close enough to reach them.

Below me, Wei found holds among roots, ledges, and cracks in the stone. Six months earlier, he wouldn't have attempted this slope. After only a few dozen days of travel, he moved as easily as if he had climbed for years.

Qi did most of the work. It flowed into his arms when he pulled, then into his legs as he pushed upward. Even an experienced cultivator would have needed practice to distribute it so precisely. He was thirteen. The raw channels left by the flood were smoothing rapidly. In the past seven days alone, they had passed through three degrees of refinement.

It was going too fast.

He stopped on a ledge with his hands on his knees. Sweat covered his face despite the cold. He had pushed too hard. At least his cough was nearly gone after three days under a roof. Warmth had helped where regulating his qi alone had failed. Only a faint rasp remained when he drew a deep breath.

The air around his hands shimmered.

I saw it from four paces above him. His channels were full; excess qi vented through his skin, wavering faintly like heat over summer stone. A passing observer might have mistaken it for mist.

He flexed his fingers. The shimmer intensified. He relaxed and it faded.

Then his knees buckled. He dropped heavily onto the ledge, too exhausted to lower himself carefully.

"Why am I so tired? I was just breathing."

I climbed down to his ledge and sat next to him.

"Stop practicing while you climb."

"I wasn't—" He stopped, looking down at his hands. He'd run a full qi-circulation throughout the climb. Twenty-one days earlier, he couldn't have managed both tasks together. Now he couldn't stop.

"I wasn't doing it on purpose."

Qi flowed on through the paths we'd opened in him, even when he ceased directing it. Each breath drew in more.

"I know."

He rubbed his hands together to hide their tremor. He watched them as he worked, searching for where the movement began.

"Does it stop?"

"Yes."

"When?"

"When you learn to control it."

"And if I don't?"

I turned and started climbing again. Below me, Wei followed, rubbing warmth into hands that weren't cold.
