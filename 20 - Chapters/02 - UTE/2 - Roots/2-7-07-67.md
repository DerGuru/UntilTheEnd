In the morning, Wei woke and stretched next to the cold fire. He made a sound somewhere between a yawn and an assessment of how much of him still worked. Thirteen years of growth under millennia of cultivation spillover. He didn't know what I'd seen in the night.

"Sleep well?" I asked.

"Better than lately. No dreams."

No dreams. No awareness of the light, the hum, and the long shadowless moments.

"Good," I said.

He ate cold rice from yesterday, with dried herbs and mountain water. He ate quickly, fueling himself for the day.

Halfway through, he pushed the tin toward me without looking up or speaking. Just the rice, crossing the gap between us.

"Your hands," I said, declining the rice with a shake of my head.

He held them out. The tremor affected both hands, small but consistent on the right. On the left it came intermittently, now even without stress. This was his new baseline, and it showed no sign of easing.

"Same as yesterday," he said.

"Worse than yesterday."

He shrugged, accepting the correction.

He trained. His compression exercises were improving, with consistent five-breath holds now. His qi-suppression was clean. His signature virtually disappeared during the holds.

But the rebounds. After each compression, the release produced a qi-flare larger than the previous day's. The signal was growing. The compression was pressing on a spring. The spring was getting stronger.

"Enough," I said.

"I can do more."

"That's the problem."

He stared at me, a boy just told that his competence was a liability.

"Rest," I said.

He rested on a rock and drank water. Then he pressed his trembling hands between his knees.

"Yun."

"What?"

"Xu Ran. His tremor. Was it like this?"

"Similar."

"How similar?"

"Same mechanism. Different stage."

"What stage am I?"

Silence.

"Earlier," I said. "Earlier than he was."

"How much earlier?"

"Early enough."

He heard the non-answer. His mouth tightened, then relaxed. He let the question join the others.

He stood, shouldered his pack, and started walking.

The mountain slopes stretched empty ahead of us, a welcome of sorts. Stone weathered slowly under the open sky. Erosion continued at its own pace.

I envied the mountains.

I suspected I always would.
