Bear was gone. Wei lay on his back where I'd moved him.

I worked with my hands on his chest. Qi flowed in the small, controlled amount that healing required. The bone responded, knitting under my palms.

My fingers trembled.

The closer my palms pressed to his core, the harder they shook. Warmth pulsed against them. My hands on his chest put my qi in direct contact with his core, triggering the stream. It accelerated.

So I trembled and healed.

"Does it hurt?" he asked. Eyes closed. The question was about me, not him.

"No."

"Your hands are shaking."

"Qi interaction. Normal."

The ribs set. The bruising resolved. The cut closed. His young body healed quickly, accepting the energy from me as readily as it accepted every other gift I hadn't meant to give.

I withdrew my hands. The tremor stopped.

Wei opened his eyes and looked at the fire.

"Yun."

"Hm."

He was quiet for a long time. The fire moved. The night pressed. Insects. Wind. Somewhere below, water over stone.

Wei sat up. He tested the ribs with one hand, then dropped it and pulled his knees to his chest.

"I'm strong," he said, sorting evidence. "What I did back there. That wasn't normal."

"No."

"So I'm strong."

"You're strong," I said. "But unstable. The two are connected."

He turned to me. Firelight slid across his face.

"Where does it come from?"

Three heartbeats.

The answer existed in my mouth, behind my teeth. I am the reason. My proximity changes you. My qi feeds yours. If I stay, you get stronger AND more unstable. If I go, you stabilize, and you're defenseless.

Three heartbeats ended.

"You're pushing too much qi through channels that aren't mature yet," I said. "The advancement is outpacing the infrastructure. Train slower."

Not completely a lie, but not the whole truth either.

The fire popped. A log shifted, sending sparks upward.

Wei held my eyes for four heartbeats. His jaw worked once, as it did when he weighed something he couldn't name.

"Okay," he said. "Slower."

Silence. The fire. The insects. Beyond the glow, the forest rustled just as it had before Bear came.

"Yun."

"Hm."

"How strong was he?"

I stared into the fire. The question deserved a real answer. The problem was that the real answer required vocabulary I'd never needed for myself.

"The sects have a hierarchy," I said. "Stages. Labels. They invented them, oh, eight hundred years ago? Maybe a thousand. I didn't pay much attention. Before that, people just cultivated and didn't name the rungs."

I picked up a stick and drew a line in the dirt.

"You're at Foundation Establishment. That's the second stage. Above that, Core Formation. That's where Xu Ran got to with his tribulation, before his core cracked. Then Nascent Soul, then Spirit Severing and after that Dao-Seeking." I tapped the top of the line. "That's where Bear is."

Wei counted. His lips moved.

"Four stages above me."

"Four stages."

"And I hit him."

"You hit him."

"And he *moved*." The words came slowly. He was handling the fact carefully, trying to fit it among the things he knew. "I pushed back a cultivator four stages above me."

I said nothing. There was nothing to say that wouldn't lead to the question underneath the question.

Wei stared at his fist, opened it, and closed it. The hand that had connected with the sternum of something that should have been untouchable.

"Where are you on that line?"

I studied the stick, the line in the dirt, the tidy progression the sects had built to organize something that had never been tidy.

He waited.

"Honest answer? I don't know exactly." I picked up a stone, small and round, polished by a river into anonymity. "The line stops. After Dao-Seeking, the sects say Immortal. After that—"

I weighed the stone and threw it into the dark. Past the firelight, where neither of us could see it land.

"Somewhere around there," I said.

Wei stared into the dark where the stone had gone, at the distance between the line in the dirt and wherever it had landed. A gap past the labels, the rungs, and every hierarchy anyone had built to make it comprehensible.

"You made him kneel. With a breath."

I said nothing.

"One breath and a Dao-Seeking cultivator was on his knees." Wei's voice was flat, a boy arranging facts into a shape. "So why have we been running?"

The question was older than Bear. It had been sitting in him since the first time I'd changed course to avoid something he could barely detect.

"Every time I act," I said, "people notice."

He waited for more. I gave it.

"The breath wasn't invisible. Every cultivator within fifty li felt it. Some of them are already calculating where it came from. Some are already moving."

"So we run because you're too strong."

"We run because strength has a sound. And sects have ears."

He chewed on that. The wood crackled in the fire. An ember detached, rose among the stars, and died.

"What if they find us anyway?"

"Then I breathe again. And more ears hear. And more feet follow. The display that solves today gets us followed tomorrow."

"So it never ends."

"It ends when they lose interest."

"Will they?"

"Eventually."

Another lie. Smaller this time.

Wei pulled the blanket higher. His voice came muffled.

"That man. He looked at me like I was... a thing. A specimen. Something to collect."

"Yes."

"Is that what the sects want? Me?"

Three heartbeats. Again.

"You're unusual," I said. Careful. Measured. "Your progression is faster than what most cultivators achieve. That draws attention. The sects notice talent. They collect it. Or they remove it, if they can't collect it."

True. All of it true. Except for the reason.

"So they want me because I'm strong."

"Yes."

"And I'm strong because—" He paused. The gap where the real question lived. The one I'd already answered with half-truths, channel theory, and the word *slower*.

"Because you are," I said.

He was quiet for a long time.

"Okay."

He turned toward the fire and drew his knees up. The sleep-position of a boy who had learned that the world contained people who looked at children and saw resources.

I sat in the dark with both lies. The small one dissolving, the large one settling.

Something locked behind my sternum. A bolt dropping into place, solid and final.

I shook my head. Once. Hard.

The bolt didn't move.
