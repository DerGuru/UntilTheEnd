That evening we camped in a sheltered hollow between two hills. Wind passed above us. Wei unpacked. The girl under the cherry tree was still with me.

He built the fire efficiently, working with flint as his father had taught him. He placed the tinder, struck sparks, and sheltered the small flame with his hands. He carried that much of the village into every camp.

Fire caught. Light spread through the hollow.

I sat across from him. My qi crossed the space between us.

"Yun."

"What."

"What happened earlier? On the trail."

I looked at the fire.

"Nothing."

"Your eyes looked like you were somewhere else. For a while."

"I was thinking."

"About what?"

He waited after the question, watching the fire instead of my face. He had learned to leave me room to answer.

"Old things."

He nodded and turned back to the fire. For a while he poked it with a stick, leaving my answer alone.

"You know," he said, "when I was little, my father used to—he'd get this look. When he was thinking about before-the-village. Before my mother. I don't know what happened to him before. He never said. But he'd get this look and it was the same one you had. Exactly."

He paused.

"I used to think it meant he was sad. But it wasn't sad. It was... heavy. Like he was carrying something that didn't weigh anything but still took up all his strength."

I said nothing. The accuracy didn't require acknowledgment.

"I never asked him what it was," Wei said. "I figured if he wanted to tell me, he would. And if he didn't, then asking would just make him carry it louder."

He met my eyes.

"I'm not asking."

"I know."

The fire burned. The night pressed in.

I'm the reason you are breaking.

Six words. I drew breath to say them.

"Want some rice?"

Wei held out a steaming bowl.

I took the bowl.

"Thank you."

He nodded and ate his own. I put a mouthful of rice between myself and the words I hadn't said. Across the fire, my qi flowed toward him.

The rice was warm. The night was cold.

I looked up at the stars over the hollow. They gave me somewhere to look while I swallowed.

You'd have asked. You always do.
