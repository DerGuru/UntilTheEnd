Across the inn, he bent over his bowl. He didn't recognize me. Not yet.

My appearance was unremarkable by design, the result of thousands of years spent learning to look like nothing. Normal was the most expensive disguise.

But his broken core had sharpened certain faculties. The crack in his cultivation left him open to things his intact cultivation would have screened out. Like a filter with a hole.

He would feel me eventually. My qi-signature registered as absence. He would feel the hole, the place where a qi-field should be. Presence could be measured. The absence would leave him searching for something wrong.

For now he ate, mechanically, until the bowl was empty. Then he stared at the wall.

Wei watched him, piecing a picture together from fragments.

"That's him," Wei said. Quiet. Under the noise. "The genius with the broken core. The one the merchants talked about."

I said nothing. The confirmation was in the nothing.

"He pays in pieces of himself. Core fragments." Wei paused. "That's what he left on the counter."

He studied the hollow face across the room.

"How old is he? Eighteen? Nineteen?" He answered himself. "He looks older."

"He would."

Wei turned his bowl. Noodle remnants circled the ceramic as he turned it again, looking for something useful to do.

"Is he dangerous?"

"Not to us."

"To whom?"

I looked at the boy across the room. The hollow face. The steady hands, steadier than Wei's. He was past struggling.

"Himself."

Wei looked at Xu Ran, then at his own hands, shaking under the table. Between them lay five years and a core-state that might be his future.

If he saw a mirror, he didn't look away.

The inn closed gradually. Pig Nose wiped the counter. The bard passed out. Patrons filtered to their rooms.

Xu Ran lingered over the empty bowl, his back to the wall.

I walked past him on the way to the stairs, close enough for him to sense the hole where my qi-field should be.

His qi faltered. He went still over his bowl.

Soon.

I climbed the stairs. Wei followed. Xu Ran sat on.
