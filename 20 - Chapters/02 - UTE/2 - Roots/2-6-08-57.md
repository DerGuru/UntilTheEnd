Wei sat on a wall outside the inn before sunrise, his legs dangling, his hands in his lap under his robe. He'd been awake for at least half a watch. I had expected him to wait there.

Xu Ran came through the door, its worn hinges creaking.

He stopped at the sight of Wei. Wei looked straight at him.

Four paces. Mud, straw, and the detritus of an establishment whose keeper prioritized function over aesthetics. He achieved neither.

I stood in the doorframe behind Wei, visible to both of them, waiting for one to speak.

"He tried to kill us," Wei said.

The sentence was directed at me but spoken into the space between him and Xu Ran.

"He tried to ascend," I said. "The village was just close by."

"Isn't that the same thing?"

"It isn't."

Wei turned back to Xu Ran. "You brought the sky down on our village. People got hurt."

Xu Ran stood in the doorway. Hands at his sides.

"I know."

"You know." Wei repeated the words slowly, testing them.

"I didn't—"

"Know people would get hurt? You triggered a tribulation. Above a village."

"I didn't know the village was there."

"Does that matter?"

Silence.

"No," Xu Ran said. "It doesn't matter."

Wei gave a small nod. The answer was honest enough to accept, too little to forgive.

They sat, Wei on the wall, Xu Ran on the step. Two paces apart.

"Your qi," Xu Ran said. Quiet. "It's—"

"I know what it is."

"Do you know what it means?"

Wei's jaw. The muscle. "I have a theory."

"What theory?"

"That I'm growing too fast for my body."

I heard it. The lie I'd given him, repeated back as the foundation of his understanding. The foundation was sand.

Xu Ran looked at Wei's hands. At the robe covering them. At the fabric, trembling in a steady rhythm.

"I had that," he said. "The tremor. Before."

"Before what?"

"Before the core cracked."

Wei's face went still.

He drew his hands out of his robe and laid them on the wall, in the sunlight. They trembled.

"Still here," he said. Looking at his hands. "Still working."

He stood and walked inside, passing me without a word.

Xu Ran and I were alone.

"He's yours?" Xu Ran asked.

"He followed me."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the only one I have."

"What does it feel like?" I asked. "When it breaks."

The question was genuine. I hadn't asked one in a long time.

Xu Ran looked at the courtyard. At the mud, the straw, and the door Wei had walked through. He said nothing. He stood inside the answer, unable to find words that fit around it.

I recognized the shape.

He nodded, stood, and went inside.
