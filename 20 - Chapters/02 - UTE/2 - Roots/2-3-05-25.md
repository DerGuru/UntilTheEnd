Half a watch later, the trail widened around a tree. A young man lay half-propped against its trunk, clear of the path. Sweat darkened his robes. His hands shook above his lap.

He wore different robes from the cultivator we'd passed. His build was different too, his qi barely strong enough for Qi Gathering. It stuttered through disrupted channels without settling. He'd probably had a training accident, a circulation gone wrong or a channel spasm.

His lips were cracked. He needed help.

"Please..." His voice was thin. "Water..."

Wei stopped.

He stood over the cultivator. I remembered him organizing the evacuation, telling a whole village where to go and what to carry. He had the same attentive face now, taking in the man's condition. The outstretched hand was within his reach. He had only to kneel.

Then he turned to me. He'd seen a hundred decisions of mine since leaving home: villages passed without entering, people avoided, and questions dismissed. I had told him to keep walking so often.

His eyes returned to the cultivator.

He walked past.

The cultivator reached out a shaking hand. His fingers hung in the air as Wei passed. Then his arm fell.

"Please... just water..."

We walked. The path turned. The voice outlasted the sight of him. Then that too was gone.

That evening we built our usual fire. Wei's food cooled in front of him. For a long while he stared at the piece of dried vegetable held between his chopsticks, letting it hang above the bowl.

"You think I should have stopped."

He spoke toward the fire without looking up.

"I don't think anything."

"You're always thinking."

"About this? Nothing."

He set down his chopsticks carefully, aligning them along the rim of the bowl.

"I walked past a man who was asking for water."

"Yes."

"Because you taught me to."

"Because you chose to."

His jaw tightened. He had looked at me before passing the man, but I hadn't told him to walk on. The choice was his.

"I'm not like you."

"No."

"I don't want to be."

"Good."

His hands lay steady beside his bowl.

Their stillness should have comforted me. When they shook, he had excess qi to release. Now his channels had drawn so low that he couldn't replenish them without rest.

His food went cold. The fire held.
