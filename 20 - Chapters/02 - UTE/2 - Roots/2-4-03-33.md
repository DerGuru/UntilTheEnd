I sat by the dead fire through the night. You know I usually don't sleep. This time I doubted I could have, even if I had chosen to try.

I'm breaking him.

Wei slept four paces away, his core glowing faintly through his clothes. My qi flowed toward him in a constant stream. Now that I'd seen it, I found it every time I looked. Discovering it had changed nothing about the flow.

For half a watch, I studied how it changed with his cultivation cycle. When his core pulled, the stream surged. When his channels rested, it eased. My qi matched what he could draw with a precision I hadn't known was possible. My strength fed a thirteen-year-old without any direction from me.

My hands were steady. They always were. Even when the thing behind them wasn't.

And I didn't know what my own body was doing.

I looked up at the stars. They had hung over me through millennia of nights. I traced their positions, trying to give my attention to something other than the boy. Every time he shifted in sleep, I looked down again. By morning the stars had faded. The stream still flowed.

Wei woke and sat up. He rubbed his eyes with the back of his hands, a childish gesture he hadn't outgrown yet.

"Morning."

"Morning."

"Did you sleep?"

"Yes."

A lie. I knew it. He knew it. Both of us refused to challenge it.

He ate cold rice. Since the descent into thicker air, his appetite had returned. He could keep his food down.

As he ate, my qi entered his channels. This could have been happening for months in front of me. Maybe longer.

"We should move."

"Where?"

"Into the hills. Valley's too exposed."

"More hills?"

"Different hills."

"At least not mountains."

He rolled his bedding, fastened his pack, and stood. He knew the routine well.

"Ready."

We walked three steps apart, close enough for conversation. I counted fourteen breaths to every sixty heartbeats. Each breath drew at the stream. The small pulls added to what he already carried.

Wei matched my pace. He'd learned that my pace was information. Slow meant safe. Fast meant something else.

Today I walked slow.

"You're quiet. Not the usual kind of quiet," he said.

"Thinking." He knew me too well.

"About what?"

"Our route."

He nodded. Later he would return to that answer, putting it with the other incomplete things I'd told him.

We entered the hills. The trees closed around us.

The river faded.

The stream didn't.

It pulsed softly in time with his heartbeat. I watched the flow I couldn't stop and tried to judge how long his channels could bear it.
