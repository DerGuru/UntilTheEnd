I sat by the fire while Wei settled into his bedroll. It took him longer than usual to fall asleep.

"You're staring." His eyes were half-closed, his voice thick with sleep.

"I'm thinking."

"That doesn't mean you're not staring."

"Go to sleep, Wei."

A distant owl called during the pause. The fire crackled between us.

"You'll still be here when I wake up?"

"Where would I go?"

He considered this. His breathing slowed, deepening as he settled against the bedding. Before the fire's next branch burned through, he was asleep.

Then I tried again.

I began a deep meditation, turning my attention wholly inward. I hadn't used it for decades, maybe centuries. It left the physical world unwatched. I avoided that depth whenever I had something outside myself worth protecting.

Wei slept in a clear qi-field. The scouts were at least two days behind us. I would have no better chance.

I closed my eyes and pulled inward.

I followed the surface flows down through the middle channels. Below them lay secondary meridians, then the smaller paths I'd refined across millennia. I continued down to the core from which my qi radiated.

I focused on the output. That stream of qi moving toward Wei.

A thread extended from my core through my channels, then across the air into his. It was thin as spider silk and immensely dense.

Its path was smooth with use, embedded in older layers of qi. I traced it back through them.

It had been there for months, since our first seven days at his home village. It reached back to the first night he'd slept in my field, before he'd told me his name.

I held the thread with all the control millennia of practice had given me.

I pulled.

The thread yielded slowly against deep resistance. It dragged at my muscles, my concentration, and the core beneath both. The pressure increased as I drew inward. It was like holding back a river with my bare hands.

First breath. The thread thinned. The flow to Wei's core diminished.

Second breath. I pulled harder. A deep ache spread through my channels.

Third breath. Sweat gathered on my forehead and palms. I hadn't sweated like that since before the founding emperor's great-grandfather was born. I pulled.

Fourth breath. The thread had thinned almost to nothing. Wei's core slowed with an uneven vibration.

One more breath. Just one more.

Fifth breath.

The thread snapped back.

It returned at full width in an instant, carrying more qi than before. The pressure I'd held back burst into Wei's channels.

He flinched in his sleep, his back arching once. His channels absorbed the burst. His core blazed through his clothing and blanket, lighting the four paces between us.

Then his breathing resumed. His core dimmed as the flow steadied. He slept on while I withdrew from the failed attempt.

I opened my eyes.

My hands shook in front of me. I ordered them still, and the muscles trembled on. I couldn't remember the last time they'd resisted such a simple command.

The words came in the old tongue I'd helped shape before the first sects existed.

It doesn't work.

I couldn't prevent my own qi from feeding the boy. Fractures were still forming in the channels that drew it in.

"You're going to burn," I told the sleeping boy. His chest rose and fell under the blanket. "And I can't stop it. Why can't I stop it?"

He slept through my words. The stream continued between us. Past the fire, the hollow was dark.

I put my hands in my lap and waited for the shaking to stop. It took longer than it should have.

The word autonomous came to me as his core glowed. My qi followed a direction I hadn't given it. It was as if something else had chosen its purpose.

I tried to put the word aside. His next pulse brought it back.
