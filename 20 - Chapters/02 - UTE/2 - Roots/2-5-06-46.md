We hurried north through dead pine toward the granite ridges. We would hold that pace for three days.

We walked at speed, technically, but we might as well have been running for the exhaustion it caused. I had turned us north, into terrain I hadn't wanted. The air thinned and turned metallic. Bear would report what had happened. The report would travel faster than we could.

Bear now knew three things. I existed. I was here. And I had a weakness named Wei.

The third was key. Location could be changed, existence denied. But Wei was a constant.

"We're going north," I said.

"How far?"

"As far as we can."

His jaw set. The stubbornness-muscle I'd tracked since his village. He gave me the look with the new layer in it, the one that had appeared after the lie, sitting on top of trust and under compliance.

"How are your ribs?" I asked.

"My ribs are fine," he said.

They weren't. But this lie was his.

Three days. North. Across punishing terrain. Scree, switchbacks, and a ravine that cost us a watch. Our patience thinned with every step.

I erased our trail as we went. By hand; qi left too distinct a signature. I obscured footprints, rearranged branches, and worked the evidence of our passage back into the fallen litter.

Wei's ribs were healing. The qi-assisted knitting had accelerated the process from many days to a few. His arm was slower; deep tissue bruising took time to resolve, however urgently we needed to move.

He marched without complaint. Wei didn't narrate suffering. It was there in every careful step, with days still to go before it eased. A hawk circled overhead, following something smaller than us.

On the evening of the third day, he stumbled.

He caught himself with a half-step. His face was white with exertion.

"Stop."

"I'm fine."

"Stop."

He stopped, his lips parted. Even arguing would have taken energy he didn't have.

I checked his channels. The march was draining reserves faster than his body could replenish them. His qi-pattern was thin. Stretched.

I fed a thread of stabilizing qi into his channels, carefully limiting it to the smallest amount that would sustain him without triggering the stream's compensatory surge.

His chin was set. Eyes forward. He had decided the terms included suffering and accepted them without accepting the logic.

"We walk."

"I know."

The forest and the silence went on.

"The next ones will be stronger," I said. To nobody. To the truth I owed him.

Wei nodded. He didn't answer.

His not-answering was new.
