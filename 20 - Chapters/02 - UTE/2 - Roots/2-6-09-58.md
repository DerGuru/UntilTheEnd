The inn served breakfast. Rice, pickled vegetables, and something that had once been eggs. Wei ate. I didn't. Xu Ran ate slowly, with the careful precision of someone who had been hungry recently enough to ration every mouthful.

Three people at a table. Two paces of scarred wood between them.

Wei's hands were steady. He'd found some way to suppress the tremor in the time between the courtyard and the table, while the porridge cooked. The cost showed in his rigid shoulders.

"How long since the break?" Wei asked. Conversational.

"Seven months," Xu Ran said. "Give or take."

"And before that, how long did they shake?"

"Two years."

Wei paused over his bowl. Two years of tremor, then a break. For him it had been a few dozen days.

"Did you try to stop them?"

"Everything. Meditation. Channeling exercises. A healer in Dragon Harbor who said it was spiritual misalignment and charged me three spirit stones for a diagnosis anyone with functioning observation could have provided for free."

Wei's mouth twitched.

"Did anything work?"

"Slowing down."

"What does that mean?"

Xu Ran set his chopsticks on the table, aligning them parallel. "It means doing less than you can. Deliberately. Consistently. The gap between capacity and execution is where the tremor lives. Close the gap and it stops."

"By doing less."

"By doing less."

Wei stared at him.

"What if you can't?" Wei asked, quieter.

Xu Ran met his eyes.

"Then it gets worse," he said. "And then it breaks."

Silence.

Wei leaned back and looked at the ceiling. Beams, thatch. He looked at it anyway.

"Thanks," he said.

"For what?" Xu Ran asked.

"For telling me."

Aimed at Xu Ran. Delivered to me.

Wei pushed his bowl aside. "We should leave today."

"Agreed," I said, and looked away.
