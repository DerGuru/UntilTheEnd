On the scree slope, the signature changed direction again. The detour wasn't enough.

Our pursuer had adjusted for our route change. Either he was tracking us by something other than our trail or he knew the terrain well enough to anticipate our limited options. Maybe both. Both was always the likely answer when the pursuer was high Dao-Seeking.

We climbed scree slopes with unstable footing. Wei climbed well. By now he was sure-footed on this terrain and scrambled upward with the enthusiasm of a boy who hadn't yet learned his limits.

The strain showed in his shortened stride and the fatigue settled into his shoulders.

We reached a ridge. Dense forest on the far side, old growth, canopy that blocked sunlight and qi-sensing with equal efficiency. I led us in.

He stood at the center of a clearing. Large. Broad rather than tall. A build that had started as muscle and added qi in layers. Centuries of refinement buried under more centuries.

He looked like a bear. His beard was untended. Genuinely neglected, a man with more important things to do than groom.

The air around him vibrated. Physically. The ambient qi rippled with the shimmer of air over heat.

"Honored elder." Bear's voice was soft. The softness of powerful men who have discovered that volume is unnecessary when mass is available. "The Iron Lotus Sect requests your attention."

I stopped. Wei stopped behind me. His qi spiked, tightening his channels. His hand found a rock. Thirteen years of village logic: if there's a threat, hold something heavy.

"Requests?" I said.

"An invitation. Honored. Open-ended." Bear smiled, technique rather than expression. "Our sect recognizes your cultivation. Your contributions. There is much we could learn from—"

"No."

His smile narrowed. He adjusted his approach. Cooperation had failed.

"Perhaps the elder hasn't considered—"

"I have."

Silence. The ambient qi tightened where the two fields met.

Bear studied Wei. Clinical. Evaluative.

"The boy is yours?"

I said nothing.

"Talented. Young. Unusual qi-pattern. The sect would—"

"No."

Bear's hand rose. Slowly. Deliberately, demonstrating that haste was beneath him.

Qi-pressure. Directed. At Wei.

The air between them hardened with qi, pressing against Wei's chest, compressing his lungs, and restricting his diaphragm. Wei gasped. His ribs compressed, his breathing went shallow, and his qi-field destabilized. He dropped to one knee, bracing a hand against the ground.

I felt it.

And then something else.

A pull. Behind my eyes. Something shifted deep inside me. The same force that fed Wei's core without permission now surged toward him, responding to the pressure.

The qi-pressure vanished. Total. Instantly.

Wei breathed. He gasped, drawing air with the desperate efficiency of lungs released from compression.

Bear frowned. I'd done something. He observed the result and couldn't connect cause to effect.

"What—"

Wei was standing again. I hadn't seen him rise.

I turned to him, already weary with recognition. I'd made no move to cancel the pressure. Something had done it for me. Through me. Autonomously.

But the damage was already running.

Wei's qi was still spiking. Stress, compression, and recovery. At every step, more energy poured through his channels. Always more.

His hands glowed briefly as qi spilled through channels that couldn't contain it. The ground under his feet cracked. Grass scorched around him, a pace out.

His nose bled.

A thin line. Right nostril. He wiped it away with the back of his hand, fast, a boy who wanted the evidence gone before anyone noticed.

But Bear saw it. His frown shifted. The confusion gave way to something worse: the hunter's smile. He'd found something unexpected and valuable.

"Interesting." He was watching Wei now, not me. "Unstable. But strong. Where does a boy of this age get—"

He didn't finish. Wei's qi was still flaring, fueled by me. By the stream. The flow accelerated in response to his stress, dumping energy into his channels with blind urgency.

I was making it worse from three paces away. My presence converted fear into fuel, fuel into instability, and instability into the very display that made Bear interested.

The loop. The same loop. The architecture I'd identified and couldn't disassemble because I was load-bearing.

Wei's qi crested. For two heartbeats, his energy output exceeded anything I'd measured. Foundation Establishment middle phase, outputting at levels twice his advancement. Then it receded. His shoulders dropped. His breathing was ragged.

Bear met my eyes. He held them. For the first time, recognition flickered across his face. He began to understand what stood before him: something older, deeper, and more dangerous than anything his sect had briefed him for.

One short glance and it was gone.

But I'd seen it. And he knew I'd seen it.

"Leave," I said.

Bear didn't leave. His hand rose again.

Neither of us moved.
