A small white mountain blossom grew in the crack between two rocks. Rain had collected around its roots in a pocket of soil.

We had stopped by a stream. Wei was filling the waterskins, crouched at the bank, his hands in the current. They were steady for once, the cold easing the tremor.

He held them there longer than necessary, watching his fingers stay steady in the current. The expression on his face, brief and private, was the closest thing to contentment I'd seen in many days.

I was sitting on a flat stone. The flower was directly in front of me. A bud. Closed. White petals folded inward.

It opened.

It unfurled in heartbeats instead of over a watch or more. Two, three. The bud opened, expanded, and spread its white petals. Perfect. Symmetric. Each one positioned with the precision of something designed rather than grown.

I stared.

It wilted.

In heartbeats the petals browned. The symmetric form curled in on itself. The stem bent and broke. Bud to flower to death in less than ten heartbeats.

Brown paper fragments where a bloom had been.

Had I seen that correctly?

"What is it?"

Wei. Standing behind me. Waterskins full.

"Nothing," I said. "Let's go."

We walked up toward the west, away from the stream, the stone, and the petals.

"Yun."

"What?"

"You're walking fast."

I was. I slowed.

That afternoon, the landscape changed. Trees stood more evenly spaced than mountain trees should be. Rocks were arranged by size, an order that would make sense on a riverbed but not on a mountainside.

Symmetry. The Heaven's Order fingerprint. Closer than I'd estimated.

I changed our route. Into harder, steeper terrain where symmetry was unsustainable.

"Why are we going this way?" Wei asked.

"Better terrain."

"Better for what?"

"For being alone."

He didn't argue.

Into the mountains. Where the trees grew without pattern and the rocks lay where they'd fallen.
