I had let the fire die for the night. Wei slept three paces from the ashes, curled tightly on his side. His breathing followed the third-step pattern. I sat in the dark with my chin on my knees, facing him.

His core vibrated.

A low hum rose from it, its pulse irregular. I could have dismissed one such variation. It was harder to dismiss the same variation returning with every circulation.

I extended my perception to the boundary of his body. Qi leaked steadily through his skin, slower than in the clearing but constant. His channels couldn't hold all of it.

His core shone in his dantian, showing years of development after only months of activity.

I followed the qi from his core through his meridians, out to his extremities, and back.

Forty-seven parts in a hundred.

The number arrived complete, as abruptly as a document placed before me. No thought had led to it.

At forty-seven parts in a hundred of qi-saturation, when the meridional flow passes the lower dantian knot, the core will generate a feedback loop. Duration: three tenths of a heartbeat. The core will fracture.

Metal coated the underside of my tongue. My fingers twitched against my knees.

Forty-seven. The precision belonged in a diagnostic report. I had done nothing but sit by a sleeping boy in the dark, yet the number felt as certain as a measurement.

To calculate it, I would have needed to model his core geometry, predict his saturation trajectory, and simulate feedback at the lower dantian intersection. That was beyond any practitioner I knew. Beyond me. No school of cultivation I'd studied possessed the mathematics.

"No."

I whispered it to whatever had put the number in my head. Wei went on breathing beside the ashes.

I tried to push it down. Each time I looked at him, I thought of forty-seven.

Dawn arrived without my having slept. That was ordinary enough. I had spent three watches with the number in my head, listening to a boy breathe three paces away.

Wei woke, packed, and ate cold rice, following our morning routine.

We walked west, then north, over downhill stretches broken by stands of trees.

At the first crossroads, where two trails met at a stone marker nobody had maintained in decades, I stopped and crouched.

Fresh qi-traces marked the crossing. Scouts had passed here, skilled trackers who had erased almost everything they left behind. Almost.

They'd come for Wei's crater. His burst had carried through the surrounding qi like a shout across a quiet room. Each outburst gave them a smaller area to search.

I erased the traces and turned us northeast into denser forest.

"Why?"

"Scouts."

He didn't ask whose. He'd stopped asking whose.

We walked. Northeast. Into trees that closed around us.
