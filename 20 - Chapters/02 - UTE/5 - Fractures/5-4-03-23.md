You sat opposite me with the fire between us, repeating the explanation in shorter sentences. You paused after each one to see whether I followed.

"The anomalies follow you. Those cultivators recorded the same thing. The qi signature matches yours." You pointed at the papers. "Someone who discovered the connection stopped writing. Every time. Something in you may be keeping that connection from being understood."

I listened. Again, I understood. The simpler arrangement in the sand showed each connection clearly. Your redrawn lines were deeper than before.

I nodded.

"Okay," I said.

"Okay?"

"Okay. I hear you. I understand."

You let out half a breath, then held still. Your gaze held on my face.

One hundred and twenty heartbeats.

You counted them while I looked at the papers. At first I understood how they fitted together. I could have repeated the explanation. The chalk mark belonged beside the signatures under the ruins. The old accounts described what I had felt. I looked from one to another, able to carry the connection with me. Then, slowly, the connections began to loosen.

First, I became uncertain of the connection between the anomalies and me. I had been sure of it a moment before. Now it seemed like a possibility someone had suggested, something I would need to test.

Then I lost track of the researchers. Six of them? Five? The scrolls lay in the firelight. One said something about qi obeying will. I remembered the phrase, but only as someone else's words.

I looked at the map. You had shown me the narrowing route. Perhaps it was coincidence. We'd walked south because the ruins were there. Ruins often stood in similar terrain. I couldn't recover the reason I'd thought the route mattered.

Gone.

I remembered the importance of what you'd told me. I remembered understanding it. The content had gone piece by piece, leaving me staring at the same arrangement in the sand.

I turned to you.

You held my gaze with your jaw clenched.

"How long?" I asked.

"One hundred and twenty heartbeats."

"Was it the same? What you said?"

"Word for word."

"And I—"

"You understood perfectly. You said 'okay.' You looked like you meant it." You spoke flatly, your jaw still tight. "Then your eyes changed."

"Changed how?"

"Like someone lowering a wick. From behind your eyes. I watched the understanding leave your face, Yun. I watched it get taken."

Silence.

The fire had burned lower. Scrolls lay in the same rows next to your third set of drawings. You'd gone over the marks again, deepening them enough that I could still see their edges in the dying light.

"One more time," you said.

"Why?"

"Because." You stood to find a new stick, thinner than the last. You gripped its sharp end while you spoke. "This time I'm going to watch for it. I'm going to see the exact moment it happens."

You repeated it a third time.

This time you held my gaze throughout the explanation. The stick hung at your side. You spoke directly, pausing just long enough for me to acknowledge each point.

"Things change around you. Your qi moves without your directing it. You lose the connection when you begin to understand. Others experienced this. All their records stopped."

I listened.

I understood.

I watched you watching me.

I began to lose it while I was still looking at you. A moment ago, I could have said what each observation meant. Now the thought wouldn't finish. I went back to the first point.

For less than a heartbeat, I could still reach the meaning while feeling its absence. I knew where the next connection should lead. Letting it go was easier. The blankness brought a terrible easing: I could sit by the fire without following the evidence to its end. I wanted to keep what I knew. I also wanted that relief. The effort of holding the thought became unbearable, then ceased.

Then it was gone.

I sat by the fire. The copied scrolls lay spread before us. You had drawn something in the earth.

"What?" I said.

I remember your face in that firelight even now. Your mouth hung open. The anger was gone. You stared at me as though you'd watched me fall somewhere you couldn't reach.

"She can't hear herself." You picked up stones to throw into the darkness, speaking under your breath. "She says it. She means it. Then it's taken. She can't hear her own truth."

"Xu Ran." My voice came out quieter than I'd intended. "What's happening?"

You sat down with your notebook. Your hands trembled as you opened it to a blank page. You began to write.

You put the date first, then the time of night.

*First attempt: I explained the pattern. She understood. Ten heartbeats later, she asked, "What were you saying?"*

*Second attempt: I repeated the explanation. She understood. After one hundred and twenty heartbeats, she had lost the connections one by one.*

*Third attempt: I repeated the explanation. She understood. I watched her eyes lose their focus. The understanding was taken from her.*

Your pen stopped. You set it down and looked up.

Leaves moved above us, yet the air on my face was still. The stones around the fire hummed. Their low vibration reached my teeth.

That vibration brought up fragments of memory. A tree stood on the right of a path where I'd seen it on the left. Someone's mouth was open mid-word. I remembered warmth in stone, then a place where I had listened for an echo that never came.

I reached to connect them. The tree belonged on a mountain path; I couldn't place the face there. As I searched for the missing link, pain sharpened behind my eyes until I could think of nothing else.

I pressed my hands against my temples and squeezed.

"Something is wrong," I said.

"Yes."

"I don't know what."

"I do."

"Something is— I can feel..." I lost the words. I pressed harder against my temples, trying again. "There's a gap. In me. Something that should be there and isn't."

"I know what it is," you said quietly. Your hands rested on the notebook. "But you won't remember me telling you."

"Try."

"I've tried. Three times."

"Try again."

"No." You closed the notebook against your chest. "Not tonight. Not like this. It gets worse when you push. Whatever is doing this, I can't fight it for you."

The leaves settled. The stones stopped humming. The clearing returned to normal, or what passed for normal.

I sat with a pressure under my sternum. Three times I had understood. I could remember each moment before the loss, yet I couldn't recover what had been there. I found the same empty place each time I reached.

"Something in me," I said, watching your face for an answer.

"Yes," you said. "Something in you."

I looked at my clean hands in the firelight. A stranger would have seen two people sitting among their papers. I turned my palms over, searching for some visible sign of what had happened.

Perhaps keeping me alive required keeping this from me. I disliked how readily those two possibilities fitted together.
