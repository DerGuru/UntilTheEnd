We rested in a narrow pass between two ridges. The rock walls stood close enough to touch with outstretched arms. There was shade, a trickle of water from a crack in the south wall, and a flat stone to sit on. While I rested, you took out a piece of chalk.

You drew a circle on the north wall, placed your initials inside, and added the date below. Your strokes were loose but confident. I recognized the hand from your notebook.

"What's that for?" I asked.

"Experiment."

"What kind?"

You smiled, then put the chalk away without explaining.

"We'll see," you said.

We walked south for a watch, descending into a valley thick with old-growth forest. Under the trees the air was cool and damp. It smelled of rotting leaves, mushrooms, and the rich soil under centuries of fallen growth.

Then you stopped.

"Back," you said.

"Back?"

"To the pass. I want to check something."

I didn't argue. You'd return to that mark with or without me. At least going with you would spare me the account of the experiment afterward.

We walked back along the same trail. I checked each switchback against my memory, then the stones next to it. The roots lay where I'd stepped over them. Even the shadows matched. I had begun making those comparisons without reminding myself.

The pass held the same shade, trickle of water, and flat stone.

The chalk mark was not.

It was three paces to the left, on a stone at the base of the south wall. Scree lay packed around it. It looked as though it had been there for five hundred years. On its surface were your chalk circle, initials, and date.

I leaned closer. Every part of the mark was there, but the handwriting had changed. The letters kept their meaning in a different hand.

You stood in front of the stone and pointed. A tremor ran through your hand, though you held the finger rigid.

"That's not my handwriting," you said.

"No."

"Whose is it?"

I studied the angles of the chalk marks. The year character leaned left under a pressure I knew. I had kept that habit from millennia of writing a script no one used anymore. I was sure.

"Mine," I said.

"But that's impossible. You didn't touch that wall. You didn't even go near it. And you were with me the whole time."

Water trickled down the wall. The same out-of-range bird we'd heard many days earlier called again. I noted it and returned to the stone.

The stone. The chalk. My handwriting.

I crouched to touch the stone. Its cold matched the ruins. The surface was rough under my fingertips, weathered by centuries. Fresh chalk came away on my skin. You had used it only earlier that day.

"The original mark?" I said. "Your mark. On the north wall?"

"Gone."

I checked the north wall. Its bare surface held no chalk, even deep in the rough places where wiping should have left dust. I ran a finger across the spot where you'd drawn the date. It came away clean.

"It moved," you said. "Whatever is in your qi moved my mark. It changed the handwriting. It put the mark on a different stone that's been sitting here for centuries."

"As though it had always been there."

"Always." You looked back at the bare wall. "The mark was on the north wall. Now it looks like it was never there. Like it was always here. On this stone. In your handwriting."

I stood. My knees creaked again. No stiffness followed; they straightened easily.

"That's impossible," you said, still looking at the letters.

"It's not impossible. It's happening."

"How? And why? What does it mean?"

"I don't know how. The changes follow me. Afterward, things look as though they've always been that way. The qi signature matches mine. And I know—" The headache pulsed once. I waited for it to ease. "Each change is followed by disturbances farther away. Wrong winds, wrong birds, and reversed qi flows. They're getting worse."

You turned from the stone to me, looking steadily enough that I almost stepped back.

"The sect," you said. "The talismans."

"They're harvesting the qi from those disturbances."

"The noise that you generate."

"Without wanting to. Without being able to stop."

You turned back to the chalk mark. My handwriting on your stone. Evidence of something that shouldn't exist, recorded in letters I never wrote on a surface I never touched.

We stood over letters I could have written. I had never touched that stone. You had watched me for the entire walk. Whatever had made those marks had done it while I was occupied elsewhere. I couldn't identify a stance, an intention, or even a moment when anything had changed.

Yet there was my handwriting. The connection remained even when I had chosen nothing. I turned my chalk-dusted fingers over.

The headache receded. I could keep looking at the mark and even admit it was connected to me. Asking why still brought pressure behind my eye.

I could know what I was. I couldn't know why.

"We need to move," I said.

"Where?"

"Away from here. The longer we stay, the worse the disturbances get. Every site I investigate or question I follow seems to add to them."

"So stop investigating."

My jaw tightened.

"You can't," you said. Not a question.

"No."

"Why not?"

My eyes went to the date on the stone. Eleven researchers had stopped, leaving unfinished words behind them. If I put the question down now, would I ever pick it up again? I wanted the choice to be mine. I was afraid of where the search led, but more afraid of losing the ability to search.

"Because the alternative is worse," I said.

You shouldered your pack, checked the straps, and adjusted the notebook under your arm.

Without a word, we moved on.
