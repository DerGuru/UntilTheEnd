When I came back from the river, you looked up from your cooking and gave me a quick, thorough assessment. You had learned the difference between my face after something happened and my face after nothing happened. You checked my eyes, my jaw, and my movements. My posture had shifted at the water, though I didn't realize it yet.

"Everything okay?" you asked.

"Yes."

Your glance lingered at the incomplete answer, then you turned back to the cooking. Rice and something green, an edible plant you'd found near the river. You enjoyed foraging for the change it brought to a practical routine.

I sat by the fire. The warmth was good. Real warmth, fire warmth, warmth that came from outside and had to be received. The cold in my bones eased. The evening chill retreated from my extremities in the slow yielding of temperature to temperature.

Xiǎohú appeared from wherever she'd been. She padded straight to me, pressed her flank against my shin, and stayed. Warm. The particular warmth of a living body settled close to yours.

I looked down into her calm amber eyes. Her weight against my leg was company I hadn't needed to ask for. For once, nothing about the arrangement needed explaining.

I put my hand on her back. The fur was coarse on top, soft underneath, the two layers that kept her warm in mountain cold. She leaned into the touch.

The sensation was overwhelming: fur against my palm, the warmth under it, and the movement of breathing. But it was also simple. Hand on fur. Warmth against warmth. The oldest comfort.

The feeling was still there. My relationship to it had changed. The nameless frequency, the bass-note vibration that the deletion couldn't reach, was the same, but I heard it differently. Before the river, it had been mysterious. Alien. Something present in me that I couldn't identify, categorize, or file under any heading my experience provided.

Now it was familiar.

It was still beyond understanding. I couldn't name it or describe it in terms that survived the wall. But it felt familiar, like a voice in a crowd I couldn't place yet knew I'd heard before. I couldn't see the speaker's face or connect it to an event. Still, it belonged to my life.

The feeling had been there always, like a sound so constant I'd stopped noticing it. I wondered how it had gone unheard for so long. The river hadn't given me something new to carry. It had made something already mine recognizable, even with its name still out of reach.

You handed me a bowl. Rice with the green plant, which you'd wilted with steam and seasoned with the salt from the market. Simple. Good. The flavors were distinct: the bitterness of the green offset by the starch of the rice offset by the mineral sharpness of the salt. Every bite was an event. Every bite was too much information for a palate still adjusting to its new sensitivity.

I ate anyway. I was getting better at the too-much, letting sensation arrive without bracing against every part of it. The first bite was sharpest. By the next, the greens came through without costing me the warmth of the bowl under my fingers.

"I saw something at the river," I said.

You looked up. Careful. The new carefulness, the post-question carefulness that weighed every interaction against the cost of the last one.

"What?"

I considered. The wall was quiet. The guardian was quiet. Whatever watchfulness governed my access to truth was uninterested in what I'd seen at the river, either because the content was below its threshold or too foreign for its classifications. But maybe whatever I'd seen was sanctioned, permitted, allowed.

"My reflection," I said. "It did something."

You waited. Notebook still closed. Hands still.

"It smiled before I did."

You weighed this. The silence was a mind turning over implications, sifting through what you knew, tracing the consequences a new revelation produces when added to a picture already heavy with unknowns.

"Half a heartbeat. Maybe less. It was smiling before I started to smile."

"And you weren't scared?"

"This time, no. It felt—" I searched for a word. Anything I could say was smaller than what had happened. I wanted to tell you without turning it into something else.

"Warm." I paused. "Like someone was glad to see me."

You were quiet for a long time. The fire crackled. The green plant wilted further in the residual steam. Xiǎohú lay at my feet, amber eyes closed.

"You're not going to write that down?" I asked.

You held my gaze.

"No," you said.

"Why not?"

"Because some things aren't documentation. Some things are—" You paused. You pausing mid-sentence was rare enough to constitute an event. "Yours."

We went back to eating. The fire burned. The river ran a few steps away, the constant murmur of water over stone that had been the background of this place since before we'd arrived and would go on after we left.

I didn't tell you all of it. Some things belonged only to me.

The stars appeared. As cold settled in, I pulled the robe tighter, letting the feeling pulse without pressing against the crack. I made no attempt to explain what had happened, what it meant, or what would come next.

I sat by the fire, warm and cold at the same time.

The fire settled. A log broke in half. Sparks flew up and disappeared into the night. The crack pulsed. The feeling hummed. The world was still loud, sharp, and full of details I was learning to bear.

The river was there, the fire was there, the fox-spirit was there, and the warmth in my chest was there.
