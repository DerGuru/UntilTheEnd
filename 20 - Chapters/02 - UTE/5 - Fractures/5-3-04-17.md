We were walking through a stretch of open grassland. Flat, exposed terrain, visible from every direction, with nothing to hide behind. Late afternoon. The sky was clear. Normal wind from the east.

Then the wind stopped.

The change was immediate. The wind ceased against my face, and the grass went rigid. A moment before, silver waves had crossed the plain. Now every blade stood motionless.

A prickling pressure reached the soles of my feet. Qi that had lain thinly across the grassland was gathering about forty paces ahead of us. The ground began to hum.

"Move," I said.

You stood staring at the sky. Qi currents had become visible above us, as the merchant had described over the northern mountains. Threads of energy pulled free of their channels toward the gathering below. They pulsed across the clear air, bending toward the same point from every direction. Your face was tilted up to follow them. From a distant ridge, I might have stopped to watch them too. We were much too close.

"Xu Ran. Move."

You moved. Too late.

The pressure released in silence. The air hardened as a wave spread from the point ahead. The gathered qi drove outward through the grass. It reached us so quickly that I had scarcely finished speaking.

The grass flattened in a radius of forty paces. Pressure thickened the air until each breath passed through something solid. At the edge of that circle, the stems bent sharply toward the earth.

You were inside the circle.

For less than a heartbeat, the advancing pressure faltered. Dust hung above a bent stem beside your foot. The wave had been moving too fast to follow. Now the edge of its reach showed.

It held just short of you.

A tug caught deep in my chest. Qi pulled toward you, and I wasn't directing it. It was the sensation from the moments when my own life should have ended, except that this time it reached past me, straining toward the place where you stood, just inside the flattened grass.

It failed.

The pull gave way. Dust dropped beside your foot as the pressure advanced again. Your damaged core could not withstand it. You had held Core Formation for only a few hundred heartbeats; the strength left to you now would barely slow the impact.

I moved.

I stepped between you and the wave, extending one hand with the palm flat. I'd made that movement more often than I could remember. There was no time to do anything else.

The wave dissolved against my palm. Within a breath, its gathered qi broke into scattered currents. Pressure eased from my ears. Grass began springing upright, and the air thinned enough to breathe freely.

You had dropped to the ground. Your knees had given way while you braced for the impact. You hunched over them after the wave was gone.

I pulled you up by one arm. You came up so easily that I had to steady you before your feet found the ground. I kept forgetting how little force it took.

"Are you okay?" I said.

Your hands shook. You had gone a pale gray around the mouth. Your breathing came in short bursts despite your attempts to slow it.

"You're fine," I said, because saying it might make it true, and because I had no other words.

You sat on the flattened grass with your palms pressed against your knees. I sat opposite while you worked to slow your breathing. Each time you got it steady, another short gasp broke the rhythm. Your fingers lay spread against the cloth, pressing down to hide the shaking, but it showed at your wrists. I waited.

Dust settled around us with the smell of turned earth. A sharp metallic tang lingered from the released qi, stronger than the air after lightning. It coated the back of my throat.

Under that sharpness, the disturbed soil brought back another patch of ground: dust rising beside the village.

Wei had stood there in our first year, in the path of a spirit beast. It had come too close to the village, outside its usual territory at the wrong season. Something neither of us could name had driven it from its range. He'd been too slow to move.

I'd put my hand between them then too. Killing the beast had cost me scarcely any effort. The forest had lost a season of growth: the qi backlash had drained the soil for fifty paces in every direction.

The cost. Always the cost.

Again. Someone close to me had stood in danger, and I'd stepped between. Afterward, I looked across the flattened grass for what my intervention had done.

Wei had stared at me that day, his eyes moving from my hand to the fallen beast. He'd been too awed to speak.

Now you stared the same way.

"You stopped it," you said.

"Yes."

"With one hand."

"Yes."

"That wave would have—" You looked toward the point where the pressure had gathered and left the sentence unfinished.

"Yes."

You studied your own hands. Steady now, or nearly; a fine tremor remained, which you masked by gripping your knee.

"For you," you said. "How significant was it?"

More blades were straightening around the edge of the circle. The soil under them was undisturbed.

"Manageable," I said.

"Like when you stopped the tribulation, when I was about to enter Core Formation?"

"Similar in scale, but different in kind."

You nodded slowly. Your gaze returned to the hand I'd raised against the wave. I turned it over, checking for damage. I'd stopped two forces that could have killed you. I had nothing useful to say about how little either had required of me.

For months we'd walked side by side without speaking about that difference. Now you sat with your knees drawn up, waiting for the tremor to leave your fingers. The wave had pressed lightly against my palm. We had stood scarcely an arm's length apart. You had used all the strength left to you to brace; I had raised a hand. Telling you it was manageable had been accurate, which did very little to make it kind.

Your hands stopped shaking.

You didn't speak.

You sat a while longer, then stood, shouldered your pack, and looked south.

"How far to the next town?" you asked.

Your voice was steady. Your hands were still.

I let out a breath. My hand relaxed at my side.

"Half a day," I said.

We walked.
