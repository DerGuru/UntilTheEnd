The third scroll was softer than the others under my fingertips. Its fibers had separated over millennia until the paper felt like cloth. I followed a line of faded characters with my thumb. At one of them, I stopped.

*Return.* The archaic character combined the symbols for *circle* and *self*: a return to what one had been. I'd been parsing the surrounding sentence when a sudden dizziness made me brace a hand against the floor.

The stone under my palm was steady. I held on to that while an image rose over the paper, vivid enough to see.

I remembered myself somewhere else, long before this library or the language written on these scrolls. My hands rested on stone, warm as a rock someone had been sitting against. I was tracing lines in its surface. For two days in this ruin I'd been making that same motion, following grooves with my fingers to feel their course.

The image lasted three heartbeats, perhaps four. Long enough to smell rich, wet earth, recently turned. Its dampness hung in the air around that warm stone.

Then it was gone.

The scroll fell from my hands and landed in the dust, its loose paper curling inward. It rolled before settling at my feet.

My hands were shaking.

I pressed them flat against my knees. I had been in that place. I knew the feel of that stone, the pressure of my fingers against its lines. Until this moment I could have searched my past for years without finding it. Now I could scarcely doubt I'd touched it.

You appeared, picked up the scroll, and wiped off the dust. You eased the delicate paper straight before looking at my face.

You didn't ask.

That was new. I would once have expected you to press for an answer. You'd followed smaller questions through several refusals, returning to them whenever I seemed willing to speak.

This time you studied my face, then put the scroll back in its place. I couldn't tell what you'd seen. You left the room. Soon afterward, water poured into a pot. You began stoking the fire.

I put my palms on the floor again. The stone chilled them. For three heartbeats, there had been another surface here, warm enough to rest against.

Somewhere, millennia ago, I'd sat tracing those lines with my fingers. I had known what I was doing.

I pushed myself up. My knees cracked. I stood still at the sound, then bent them cautiously. They moved as easily as ever. After so many years without the small complaints of aging, I'd heard a sound I associated with old women rising from their work.

Outside, the evening air was cool with the smell of pine. You had tea going. Steam rose from the water as you poured without asking. I took the cup without commenting, closing both hands around it.

We sat on the slope below the library entrance, looking out across folded hills, strips of forest, and a distant river catching the last light. I checked each element against my memory. The landscape matched. I let my gaze travel along the river once more before looking down at the cup.

The tea had the clean bitterness of its leaf variety. You had steeped it correctly. The taste lingered after I swallowed, sharp enough to clear the remembered smell of damp earth.

"The scroll?" you asked.

"Yes."

"Something in it."

"A word."

"What word?"

"Return." I held the cup in both hands. The warmth was comfort I wasn't willing to acknowledge. "The old form. Circle-self. The kind of return that means coming back to what you were."

"And?"

"And something happened. A flash. An image. Me, somewhere else, a very long time ago. Doing what I'm doing here. Reading, sitting on stone."

You were quiet for a long time. The tea cooled. The valley darkened. Stars appeared, correct stars, in the right positions, which I confirmed, because I no longer took the sky for granted.

"A memory?" you said.

"Not one I could recall."

You glanced toward the library entrance. Then your eyes went to my hands around the cup. Your mouth tightened before you spoke again.

"You remember doing things you had forgotten entirely," you said.

"Yes."

"From when?"

"I don't know. The image had no context. Just sensation. Stone. My hands moving."

"Moving how?"

"The way they move here. The same motion. Tracing lines."

You set down your cup, picked up your notebook, and put it down again. Your hand rested on the cover. You'd been working toward a larger question since I described the memory. I waited to hear whether you would ask it.

You asked it anyway.

"How old are you really?"

I drank the last of the tea. Bitter. Sharp. Real.

"I don't know exactly how old I am. But very old. When I started cultivating, there were no sects. No masters. We lived as nomadic groups of people and qi was instinct, not technique." I held the cup. "I took part in forming our language. I saw kingdoms rise, fall, and rise again as something else. Rivers changed their courses. Forests became deserts. Deserts became forests. I was there when someone first scratched a symbol into clay and someone else later called it writing." I set the cup down. "But I've just remembered something from a time even I can't place. There are parts of my history that I have lost."

You waited for more. That was enough truth for one evening, perhaps too much.

The stars turned. The valley fell into complete shadow. Somewhere in the library, a shelf settled with a creak of wood against stone. After the sound my knees had made, I almost smiled.

Almost.

"We leave tomorrow," I said. "There's nothing more here that the scrolls can tell us."

"What can they tell us?"

"That ten others had what I have. That all ten were erased. That whatever is doing this has been doing it for at least five thousand years." I set the empty cup on the stone. "And that it keeps doing it."

"To everyone except you."

"So far."

You picked up your notebook. This time you wrote. The pen moved with measured certainty, recording a fact you wished weren't one.

I went inside to the most intact corner. I lay on the stone floor with my robe bunched under my head, looking up at a single star through a crack in the ceiling.

The star was in the right place.

Everything was in the right place.

I stared at the ceiling until the star moved.
