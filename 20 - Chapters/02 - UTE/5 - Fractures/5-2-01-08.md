I crouched under the roots covering the library entrance. Collapsed earth had left a low gap between rock and overgrowth. Vines screened it so thoroughly that from ten paces it looked like solid hillside. I'd walked past it twice before the qi under the slope gave it away.

You pushed through the undergrowth behind me, thorns catching your sleeves, your pack scraping against the rock overhang. You didn't complain. You scraped dirt from the lintel stone with your thumbnail, found carved characters underneath, and wrote them down before you'd finished standing up.

The air inside was thick with dry dust. Chalk settled on my tongue, as fine as flour, from centuries of crumbling stone and plaster. Grit crunched between my teeth at once. The collapsed shelving still held iron brackets, hinges, and the remains of a locking clasp. Their cold reached my fingers before I touched them. Under the dust ran the musty sweetness of incense. I searched the dark floor for a source.

The same three at every site: chalk, cold metal, and incense. I had begun looking for them as soon as I stepped inside a ruin. Here they were again, even before we'd cleared the entrance.

The interior was more intact than I'd expected. In the main hall, stone and timber lay splayed across the floor where the ceiling had collapsed all at once. The side rooms still held. Most of their shelves had fallen, scattering their contents, but I could read the cracked stone tablets among the debris. There were scrolls too.

Hundreds of scrolls.

Most were ruined. Moisture had once seeped through rock fissures, pulping the paper and washing out the ink. Some had been stored in stone cylinders with wax caps. Those seals had held. A dozen scrolls might be intact. Perhaps twenty. Enough to search.

You sneezed three times in quick succession, each louder than the last. Afterward you stood blinking in the dust, waiting to see whether there would be a fourth.

I was already working.

I worked along one collapsed shelf at a time. I lifted each scroll, examined its bindings, and unrolled it as far as the paper allowed. The damaged ones went aside. I held the others open to check their contents. I'd searched enough ruins to know that haste cost more than patience. A hurried thumb could destroy the line I'd come to find.

You recovered, wiped your nose with your sleeve, and joined me. You took the left side of the room. I took the right. We worked to the scrape of stone on stone, the rustle of unfurling paper, and occasional muttered curses when a scroll crumbled at a touch.

Narrow beams of light came through cracks in the walls. Dust hung bright inside them, and past their edges the room lay in deep shadow. I held each scroll to a beam, turning the paper until I could distinguish faded ink from stains.

Most concerned sect business: rosters, inventories, and technique manuals. People had once needed every entry on those pages. Now I set them aside and wondered whether the sect had outlasted its usefulness or fought itself apart.

I finally found what I was looking for.
