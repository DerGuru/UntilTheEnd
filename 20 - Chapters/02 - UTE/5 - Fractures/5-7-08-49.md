The morning was cold, clear, and beautiful with the simple shapes of rock against sky. Light fell across the camp as Xiǎohú stretched and yawned.

You packed efficiently. You always did, but this morning the efficiency had a finality to it, as if something had ended during the night. The morning was about beginning what came next.

You closed your notebook.

You secured it in the waterproof case and put the case in your pack. The notebook was full. Dense, precise handwriting covered every page, front and back. You had treated blank space as wasted potential, filling it with the record of everything since we'd entered each other's lives for the third time.

Everything you'd observed. Everything you'd measured. Everything you'd asked me and everything I'd forgotten. Everything the world had done in response to whatever I was becoming.

The notebook was complete.

You'd done everything you could. Your documentation was thorough, your observations precise, and your analysis as far as mortal analysis could go. You'd pushed me to the crack. You'd gotten the feeling through the wall. You'd sacrificed something important: the knowledge that you had never deliberately used my trust to hurt me. But your sacrifice had produced a result.

From here, it was mine.

Mine. The crack was mine. The feeling was mine. The reflection that smiled before I smiled was mine. Whatever the next step was, wherever it led, however much it cost, it was mine to take.

You knew this. It showed in the economy of your movements, in the closed notebook. You shouldered your pack, looked at the trail, and waited for me to choose the direction.

I went forward. The world's insistence on directing me toward isolation had softened since the river. The paths were paths again, going where they geographically went, behaving like terrain rather than like forced arguments.

Forward was enough. Forward was what I had.

I walked. You walked three paces off, the old companionable distance that had survived every impossible thing that had happened. Xiǎohú trotted alongside. The world was sharp, loud, cold, and full of details.

The sky was clear. The trail wound between pines, their needles dark green against gray rock. The air tasted of stone, altitude, and the thin emptiness of high places.

Somewhere behind us, the sect observers followed. Still there. Still patient.

Let them watch.

My head hurt. The river had quieted it for a while, but it hadn't lasted. The headache was part of me now, like the cold and the sharpness.

I rubbed my temples as I walked.

The world turned. The mountains stood. The trail went forward. And forward was where I went.

When we made camp again, you cooked. Rice, with some dried mushrooms from the market and a handful of wild greens. The food was warm and adequate. I tasted every component with the overwhelming, beautiful, exhausting sensitivity that had become my relationship with physical experience.

The fire burned well, lit at the first try. For the moment, the small failures had stopped. I watched the steady flame without knowing how long the respite would last.

The fire. The coals. The small, patient geometry of heat doing its work.

Then I looked up. The sky. The stars. The void between them, full of things too far away for eyes that hadn't been designed for that kind of seeing.

Something up there. Or behind me. Or inside me. Or all three, the distinctions collapsing into irrelevance in the face of a truth I could feel but not articulate.

I put my hand on my chest. The heartbeat. The feeling. The warmth of being myself, still here in the world despite everything.

Xiǎohú curled near the fire, amber eyes reflecting flame.

We sat between light and dark, warmth and cold, the known and the unknown.

Tomorrow we would walk again. The crack would pulse. The feeling would hum. The world would be sharper than before, louder than it should be, and full of small unfairnesses accumulating like rain in a basin.

But tonight, right now, the fire was warm and the rice was good. Stars burned above the mountains while Xiǎohú slept. Somewhere deep, under the wall, under the crack, in the bedrock of whatever I was, something shifted. The feeling pulsed. Stronger than before. Stronger than it should have been.

The headache spiked. My hand went to my temple. The fire dimmed, or my eyes did.

You studied my face, noticing something in it that I couldn't feel.

"Yun," you said. Quiet. Careful.

"What?"

You didn't answer. Your eyes went to my hands. The ground under them. The air around my shoulders.

Xiǎohú opened one eye. Her ears flattened.

I didn't know what they were seeing. I didn't know what was changing. The crack pulsed. It carried something heavier than warmth, something that pressed against the inside of my ribs with the weight of a question I couldn't ask and an answer I couldn't survive.

The fire cracked. The coals shifted. Above us, the stars kept burning.

My hands were shaking.
