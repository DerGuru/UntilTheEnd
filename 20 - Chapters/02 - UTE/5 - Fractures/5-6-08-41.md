The fire settled into coals. I sat by it and did not move.

The cold was still there, a persistent awareness. Air against my skin, ground under me, the gradient between the side of my body toward the fire and the other. What had once been mere awareness was now sensation.

I pulled your outer robe tighter. It wasn't enough. A mortal garment for mortal cold, and the lesson that mortal solutions fall short was one I hadn't been prepared for.

Above me, the sky was clear. The afternoon light had sharpened every ridge, tree line, and shadow. At altitude the thin air left each edge distinct, even across the valley.

The feeling pulsed.

There. Behind the sternum. Anatomists put the heart in that space, poets the soul, and I the thing that had no name.

I reached for a thought about it.

A thought began to form with the ordinary shape of a sentence, a subject, a verb, and an object. I felt something when...

Gone. Quick and efficient, the wall had run this pattern long enough to recognize threats by their opening syllables. The thought was taken before it could complete, and in its place lay the gap, the smooth surface where content had been.

I tried again.

During the...

Gone.

What you...

Gone.

The wall held through every attempt. Indirect approaches, circuitous logic, and the patient accumulation of fragments all met the same quick erasure.

So I stopped trying to think.

I sat looking at the mountains, the cold on my skin. I let my attention rest on my chest and left the sensation without words. It lay somewhere below thought and above reflex, present even when I couldn't explain it.

And the feeling responded.

It resonated under my hand, steady enough to feel real. Mine.

Ancient.

Oldness lay in it as plainly as age shows in the contours of a mountain. I had no number for it. Years were too small a measure, even all the years I remembered. It was old in a way that predates counting.

Lonely.

Loneliness ran through it as cold runs through stone. I couldn't separate it from the feeling itself or imagine circumstances that could take it away. Even with you asleep next to me, it ran too deep for company to touch.

Certain.

This was the strongest quality. Whatever it was, I had no doubt of its presence. Other emotions shifted with circumstance, time, and what I learned. A word could alter them; a new fact could make yesterday's conviction look foolish. This feeling was as fixed as any law I'd encountered: gravity, causality, and the principle that actions have consequences with weight of their own.

I had always been here.

Another quality, present without words. It would last even if I never found a way to say it.

I put my hand on my chest. The heartbeat was there, steady, the mechanical rhythm of a muscle doing its involuntary work. Under the heartbeat, the feeling. Under the feeling, something deeper, something I could spend years trying to reach, decades trying to understand, and centuries learning to live with.

But right now it was enough to know it was there.

Enough.

The word had found me before. But this time it was affirmative.

Enough as in sufficient. Enough as in this will do. The feeling was present, nameless, formless, and ancient as stone. Its presence gave me a thread to follow. Following it meant moving forward, in a direction I wanted to go.

The fire crackled. A coal shifted and sent a small cascade of sparks upward, bright against the afternoon light. They rose, faded, and were gone.

Your breathing deepened and lost its control. Real sleep at last, from a body that had held tension too long.

You had hurt me on purpose. You admitted as much. Yet I had forgiven you without knowing what I was forgiving. I trusted that you wouldn't have done it if you'd seen another way. The event itself was gone, but I could still sit by your bedroll without flinching when you moved in your sleep.

Trust was a feeling.

The afternoon continued. The coals glowed. The mountains stood. Cold pressed through the robe, through every thin layer of cloth between my body and the world. I had spent so long insulated from sensation; now, for reasons I couldn't remember, each chill reached my skin.

My hand lay on my chest. The heartbeat. The feeling. The nameless frequency that hummed below awareness and above silence.

The afternoon softened. Shadows lengthened across the camp, your sleeping face, and the dimmer coals. The mountains lost their sharp edges, their ridges blurring into the sky as the light drained from them in stages: gold, copper, and the deep blue-gray of stone at evening.

The first star appeared. Then another. Then enough to stop counting.

They were right. I don't know how else to say it. They burned where I remembered them, each in its proper place. The stream had felt right like that, as had the cold and the feeling behind my sternum. They were there, available to my senses, without a gap where knowing them should be.

Quiet settled over the camp. The stream ran on, with the wind and the small sounds of cooling earth. Wood shifted in the fire. Gradually the sounds became easier to hold together, one evening instead of separate sensations I had to attend to. I sat among them without having to choose.

The moon rose slowly over the ridge, its edge showing first, then its whole pale circle. It cleared the treeline and the camp turned silver. Your bedroll. The pot by the woodpile. My hand on my chest. All of it washed in colorless light borrowed from the sun.

It would be enough for now. For whatever came next. For the next morning, the next trail, the next question that I would forget or the next answer that would be taken.

The feeling would last. The content would go. The gap between knowing and feeling had been a prison. Now it was widening, with room in it for something I could keep. The change was tentative, fragile enough that I still feared losing it. But I had sat here through the afternoon, through evening, into moonlight, and it held.

I pulled the robe around me and looked at the fading coals. By morning they'd be ash.

I got up to lay two of the branches I'd gathered across the coals, angled so the air could reach them. The bark caught, then the wood under it. The fire climbed back from ember to flame, small but definite, enough to last until dawn if nothing disturbed it.

I lay down. The ground was hard, cold, and real against my back. Your breathing was steady nearby. The moonlight lay across the camp like a second blanket, thinner, colder, but present.

I closed my eyes. The stream, the birds, and the wind went on. The cold was still there, and the feeling under it. Gradually it all softened, quieter a little at a time.

Enough.
