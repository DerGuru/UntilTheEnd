We stopped near midnight. A clearing at the edge of a ridge, sheltered by an oak broad enough that its canopy swallowed the firelight before it reached the sky. The ground under it was dry, layered with seasons of fallen leaves compressed into a surface that gave underfoot, like walking on leather.

You built the fire with the care of months of practice. First came the kindling, then larger pieces arranged to burn steadily with little light. We needed warmth without drawing attention. By the time the larger wood caught, the heat reached my knees.

The oak creaked as heat reached wood that had been cooling all day. I pressed my back against the trunk. Through the rough bark, a slow vibration faded after each creak.

Moths came to the light first. Gnats, midges, and smaller night-fliers followed them. They circled above the flames, sometimes passing so close that their wings caught. Others rose into the leaves past the heat.

Low clouds hid the stars and moon. Past the small radius of firelight, the forest was dark. Undergrowth rustled nearby. Farther off, a night hunter called. Branches creaked as they settled after the wind. I listened to place the sounds while you tended the fire.

The air smelled of damp earth, leaf rot, and the mineral sharpness of cooling stone. Smoke thickened around us from the green wood you'd chosen. It gave less heat than dry wood, but little light escaped upward.

I sat with my back against the oak. The bark was rough, furrowed, the texture of something that had been growing for longer than most buildings had been standing. You sat across the fire, your pack open, your notebook already out.

Neither of us spoke for a while. Sap popped in the fire. Occasionally an ember collapsed, sending sparks up toward the leaves. You set water to heat. The kettle began to hum, and the last of the jasmine we'd bought scented the steam.

"We should talk about what we have," you said.

I studied you through the fire. Flames cast light across your face and left it in shadow.

"All right."

You'd arranged copies of the scroll passages by age on the flat ground next to the fire. The anomaly map lay next to your comparisons of the qi signatures, each one linked to an entry in your notes. Next to them were the charts connecting our route to the reported disturbances. You'd also set out the dates of the researchers' lives and the abrupt ends of their accounts. I could follow the order you meant me to read them in.

While I searched ruins, you'd been assembling what I found. You had compared my observations, put dates beside them, and left room where I'd refused to explain something. I recognized several of those gaps. You had been listening to my silences too.

You pushed a bowl of rice toward me. I set it by my knee. Steam rose, thinned, and disappeared. You watched it cool. I watched the evidence.

Shadows shifted over the papers. The scroll copies fluttered, their edges curling in the night air. Firelight gave the ink an amber warmth. Three-thousand-year-old words looked almost freshly written.

"I can feel it," I said.

You looked up from the map you'd been adjusting.

"The answer. It's here. In all of this." I gestured at the spread. "The evidence is sufficient. The pattern is clear. I know what it points to. I just can't—" I closed my eyes. I could remember each discovery, place it beside the next, and follow the connection up to the last step. There I lost it. I opened my eyes to the same papers, in the same order.

"I know," you said quietly. "That's why I did this."

You stood, lifted a stick, and began to draw.
