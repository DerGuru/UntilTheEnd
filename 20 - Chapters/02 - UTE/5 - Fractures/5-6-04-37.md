Your face was still in front of me when the truth came in. Everything, at once, from every direction, pouring through a gap too small to hold it.

I was...

I was once...

I was always...

I was here before...

I...

Five echoes. The same beginning, each one carrying a different ending that arrived too fast to read, too dense to separate, stacking on one another like pages turned before the words could be absorbed. Each echo louder than the last, each one carrying more, each one filling the space the previous one had opened.

A place beyond any geography I'd seen, mapped, or walked through. I had no vocabulary for where it was, yet I knew it with the certainty of ground underfoot. It was as plain as the clearing where I stood, though I couldn't hold a single detail. I felt no need to verify it, visit it, or ask how I knew. The certainty itself was overwhelming.

A name. A name I'd never heard, spoken in a voice I'd never heard, in a language I'd never learned. But recognizable, as one's own name is when a stranger speaks it. Instant, automatic, bone-level recognition of something that belongs to you regardless of who says it.

Between the echoes, almost lost in the flood, came a flash of something specific. Scrolls. The ones I'd read in the library and dozens more, hundreds more. They bore hands that weren't mine, scripts from eras I remembered without knowing anyone had recorded me then. They lay sealed in ruins I'd walked past without knowing what was inside. Next to each scroll, always, a presence. A face I couldn't see. Someone sitting close, writing it down. Someone who kept the record I couldn't keep for myself.

The flash passed. Too fast to parse. Too fast to keep.

With it came a feeling, deeper than the emotions that made rain, split trees, or moved mountains. It touched the foundation of whatever I was, below personality, memory, and experience, in the place where being myself was rooted.

Ancient. The word is insufficient, but it's the closest language gets.

I had always been here.

I had always been.

Always. Always.

Always.

All of this came in that one moment when the crack appeared and the truth pushed through. It was complete, compressed into too little time to understand, more than I could hold. Each thing arrived with the weight of a whole memory, yet I couldn't separate one from the next.

Something happened to my eyes. A sting, sharp behind both eyes at once, as if someone had pressed needles into the back of my skull from the inside. I blinked, and the world shifted.

I was still standing in the clearing, facing you. But my sight was strange, as if something else looked out through my face, visible behind it like a flame through the openings of a lantern.

And it saw you.

Usually I saw you through the history between us, through my own limitations. This seeing was total. Every part of you was present: every history, every decision that had brought you to this clearing to stand five paces from something wearing a human shape. For one heartbeat, it was not wearing that shape well.

I couldn't move. The thing looking through my eyes was so complete, so absolutely present in its attention that I was a passenger in my own body, watching it watch you with a focus that made everything I'd ever called *seeing* feel like squinting through fog.

One moment of eternity, as you described it later to me.

Then it ended.

Everything was gone. The wall, the guardian, whatever I called it, had closed harder than ever. The crack had opened and the truth had come through. It had been too much, and the defense had taken the whole moment.

It had erased the moment.

Erased. Completely, as if the last few heartbeats had been cut from a scroll and the edges on either side pressed together.

I looked down at my hands. At the grass. At you. Your face was wrong. Cracked, as if something behind it had broken and the face held the pieces together through will alone.

"Weren't you going to ask me something?"

Your mouth opened, then closed.

"Then ask," I said.

Something slackened in your face at the proof that everything you'd done had been undone. Your jaw loosened. Your shoulders dropped. You said nothing.

"What happened?" I asked.

The gap was total. I remembered walking here, the conversation before your question. Everything after it was missing. How long had we been here? The sun was still in about the same place. It could have been heartbeats, at most half a watch. Everything in between was possible.

And the in-between? Gone. All of it taken, erased, and locked into the void where the guardian kept the things it decided I couldn't have.

Yet something had survived. That was everything.

Somewhere deep, below the gap, in the place where the wall couldn't reach because the wall was built to block thoughts and this wasn't a thought, something was left.

A feeling.

A resonance deep inside me, steadier than a mood. It ran under the emptiness where the moment of seeing had been. Something had set it trembling. The experience that caused it was gone, but the vibration wasn't.

I couldn't name it, describe it, or even think clearly about it. Every attempt dissolved as the wall intervened. But it was there as a bass note is in the chest, too low to hear clearly, present as vibration, as pressure, as an awareness of something you cannot make out.

I put my hand on my chest. The feeling sat under the ribs, behind the sternum, deep among the movements that kept me alive. My palm lay over my heartbeat. Both were there at once, the ordinary rhythm and this unfamiliar presence beneath it.

Something real. Something old. Something mine.

I didn't know what it was or what it meant. I didn't know what had caused it, why it was there, or how long it would last.

But it was there.

For the first time since the forgetting began, since the wall rose and my mind started removing pieces of itself with such terrible precision, something had survived.

A feeling I could hold there under my hand.

But it was everything to me.
