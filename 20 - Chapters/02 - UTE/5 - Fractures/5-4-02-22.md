You drew our route in the earth next to the papers. With the tip of the stick, you marked a circle at each anomaly, then added numbers and dates. Arrows connected the sites along the narrowing path.

"Here," you said, tapping the first marker. "The shrine. Qi flowing toward you. A foundation layer with your signature." You moved to the second. "The library. Same foundation. Same signature. A researcher documented these phenomena three thousand years ago." You tapped the third. "The caves. Same. Each of the later sites too. The qi signature matches. So does the age pattern. The researchers' accounts all stop in the same place."

You connected the marks with two lines, then circled the point where they converged.

"The anomalies follow your presence. Not perfectly. There's a delay, and their reach varies. But you've documented the connection yourself. Fourteen anomalies in twenty-one days, all centered on your route."

Your hands moved with clarity, each line deliberate, each symbol chosen.

"The historical fragments confirm it. Ten accounts across at least five thousand years. The same descriptions: qi obeying will, bodies refusing to die, and changes in the surrounding world." You paused. "Someone close to each subject recorded what happened. They set down observations alongside the cultivator's own words. All ten accounts end mid-sentence."

You tapped the sand. "Ten companions. Documenting ten cultivators. Across multiple millennia. Using the same method."

The same method. Your notebook lay next to the older accounts. I was close to understanding why that mattered.

The headache flared, then passed.

"Things change around you," you said. "Paths shift. Forests die or grow where you remember clear ground. Afterward, it looks as though they were always that way. The chalk test showed the connection. A mark I made reappeared in your handwriting. Your qi signature lies under ruins built centuries after it was laid down."

You drew lines outward from the X, radiating like the spokes of a wheel.

"Whatever changes these places may also protect you. It may have stopped those ten researchers. The traces lead into you, Yun. Something happens without your choosing it. Perhaps it begins deeper than the thoughts you can follow."

You spoke calmly, leaving pauses where I might object. I looked from the X to the scrolls. You had put the evidence within reach of my hand. If I doubted a date, I could check it. If I questioned a connection, the two records lay side by side. I found no place to dismiss the argument.

I listened.

I understood.

Each line in the sand made sense. From the shrine to the altered chalk, the discoveries connected, and I understood why you had arranged them in that order. My chest went heavy as I looked back at you.

I nodded.

Your shoulders dropped. You lowered the stick, loosening a grip you'd held tight throughout the explanation. For many days you had watched me turn away from these same observations. Now I had followed you to the end. You had been waiting for that carefully.

A small gray moth landed on the edge of your map. It was a common forest species, one that usually navigated by starlight. Cloud hid the stars tonight. The moth folded its wings and sat still while the fire crackled beside it.

"Okay," I said. "So what do we—"

Darker lines patterned its gray wings. I followed them in the firelight, still thinking of the lines you'd drawn in the sand.

I blinked.

The moth was gone. Its wings hadn't moved. The paper lay undisturbed, with no dust where it had rested. I searched the dark above the map.

The sand drawings lay by the fire. You sat opposite me. Every scroll was still in its place.

But the meaning was gone.

I could count the anomaly markers and read your notation beside them, yet I no longer understood why one led to the next. I followed a line with my eyes, then had to begin again at the mark where it started.

I turned to you.

"What were you saying?" I asked.

Your mouth tightened. You looked from me to the drawings.

"What?" you said.

"You were saying something. Something important. I can tell. Your hands were moving, you were drawing." I stared at the sand. Lines. Circles. Arrows. They looked like... "What do these mean?"

You stared at me.

"You just—" You closed your eyes. "You nodded. Ten heartbeats ago. You said 'okay.' You understood."

"I agreed to what?"

Silence.

The fire cracked. A spark rose. The scrolls fluttered.

"Yun." You held each word carefully. "I explained the pattern for the time it takes a short incense stick to burn. The evidence. The connections. You listened. You nodded. You understood. Now, ten heartbeats later, you don't remember what I said?"

I reached for your explanation, starting with the first mark.

Nothing.

I knew you'd said something important. The heaviness was still in my chest. I remembered watching you draw, then understanding why you'd done it. That moment was clear. What I had understood was out of reach. I looked from the first circle to the last, waiting for the connection to return. They were separate marks in the dirt.

"I remember you talking," I said. "I remember thinking it was important."

"But not what it was."

"No."

You threw the drawing stick into the dark. It struck grass past the firelight. You stared after it, your hand still open.

You sat down. Hard. Your jaw worked.

"Again," you said.

"What?"

"I'll tell you again."
