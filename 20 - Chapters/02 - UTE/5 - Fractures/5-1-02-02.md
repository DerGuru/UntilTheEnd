By the third morning, grit had worked under my fingernails. I scraped at the inner wall, following the edge of a carving behind a shifted column. There, at last, I found something.

The panel faced east. The column hiding it had cracked at the base and leaned from vertical. I could see where it had scored a line in the floor tile during its slow, centuries-long topple. Behind that unintentional shelter, protected from the weather, the carving was crisp.

A standing figure, its arms raised in a position I knew.

I could have taken that stance with my eyes closed. I knew how the weight should settle through the feet, where the shoulders should loosen, and how far to turn the wrists.

I reached out to trace the grooves. The left hand was higher, angled inward. The right foot turned, its weight shifted to the outer edge. The qi indicators ran along the figure's spine, bifurcated at the shoulders, and pooled at the palms.

I knew where the pools were before my fingers reached them.

I stepped back.

The figure was me.

The proportions were wrong. The face was a stylized mask; shrines of this era didn't do portraiture. But the stance was mine. The flow was mine: the exact arrangement of body and energy that lets qi pass through a person instead of merely collecting inside.

I knew this technique. I could do it right now, standing here in this half-collapsed ruin with chalk grit between my teeth and cold stone under my boots. I could raise my arms; the qi would respond. The certainty was so complete that I searched my memory for a lesson, a place where I'd practiced it. I found nothing.

"You're staring."

You'd come in quietly, keeping clear of the loose tiles. Your notebook was in your hand, already open.

"I'm reading."

"You've been reading for a third of half a watch. The same panel."

"It's a long panel."

You came closer to study the carving over my shoulder. Your breath was warm on my shoulder, faster than resting pace. Your weight shifted forward. Your pen hovered above the notebook, and your eyes narrowed at the lines under my fingers.

"A cultivation technique," you said.

"Yes."

"Old."

"Pre-Consolidated."

"Can you read it?"

I took a moment. The honest answer was complicated. It was like reading a language I hadn't spoken in decades. I had to linger over each shape before it opened.

"Slowly," I said.

You wrote something. I didn't ask what. More of your observations went into that notebook than into our conversations. You opened it most often right after letting a question drop.

I went outside. The morning air was sharp with pine resin and the fading coolness of a night that hadn't been cold enough. Another wrong thing. This altitude, this season, the nights should bite. They didn't.

I raised my arms.

I lifted them only halfway, enough to test the first form. From that initial stance I opened the channels.

It responded at once, easy as an old exercise.

The qi flowed from my dantian through meridians I'd never consciously opened. A trickle had passed through those channels for so long that I had barely felt their course. Now they filled effortlessly. I followed every turn, every branch. The energy met no resistance.

I held the position for ten breaths. Each breath was easier than the last. Each one opened another branch, adding to a map I could follow without understanding it.

I dropped my arms.

My hands were shaking, though the exercise had taken scarcely any effort.

You stood in the doorway with your closed notebook pressed flat against your thigh. Your other hand rested against your sternum, over the old wound.

You dropped your hand when you saw me looking.

"How much was that?" Your voice was measured. You glanced from my hands to the grass, looking for something you could put a name to.

I walked past you into the shrine.

You followed after a moment, then returned to the panel to sketch the carving. Your reproduction was precise. You caught proportions that most people would blur into approximation. You'd labeled the qi indicators with your own notation, a shorthand I'd seen you develop over the months.

"Since when can you read Pre-Consolidated script?" you asked without looking up.

I let the question hang. The chalk dust was back between my teeth. That sweet incense smell hung in the ruin, though I still couldn't find its source.

"Since when do I read anything in front of you?"

"You didn't just read it. You traced it. Your fingers followed the flow lines before they got to them. I watched." Now you looked up. "You knew what came next."

Silence.

In the courtyard, the wind shifted just enough to stir the smoke from last night's embers. It drifted across the hearth instead of toward the doorway. I noted it. Wrong wind. Again.

"Write it down," I said.

You blinked. "Write what down?"

"Technique recognized. Never learned. Date." I paused. "Location. Conditions. Whatever you think matters."

You studied me for a long moment. Then you wrote. Your pen moved in clean, measured strokes and I could read the entry forming even from this distance. Your handwriting, unlike your personality, was transparent.

*She knew it before she read it,* the script said. Or something close to it. I didn't lean in far enough to read the rest.

I went back to the wall. There were more panels, more carvings, and more things I might know without having learned them. I had come looking for a mystery I could follow. Here it was, under my fingers.

I pressed my palms against the stone. Its cold sank through my skin, sharp as the metal fittings'. I had walked into an abandoned shrine, and now every intact panel held something of mine.

By afternoon I'd catalogued three more panels. Two were too damaged to read. Their symbols had worn down to shallow depressions in the stone. The third showed a formation I didn't recognize, and I breathed more easily.

I stood in front of that unknown panel for a long time, just to feel what it was like to look at something I had to actually read to know what it meant.

Normal. It felt normal.

Everything else felt like a homecoming I hadn't asked for.
