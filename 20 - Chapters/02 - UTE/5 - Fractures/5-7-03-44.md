As we walked I pressed against the crack, testing it like a loose tooth, checking whether the change continued. It gave. I leaned into that give, close to a memory, when the warning came.

The headache changed. The hot wire behind my eyes that had been the same for many days shifted and focused behind my left eye, aimed rather than general.

I stopped walking and pressed my hand to my face. I let the thought retreat. This time I backed away on my own, leaving the almost-memory where it was before anything took it from me.

Warning rather than deletion.

I'd felt this for months, since the headaches had started punishing the thought before I could think it. I'd read it as escalation, the defense getting smarter and crueler, tightening the perimeter. A strike.

This felt more like someone stepping into a doorway so I could see the path was blocked before I hit the barrier. A pointed, precisely located warning. Whatever kept me from remembering seemed, this time, to be asking me not to try.

Asking.

The word sat wrong. Asking implied someone who asked. Asking implied preferences, strategies, and the capacity to weigh approaches and select the one most likely to succeed. The wall hadn't asked, didn't ask, wouldn't ask. The wall erased. It was a reflex, a force, an automatic process that operated on rules.

But reflexes don't change strategy. Something had.

The thought arrived and was not taken. It sat in my consciousness unmolested. Something inside me was concerned about me.

I pressed against the crack again, gently, just enough to feel the movement, not enough to force the extraction.

The headache came. Behind the left eye. Sharp, exact, and immediate.

I stopped pressing. The headache receded.

Don't go there. I understood.

It felt almost tender. That unsettled me most. You might stop a child from touching a hot surface like this, with an urgent refusal born of knowing the consequence better than the child did.

"Something wrong?" you asked. You'd noticed the hand on my face. The trained observer, reading my body language as you read texts: for meaning, for pattern.

"Nothing. Just the headache."

You looked unconvinced. You took out your notebook and paused, the new hesitation, the request for consent that lived in it. Then you opened the book to write.

I didn't tell you what I'd felt. For once, I chose the silence myself. The feeling was private. Mine. It gave me the first sense of care behind the crack, of a part of me doing the only thing it knew how to do. I had spent so long treating it as an enemy that the possibility was hard to hold. The pain was still pain. Nothing about that had become kind. Yet there was a difference in how it let me withdraw, leaving the almost-memory unbroken behind it.

Protecting.

Badly. Bluntly. With the clumsy, everything-must-go approach of a guardian that had one tool and used it on everything. But protecting nevertheless.

I walked. The headache faded. The crack pulsed. The fox-spirit padded behind us, amber-eyed, patient.

The trail opened into a clearing. I braced for the full light, but it hit softer than yesterday. Bird calls still separated into individual voices, each with pitch, rhythm, and direction, though their sharpness had dulled to something almost bearable. I was learning to manage. The senses were still too much, still too detailed, but the too-much had shifted from drowning to wading. Progress, measured in the distance between unbearable and merely exhausting.

Somewhere under the wall, under the guardian, under the defenses that had been with me long before I knew of them, something eased. The headache loosened. As if something that had been bracing for a fight had realized the fight wasn't coming.

Not today. But I'd be back.
