I sat cross-legged on the rock shelf above our camp, eyes closed, breathing regulated, doing everything the cultivation texts said to do when seeking internal clarity. Breathe. Center. Focus. Turn awareness inward. Find the point where consciousness meets the deeper architecture. Observe.

A wall. Dressed as emptiness. A closed door painted to look like an open field.

I tried anyway. Every morning for days I'd sat here and tried. The rational approach: reconstruct what you had told me. What had been said. What it meant. What truths had been offered and taken. If I thought hard enough, carefully enough, approached from the right angle with the right technique, maybe the wall would thin. Maybe a crack would appear. Maybe some fragment of what had been erased would surface.

Think. For a fraction of a heartbeat I almost remembered, the truth as close as a word on the tip of my tongue. Then it dissolved. Frustration followed, and a seismic whisper ran under my feet as it turned to force again. Breathe. Suppress. Try again.

I thought, almost remembered, forgot, and began to shake with frustration. Try again.

Each attempt ended exactly where the last one had, with the same wall, the same gap that pulsed behind my eyes like a second heartbeat.

You sat thirty paces below, at the base of the slope, trying to be present without registering, occupying space without demanding attention. Ostensibly you were reading. Your notebook was open, but your eyes weren't on it.

You were studying me.

Your attention had the patience of a sculptor reading a cracked stone, searching for the fault line, the place where pressure had done its hidden work. You watched through each long silence, your notebook resting idle on your knees.

I closed my eyes again. Another cycle of thinking, almost remembering, forgetting, and frustration began, with the stubborn persistence of someone with no other strategy.

I shifted on the hard rock, then tried once more, apparently expecting a different result from a different sore spot.

Here is what I know now, from what you told me later and what I've pieced together.

You watched for a watch. A full watch of me sitting still, breathing, trying, and failing. You recorded the cycle, even timed it. About ninety heartbeats, on average, from attempt to failure. A neat package of intention and erasure that repeated with the regularity of tides.

You had opened your notebook to the pages from the past several tendays: the careful documentation of everything I'd done, everything I'd said, and everything that had happened near me since we'd entered these mountains. Things done to me. Things that happened because of me.

You already had the events mapped, cross-referenced, and annotated. Now you were looking for a pattern in what survived them.

The trees I'd split: I remembered being angry, standing there, hearing the wood tear. But I couldn't recover how the force had reached out from me to pull wood apart along the grain. The anger was clear, hovering in my chest without an anchor.

The rain: I remembered the grief and the rain falling from a clear sky. I remembered being wet. But the connection between my grief and the weather, the moment where one became the other, was gone. The sadness had nowhere to go.

The tremor: I remembered my frustration and the ground shaking. I remembered setting down the scrolls, watching stone move. But I couldn't reconstruct how emotion had become force. The frustration was intact. The path it had taken was blank.

The field: I remembered most of it as one remembers a fever dream, but its workings, the raw sensation of locking reality, and the understanding of what I'd done were fading. The edges softened every day. The details dissolved. But the horror held. So did the feeling of being something I didn't want to be.

The thoughts were deleted. The emotions were not.

Whatever governed the erasure operated below my awareness, without my consent. It erased what I knew but left the tremor in my hands, the tightness behind my ribs. Every experience lost its meaning but kept its residue, like a book with the text removed and the tear stains left on the pages.

You could trace the same distinction through every entry.

If the pattern held, you could reach me through what survived. Feeling might carry a truth past the erasure, past whatever stood between me and understanding.

You sat with this for a third of a watch, staring at your notebook, understanding what it meant, what you would have to do, and how much you hated every implication.

Making someone feel the thing you needed me to feel, deeply, viscerally, below the threshold of thought, would require cruelty. You had little practice in that and weren't ready to pay the cost of learning.

You closed your notebook.

You didn't tell me. Not yet. You stood, stretched, and walked back to camp as casually as if you hadn't just decided to do the worst thing you would ever do to someone you cared about. You boiled water. You prepared rice. You gave your hands the routine of evening, precise movements, while your mind worked.

I came down from the rock shelf at dusk, headache pounding. The failed meditation had left a deep exhaustion, as if I'd spent the day pressing against a locked door. I had sore shoulders to show for it, and the knowledge that the door was still locked.

"Anything?" you asked.

"No." I was too tired for more.

You handed me rice. I ate. The warm food tasted of nothing in particular and served its purpose: keeping my body functional while my mind ran the same failing pattern.

"It doesn't work," I said. It was the verbal equivalent of pressing a bruise: the pain was still there, nothing had changed.

"No," you agreed. "It doesn't."

Something in your voice. A color I hadn't heard before, under the agreement.

I almost asked. What aren't you telling me? The question was ready, loaded with the accusatory weight of a person who has learned that the people around her keep saying things she forgets.

But I didn't ask. I ate my rice, then worked at your meridians while my headache throbbed.

Night darkened the rock shelf above us. In the space between your silence and my exhaustion, you were forming a plan I knew nothing about. I wouldn't have approved of it. But it would shatter something that had needed shattering since before either of us understood what the word meant.
