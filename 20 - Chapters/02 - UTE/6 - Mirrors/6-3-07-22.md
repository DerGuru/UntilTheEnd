I opened my eyes in the morning to amber eyes close to mine. Xiǎohú's muzzle rested between my collarbones, her body curled against my ribs. The weight on my chest was slight, warm, and steady. She had slept close before. This morning she had settled fully on top of me.

I breathed. She breathed. Our breaths fell together for a while, her body rising gently against mine.

I reached up and scratched her ears.

You stirred, drawing a single deeper breath. You held it briefly before letting it go. Your eyes opened and found Xiǎohú on my chest. You looked from her to me with the small acknowledgment I had learned to recognize.

We ate the leftovers in silence before you started packing. You reduced the campsite to a carried load, dealing with the bedrolls, provisions, and fire in turn. You had made camp a thousand times and packed it away as often, always with the same attention to small things. Each object had its place. You checked each strap's tension and accounted for every item before securing the load.

I finished packing my stuff and stood by the dead fire.

"So," you said, adjusting a strap. "Did you?"

The question was careful.

I shook my head.

You waited instead of taking the headshake for an answer. I could neither accuse nor absolve myself, and I had no verdict to give you.

"I don't know," I said.

There was a third answer, though I had spent days searching for one of the other two.

I didn't know.

I let the question stay open.

I looked at the cold ashes. I had asked the question. Perhaps that was all I could do with it for now. Asking it had mattered, even if I could never make an answer follow.

This was the first act of letting go: the answer, the need for it, the demand that the universe produce a verdict.

I had no claim on that verdict.

Standing by the dead fire, I found something in uncertainty that certainty had never given me. A certainty could break under new evidence, a different perspective, or an understanding I hadn't had before. I had seen that happen. Admitting I didn't know left room for whatever came next. It held every possible answer, including the one I feared, without requiring me to choose before I could.

I picked up my pack. I shifted the objects inside it, adjusted the straps, and settled the load against my shoulders. It was a weight I knew how to accommodate.

I carried it.

We walked.

The southern trail held no surprises. Rocks lay where I saw them; roots crossed the ground ahead. I stepped around the ordinary obstacles of a path I wasn't shaping. Whatever in me had made those casual changes was quiet. I still carried the question, unable to produce the certainties that had opened the trail before us.

But I was walking. We were walking.

It felt different from the walking of the previous days. Then I had followed you because you moved, letting an old habit carry me until you stopped. I could have crossed another valley without deciding why I was crossing it.

This morning I decided to walk.

I chose to carry the pack with the question still unanswered. I would bring the uncertainty, the guilt, and the memory with me. Walking was what I could do, and I let that be enough for the moment, though it settled nothing. I could take a step without knowing whether the step would be sufficient. I could take the next one too.

Xiǎohú walked next to me, her ears barely reaching my knee, her fur warm against my calf when the trail narrowed. She had moved closer since the anomalies stopped. Perhaps I had become easier to approach. In the quiet left by those changes, her body pressed lightly against my leg. I let her keep that place.

I walked with the question still present. Sunlight reached the trail, but the uncertainty came with me through each bright patch.

You walked behind. Three steps. Your distance. Your place in the formation.

"South?" you asked.

"South," I confirmed.

The mountains rose. The trail climbed. The world was imperfect, unshaped, and ordinary. I continued with it.

The question followed.

It would always follow.
