Xiǎohú returned from her day's wandering at dusk with a small rabbit in her jaws. She dropped it by the fire, sat down, and looked at us. You cleaned it while I watched. We shared it in three portions, two for us and one for her. She ate hers slowly in the grass, then curled up by the fire and closed her eyes.

Later, your breathing settled into a rhythm that might have been deliberate. You were asleep, or you were giving me the appearance of being alone, having learned that some experiments were easier without a witness. The fire you had lit with flint burned steadily through the wood.

I held my hand over it.

Xiǎohú opened one eye.

"I am mortal."

I whispered the words this time. I had brought the experiment close enough to watch every detail. My intention rested on a single hand, with the firelight on it, and I knew exactly what I was asking to change.

The flame bent.

Flame curved around my fingers, dividing as water divides around a stone. Orange light moved across my skin. Its bright edges passed within a hair's breadth, and the heat they should have carried never came. The glow lay where I expected a burn.

I pressed my hand into the fire.

I reached into the center, where the wood glowed brightest. Even a cultivator's hardened body should have burned. I held my palm there long enough to be certain of where I had put it.

Nothing.

The flame wrapped around my palm and fingers without damaging them. The warmth was just enough to tell me where I had put my skin, and it stayed mild while the wood burned beneath it.

You were up.

"What—" You grabbed my arm and pulled. You knew what I could survive, but you had just seen my hand in a fire. You held my wrist firmly after pulling it free. Your eyes moved over the palm, then along each finger.

Nothing. Perfect, unmarked, impossibly intact skin.

You breathed heavily, looking from the hand to the fire, then at me. Your grip loosened.

"It doesn't work," I said.

"What, believing you're mortal?"

"That's the point." I looked at my unmarked palm. I could say mortal as often as I wanted without meaning it where it mattered. "I can say it. I can want it. But I don't really believe it. Not in the way it counts."

You sat back while the fire crackled. I had put my hand into it wanting to be vulnerable. Under that wish I had stayed certain of my own invulnerability. One was a wish I had formed by the fire. The other I had lived for millennia, so deeply established that I scarcely recognized it as a belief.

"It's a circle," I said, following it aloud. "I know I'm making myself immortal. I want to stop. But I have to believe I can stop. That means believing immortality isn't what I am. Every part of my life tells me it is. The belief in stopping comes up against the belief in being. Being wins. It's older, deeper, and more complete than anything I can reach by wanting."

"To believe you don't believe," you said.

"Exactly."

"That's a problem."

"Yes."

The fire burned while I walked the same circle again. I had told myself I no longer believed in immortality. The statement gave me another thought to hold, but the old certainty lay under it. I could add explanations all night without removing what I was trying to change. The fingers you still held were perfectly whole.

You looked down at my hand, still resting in yours now that the urgency had passed. Your fingers were warm. The same fire could have burned them. I held still.

"Then how?" you said.

"I don't know."

We sat in the quiet, looking at a difficulty neither of us could find a way through. The wood settled in the fire.

"Yet," I added. My frustration was still a reason to try. Failing tonight didn't require me to fail forever. As long as I was immortal, time was the one thing I could afford.

Xiǎohú watched with eyes that reflected a fire that couldn't burn the woman she watched.

You let go of my hand.

"But I will figure it out. I have to. I want to. I need to."
