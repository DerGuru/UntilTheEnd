We walked under trees I hadn't seen the day before. Their roots crossed stretches of trail we had already walked; by evening, even the streams had shifted. Over the next three days, stone formations appeared on hillsides with weathered faces that should have taken ages to form. You remembered each one as part of the mountain.

The sect kept to the ridges above us. Each time I felt for them, their cultivation was stronger. I could neither control the changes feeding them nor explain why they continued.

Yet I walked easily.

That ease troubled me more than the altered hillsides. Behind the crack, the images of hands in light had settled into a quiet presence. The jumbled impressions that used to break through were gone. I stepped around new roots without anger. Occasionally a changed slope looked so natural that I wondered why it had taken this long to become what it was.

That thought should have triggered the wall.

It didn't.

On the fourth morning, you unrolled a map. Your small, precise script filled the spaces between hand-drawn contours. You had recorded this terrain at every stop, checking distances before committing them to ink.

"Wrong," you said, looking at it.

"What?"

"The map. It's wrong. I must have made a mistake. The trail I drew six days ago doesn't match the trail we walked yesterday. The creek is in a different place. The slopes are—look."

You held the map toward me. I followed your careful lines with a finger, then looked out at the landscape. The creek had moved. The ridge had shifted. The valley you had placed to the east now lay south of us.

Your lines matched what I remembered. I held the map steady while you looked again.

The ink was exactly where you'd placed it six days ago. What it described had changed so completely that you could no longer remember drawing an accurate map.

You studied the creek's course on the paper. Your hands had gone still. You had drawn this so you would have something to trust beyond your own memory. Now you could lay the record beside the landscape, but you doubted the record.

"It's changing faster," I said.

"Yes."

"We can't stay here."

You looked at me. "Where would we go?"

"Forward. South. Toward whatever this is leading to."

"You think it's leading somewhere?"

The pull was strongest when I faced south. Metal lay thick on my tongue; the vibration continued through my teeth. The hands in light persisted behind the quiet wall. Xiǎohú had been turning in that direction since yesterday, even while we rested. I couldn't prove that any of it led somewhere. But each day brought me closer to whatever the wall had hidden.

"We can't prepare for this," I said. "There's nothing to prepare."

"Then what?"

"We move. We go toward it. Because standing still is just letting them farm the changes while whatever's coming takes its time arriving."

You folded the map carefully along its original creases.

"The sect," you said.

"Will follow."

"The anomalies."

"Will continue."

"Your wall."

"Dormant."

You stored the map, stood, and looked south. Higher mountains rose past our valley, their steep faces weathered bare. Xiǎohú stood at the edge of the camp facing them. She took a step forward, then looked back at us, as she had been doing since yesterday.

"You know where you're going?" you asked.

"I know where I'm being pulled."

"Same thing?"

"I don't know yet."

You shouldered your pack, adjusted the straps, and checked the knife at your belt.

"Alright," you said. "South it is."

Xiǎohú was already moving south. She had started before you agreed, following the direction she kept returning to. She passed the first bend. She went without hesitation; I still had a folded map's worth of reasons to hesitate.

We followed her. The trail ahead widened. Rough ground smoothed under the next bend, leaving a clear route through terrain that had looked difficult from the camp. I watched for each alteration. The path was opening before we reached it.

Behind us, the sect followed. A hundred and twelve figures moved parallel to our path at the same careful distance. They could keep harvesting what happened around me without coming any closer.

The metallic taste intensified. The vibration hummed through my teeth. Behind the thinning wall, the hands went on shaping light. Their movements were quiet, constant, and certain.

Forward.

We moved.
