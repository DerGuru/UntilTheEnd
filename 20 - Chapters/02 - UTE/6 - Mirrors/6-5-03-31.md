I woke under six mountain pines arranged in a perfect circle. Their spacing was equal. So was their height. Each trunk rose straight to a balanced triangular crown. Their roots spread in matching patterns. I sat up and looked along a circle so precise that it was hard to believe these were the uneven trees we had camped under.

"Those didn't stand like that," you said. You sat by the dead fire with kindling in your hands. You had stopped trying to light it while staring at the trees.

The trees looked as if they had always stood like that. The moss confirmed it. The earth confirmed it. The undergrowth between the trunks had grown in patterns consistent with decades of circular symmetry.

"No," I said. "They didn't."

Yet every old root suggested they had. My beliefs had gone on shaping what surrounded me while I slept. The changes required no decision. Sleep took away even the chance to notice what I was doing.

I shaped the world in my sleep.

I sat with that, looking from one pine to the next. My beliefs went on through every moment, every breath.

Day and night. Every day of the year. Multiply it by a thousand years, then by far more. I had never stopped believing long enough to leave even one night untouched.

Every tree I'd ever walked past. Every stone I'd ever noticed. Every path I'd ever traveled. How much of what I saw was what existed and how much was what I believed existed? How much of the world was the world and how much was me?

"I can't turn it off," I said. "Not even in sleep."

You studied the circle before turning back to me. "That means everything you interact with—"

"Gets shaped. Yes."

"Everything you see."

"Yes."

"Everything you believe about."

"Yes."

"Even me."

"Yes."

We both looked at Xiǎohú. She lay between two of the perfect pines, sprawled on her side, one ear folded, one paw twitching in whatever foxes dream about. The only asymmetric thing in the clearing.

The circle stood in the morning light, beautiful enough to make the change more disturbing. It was my idea of what trees should look like, imposed on their growth while I slept among them. My certainty had been so quiet that it hadn't woken me.

I couldn't turn it off.

I had understood some of that already, but the pines made it visible. I walked around each tree, examined the bark, and touched the old roots. Every part fitted the new arrangement.

"The sect—" you started.

"Is coming."

Their cultivation signatures had sharpened since I knelt in the river. More than a hundred cultivators moved through the mountains toward us, their approach distinct from the background presence I had grown used to.

They were closer than they'd been.

They were always going to come. The anomalies had fed them before. They would feed them again for as long as I existed and they were within reach of the changes I made.

Hiding would give them a new place to find me. Running would leave a trail. I had shaped things through millennia, tree by tree, stone by stone, and night after night. They fed on what those changes left behind.

Only one way would end that supply. I liked it no better for being clear. It was what I had been approaching all along.

Xiǎohú stood up to stretch her front legs, then her back ones. She shook herself, trotted past the perfect pines, and disappeared into the undergrowth without looking at them.

Hunting. Probably.

We gathered our belongings and walked. South.
