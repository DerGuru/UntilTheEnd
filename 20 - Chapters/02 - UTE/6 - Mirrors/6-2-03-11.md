We kept walking after the village had disappeared behind the trees. You trailed three steps behind, silent. Now and then your breathing changed, the small pause before an exhalation that meant you were working through something difficult.

We stopped at a clearing farther south, where the canopy opened to sky and afternoon light fell in long, slanted columns that turned the dust into gold.

I sat on a rock, looking from the dirt at my feet to the stones of an old fire pit. A broken tree lay across the ground next to its stump. I studied the split wood, then looked at my hands.

They had the same lines, the same uncallused skin, and the same short nails. I had held chopsticks with them that morning. These hands had pulled weeds, touched bark, and pressed against the cold earth two nights ago. I turned them over. When I made fists, the tendons rose as they always had. I opened them again. Nothing visible had changed.

Half a watch ago, I had believed thirty people elsewhere.

I had done it consciously.

I held each part of that thought while looking at my palms. I had stood in a village square, believed thirty people elsewhere, and watched them vanish.

The wall didn't intervene.

I waited for the headache to sharpen, for the pressure that preceded a missing thought. The wall was still there when I reached inward, and quiet. I considered the empty square again, deliberately this time. The memory held.

Perhaps I had outgrown what it could keep from me. Perhaps this particular thought was safe. I couldn't tell.

"Thirty people," I said into the clearing. Dust drifted through the golden light. Xiǎohú lay awake at my feet, her warmth against my ankles. "Same effort as one. Same effort as none."

You leaned against a tree with your arms crossed, far enough away to leave me my silence.

"Not killed," you said. "Not fought. Not even touched."

"No."

"So how did you do it?"

"I just decided they weren't there."

You let a breath out slowly before answering.

"That's not a technique," you said.

You uncrossed your arms, pushed off the tree, and walked two steps closer. Your shadow fell across my hands. I looked up. You pressed your lips together, watching to see how I would take the question.

"When you believed them gone," you said, "did you choose where they went?"

I tried to recall a destination. In the square, I had been certain only that the cultivators were absent. I hadn't pictured the forest or considered a distance. Yet they had appeared two hundred paces away. Perhaps that was simply the nearest place outside the village where they could all stand.

"No," I said. "I just believed them not there."

"And the not-there became somewhere." You glanced toward the forest. "Not dead. Not injured. Placed two hundred paces in the forest."

You were making a point.

"Your belief did not exclude harm," you said. "You didn't choose their safety consciously. But something in how you believe seems to spare people where it can."

I stared at you.

"This could have been used as a weapon, but you aren't one," you said.

Your shadow still covered my hands. I remembered another man standing that close, a courtyard under my feet, blood dark on the flagstones. His hand had rested heavily on my shoulder. He had learned that he could name a place while touching me, then send me there to destroy it. A direction, a city. I had gone every time.

The city had been rubble by the time I finished. Soon afterward I had destroyed the man who sent me. I walked away from the ashes.

I had walked for a long time after that, until I found a boy with the wrong mushrooms in his basket, a spirit boar bearing down on him.

I looked at my hands again. The same hands. Always the same.

I was a weapon once. Never again. No killing, if I can avoid it.

The metallic taste was fading. The vibration in my teeth had quieted to a hum. Warm afternoon light reached past your shadow into my lap, and my hands lay in it while I thought about what I had done. Thirty people were alive in the forest because I had believed them out of the village.

I remembered choosing it. Belief had been enough.

And the wall had let me.

I closed my hands.

I opened them.

The same hands.

Everything different.
