Your hand pressed my shoulder, gently first, then harder. You had spent a watch and a half trying to reach me. You had used my name in every tone from whisper to shout. Now you shook me, the gentler ways exhausted.

I blinked.

The clearing arrived with painful sharpness. Everything was too much: the light too bright, the air too cold, the grass too green. Each of your fingers registered separately on my shoulder. My senses had sharpened again during the absence, and I had no distance from anything they gave me.

"How long was I gone?"

"More than a watch and a half."

Your face was heavy with tiredness. You had spent that watch and a half watching me and writing what you could, unable to know whether you were helping. Your eyes were red. Your hand trembled when you took it from my shoulder.

I reached for the ground. Blades of grass pressed into my palm, each impossibly exact in its shape, green against the soil that held them. I gripped hard. The blades bent and the roots held. I left my hand there, pressed against the resistance.

I needed the earth under me, its weight against my knees, until I could be certain I was here. The place of creation lay behind me. I was in a clearing on a mountain, and beside me sat a man with a notebook and a knife, more patient than I had any right to expect.

"You were speaking," you said. "Words I didn't recognize. But I wrote what I could."

Your notebook lay open in your lap. Tight, rushed handwriting filled the pages. You had done the one thing you could.

I couldn't read it yet. Creation still echoed through the clearing, the remembered place bright behind what I saw. That earlier self, before names or forms, believed with a certainty indistinguishable from making.

The connections came quickly. I held the grass while moments from every age of my life turned toward a meaning I had avoided. The events were the same. What I understood about them changed.

A wound closed under my certainty that I couldn't be harmed. Through it I saw Wei's face, too young, too stubborn. The mountain cracked in the memory of the shockwave. Stone split where I had been. Paths opened ahead of me. Weather shifted around expectations I hadn't known were shaping anything.

Wei fell in the account Shen had given me. The settlement gate stood behind the body I reached too late. Everything I loved died. I had carried that belief while wanting him to live.

Not now. Enough. Keep going.

The voice was mine. It had always been mine, speaking through the few phrases I let past the barrier. Every healing belonged to me. Each survival came from a conviction as complete as the one that moved a stone or sent water along a different course.

The reflection smiled before I did. The field closed around us with rules I hadn't consciously chosen. Coincidences gathered into a history of my own actions. The paths cleared because I believed in clear paths. The weather matched what I expected of it. People appeared at moments when I was certain of an outcome.

Wei returned through all of it. The deeper conviction had held against my wish to save him. Your words about a pattern stood beside the question of murder. I had believed everything I loved died as surely as I had believed I couldn't burn. Both beliefs were mine.

The second voice knew. Some part of me had always known, speaking from behind the wall in the only ways I permitted. I recognized it now through the pressure behind my eyes.

The missing mountain flank, the reversed water, and the shifted stones arrived together. The reflection showed through the memory of the shockwave. The field pressed in while I held grass in the clearing. Every impossible thing illuminated the others. I couldn't separate one revelation from the next, because each led back to the same source.

The recognition passed through my whole body. I gripped the grass harder as each memory changed its meaning. I had spent ages looking for the source of my survival outside myself. I had been looking from that source all along.

I hadn't been surprised. I should have been.

The reframing produced only confirmation, the terrible kind that arrives when the thing you suspected turns out to be true. I'd known. The part of me behind the wall, the part that smiled in reflections, had always known. It was old information, finally acknowledged.

I sat in the grass. The clearing was bright. The fog was gone. Direct sunlight showed every blade between my fingers, every mark on your tired face.

I knew.
