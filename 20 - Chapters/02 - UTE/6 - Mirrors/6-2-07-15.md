Wood shavings lay at your feet in the evening firelight. You sat with your knife against a pale piece of wood, drawing another thin curl from its surface. The fire crackled between us.

I hadn't seen you whittle before that afternoon. You had picked up the wood by the trail, drawn your knife, and begun shaping it as we walked. I had tried to put the collapse behind me while you worked. Now the same careful rhythm continued at the fire. The blade scraped quietly along the grain.

While you carved, I pulled out the notebook.

It was the second notebook, the one you'd made for me when the forgetting started. Clean leather covered pages of your larger handwriting, divided into sections I could find even on a bad morning. For months I had read it every day. Later I had begun adding my own observations in the spaces between yours.

I turned past your headings, summaries, and careful record of my life. My own entries began on separate pages. Under each one I had left room for observations I never added.

*It intervened again. Whatever protects me acted to prevent the fall. I neither asked for this nor invoked it. Something beyond my control kept me from harm.*

I ran my thumb below the words. I had written about a protector outside myself with total confidence, though I had never found a guardian, an object, or a god I could point to.

I tore the page carefully along the spine. The quiet ripping sounded louder than your knife against the wood.

Xiǎohú's head came up. She'd been dozing by the fire, but the sound of tearing paper brought her over in a quick trot, paws silent on the earth. She sat down a pace away, watching.

I laid it on the ground by my knee.

Next page.

*Something shields me. I do not understand its nature or its motivation. The shielding is consistent. It activates in moments of physical danger and produces outcomes that redirect or eliminate the threat.*

Something shields me. Again I had placed the source outside myself without showing why. My steady handwriting made the claim look better supported than it was.

I tore it out and laid it beside the first.

Next.

*The healing was instantaneous. The wound closed between one breath and the next. I had used neither cultivation nor medicine. I cannot account for it. Conclusion: something outside me hastened the healing. Its origin remains unknown.*

Something outside me. I removed that page too, laying it beside the others.

I read the fourth page, tore it free, and laid it down. The fifth followed. You carved on. Once your eyes went to the growing row by my knee. You asked nothing.

Xiǎohú moved to the edge of the row. Her head tilted left as each new sheet joined it. When I put down the fifth page, she sniffed the paper before looking up at me. Her ears flattened. I held my hand still until they rose again.

Sixth.

*I cannot account for surviving so many lethal encounters by chance. Whatever protects me is consistent, powerful, and concerned specifically with my survival. I suspect a guardian, perhaps bound to me when my cultivation began.*

A guardian. I nearly laughed at the word under my finger. I had investigated my own survival thoroughly enough to blame someone I couldn't find. I laid the sixth page down.

Seventh. Last.

*I do not know what protects me or why. I know only that it exists, that it is separate from me, and that it has consistently kept me alive. For now, this is enough. Understanding can wait.*

Understanding can wait.

I pulled the final page free.

Seven pages lay by my knee. The observations were accurate, written in a steady hand. I read along the repeated conclusions. Each put the cause outside me. After the village, I could no longer believe that. Whatever was happening involved me more directly than I had admitted. Beyond that I couldn't say.

The explanations had been convenient. I could blame an unseen protector, then leave the question alone. I had done exactly that on the page under my hand.

Perhaps the wall had helped me settle on those words. It took memories from me; I filled the gaps with explanations that kept my own part out of sight. Together, the pages showed how seldom I had questioned that assumption.

I picked up the first page and put it in the fire.

The paper curled, blackened, and broke into ash. I watched the words disappear along its edge. The flame hesitated for an instant. I held still until it rose again. It might have been an ordinary flicker.

I fed the remaining pages into the fire one at a time. I could still read each conclusion as I lowered it. Then the ink vanished into the blackening paper.

Xiǎohú watched each page travel from my hand to the fire. Her nose twitched at the smoke. She leaned forward when the paper curled, then drew back from the heat.

You carved on. The pale wood had begun to take a shape I couldn't yet name. Another shaving fell by your foot.

"Done?" you said without looking up.

"Done."

The last strip of paper burned. I rested my hand on the open notebook. Your record was still there, its headings intact. Where I had written explanations I no longer trusted, seven torn edges showed along the binding.
