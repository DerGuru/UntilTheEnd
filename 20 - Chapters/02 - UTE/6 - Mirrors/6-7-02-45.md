The tree was old. Older than you, younger than me, a comparison that applied to most things, a taxonomy so broad it was functionally useless. Old in the tree-sense. The trunk was wider than two men standing shoulder to shoulder, the bark ridged deep enough to lose a finger in. It had been growing long enough to stop being a tree and start being geography. The roots had wrapped around stones and incorporated them. The canopy was its own weather system.

We rested under it. The shade was cool, the filtered light and temperature large trees make for themselves. I worked on your meridians first. The channels were mostly clear now, clean enough that the qi moved through them like water through an unblocked stream. What remained was the core itself. The crack. The fracture I had caused, the one everything else had been compensating for. The channels around it were ready. The core wasn't.

You sat against a root afterward, your pack next to you, the knife in your hand, a small folding blade you used for everything from peeling ginger to cleaning your nails to the activity you were performing now.

Carving.

A stick. Pine, probably. You'd been shaping it since we sat down, long strokes with the blade, the shavings curling off, the rhythm of someone who knew how to use a knife on wood and wasn't invested in the outcome. The last time you'd carved, months ago, you never finished it. Whatever it had been not-becoming, it had burned bright when you fed it to the fire.

You stopped. Looked at the shape. Whatever it had been becoming, it wasn't that anymore. You snapped it and tossed the pieces.

The gesture was casual, the disposal of something that had served its purpose, hand-occupation, and no longer needed to exist.

"I knew," you said.

The words came as casually as the tossed wood.

I waited.

"Not the details." You folded the knife. Put it back in your pocket. Looked at the roots, the stone, the tree's patient architecture. "The feeling."

You turned your head. Your gaze was direct and unguarded, the openness you allowed yourself every now and then when the clinical mask served no purpose and the rawness underneath was the more accurate communication.

"I've known since the beginning that something was... not right isn't the word. Not wrong. Different. That you were different in a way that wasn't just ability or cultivation or any of the explanations I pretended were sufficient. I didn't write it in the notebook. Not explicitly. But the feeling, the feeling was there from the start."

I looked at the tree. The roots wrapped stone, incorporating what couldn't be moved.

You continued. "When you knelt in the river, when you said it, I wasn't surprised. I was confirmed. The way you confirm a diagnosis that you've suspected since the first symptom. Relief and grief simultaneously. Relief because the uncertainty ends. Grief because the certainty is large."

No shock.

I nodded.

The leaves rustled. A breeze, nothing else.

What you didn't say was why you had stayed.

Maybe because it didn't need saying. Maybe the saying would have been smaller than the fact. Some truths are better demonstrated than articulated.

You could have walked away at any point. You had legs, a mind, and the competence of a scholar who could survive alone in any terrain. Each morning, each step, each day, you chose to walk with me, into the mountains, toward the thing I needed to do, which was also the thing that might end everything.

You stayed because staying was your answer.

The leaves rustled again. We were silent for a while.

"The blossoms are early," you said.

"You already mentioned that," I said.

"I guess I did."

Almost a smile. Yours. Mine. The shared micro-expression of two people who had traveled together long enough that their humor had synchronized, their references a private language no one else would find funny or even comprehensible.

We sat under the tree that was older than you and younger than me. The silence was communication. The breeze moved the leaves, the leaves made sounds, and the sounds were enough.

Xiǎohú appeared from wherever she had been investigating and sat near us. The triangle of woman, man, and fox that constituted whatever we were. Something without a name, no less real for being unnamed.

After a while, we packed and continued. The path climbed.

You walked beside me.
