Our rice, the evening portion, cooked with whatever herbs the trailside offered, seasoned with the care of a man who treated meals as architecture. The rice was warm. The herbs were adequate. The eating was routine.

Until the voice. It said: *You already know.*

Someone else's words, arriving from inside, uninvited, as clear as a stranger's voice across a quiet room.

I stopped chewing.

*You already know.*

The voice was...

It wasn't like before. It wasn't compressed, or a vague impression arriving like morse code from behind the wall. It wasn't an internal dialogue between my surface self and someone else deep inside, arguing across a divide.

This was something else. Clear. Conversational. A part of me sitting across from me, speaking in my own voice from a chair I hadn't known was occupied. It came from somewhere that wasn't the place where I was thinking, yet I recognized it, the way you recognize your own voice on a recording even though it sounds wrong.

I set the chopsticks down.

The thoughts went on, quiet and present and available, like a conversation partner who had always been sitting at the table while I couldn't see them because the chair faced the wrong way. Now the chair was turning. Slowly. We weren't face to face yet, but I could see the outline, and I could hear the words without decoding them first.

The split that had defined my interior was closing. The two voices were no longer arguing across a wall. They were in the same room, speaking the same language. And I was beginning to suspect they were the same person.

The split had been an illusion, or was becoming one, the recognition arriving as a series of small corrections, each making the previous understanding less wrong.

The voice had been real. The content had been accurate. The split itself, the perception that there were two of me, that the surface self and the deeper self were separate entities, that those fragments had come from somewhere else: that was the illusion. The wall's last defense. If you can't hide the truth from someone, convince them it's coming from outside, so they can disbelieve it.

My self-protection had made me think my own voice was someone else's.

I sat with the recognition and let it settle. The voice that had said *Not now* at the field. The voice that had said *Enough*. The voice that had said *Keep going*. They were all me. No guardian, nothing separate. Me, hearing the words and attributing them to an external source. Me, speaking to me through the only channel the wall left open, three words at a time, compressed, urgent, disguised as foreign.

I'd been talking to myself. I guess that's what happens when you are alone for too long.

The foreignness was fading. What replaced it wasn't certainty, not yet, but a growing fit. The voice that had felt like someone else's was settling into place, the way a glove that's been in a drawer for years resists the hand at first, then remembers the shape.

"You stopped eating," you said.

I looked at my bowl. Rice, half finished, the chopsticks resting across the rim. It had been sitting in front of me while my attention was elsewhere. The world had gone on with its physical operations while the interior landscape reconfigured.

"Hearing something," I said.

Your face shifted. "Hear what?"

"Me." The word felt almost right. Almost.

You narrowed your eyes and held your breath, the pause that preceded your most careful responses.

"What's different?"

"It's clearer now. Not just fragments. Just... thoughts. Mine. But from deeper inside." Each word was a small act of claiming, of trying the voice on like a coat that might be mine.

Something shifted, quiet and almost invisible. The voice I'd feared had been my own all along, and it had been trying to help.

The self-protection had been me.

A part of me, the oldest part, the part that remembered, had been protecting me from knowledge I had decided I wasn't ready for. It hid the truth out of care, not malice, the way a parent hides sharp objects from a child until the child can handle the sharpness.

I had been protecting myself from myself.

And now the protection was no longer needed. The truth was no less sharp, but the self was ready for sharpness.

Or perhaps just no longer able to look away.

I picked up the chopsticks, found the rice, and went on eating.

Slower than before. Deliberately slower. Each grain a presence, each bite a choice, each swallow an act of attention that the previous version of eating hadn't contained. I finished the rice. Slowly. Completely.

You watched me eat the way you watched everything, with the sustained attention of a man for whom observation was devotion.

*You already know*, the voice had said. And it was right. I had known since I knelt in the river. But knowledge and experience are separate things, like the distance between reading about fire and holding your hand over a flame.

Now, with the rice eaten and Xiǎohú's breathing soft nearby, I was beginning to *experience* it. And that was something else entirely.
