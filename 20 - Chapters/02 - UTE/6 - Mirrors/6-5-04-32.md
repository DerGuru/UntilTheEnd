They found us on an open field. Grass. A few wildflowers. A space that exists in mountains because mountains occasionally produce flatness. Unremarkable.

A hundred and thirty-nine cultivators. I counted because counting gave me something to do with the part of my mind that wasn't preparing for what I was about to do. A hundred and thirty-nine. Twenty-seven more than the ridge. Six weeks of feeding on my unconscious edits and they'd grown by a quarter. They'd recruited, or merged, or produced new members through whatever mechanism sects used to grow.

The Pencil stood at the center. Lian Zhuo. The name arrived unbidden, from somewhere I hadn't stored it. His real name, the one I'd avoided knowing because knowing names meant knowing people and knowing people meant caring. I didn't want to care, given what I was about to do.

His gaze settled on me, curious, a man who had studied something from a distance and was now close enough to see its details.

"We want to understand," he said.

The words carried clearly across the field on the resonance of a cultivator's controlled voice. Simple words. Honest, possibly. They came from someone who had been harvesting my unconscious edits and wanted to know the source.

I stood between them, Xiǎohú pressed against my leg.

You were fifty paces behind. I'd sent you back, my hand on your chest, the push that said *not you, not here, not now*. Your face had shown the argument you didn't make. You'd looked at mine, and whatever you saw there was enough. You swallowed the protest and stepped back.

Lian Zhuo waited. Patient. They were always patient.

"You don't understand," I said. My voice boomed across the field without effort. I didn't need a cultivator's resonance. "I want to be left alone."

Simple. Clear. The same thing I'd wanted since the mountains. Since the ridge. Since the first time I'd noticed them following, collecting, building their little empire on the residual energy of my existence.

Lian Zhuo's expression didn't change. The curiosity stayed, but something harder moved behind it, something that had been patient for six weeks and had decided that patience was no longer the strategy.

"We've been following you for months," he said. His voice was different now. The scholarly politeness was gone. He had committed too much to walk away, and it showed. "Tracking your movements. Mapping your patterns. Adjusting our entire sect's trajectory to accommodate your wandering."

He stepped forward. One step. The seventeen behind him shifted with him, a formation move, practiced.

"You will come with us."

Xiǎohú hissed.

The sound cut across the field, small, sharp, disproportionate. A fox-spirit the size of a cat, ears flat, teeth bared at a hundred and thirty-nine cultivators. Absurd. Brave. The hiss of something that didn't calculate odds.

Hadn't he learned from the stasis field? Was he so sure now of himself, with his gathered power, with my power, that he could force me?

Me?

Something broke. Like a rope that has been fraying for weeks: one strand, then another, then all of them at once, and the thing it held crashes down.

Months. Months of being followed, of being farmed for the excess. Months of walking south and knowing they walked behind. Of adjusting routes. Of pretending the shadow wasn't there. Of being someone's crop, someone's resource, someone's *project*.

And now demands. Now *you will come with us*. As if I were something to be collected.

Lian Zhuo didn't look at the fox. His eyes never left me. "The energy you produce unconsciously could advance our entire—"

My gaze went to Lian Zhuo, then to the seventeen around him, his inner circle, the ones who understood what they'd been harvesting, who had built their methodology around it, who had turned parasitism into practice.

*Gone.*

A single thought, a statement of fact aimed at eighteen people who had made themselves into a problem I no longer intended to have. Whatever the Pencil was about to say, he would never finish the sentence. The seventeen around him, the architects, the strategists, the ones who had known exactly what they were doing. Gone.

I turned to the rest.

A hundred and twenty-one faces. The outer ring. The followers, the joiners, the ones who had come because sects offered purpose, structure, and the illusion of progress.

"Scatter."

Gone as well. Distributed across the land like a hand sweeping pieces off a board. One to a southern province. One to a mountain pass. One to a river delta. A hundred and twenty-one people in a hundred and twenty-one different locations, spread everywhere, alone, separated, the sect dissolved by geography instead of confrontation.

The field was empty.

Grass. Wind. Wildflowers. Morning light on nothing.

The silence was absolute, the silence of an emptied stage.

I stood on the empty field. Grass under my feet. Wind in my hair. The metallic taste overwhelming. My hands were steady. That was the worst part, the steadiness.

You walked toward me fast. A hundred people had vanished before your eyes, and I was the only one left to ask.

You reached me.

"What did you do?" Fear thinned your voice.

"I removed them."

"Where?"

"The inner circle, from everywhere. I don't know." I gestured at the field, the treeline, the mountains, the nothing. "The rest are scattered. Across the land. Individually."

"You don't know?" You repeated it, testing the words. "You don't know where eighteen people are?"

"No."

"Are they alive?"

Wind. Grass. The empty field.

"I didn't believe them dead," I said. Which wasn't the same as believing them alive and we both knew it.

Your eyes went over the field. The grass, untrampled. The wildflowers undisturbed. As if a hundred and thirty-nine people hadn't stood here thirty heartbeats ago. Even the evidence was gone.

Power outward. Failure inward.

I'd solved the external problem. Completely. Permanently. With casual efficiency.

And nothing inside me had changed.

I was still immortal. Still unable to burn. Still shaping the world. Still running the machinery. But at least there was nobody left to harvest.

I sat down in the grass. The field was empty. The sky was clear. The world was exactly what I believed it to be.

I was cold inside, lodged somewhere behind the sternum, the residue of what I'd just done as easily as brushing crumbs off a table. A hundred and thirty-nine people. Brushed away. My hands hadn't even trembled. No hesitation where hesitation should have been.

You stood beside me. You didn't sit down.

"Now what?" you said.

I didn't answer. Didn't have a real answer.

That was the problem.
