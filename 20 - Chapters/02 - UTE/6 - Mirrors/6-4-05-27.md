Memories crowded in as my knees bent. I went down into the river, and the cold rose to my hips. My clothes spread around me, darkening as they took up water. Whatever I had understood while standing, I was kneeling now, soaked to the skin.

You stepped toward the bank's edge, then stopped.

You shifted your weight forward. Your hand rose toward me and halted. You looked ready to come in, then saw me hold myself upright in the water. Earlier you had written for more than a watch before shaking me. Now you were deciding again whether I needed reaching for.

You stayed on the bank.

Memories came from the whole span between that remembered beginning and the river where I knelt. I couldn't arrange them by time. One arrived with the force of what I had felt there, followed by another from ages before it. I held them together while the cold pressed against my waist.

I saw the original hands, before the hands I had now, shaping light into form. Whatever I believed took shape. The solitude of that act stood out more sharply than its scale. Creating was what I was. I went on creating because the alternative was the ending I feared.

Then came the fear that had preceded immortality. I might cease to exist. The dizziness of that possibility returned, the grasping, and the desperate conviction that I must not end. The belief became true. I built my form around it, and I built the self who would carry that form through everything that followed.

Then came millennia of fighting, killing, and walking. Thousands of faces appeared, people I had spoken to or simply passed. I had forgotten most of them. Time had worn them away beyond what the wall could restore. I had lived long enough to forget languages, cultures, continents, and epochs. New lives occupied places where old ones had once been clear.

Among the thousands, some faces came closer. These people had walked with me, watched me, and written things down. Their features and names were gone. But I remembered their patience, their precision, and their conviction that the record mattered more than the recorder.

Their work survived in scroll margins and in accounts of places they had visited with me. They recorded dates next to locations, compared me before an event with the woman afterward, and noted what had changed. Across the millennia, someone always took up that work. Each found me after the person before had been lost.

They had left scrolls, tablets, and carvings in shrines, caves, and temples. They had been writing about me.

On the bank, your notebook lay open, the pen resting over the page. Suddenly that long history was as particular as the hand holding it. You were one of those people. I wasn't prepared to see you among them.

Wei's face stayed exact among the thousands. The attachment had held against the wall. His wide eyes on the forest path, the fear in a child who couldn't save himself. I heard him calling for help as clearly as I felt the water around my knees.

His death was there again. The gate stood behind him. I had wanted him to live while remaining certain that everything I loved died. Your distinction between a pattern and murder came back with the memory. I could understand the difference without knowing whether my belief had killed him.

My pattern.

The memories washed through me together, separate currents in the same flow. I was the self creating in light and the woman who walked away. I loved someone I had forgotten. I lost someone whose face was still clear. All those lives pressed on me at once.

My breath went shallow.

The river where I knelt had become still. I noticed it at the edge of the memories, a small detail beside the immense span of what I was recalling. The current no longer pressed against my knees. Water rested motionless against my clothes, its surface smooth.

I had done that without deciding to. The knowledge had brought certainty back with it, enough to change the river while I remembered. I had needed stillness, and the water gave it.

I didn't drown as the water settled.

Of course I didn't. I believed I couldn't drown. That certainty was as complete as the one holding the water still. I knelt in both, a woman who could keep a river from flowing while remaining unable to let herself end.

You sat on the bank and didn't approach, close enough to reach, far enough not to intrude. Your notebook lay open on your knees. The pen had stopped.

From the bank you saw a woman kneeling in still water. You couldn't see what I held, more of my life than I had ever remembered at once. You were watching me remember myself.

The weight settled. These lives led into the one I was still living. I would keep adding to their span until I stopped believing I must continue. Gradually the memories separated. They were all still there, but I could hold them without being swept through each at once.

My breath steadied. Slowly the river resumed its current. The first pressure returned against my knees, then the cold movement of water through my soaked clothes.

I closed my eyes. I opened them.

Light. Water. Sky. Trees. Stones. You.
