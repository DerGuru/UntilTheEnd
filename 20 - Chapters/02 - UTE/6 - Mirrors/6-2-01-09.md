Smoke reached us on the wind. You shortened your stride and lifted your chin. Your nostrils flared as a thicker gust drifted across the trail.

"Fire!" you exclaimed and dropped your pack. You ran. I followed.

We ran into a small market square. A handful of houses stood around the well, their walls following the uneven ground. I recognized the layout from villages where we had bought rice and salt. Wei had lived in a place this size. The smoke was too thick to see the far side of the square.

Three houses were on fire. Flames ran through the thatch and climbed the wooden walls. Black smoke billowed above the roofs.

The sect cultivators were in the square.

I counted thirty. They stood among the villagers with room between them, relaxed enough to watch the road. Nobody tried to get past them. The sect had brought a detachment down from its patient watch on the ridges. It had also brought its customary lack of subtlety.

I searched the faces for the Pencil. He was elsewhere, presumably on a ridge, waiting to see how I would respond.

The villagers stood clear of the cultivators. Their eyes followed the same road, then turned toward me.

A child of four or five cried against its mother's chest. She held it so tightly that her fingers pressed into its clothes. Her face was blank. A cultivator stood between her and the well. She planted her feet and rocked the child without taking a step.

The cultivators waited in the smoke while the houses burned. I stepped into the square.

Thirty faces turned. The burning houses were spaced far enough apart to keep the flames from spreading immediately. Their smoke had blown straight toward us. Someone had chosen the places carefully.

You stopped at my shoulder, breathing hard from the run, your hand on the knife at your belt. Xiǎohú crouched behind us. Her amber eyes followed the cultivators, and her flank pressed against my legs.

The smoke rose. The child cried. A beam collapsed in one of the burning houses, sending sparks and a gust of heat across the square.

Heat struck my face. The mother bent over her child. Through the crack behind the wall came the same certainty I had felt with my palms on the earth, the same certainty in those hands shaping light.

They are not here.

I looked across the square. I knew where each cultivator stood, and the certainty held more firmly than the sight of them. The space by the well was empty. The mother had room to move.

They are not here.

I believed it completely.

And they were gone.
