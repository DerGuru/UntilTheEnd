I let go of the grass and stood. You searched my face. Whatever you saw there made you lower your hand. After days of waiting for me to do something, you gave me room to do it.

I walked to the river.

The river had been there all morning, running along the base of the clearing beyond the fire's remains. The fire had gone out more than a watch ago, and you hadn't relit it. Clear mountain water moved over stones with a sound older than any memory I could hold. I walked down the bank toward the murmur.

I stepped in.

The water bit into my feet and ankles. The cold went through the skin into the bone. Cold had been the first sensation to reach me through the breach in that earlier clearing, and I hadn't been able to set it aside since.

The cold was real. It was mine. My body responded to the water as bodies do, though I now knew what belief had made that body possible. I believed in temperature. I believed in cold. Knowing the source made the sensation no less real.

The water divided around my feet and closed again over the stones beyond. Each small change in the current registered against my ankles.

My pale feet showed against the dark stones. Light from the cleared sky lay on the surface, and my reflection floated above what the water covered.

My reflection.

I looked at it.

I remembered the reflection that had smiled before I did. The image moved before the woman whose face it showed. I had turned away from that impossible precedence, unable to face what it implied.

Now I looked.

The reflection was still. It followed my movements faithfully. I studied the face, the mouth that had smiled before I was ready. Its calm was my own. I had carried it inside me while searching for whoever looked out through my eyes.

There I was. I held the sight without turning away.

I was seeing myself from the other side of the mirror.

I stood in the water. Cold against my ankles. Light on the surface. My reflection and I in the same river, the same moment, the same body, the same self.

"It was me," I said.

So quiet that the words barely disturbed the air or competed with the water's sound. More breath than voice.

"It was always me."

The water rippled.

Small, precise circles expanded from my ankles toward the banks. I had kept my feet still. The rings spread evenly across the current, carrying a disturbance I couldn't attribute to wind or movement.

My belief had produced those ripples. I had recognized myself with the same complete certainty that moved a stone, and that conviction now altered the water. The circles spread outward while I watched what knowing could do.

It was me. It had always been me. I had created. I had shaped. My belief had become reality. I was what the wall had hidden. The fragments, the images, and the stray thoughts belonged to me. So did the second voice. So did the reflection.

Me.

I stood in the river with cold water against my ankles. Ripples spread from my feet. I had made my own existence.

You stood at the bank.

At the edge of my vision, a figure on solid ground watched a figure in water. You held your notebook. Your pen was still. I had seen you stop writing when words failed you. This time you needed a moment simply to watch.

Then the pen moved.

You wrote two small words quickly, then closed the notebook. You looked at me and said nothing. I let the quiet stand between us.

I think you remembered every letter.

I didn't ask what you wrote. The two words were yours.

I stood in the river as the ripples faded. Water flowed over the stones, followed the slope, and obeyed the laws it had been given.

The laws I had given it.

For the first time in millennia, I knew who I was.
