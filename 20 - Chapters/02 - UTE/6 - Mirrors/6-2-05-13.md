Your boots scuffed along the southern trail. My feet were bare. Xiǎohú moved silently ahead over stone and earth. In the morning light, each ridge rose higher than the last. The same pull lay beyond them that had brought us this far.

You had been quiet since dawn. Now and then you glanced at me, then back at the path. I knew that long consideration from our arguments over your notes, and I let you take the time you needed.

Around mid-morning, we stopped at a stream. You refilled the waterskins while I sat on a boulder and watched Xiǎohú investigate a beetle. She lowered her nose almost to the stone. The beetle changed direction. She changed direction. The beetle stopped. She stopped. Whatever else might occupy a fox-spirit, this apparently required her full attention. When it continued, she let it go and curled around my ankles.

You handed me a waterskin. The water was cold and tasted like granite.

We rested until I had finished drinking, then walked on.

"I've been thinking," you said. "About everything."

I waited.

"About the five stages."

You had divided the events into five stages in your notes. I remembered the numbered headings under your hand, the order you kept finding in experiences I could barely account for.

"The healings in the first," you said. "When you were hurt and then weren't. When things that should have killed you didn't. The timing is too precise for coincidence. Too consistent for luck."

You were listing what you had seen. I walked on.

"The coincidences are the second. The paths that opened when you needed paths. The weather that changed when you needed change. I wrote them down. All of them. Forty-seven instances."

Forty-seven. You'd counted. Of course you'd counted.

"Wei's death," you said.

My foot caught on the trail. Until then I had listened without looking at you. At his name, I turned. You had placed him among the things you believed my power explained.

"The one time it didn't work," you said. "Someone you cared about died. Whatever had kept you alive for centuries let it happen."

I walked on. The trail had been clear since we'd turned south. Each bend opened onto smooth ground, as if someone were preparing it ahead of us. Xiǎohú trotted along the path, glancing at things I couldn't see.

"The shockwave is the fourth," you continued. "When you lost control. When the ground split and the forest shattered around us." You touched your ribs while looking down the trail. "And the crater. The lake that used to be a mountain. I stood at its rim. I saw the scale of what you'd done to it. That wasn't cultivation. That wasn't qi. That was something else entirely."

You took a breath. Your hand fell from your ribs and settled at your side.

"The field is the fifth. When the air became yours. When reality obeyed rules you hadn't written. Four hundred and eighty heartbeats. I counted."

"And the question in the clearing. When something looked through your eyes." You paused. "Afterward you remembered none of it."

You stopped walking.

I took three more steps, then turned toward the silence behind me. You stood with your arms at your sides. Your face was set as it had been the night you decided to stay.

"You've always done this," you said.

"The healings. The coincidences. The protection. The shockwave. The field. All of it was you. Acting on the world. Unconsciously. The way the heart beats, without decision, without awareness."

I stood on the trail.

"But yesterday," you said. "In the village. That was the first time you did it consciously. The first time you saw yourself doing what you've always done. Whatever makes reality bend when you believe hard enough has been there since the beginning. Since before the beginning."

Wind moved through the trees. The three-note bird called somewhere in the canopy. I had first heard it only days ago.

"Only without knowing it," you said.

I listened until the bird went quiet. You had put your observations in order, then brought each one back to me. The conclusion came from the records you had kept while I tried to put events behind me. I could argue about what they meant. I could no longer claim you had nothing to support them.

Me.

"I'm not saying this to frighten you," you said. "I'm saying it because you need to hear it from outside. You need someone standing at a different angle to show you what you can't see from yours."

I prepared an argument out of habit. You might have mistaken coincidence for protection. Cultivation could affect the ground around us. A man looking for a pattern could find one because he wanted it there.

I opened my mouth. Metal coated my tongue. I remembered the empty square, the thirty people I had found in the forest. Under that memory lay the vibration through my palms, the shape I had almost seen in the dark. I had watched a tree arrive overnight. I had watched water run uphill.

Behind my eyes, the hands still shaped light with complete certainty. I closed my mouth without offering you the argument.

"I don't know what that means," I said.

"Neither do I."

We stood on the clear trail with the mountain ahead of us.

I turned your words over again. I had always done this.

The wall was quiet.

I waited for the pressure behind my eyes to sharpen. It didn't. Perhaps the wall could no longer prevent me from hearing you. Perhaps I was letting myself hear you. I touched my forehead, as if I could find the place where one explanation ended. The distinction was becoming difficult to hold.

We continued south. The path opened beneath the rising mountains.

Xiǎohú's eyes caught light from a source I couldn't see.
