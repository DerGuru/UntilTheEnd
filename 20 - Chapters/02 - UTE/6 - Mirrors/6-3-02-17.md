We continued the tests beside a small mountain stream. It flowed east, dropping past stones on its course downhill. You stood downstream while I took my place on the bank.

"The water flows there," I said, pointing uphill toward the west.

The water flowed west. The rocks along the bank were worn in that direction, showing decades of erosion by an uphill current. Tiny fish swam with it, their bodies aligned toward the higher ground. Everything in view supported a direction I knew was new.

You crouched at the water's edge, frowning at the worn stones.

"You didn't say 'I want the water to flow west,'" you said.

"No."

"You said 'the water flows there.' Present tense."

"Yes."

"And it does."

"It always did."

We built a fire. You struck flint and steel over the tinder until it caught. The flames grew steadily through the kindling. You held a hand toward the heat.

"There is no fire," I said.

Cold ground lay inside the ring. Its stones were pale gray, clean even on the faces that had touched the flames. The air smelled of earth where there had been smoke. The wood was gone. Where it had lain, the soil was dry and packed, undisturbed for days.

You blinked at the empty ground. Your hand was still extended toward the place the fire had been. You looked from it to the clean stones.

"Did you—"

"I put the fire out."

"There was no fire."

You drew your hand back, unable to account for having held it there. I remembered the flame under it.

I believed the fire was there again. Flames rose from charred wood. Heat reached my face at once, strong enough for a fire that had burned for watches. Smoke drifted above us. Blackened kindling lay under the larger pieces, already consumed by a flame that had just appeared.

You sat down by it. The clearing was bright in the same sunlight. You kept your hands near your knees instead of warming them, your gaze on the charred wood.

"Can you warm my tea?" you said.

You offered the cup with a faint attempt at lightness. It held the last of the morning's brew, lukewarm after we had neglected it through the tests.

I looked at the cup. The tea was hot.

Steam rose. You loosened your grip as the clay warmed under your fingers. You looked from the steam to me, then took a sip.

"Okay," you said.

Pause.

"Okay, that scares me."

Your voice had lost the lightness. You wrapped both hands around the cup.

My hands were shaking.

I had moved two stones, turned water uphill, and erased a fire. Then I had brought the fire back before reheating your tea. The thinning I had felt in the village returned more strongly with each act. A hollow opened behind my sternum. Iron rasped at the back of my tongue. I rested my hands on my legs, waiting for the trembling to stop.

You noticed. Of course you noticed.

"How much did that cost?" you said.

"I don't know the currency."

"But there IS a cost."

The tremor was already subsiding. A steady hum ran under the hollowness in my chest, and the emptiness eased, though more slowly than I had spent whatever sustained the changes.

"I could do this all day," I said. "But not all at once."

You nodded, looking at my hands again. We had found a cost, a limit to how quickly I could keep making changes. That gave you something to work with. It gave me something to watch for.

I looked past my hands to the fire. The acts we had tested were so different that I could find no common technique between them. Belief had been enough each time. Any certainty I held could do this. How many assumptions did I carry about the world? How many had I carried for millennia without examining them?

Healings, coincidences, clear paths, changing weather, and the protections I had blamed on something outside me: each might have followed a belief I didn't know I held.

"Everything," I said quietly, watching the steam rise from your tea.

"Everything," you agreed.

The fire burned between us. Behind me, water continued uphill past worn stones. The fish had followed the new current. The moved stones still carried their old moss. I couldn't see an end to what those small tests implied.

Everything.

The warmth of the fire reached my knees. Since the metallic taste began, I had been approaching something I couldn't name. The tests gave it a plain shape. I had moved thirty cultivators by belief. I had just changed water, stones, and fire.

If belief shaped reality, the certainties I had carried all my life might have shaped it too.

I stopped there deliberately. I knew where the thought led. I couldn't follow it yet, sitting by a fire we had remade with steam rising from your cup. I needed courage I hadn't found.

You watched me close my mouth. Your gaze moved to my hands, clasped now in my lap.

You didn't push.

You drank your tea.

It was warm.
