At midday, the southern trail lay open under a high sun. Short shadows edged the stones at our feet. Metal coated my tongue. The vibration in my teeth had become so constant that I forgot it until something drew my attention back.

A fist-sized stone sat in the center of the path. I walked toward it, ready to step around. Between one stride and the next it disappeared. Then I had the disquieting certainty that the path had always been clear.

I looked at the place where I had seen it. A thought came with the empty patch: the trail was clear because I believed it was clear. I had done nothing deliberately. Yet the stone was gone.

Something prickled across my scalp. In the village, I had consciously believed thirty people gone. Here I had barely noticed an obstacle before removing it. The scale was smaller. The result was as complete.

Your words had followed me all morning. You had said I had always done this without knowing. With each stretch of trail, I had found another event I could no longer dismiss as luck. A healing, a path opening at the right moment, a danger that passed me by. I remembered the same events I always had. Now I kept finding myself at their source.

The creation memory broke through as I looked at the cleared ground. Behind the crack, pressure built until the images pushed through.

The images came back. Stronger. Longer. Clearer.

They continued behind my open eyes, one movement following another. I saw myself before I had become a weapon. Before the long wandering. Before any forest, mountain, name, or loss I could remember.

Before everything.

The space extended everywhere and lay as close as my own skin. I tried to judge its size, but size had no meaning there yet.

I stood in the raw material.

And I shaped it.

The memory showed me hands, the only shape I could comprehend for what I had done. Belief ran through each movement, the same certainty that had shifted the cultivators. In that remembered space, whatever I believed came into being. This exists now. I had known it before I had words for it.

Light arrived as I became certain of light. Form gathered where I knew form belonged. I watched the hands move, trying to follow each creation.

I stopped on the trail with my hands pressed to my temples. My mouth filled with a taste of copper, iron, and something I couldn't name. It was older than either metal. I tried to swallow while the images grew brighter.

Pressure filled my bones. The memory brightened across the trail until the stones themselves shivered. I could no longer separate the place I had walked into from what I was remembering.

The missing stone, your words, and the hands shaping light met in one painful certainty. I had been there. I had done this before.

My knees hit the trail.

The world shimmered. A vibration passed through solid ground, holding the stones in their shapes. For an instant the conviction behind their continued existence was plain.

Was it mine?

I tried to follow that thought. The wall closed against it.

A spike of pain went through my forehead. The memory contracted until the whole sequence narrowed to one bright point. Then I lost the hands, the strange place, and the act of shaping.

Pain.

My nose bled.

Warm blood ran from my left nostril across my upper lip. It dripped onto the brown dust. Iron smelled sharp under the metallic taste.

I'd never bled from a memory before. I pressed a hand below my nose. The wall had used pain to turn me away before; now it had left blood on my fingers.

The thing behind the wall.

The thing that was a part of me.

"Breathe." You were there. Beside me. On the trail. Your hands on my shoulders. Your face close, your eyes searching.

"Breathe," you said again.

I tried. My chest had seized. The first breath barely passed my throat. I dragged in another, then more. Your hands held my shoulders while I struggled to fill my lungs.

The bleeding slowed. The sharp pain receded into a throb. I could lift my head without making it worse.

Your hands trembled.

The trembling came through your grip as the pain eased.

"I'm alright," I said.

"You're bleeding from your nose and you just collapsed on a trail."

"I'm alright."

You took your hands from my shoulders and sat back on your heels, watching me.

The metallic taste was blood now, mixed with metal, the two flavors indistinguishable.

I wiped my nose. Red on my sleeve. Red on the trail.

I reached for the sequence again, but the wall held it out of reach. Only its edges remained: hands in light, a place before other places, and the certainty that I had been there. I knew I had shaped something. I could no longer see the act itself.

Blood dried on my sleeve. The wall had stopped me again, at a greater cost than before. Yet it had left enough behind for me to know what I had nearly reached. I held on to that much.

"Almost," I whispered.

"I know," you said. "I saw."

You had seen me stop, fall to my knees, and bleed onto the path. The memory itself had been hidden from you. I looked at your hands resting on your knees.

You had said you knew and hadn't asked me to explain.

I could rest against that.

For now.
