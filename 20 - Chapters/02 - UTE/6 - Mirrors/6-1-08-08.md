The fire had burned down to orange coals in its circle of stones. Past it showed no moonlight, stars, or distant windows. You were a dark shape by the embers. Your slow breathing reached me across the cold ground.

I couldn't sleep.

I lay still enough to hear each of your breaths. Usually on a sleepless night, I would be returning to a thought I couldn't put aside. Now the quiet inside me had deepened. I searched it without finding an argument to continue. The ground pressed through my bedroll, the air touched my face, and nothing urged me to move.

I got up.

I had been lying down. Then I was standing, with a disquieting certainty that I had been on my feet all along. I looked at the bedroll. I couldn't recall getting out of it.

I walked to the edge of our camp. Past the coals' glow, the darkness was absolute. Xiǎohú raised her head. Gold shone in her eyes, though I couldn't tell what light they were catching. She watched each step I took away from the fire.

I knelt.

Cold pressed through the fabric of my trousers into my knees. It reached deep into the bone. Since the crack opened, I had been unable to set that sensation aside. I remembered telling you I was cold. Such a small thing to say, after so many years of being able to ignore it. Now I shifted my weight to ease the chill.

I put my hands on the ground.

I spread my fingers. Loose grains pressed into my palms. A child set on the ground reaches down like this, learning the surface by touching it. I held my hands there, feeling past the first sting of cold.

The earth vibrated.

The soil trembled under my palms. A vibration rose through it, so low that no sound came with it. I followed it under the loose surface, into the rock below. It continued deeper than I could reach, through layer after layer of stone.

The vibration carried something I could understand.

I kept my palms flat. The cold ached in my knees, but the quiet behind the wall held. Under it all ran a sustained note, older than any language I knew. If I listened more closely, I might hear what was in it.

Almost.

The whole vibration ran through my hands, yet a gap lay between the sensation and its meaning. It was like hearing a word in a language I had nearly learned: close enough to reach for, gone when I tried to answer. I held still, afraid to lose even that much.

I pressed my palms harder into the earth. The vibration intensified. Images came with it: my hands in light, shaping something I could nearly distinguish. They were closer now. The fingers turned, the light gathering between them. Another moment might have shown me what they held.

The headache arrived.

Pain drove through a single point in the center of my forehead.

I convulsed. My hands jerked off the ground despite my effort to hold them there. The wall closed with a force I hadn't felt before.

The vibration stopped. The images contracted to a bright point, then vanished. I tried to recover the shape between the hands, but it was gone. I knelt in the dark. Blood mixed with the metallic taste in my mouth.

I'd bitten my tongue. During the seizure my jaw had clenched and my teeth had found my tongue. I spat. Blood on earth. Dark on dark.

Xiǎohú stood half a pace away. Her amber eyes were level with my face. I thought I saw something reflected in them, a light I couldn't find anywhere around us.

My mouth filled with the taste of iron, sharper than it had ever been. I spat again, trying to clear it from my tongue. My teeth ached. The ache spread through my jaw into my skull, where the vibration lingered after I had lost contact with the ground.

I'd almost heard it.

Almost.

I stared at my dirty palms. I had been close enough to feel the shape. At the moment I might have seen it clearly, the pain had torn me away.

But it had been closer than before. The wall had needed more force.

I was getting closer.

The sharp point of the headache slowly spread into a dull ache. I sat back on my heels. My palms were cold and dirty. The earth under my knees was still.

Your breathing sounded carefully even. I thought you might have woken, though you lay still by the coals. I listened for a question. None came. By morning I would have to decide what to tell you; for now I needed to hold the experience without explaining it.

You seldom pushed.

Xiǎohú lay down against my knee. Her fur warmed me through the cloth; the strange reflection still showed in her eyes. I began petting her. She leaned into my hand while watching the darkness.

She glanced from my hand to my face. I looked at the patch of earth I had touched.

I would try again when the headache eased. Blood still lay on my tongue; tonight I had gone as far as I could. Tomorrow, or the night after, I would put my hands down again. I had felt the vibration once. I could reach for it until I understood.

The calm deepened as the metallic taste faded to its usual strength. I went on petting Xiǎohú in the dark. Behind us, the last coals glowed faintly.

Something hummed quietly in my chest, behind the crack.

Almost.
