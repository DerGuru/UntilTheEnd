We made camp where the trail leveled onto a shelf of rock. Pines sheltered half of it. We set our things under them, clear of the wind that crossed the exposed stone.

You made fire with flint, steel, and patience. Your routines had held through everything we had encountered. I sat with my knees drawn up, chin on my forearms, watching the small flames settle into a steady burn.

I'd worked on your meridians before the light failed, while the last warmth of the day still clung to the rock. The lower channels responded with less resistance now, though they still needed healing. The core was still the problem. The crack ran deep; qi had found routes around it as water finds a passage around a boulder. Fixing the core would mean undoing the adaptations that had kept you alive. I wasn't ready for that yet.

Everything was converging.

The tests belonged beside the guilt. Wei's death lined up with the creation fragments. Your words had let me see each from another angle. The changes had stopped, the question was open, and the wall was thin. Each thing that had happened since the first metallic taste brought me closer to a connection I could almost hold. I approached it from one memory, then another.

A spark jumped from the fire.

A small bright fleck rose as the burning wood shifted. It arced through the gray air above the fire, carrying its brief light toward me. Then it landed on my hand.

I didn't flinch.

The spark went dark against my skin. There was no heat, no pain. I turned my hand in the firelight, looking for any reddening where it had touched.

Xiǎohú flinched next to me. The spark had passed in front of her as she dozed; she jerked back with her ears flat. Now she came forward to sniff my hand. She sniffed again, then looked up at me. I held the hand still while she inspected the place where nothing had happened.

I looked at my hand.

The skin was unmarked. Smooth. It had been that way for centuries, without scars, burns, or the small changes of age. Time had passed through whole civilizations while leaving this surface as it was.

I'd always known this. I had attributed that casual invulnerability to cultivation, affinity, or talent. Later I had written careful accounts of an unseen protector. Those notes were ash now. Sparks didn't burn me. Cuts didn't leave scars. Falls didn't break me.

I'd never known WHY.

I stared at the hand that had touched stones, worked through meridians, and pressed against surfaces I expected to yield. It belonged to the woman who had believed cultivators elsewhere. In the creation fragments, I had seen that same hand shaping light in a place before places. The wall was losing its ability to hide the connection from me.

The spark hadn't burned because I believed I couldn't burn.

The cuts hadn't scarred because I believed I couldn't scar.

The falls hadn't broken because I believed I couldn't break.

Belief. My belief.

The recognition began slowly. I followed it from one certainty to the next, my hand open in the firelight. I had believed the stone was somewhere else. I had been equally certain that the spark couldn't burn me.

I stayed with those two things. The next thought would follow if I let it. I knew that with the same quiet certainty that had moved the stone. What waited behind it was what I had been approaching since the first morning on this ridge.

Gray fog gathered against the clearing, obscuring the trees past its edge.

You put food in front of me. I didn't eat.

Xiǎohú climbed into my lap. The full weight of her, slight but undeniable, curling into the space between my crossed legs and my chest. Her nose pressed into the crook of my elbow. Her breathing slowed.

She slept. I didn't.

I let the next thought come.
