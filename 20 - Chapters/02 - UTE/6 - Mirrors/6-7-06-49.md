I woke before you. Before Xiǎohú too, still curled near the dead fire, breathing the small breaths of sleep. The sky was the gray before gray, the pre-dawn absence of dark that preceded light.

I packed.

Your bag first. The straps tightened, the weight redistributed: dried meat to the bottom, water skin accessible, notebook where you could reach it without stopping. Then mine, the small bundle that constituted everything I carried, which was almost nothing, which was enough.

I set them side by side near the trailhead. Two bags. Yours heavy. Mine light. The morning was cold and the cold was good.

You woke to the sound of me scraping the pot, sat up, and blinked.

"I packed yours," I said.

You looked at the bags. At the trail. At me.

"You repacked the meat to the bottom."

"I left some out for later."

You pulled on your boots. Stretched. Checked the sky as you did every morning, measuring, like a man who had learned weather through walking. "A clear sky."

"For now."

You stood and dressed, the morning motions. I heated water over a small fire, not for rice, we'd eat the dried meat cold, but for warmth. Something to hold. You took the cup when I offered it and wrapped both hands around it.

Steam rose between us.

"I know what I have to do," I said.

The same volume I'd use for *it will rain* or *the trail goes north*. Factual, something true for a while and only now said aloud.

You drank and stored the cup away.

"Okay," you said.

You shouldered your pack. The weight settled across your back in a shape so customary now that its absence would have felt stranger than its presence.

"There is a cave uphill," I said.

You nodded. "How far?"

"Higher."

You accepted that. Higher was a direction. Higher was sufficient.

I picked up my bundle. Xiǎohú woke, stretched, and joined our departure.

The trail narrowed as we climbed. You walked beside me, pen tapping against your thigh.

We walked. Xiǎohú trotted ahead, fell behind, then took a position at our flank. Three beings on a mountain path heading higher.

The morning brightened. The sky took on its color, the high-altitude blue above the haze of lower elevations.

I knew what I had to do. The doing was all that was left.

The trees were gone. The tree line had passed below us somewhere in the day's climb, the last struggling pine giving way to scrub, then to rock, which was all there was. Gray stone, veined with quartz, rough in one place and smooth in the next. The sky was enormous, its dome swelling as the horizon dropped, the vastness altitude reveals by removing everything between you and the edge of the visible world.

The cold was constant.

Real cold, if not dangerous. The ambient, persistent cold of high altitude in early spring, the temperature that informs every breath and sits against the skin like a second garment.

A hollow in the rock held rainwater, a palm's width of accidental mirror. I passed it without looking down. I already knew the face that would look back.

I walked.

The path was barely a path now, a suggestion inscribed in rock by the passage of animals. Single file. You had fallen back, not to your three-step distance but to the practical spacing the terrain demanded, close enough to hear your breathing, your footsteps, the small sounds of a mortal body navigating difficult ground.

I knew where I was going. I couldn't have pointed to a spot on a map, given coordinates, or described the entrance. But I knew the direction, a destination I couldn't locate and my whole body leaned toward.

Higher. Toward the place where the mountains folded in on themselves and the rock turned hollow, a cave, and the cave was...

I didn't finish the thought. It didn't need finishing. The cave was what it was. The cave was where it was. I was walking toward it. Each step was a placement, a decision, a continuous negotiation with terrain that wanted nothing, offered nothing, and required everything.

Your breathing behind me was elevated, the altitude, the effort, mortal lungs doing mortal work in thinner air. You didn't complain. Had never complained. The walking staff tapped, the metronome of your progress, each tap a confirmation of presence. I am here. I am behind you. I am walking where you walk.

Xiǎohú went ahead, a shape against the gray rock, amber eyes visible when she turned, then lost again as she continued. Either she knew the way too or she was just going the same direction.

There was no sign of human presence beyond the vestigial path. The world had simplified to the basic vocabulary of stone, sky, and cold. The complexity of the lower world, towns, sects, relationships, the entire social architecture of civilization, lay below us now, in valleys we couldn't see, behind the curve of the mountain.

Up here was essential.

Two people. A fox. A path. A direction.

And somewhere ahead, ahead and above, in the place where the mountains held their oldest secrets, there was a cave. The place I wanted to go to. My final resting place, or the place where I wanted to stop not-dying. The place where a lifetime of walking would either arrive at its destination or discover that the destination was a different kind of question.
