Morning shade lay across our packs at the clearing's edge. We'd camped where the trail curved around a granite outcrop, on a natural circle of packed earth and sparse grass. I remembered arranging our things there. I was certain, as certain as I could be now, that we had slept in the open.

A tree stood above them.

It was an old jujube with a thick trunk, its weathered bark showing centuries of growth. Roots ran deep under the packed earth. The wide branches shaded half the clearing. Late-summer leaves spread above us, deep green, finely veined, and distinct down to the smallest curling edge.

I stared from the roots to the canopy. I found no place where the tree looked freshly settled into the ground.

"We should eat," you said. You were sitting on one of the tree's roots, your back against the trunk, the morning light filtered through its canopy. Your notebook was closed on your knee. You looked comfortable. You looked as if you had sat under this tree every morning of your life.

"That tree."

"What about it?"

"It wasn't here yesterday."

You glanced up at the branches, then back at me. You made no move to leave the comfortable shade.

"It's obviously been here for years," you said.

Obviously.

You sounded completely sincere. Your back rested against the bark, as easily as it might have rested against the granite outcrop. Whatever had changed here had reached your memory too. You had begun the morning under a tree whose arrival you hadn't noticed.

Mine hadn't.

One of us was wrong. I was wrong, or reality was wrong, or the distinction between the two had stopped mattering.

I walked to the trunk and touched the bark. It was rough under my fingers, warm where the morning sun reached it, and ridged with old growth. I pressed my palm flat against it. The vibration in my teeth changed pitch. Its strength held, but it settled in a different part of my jaw.

The tree was a fact. Verifiable by every sense I had learned to distrust but couldn't stop using.

"How long?" I said. "How long has it been here?"

You waited until I took my hand off the bark. "You planted it," you said.

"I didn't."

"Spring before last. After the rain."

A pause. You looked along the thick, centuries-old trunk. For one breath your brow tightened. Then you settled back against it.

You spoke about the planting as easily as you'd recall a shared morning. I tried to picture my hands in that dirt, a sapling in the rain. I had never done it. You had offered the memory without the slightest doubt.

I stepped back from the tree.

Birds called somewhere past the clearing. Wind moved the leaves above your head. Xiǎohú stretched in a patch of sun next to the tree's shadow. I watched that shadow move across her fur. An entire tree had appeared overnight, complete with centuries of growth. You sat under it waiting for breakfast.

We had walked south for half a watch when the path began climbing toward a ridge. At the narrowing bend, I stopped again.

The stone there had turned. Moss grew on its sun-facing side, yellowing in the direct morning light. It had grown in shade; the bleached edges showed what this exposure was doing to it. The weathering was wrong too: rain had eroded the sheltered face, while the exposed side was still smooth. I walked around it, checking the angle against the trail. Its surfaces belonged in a different position.

Something must have rotated it without moving anything around it.

I knelt and touched the cool stone. The earth around its base was undisturbed. There were no fresh scrapes or gaps in the soil, nothing to suggest it had moved since settling here.

The vibration in my teeth intensified.

I stood and looked along the trail in both directions. First the tree in the clearing, now the stone at the bend. Two things wrong in one morning, each with old roots or weathered surfaces to contradict me.

Xiǎohú padded past the stone without looking at it. Her eyes tracked something above the treeline. I followed her gaze across the open air. Whatever moved there was invisible to me, but her head followed it.

She tracked something that wasn't there.

Or something that was there now.
