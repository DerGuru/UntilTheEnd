We arrived at a river. It was wide here, shallow, clear-bottomed, the kind of water that showed every stone individually and made you think you could count them. Sun came down through the tree cover in patches. Mid-morning. The air smelled of wet rock, pine sap, and the freshness of water that has been running a long time without interruption.

You prepared camp and I washed clothes. Scrubbing fabric against river stones kept my hands busy while my mind moved through landscapes that words couldn't adequately map.

The fabric of your outer robe, the one with the ink stain on the left sleeve that you claimed was intentional and I knew was a dropped brush, went into the water and I scrubbed. The motion was ancient. I had performed this labor in lives I couldn't fully remember, and the muscle memory sat in my hands like notation in a margin.

I hadn't done this for someone else in... I couldn't even date it. When you walk alone for centuries, who are you washing clothes for? You wear a robe until it falls apart, replace it, wear the next one. Maintenance stops mattering when there's no one to maintain for and no version of yourself that cares about the state of fabric.

It didn't matter. My hands remembered.

Scrub. Lift. Wring. Submerge. The rhythm was meditative in the original sense, consciousness-altering. The repetition created a channel, and things moved through it that the active mind would have blocked or deflected before they could finish arriving.

Water through fingers.

The fabric was in my fists, the water squeezing out, running between knuckles, escaping through the gaps that tightened hands couldn't close. The harder I squeezed, the faster the water ran. It wasn't fighting my grip. Water's nature was to move, and my fists were irrelevant to that nature. Squeezing didn't hold the water. It hurried the water's departure.

I stopped wringing and opened my hands.

The fabric lay flat across my palms, wet, heavy, dripping. The water that hadn't been squeezed out sat in the cloth, soaking, unhurried, neither escaping nor running. The way water sits when nothing is forcing it anywhere. Gravity or the air would take it eventually. But in the moment between opening my hands and gravity's claim, the water was simply there.

Fists: the water escapes.
Open hands: the water stays.

I looked at my hands again. The fabric. The river moving around my ankles, cold, a genuine sensation. My fingertips were pruned from prolonged immersion, the body's preparation for a better grip on wet surfaces. My body still did this. It still went through the motions of being a body, adjusting, adapting, responding to stimuli with the competence of a body that didn't know it was running on conviction rather than biology.

The insight arrived as water. As the physical experience of water flowing through fingers and sitting in open palms. As the difference between gripping and holding, an understanding a body learns before a mind can articulate it.

Something was beginning to form, to crystallize. The raw material of understanding, present but unprocessed, the way ore exists in rock before anyone identifies it as metal. I couldn't name it yet. Some things need to exist as experience before they can accept the reduction of language.

You were upstream, sitting on a flat rock, your tasks finished. Your notebook was open, pen in hand, but your attention was aimed sideways. You were watching me. The water. The way I moved and the way the water moved around me, the way I handled the fabric, my own hands.

Xiǎohú was downstream, drinking. She raised her head, water dripping from her whiskers, and her amber eyes found me.

I looked back at her.

Then I looked at my hands. At the fabric. At the water.

I submerged the robe again and held it this time, open-handed. The water moved through the fabric, through my fingers, through the space between all of it, the way water moved. My hands were incidental. My intention was irrelevant. The water moved because it was water.

Something was crystallizing. Not yet. Almost.

I waited for it.

Patient.
