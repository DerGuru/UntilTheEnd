The trail wound south through pines, narrow enough that I had to watch the loose stones underfoot. The pack sat heavy on my shoulders. The morning was cool; the walking was good. My steps settled into a rhythm that filled the space between thoughts.

I stopped with my right foot forward and my left foot back. A loose stone pressed through my sole. I stood mid-stride on the trail, unable to take the next step.

Images came.

The pines showed through them. The stone still pressed under my foot, though the images were as vivid as a dream. I searched for the pressure that came with a forbidden memory. The wall had let these through.

I saw hands whose shape I recognized as my own. Their movements were strange to me. They passed through light, bending it with each gesture. I followed the turn of a wrist through the brightness. Something took shape between the fingers.

Creating.

The word came with the image. I could no more separate them than look away from the hands.

That other version of me was creating with a technique that looked ancient, older than the practices you call cultivation now. Every movement was deliberate. Those hands shaped the light without hesitation. I watched them, unable to recall what was being made.

More images crowded in before the first had faded. Hands passed through light; the brightness curved around them. A shape emerged from the curve, intricate enough that I couldn't hold all of it in view. Under each image lay the same concentrated intention, a certainty I had lost so completely that I recognized it only now.

Then they were gone, as abruptly as they had come.

I stood on the trail with my feet still apart. Pines rose above the stones ahead of me. The same patch of sky showed between their branches. I shifted my weight off the stone under my sole.

"Why have you stopped?" you asked from behind me. Your voice was calm, curious. You sounded as though you already had an answer but wanted to hear mine.

Turning to you took effort. The hands lingered in my vision as an afterimage, bright across your face. I blinked until I could distinguish your features through them.

"Did I?"

The question was genuine. I didn't know how long I'd been standing.

"About thirty heartbeats," you said. You were watching me closely. The field had taught you what it looked like when I went somewhere else without moving. Xiǎohú sat next to you with her head tilted, her eyes fixed on my face.

"I thought I saw something," I said.

I pushed the images away. Seeing something was dangerous; examining it invited the wall to take it from me. I had learned that much through the gaps it left. I fixed my attention on the trail, giving it nothing more to take.

It came back.

A short stretch farther down the trail, my footsteps settled back into their rhythm. The images returned, softer this time. The path showed through the bending light, but the hands still moved in it.

I pushed again and it returned.

Each time, the hands reappeared.

Before the crack, dismissal had worked. A thought I pushed away was gone; often I lost the decision to dismiss it too. I had been left with gaps I couldn't even measure. Now I could turn my attention elsewhere for a few steps, yet the same images returned as soon as I relaxed. I remembered each attempt to put them aside.

I walked on with the hands bright in my vision.

The metallic taste grew stronger. The vibration in my teeth settled into a constant hum. My jaw ached. I tried loosening it, but the ache came from deep in the bone.

You walked three steps behind me, close enough to speak without crowding me. Your measured breathing carried forward. You were fit enough for the trail, though it still cost you effort, and each climb made that audible. We continued our work on your meridians as we walked; there was still a great deal to clear.

Two cracks. I thought about that while I worked. I had cracked your core; you had cracked my wall. Or the woman whose hands I kept seeing had done that. Or I had. Your qi bled through fractures that should have contained it. Through mine came images whose source I couldn't place. We walked the same trail, carrying what we had done to each other.

"Are you going to tell me what you saw?" you asked, losing your patience.

"I'm not quite sure what I saw."

"Just tell me your observations, then."

I looked down at the loose stones. You had asked for something I could give you without pretending to understand it.

"Hands," I said. "Creating something. Light that bent."

You were quiet. "Your hands?"

"I don't know. Mine but not mine. Like an earlier version."

"An earlier version?"

"Older. Before."

My jaw tightened around the word. Since the crack, I had approached that earlier time over and over. Each approach brought me closer to thoughts the wall took away.

"Before what?" you asked.

"That's the thing. I don't know. I don't remember doing something like that. But they felt like my hands." The pressure behind my eyes grew, then eased again. "Something must have happened that made me lose those memories. Not that it would be the first time. The wall has already taken a lot from me. Maybe it took that too."

The wall did not intervene. I'd said "before," "creating," and "older." I still remembered saying them. Its grip might be weakening, or I had simply found words I could use without losing the thought behind them.

We continued walking. The images pulsed faintly, as persistent as the feeling in my chest. I could no more explain the hands than the humming. Xiǎohú padded at my heel, glancing between my face and the trail. The clouds thinned as the morning advanced. I thought the sun might actually come out.

I pushed the images away one more time.

They came back.

I let them stay.
