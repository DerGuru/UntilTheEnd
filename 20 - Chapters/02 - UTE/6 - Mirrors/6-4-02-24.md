I sat by the fire with my hands on my knees, Xiǎohú still in my lap. The remembered light spread through the flames. The clearing was still around me while the memory opened inside it. The fox-spirit's weight pressed on me in one place while I saw another.

The memory was glass-clear, sharper than memories I knew I had pieced together. I couldn't distinguish what I had preserved from what I might have reconstructed. Everything arrived with the force of something seen for the first time.

I was there.

Light surrounded me in that place before places. I couldn't find a boundary between myself and the brightness.

I created. Or so the vision insisted.

I saw it through the wall's remaining interference, further than the fragments had allowed. I saw an earlier self, before Yun the wanderer. Before the cultivator who stood in rivers and moved stones. Even names came later than this. So did forms, and the distinctions I used to separate one experience from another. I tried to recognize myself without them.

I created because creating might have been what I was. The creating was the being. Or so the memory insisted.

I believed.

I recognized what had moved the stones and redirected water, at a scale beyond recognizing. I believed things into existence. That certainty came before language. I couldn't separate it from what existed around me; believing made the reality in which the belief occurred.

But age had warped the memory. The wall distorted it further, as did the distance between that self and the woman by the fire. I saw light, form, and time as things I had created. The world seemed to start with me. It resembled an origin story told until the teller could no longer distinguish being present at a beginning from being the beginning itself.

I didn't trust it fully. The memory was real, but interpreting it was still my work. I had only just learned that belief could move stones. I had only just asked whether my beliefs had killed a child. I might easily reach for the largest possible explanation while trying to understand those things.

But it also showed me why.

Fear. The oldest fear. I could end. The creating could stop. I could dissolve back into whatever I had emerged from. It made no sense inside a memory that claimed I had created beginnings. A self that could do that should have been able to create continuation. But I had been afraid before I could reason about it. The fear of ending sat under every other certainty I held.

So I believed myself immortal.

In the memory, I believed myself immortal as completely as I had believed light into existence. I could find nothing under that certainty to question it with.

The belief became true, as my other beliefs became true. I had created my immortality. It came from me, from a fear I had carried so long that I could no longer see it. I had looked everywhere else for what sustained me.

The vast claims were harder to accept. I held them apart from what I recognized. The wall was thin, but it still stood. Xiǎohú still lay in my lap by the fire. That part of me, the woman called Yun, could see reasons to doubt the story even while remembering it. Perhaps I would accept it later. Perhaps I never would.

My lips were moving.

In the clearing, my hands stayed on my knees while my lips formed words. Fragments of the memory came out in a voice I scarcely recognized. The syllables were older than the language I spoke by the fire. I heard them but couldn't bring myself back to explain.

"Yun?"

Your voice reached me muffled, like sound through water. The name arrived while the light of the memory went on around me.

No response.

"YUN."

You called loudly enough that the name cut through the memory. It sounded wrong from where I was. Your calm had worn thin during a watch and a half next to a woman you couldn't reach. I knew you were calling me. I still couldn't answer.

No response.

Fog had risen from the valleys after dark. During that watch and a half it filled the spaces between trees, thickening around the clearing. The slopes around us had disappeared into gray.

The fog stopped at the edge of the clearing. A wall of gray, dense and motionless, pressing against the treeline as if something inside the clearing was holding it there. The air inside was sharp, clear, unnaturally still.

The moon had risen. Cold silver light filled the clearing, reaching every open patch up to the edge of the fog.

I could see her. The woman by the fire with the fox in her lap, hands on her knees, lips moving. I saw her from above, from outside, from the place where the memory held me. She looked small. She looked like someone I'd known a long time ago.

The light on her face intensified until it was brighter than the moonlight around her. Her eyes were open. Through them I was seeing something past the fire, past the clearing, past the mountain. I saw her seated there, and I felt how far away her attention had gone.

Something older.

Something that was me.

You sat next to her with your notebook open. After leaving it closed through things you couldn't account for, you were writing again. Fast. You recorded the syllables her lips formed. You caught changes in her expression. You saved whatever you could of an experience you couldn't see from where you sat.

Your handwriting was tight, rushed, nearly illegible. You were trying to hold her in ink, to pin the moment to paper before it dissolved.

But she was somewhere before memory, remembering something that memory couldn't contain.

You wrote.

I remembered.
