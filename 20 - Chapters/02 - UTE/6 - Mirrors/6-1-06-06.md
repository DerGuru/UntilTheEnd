Your footsteps trailed mine by three steps for most of the morning. We walked without speaking. Sometimes you drew a deeper breath, loud enough to carry over the stones. I waited for the question, but each time you let the breath go.

At noon we stopped by a stream. Water ran downhill over the stones. I watched it before kneeling; by then I half expected it to climb the slope. For now, I could fill our water skins from it. You sat on a rock while Xiǎohú drank at the stream's edge, her tongue quick and precise.

"You're different," you said.

I didn't look up. "Different how?"

"Calmer."

I finished filling the water skin, capped it, and set it next to the other one.

"That scares me more than your anger," you said.

I looked at you then.

You leaned toward me on the rock. Over the past months I had learned to recognize that intent look, particularly when I tried to avoid it.

"My anger?"

"The field. The rage. The tree that burst when we spotted the three scouts. I understood that. That was proportionate."

"And this isn't."

"This is you sitting by a stream while reality rewrites itself around you and more than a hundred cultivators harvest your unconscious power. And you, you're just filling water skins."

You were right. I could set down a water skin with steady hands while remembering the cultivators on the ridge. I hadn't been able to do that before. The strange hands still moved through light at the edge of my vision. Since I had let those images stay, I had felt less need to push against anything. I watched water drip from the stopper into the stream. The crack might have eased something in me, or I had simply given up resisting.

I couldn't tell you which.

"I don't understand it either," I said.

"That scares me even more."

"That I'm calm?"

"That you don't understand why." You leaned forward, elbows on your knees. "You understood your anger. You understood your fear. You found things you could test about the wall, the erasure, and the field. Eventually you understood enough to act."

"And now?"

"Now you're calm for no reason while things happen that should terrify you. And you're just accepting it. Like someone who's stopped fighting a current and now floats with it. That's either wisdom or it's drowning."

The stream moved past us. Xiǎohú finished drinking and settled on the bank with her eyes half-closed. Noon light warmed my sleeves. My hands rested on the full water skin.

I considered your words. From your rock I must have looked entirely comfortable. I searched for an answer that would explain why I kept waiting.

"I'm not accepting it," I said. "I'm not fighting it either. I'm waiting."

"For what?"

"I don't know."

"That's not making it better."

"What would you have me do? Fight them? I won't do that."

"No. I'd have you care that it's happening."

I sat with that. A three-note call sounded in the canopy above the stream. It might have been the bird I had first heard the previous day. I listened until the final note faded.

Light shifted on the surface of the water. I followed a ripple downstream. It passed the rock under your feet.

"The images," you said. "From yesterday. The hands."

"They're still there."

"Still the same?"

"Quieter. Like the sound of this stream. After a while you almost stop hearing it."

"But still present." You paused. "And the wall?"

"It hasn't reacted to anything lately. Not since you asked the question I forgot. Something spoke through me then. The wall is still there; I can feel it. But it's quiet. Like a guard sleeping at his post."

You glanced at your closed notebook, then back at me. You had stopped writing everything down. The questions continued.

"You said the editing has changed," you said. "That when something changes, everyone's memory changes with it now. Except yours."

"Yes."

"And you said you don't know how you're producing the changes."

"Yes."

"You were going to say something last night. You started, but then you stopped before anything came out."

The light on the stream brought back that half-formed connection. I had almost put it into words by the fire, then looked down at the ash instead.

"You saw that?"

"I saw you start a sentence and swallow it. I've been watching you do that for six months. I know what it looks like."

Xiǎohú's breathing carried over the running water.

"What were you going to say?" you asked.

I looked at the water passing your rock. Then I said it.

"The immunity and the editing. They're not separate things."

You waited without speaking.

"Everyone's memory changes when the world changes. Except mine. I don't know how the changes are being produced. But what if those two things are connected? What if I'm immune because—" I closed my mouth. My fingers tightened on the water skin. Then I made myself finish. "Because I am truly the one doing it?"

You went still. Your brows drew together; your lips parted briefly. Then you looked straight at me.

"Say that again," you said.

"I think I'm the one doing it. Not something in me, me."

I watched you consider it, rubbing a thumb across your knee, eyes on the water skins between us. I knew that pause from the arguments we had made over your notebook. You were searching for something that didn't fit.

"That would explain the immunity," you said slowly.

"Yes."

"But if it is truly you, why can't you stop it?"

"I don't know."

"So, that's what you weren't ready to say."

"I'm still not sure I'm ready. But you—"

The stream had stopped.

The surface had gone flat while I spoke. Beside your rock, the ripple held its shape. Water sat motionless in its bed, reflecting an unbroken patch of sky.

Then it reversed.

"Asked." I dragged out the word, my eyes fixed on the water.

The water began flowing gently uphill. It passed your rock in the opposite direction, climbing the slope in a smooth current. I watched it rise over stones it should have fallen past.

"Yun?"

You watched my face. You had seen the water while we talked, yet you gave it no second glance. When I looked at you, you didn't look away.

"What is it?" you said.

"Nothing."

You held my gaze longer, then reached for a water skin.

The metallic taste coated the inside of my mouth. The vibration in my teeth settled into a continuous hum. Quiet water climbed the slope past us. You dipped the water skin into it without hesitation, accepting this direction as easily as the last.

I had barely finished saying I was the one doing it. Now I sat by an uphill stream, unable to make it stop.

You stood, picked up your pack, and adjusted the straps. You had carried it across enough mountains to settle the weight in one movement. I lingered by the stream a moment longer.

"Well, I'd prefer it if you were ready soon," you said. "The calm ones are always closer to the edge than they think."

You walked away, your pack shifting against your back with each step. In a moment I would have to follow.

You weren't wrong.
