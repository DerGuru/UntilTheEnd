The trail curved east around thick bamboo on a south-facing granite slope. The stand had decades of growth. Yet the soil was wrong for it, the elevation was wrong for it, and the worn edge of the trail ran straight toward its roots. I followed the old track with my eyes. It should have passed through the stand, but the bamboo was older than the trail.

You walked through it without hesitation, your feet finding the curve through the green shadows, as if you knew every turn of a path that had just changed.

Another change, fitted so neatly into the slope that I had to look back to convince myself.

Then the bamboo thinned. I looked across the valley at the open ridge.

Cultivators stood along its spine. I counted a hundred and twelve. Their formation had the spacing of a military line, but they held themselves with ceremonial stillness. Nobody stepped forward.

They'd reformed.

I had seen the sect's order come apart in the field. They should have scattered after that. Instead, the survivors had gathered others into their ranks. The intervals between them were precise. Their cultivation signatures reached me through the valley air, stronger than before, held close enough to reinforce one another.

The Pencil stood at the center.

I called him Pencil because that was how I'd first seen him: thin, angular, and precisely drawn. I knew his name was Lian Zhuo. Using it would have required an acquaintance I had taken some trouble to avoid.

He looked different.

The last time I'd seen him, the field was dissolving. Through the noise and haze of that collapse, his face had been pale. He had looked shaken by how far events had escaped his control. Even his cultivation had looked diminished.

Now he looked fed.

His cultivation signature was thicker, more resonant. Within it sat something I knew from the changes around us. I tested that impression against the cultivators on either side of him. It was present in them too.

They had found a use for what was happening to the mountain.

The tree, the stone, the flowers, and the bamboo had each left something behind as they changed. That same force ran refined through the signatures across the valley. The sect had caught it. Of course they had. Cultivators could watch the world coming apart with sincere horror while considering what might be made of the pieces.

A hundred and twelve cultivators overlooked the valley where we had been walking for three days. They had grown stronger from the changes that followed me. I looked from face to face. They watched us from the ridge without a word.

In our previous encounters I had felt their urgency. They had confronted me, manipulated others, and laid plans to claim what they could. Ambition had made them hurry even when they tried to hide it.

Now they waited.

"How many?" you asked next to me. You kept your voice level as you looked at the ridge. Your hand hovered near the small knife at your belt.

"A hundred and twelve."

"A lot more than before."

"Yes."

"They look—"

"Different. I know."

The Pencil held his eyes on me. When you shifted next to me, he didn't glance at you. His face was still. He held his cultivation close, conserving the strength beneath that restraint.

He was waiting for me.

The others held their positions around him. Another change would leave more of that force for them to gather. They could wait on the ridge until it happened, then come back stronger the next day. I looked at the nearest man's relaxed hands.

They were farming me.

My fingers tightened around my pack straps. They could let me walk through the mountains with you, stopping to make camp or fill our water skins. The changes would keep coming. The sect would follow after them, gathering what they needed. They no longer had to provoke me.

I looked back at the Pencil across the valley. He had brought enough people to make their presence unmistakable, then kept them out of my reach. I understood why he could afford to wait. Each day I spent failing to understand myself supplied him with more strength.

"We're out of time," I said.

You looked at me.

"The changes are accelerating. The anomalies are getting bigger and they're getting stronger from the anomalies. And I can't stop producing them because I don't know how I'm producing them."

"Can you—"

"No."

I answered before you could finish. You might have been asking me to stop them, leave, or fight. I had considered each possibility while watching that ridge. I couldn't halt changes I didn't understand. Fighting them was within my power; surviving my own pleasure in it was another matter. The rush of the fight in the woods came back as I tightened my grip on the straps. I would probably win here too. I was afraid of what I might do while winning.

Leaving the mountain would take the changes elsewhere. Their scouts could follow. I glanced at your hand by the knife. Whatever route I chose, I would have to bring you with me. I was keeping that much.

Xiǎohú stood tense at the trail's edge, her eyes fixed on the ridge. Every hair on her back was raised. Her tail hung low and still. She didn't leave our side.

Her head turned at the same angle I had watched all day. I followed her gaze to the cultivators' flank. Scouts. She had been tracking their approach above the treeline.

The Pencil raised one hand in a small acknowledgment. He had seen me notice them. His fingers lowered with the same precision.

The formation dissolved. They turned together, moving along the ridge's spine until the slope hid them. The last figure disappeared over the crest.

Evening light fell across the empty ridge. You lowered your hand from your belt. Beside my leg, Xiǎohú's fur slowly settled. I didn't move, looking at the place the Pencil had stood. I had been putting off what I needed to think about. He had just shown me the cost.

"We need a plan," you said.

You were right.

I had no idea what the plan could be.
