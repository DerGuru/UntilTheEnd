Wei slept, deep in the exhaustion of a boy whose days were full. Warmth from his core reached me at the doorway. Fourteen days ago it had reached a pace. Seven days ago, two. Tonight, four paces.

The range was expanding.

I crossed the room and knelt at his bed. The nightly arrangement: knees on the floor, my hand hovering a fingerbreadth above his shirt.

Light bled through his shirt, golden and steady, a candle behind paper. Beautiful, and proof of how much qi his body was failing to contain.

Four twitches in sixty heartbeats.

I counted. The count was automatic. Four contractions of qi-stability: four moments when his core stuttered, flickered, and settled again. Four interruptions in what should have been smooth flow, each a sign of strain, fatigue, and progression.

Four. Yesterday: three. The day before: three. Half a month ago: two.

The arithmetic was simple. Two, three, and four. Slow and consistent, night after night, each count confirmed by the next. Progression had an endpoint.

I withdrew my hand.

"Your qi is loud," I said. To him. To the room. To no one.

He stirred. "Mmh."

"Turn over."

He turned, mumbling something about radishes. The glow dimmed; sleeping on his side narrowed the visible light. A half-measure, like holding a candle sideways.

I went outside and shut the door quietly behind me.

The night air was cool, the settlement sleeping around me: dark houses, dark paths, and the occasional ember-glow of a fire banked for morning. Somewhere a dog. Somewhere a stream.

I extended my perception a hundred paces across the qi-field, to the settlement's perimeter. Lao Chen was asleep, his qi dull as old iron and stable, a man who had peaked decades ago and settled into peace. Auntie Huang was bright. Loud even in her sleep. Shen was dim, muted.

I stopped.

Past the settlement, the qi-field carried something subtle: the pressure of attention directed at one location. This settlement. The boy in it.

Days ago, at the market, the attention had been directional. Three cultivators, three lines of interest converging on Wei. Each presence distinct enough to follow.

Now the pressure spread through the qi around the settlement. It had no single direction to follow, as though someone were watching from several places at once. The look had weight.

It wasn't me. My presence was suppressed, compressed to the signature of a void.

Someone else.

The attention was on the boy. On the golden light shining through cotton. On the signal that he was and that I could not stop him from being.

If it were only me, it would be simple. I hide. They seek. I move. They lose track.

But if they sought him, hiding was a problem. His qi screamed, and his core glowed through fabric.

I could hide myself. I could not hide him without permanent suppression, and permanent suppression would do more damage than was acceptable. My qi would accelerate his growth, and the instability would increase. Until...

I went back inside and stood over him as he slept. The glow was softer now, the core settling, but still visible. Still shining.

Four twitches.

I knelt and pulled his blanket higher, the same gesture every night.

"You're still there," he murmured. Half-asleep. Not a question.

"Yes."

"Good."

He was gone again. Into sleep. Into the depth that was trust made horizontal.

Tomorrow he would wake. He would stretch, yawn, and run to Lao Chen's. He would be bright. His qi would shine.

And I would sit, count, and not tell him.

Because not telling him was the thing I could not stop doing. Telling him was the thing I could not start. The space between the two was where I lived now.
