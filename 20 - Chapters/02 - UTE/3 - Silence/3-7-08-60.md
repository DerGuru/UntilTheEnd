It was night.

I did not make fire. The absence of fire was a statement: no one sat opposite, no one waited for the warmth, no one complained about the smoke, the positioning, or the kindling being wet again.

I had always made fire. Every night. A routine that was my anchor: survive the night by filling it with tasks. Fire, tea, qi-practice. The sequential occupation of watches converted darkness from threat to schedule.

Wei had expected fire, had assumed it, had sat down at the place where fire would be and waited for it to appear or built it himself. Fire appearing was the constant.

As I was the constant.

No one expected fire now.

The forest was dark, the sky dark, the clearing where I sat dark, the brown circle under me invisible, the death-radius continuing unseen.

I sat in the dark without fire.

And then something rose from somewhere deep, below conscious access.

It was an imprint. A shape felt rather than recalled, the impression a person leaves in another person. Permanent alteration, not departure.

He would have hated this.

The giving up. A woman sitting in a dead clearing without fire, without food, without the basic operations of survival.

"What do you mean, you give up? Are you STUPID?"

No voice, no hallucination, none of the ghost-hearing grief produced in people who wanted to hear. An imprint: the shape of his character pressed into my consciousness by long proximity. His stubbornness. His refusal. His constitutive inability to accept that things ended.

He was stubborn. That was his core quality, more than qi, talent, or the potential the sect had valued. The irrational, magnificent stubbornness of a person who looked at every closed door and took it as a suggestion, not a fact.

He would have seen me sitting there, dark, fireless, and his face would have made the expression fourteen-year-old boys made when they saw something they considered wrong. The lips compressed, the eyes narrowed, the chin forward at the angle of challenge.

"Get up," he'd say.

Something inside me answered his non-voice, a reaction rather than a feeling. Hope was the word people used when the future seemed possible. This was something else.

Refusal.

The refusal to give the universe the satisfaction of my stopping. The refusal to let the thing that had taken him also take what he had left, the echo of a character so forceful that the echo survived the source.

He would not have wanted this.

That was not enough. But it was something, and something was the difference between sitting in the dark and standing in the dark. Standing was the difference between this night and every night after.

I did not light a fire.

I did not leave the clearing. The sitting could become either permanent or temporary, and the choice between the two was what the imprint demanded. Not permanent. Not this. Not you. Not like this.

A coal, in the dark. Unlit, but not cold. The temperature of something that had been burning and retained the heat. The possibility. Not for tonight. But maybe.

Not tonight.

But maybe.
