Night became morning and I went back.

I went to the garden. His garden. I couldn't face the house yet, the room that contained absence. The three pots at the edge of the path, the blade fragment, the stone, his collection. That I could face.

Two of the pots were dead.

The dock grass had turned brown and dry, the stems collapsed inward, a plant that had lost its water source because no one had watered it in the days between the battle and now.

The nettle-cousin was the same, brown, curled, the leaves fallen from stems that were stalks that were sticks that were dead.

Two wrong herbs, chosen by a child who knew little about herbs. The charm of knowing nothing was irrelevant now. The relevance died with the watering.

The third pot, however. The lemongrass, right by accident, the one he'd chosen without knowing what he was choosing, the herb that worked because it had root structure, a biological investment in substrate that the other two lacked.

It was alive.

Green in the morning gray, green amid the brown circle of my proximity, the dead pots, and the general browning of everything within three paces. It was still growing, the stems upright, the leaves extended, alive.

I picked it up.

The pot was heavier than expected: earth, clay, root mass. The weight of a small thing that contained a smaller thing that was alive.

My fingertips were numb, the qi-emission turned inward as well as outward, feedback from sustained uncontrolled emission. I barely felt what I touched. The pot registered in my hands as pressure, without texture.

Earth crumbled at the rim, a pot watered days ago, enough for the roots but not for the surface. It fell onto the dirt in small amounts, brown on brown.

I held it.

The pot sat in my hands, the lemongrass rising from the soil, green in the gray, alive in the dead.

I didn't know why I was holding it. Something had moved my hands to the pot, a reflex rather than a decision, like the reflex Wei had when he heard moaning. The involuntary orientation toward whatever was alive in a space where alive was rare.

I held it, and it didn't die. In my hands, within the three-pace radius that killed grass, dropped insects, weakened trees, and nauseated people. Alive. Green. Holding.

I sat at the edge between the settlement and the forest, between the place where people were and the place where I was. The boundary. The margin.

The pot stood next to me, upright, the lemongrass green.

I sat with the pot in the margin, in a gray morning, gray as mornings were when the person who made mornings bright was not in them.

The settlement behind me was damaged but standing, the people in it alive and cautious, maintaining the distance my radius required.

The forest ahead was green, weakened at the edges by my proximity, green beyond, and the world continued.

Between them sat me, with a pot that did not wilt.

No sound. No wind. No voice, no argument, no complaint about the herbs, no declaration about cultivation, no stubbornness embodied in a fourteen-year-old body that moved through the world as if the world owed him an explanation and the explanation was overdue.

Silence.

The pot's green.
