Mao walked slowly, slower than usual, his habitual chin-tap absent. His hand hung at his side, a gesture exhausted.

It was early morning, the half-watch before Wei woke. That was the space I occupied for the things that couldn't happen while he was present.

Mao stood next to me at the well without speaking, his mouth tightening over the sentence.

"The boy—"

He stopped, then started again.

"I can't say what it is. My experience isn't enough. But—"

He met my eyes. He had arrived at a conclusion beyond his vocabulary and was bridging the gap with *but*.

"It's getting faster."

Three words. His diagnosis, his prognosis, and his entirety. He had felt Wei's core with palms accustomed to fevers and broken bones, and that rudimentary sensitivity had detected what mine had been measuring: the acceleration of Wei's flickers. The trend.

He waited for me to say something.

I said nothing.

He nodded, accepting my silence, the wall between his knowing-less and my knowing-more.

"You know what it is, don't you."

A statement.

I said nothing.

He nodded again, turned, and walked away, chin-tap still absent. A healer who had identified a problem he couldn't solve.

That evening, Wei sat at the table and met my eyes straight on.

"It's getting worse."

He gave me his reading. He'd been feeling it: the symptoms he'd been calling *tired*, the flickers he'd learned to notice since our last conversation about it.

I put down my tea.

"How much worse?"

A long silence while I calculated which update to share, how much to add to a truth already partially told.

"The acceleration hasn't slowed."

He absorbed it, tightening as if he had suspected the answer and was hearing it confirmed.

"The training. The dampening. All of it. It's not working?"

"It's slowing the progression. Not stopping it."

He looked at the table, at the gouge, and at the tea.

"Why didn't you tell me it wasn't enough?"

The question hung over the gouge in the table, a variation of the same question he'd asked before, on the road, by the fire. The pattern between us. He asks, I partially answer, he calls me on the partial, I adjust the partial, he accepts the adjustment, and the truth recedes one more layer.

He knew the pattern. He was tired of the pattern. And so was I.

All of these. None spoken.

I also didn't say it was my fault. My qi accelerated his development. The proximity, the exposure, the ambient transfer my presence produced: those were the cause. I broke him before he knew what breaking meant. Every symptom he had was the consequence of my existence near him, of what my nature produced and his young core absorbed.

The guilt-truth. The full truth, the version that included me as cause, as origin.

Instead I told him it wasn't enough. I told him about acceleration, which was true. Partially.

I held the other half, the half that was mine. For protection, I told myself. But who did I want to protect? Him, from the knowledge that his teacher was his damage? Or me, from the acknowledgment that my teaching was his breaking?

I didn't know. The not-knowing was the honest answer, and the honest answer was the only thing I was not providing.

I know. You would have told him. You were always braver than me in that way.

Wei held my eyes across the table, across the tea.

He didn't ask again.
