I stood up slowly, like someone lifting not their body but the weight of everything it contained. Slowly was the only available speed.

The silence held, persistent and total. The world was not quiet. My perception was refusing its noise.

Under my feet the grass was green, late-summer settlement grass that had been walked on by settlers, animals, and children for years. Alive because grass was alive.

Then it died.

Literally: under my left foot the grass turned brown, spreading outward from the sole like a stain. Under my right foot, the same. Brown, dry, plant tissue exposed to qi that was grief expressed as energy expressed as destruction.

The circle widened. Five paces. Ten. Fifteen. The dying spread outward from where I stood, qi leaving my body like heat leaving a furnace, through every surface, in every direction, without intention.

Twenty paces. The flowers at the edge of the communal garden wilted, stems bowing, petals falling. The small herbs along the path curled inward and browned.

Twenty-five. Thirty.

It stopped at a radius, limited by limits I possessed, the only thing between the dying and everything dying.

The settlers felt it.

The man by the well, one of those who had stood at the wall with farming tools, staggered, his hand on the well's edge, his face going pale, then green. He bent double with nausea, the response of a human body to qi-emission at a frequency human systems rejected because rejecting was surviving.

A woman by the north wall had blood in her mouth, from the capillary response qi-saturation produced in untrained bodies, not from injury. She spat red, stared at it, then at me.

The expression on her face.

A person seeing something she could not categorize, a woman standing in a circle of dead grass whose standing was the cause.

Another man was on his knees, his balance disrupted at high frequencies. He couldn't tell up from down, and his hands searched the ground.

The air hung heavy, metallic with blood, ozone, and something older, something predating this century.

I was not doing this. I was. Both. The distinction between intention and expression, between murder and weather. This was weather.

I stood.

The dead grass spread, the nausea spread, the blood-taste spread, each a symptom of the same cause, and the cause was me. I stood in the middle of a dying circle in a gray morning beside a dead boy with torn hands.

The settlers who were close enough moved away, staggering, crawling, like animals from fire, because fire's nature included burning, regardless of intent.

No one approached the body. No one approached me. The dead grass became a border, and the border was mine.

I stood in the circle. Only me. No boy of fourteen with a bright core. The core was empty now.

The silence. The dead grass. The spreading radius.

Shen, the boy Wei had saved, sat outside the circle, fifteen paces past the edge, crying soundlessly in my perception. His mouth was open, his hands over his ears. Whether the sound was too much for him or the silence was too much, I could not tell.

I stood in the dead grass, holding nothing, touching nothing, looking at the body I did not dare to touch. Touching was the thing I denied, and the denial still held because denial was the only structure remaining.

*I will not collapse into this.*

*I will not become the version that collapses.*

*I will stand.*

*In dead grass.*

*In silence.*
