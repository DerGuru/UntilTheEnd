A signature was closing, broken, the frequency of a core shattered by something other than death, a forced disruption that sounded like a bell struck wrong, audible in the overtones.

You, Xu Ran.

You stopped in your torn coat at the edge of the clearing where I stood. My standing had made the clearing, the trees around it intact, the grass beneath me brown and dead.

You saw me, and I saw you, peripheral, unimportant, another body in a world of bodies that either lived or didn't.

You must have felt my aura.

Your face went pale, then green with nausea. Your hands shook from the qi-emission hitting your already-broken system, additional stress on a structure with no tolerance for it.

Blood came into your mouth, the capillary disruption. You spat sideways, discreetly, a person who maintained propriety even while their body rejected proximity.

You held your ground at fifty paces, far beyond the brown, where my aura thinned but did not end, present. The trembling was continuous, the nausea continuous, the blood-taste continuous. You endured all of it by clenching your jaw and standing.

Your eyes found me.

I didn't look back. Your presence registered like weather, atmospheric, environmental, a condition of the space rather than an occupant.

You understood something. The shape of loss was visible in a woman standing in dead grass in rain, a shape that needed no explanation because it was the explanation.

Your core was broken. Core Formation, once, broken by me. Different mechanism. Different cause. Same result.

You never asked what happened. That was the first thing about you I could stand.

You stood there at fifty paces, offering no words, no comfort, no gesture. You had nothing to offer. Offering required having. Having required wholeness. Neither of us possessed it.

The moments passed, the rain drizzling, your coat wet, your hands trembling.

Then you turned slowly, like someone for whom turning requires reassembling the intention to move, and walked away. Your gait was uneven, a body compensating for a broken core, a limp that was not a limp but close enough.

You did not look back. You had not arrived. Arriving required an invitation, and the host, standing in dead grass, was not hosting.

Fifty paces.

The distance of two broken things that recognized each other. The closest thing to comfort. Insufficient.
