I couldn't see Guo, whose discipline included visual concealment, but his signature was readable. Executive function, decision-making qi-patterns, a mind processing options and selecting.

His strongest operative, the one closest to me, part of the four-point killbox, a survivor of Wei's discharge, was on the ground, felled by me, by the emission, rather than by the discharge. His qi-channels were destabilized, his body rejecting equilibrium. He crawled away on hands and knees, a diagnosis in motion: too close for too long.

Good.

Guo calculated, functionally rather than emotionally. A mind trained to optimize outcomes weighed resource expenditure against gain, risk against extraction. The arithmetic of operations that included a variable labeled "acceptable losses."

He knocked three times against something, his signal board or his thigh, a surface his unit recognized. Three knocks, clear, rhythmic, final. The same pattern that had triggered the trap, the same cadence that had sent Wei running toward the false scout. Same rhythm. Different meaning.

Retreat.

The signatures pulled back, east, south, north, the formations dissolving, bodies that had been positioned with precision departing with precision. An organized withdrawal, planning rather than panic. They left as they had arrived.

From the western hill, gentle, the lightest possible touch across the distance, came a single word. Transmitted rather than spoken, qi-carried, at a frequency only beings of our magnitude could produce or receive.

*Don't.*

One word, in the voice you use on someone standing at an edge.

I let them go.

Two of them did not leave.

They were close to me, inside the radius, in the dead grass at its outer edge. Two operatives had been too close when the emission started, and being near me was now a liability. Their commander was leaving and they could not. Leaving required moving, moving required functioning, and functioning required being outside the radius.

I stared at them, cold-eyed.

Guo's signature oriented toward me. A pause. A commander acknowledging that two of his people were being left behind.

He turned and walked, retreating with his unit, his signature receding through the treeline, through the positions his unit had held, toward a distance that would absorb him and his people into the larger pattern of things that came and went.

He left them.

The two lay in the grass. One was barely conscious, the nausea having progressed to systemic qi-disruption. The other was conscious, aware, trying to move, but his inner ear was gone and moving without it produced crawling in circles.

They would not survive the radius, killed by exposure rather than violence. The emission was lethal by duration rather than design, and I was still standing. Still leaking. Still burning everything near me because I did not know how to stop.

I stayed in the circle with the body of my boy.

The two operatives lay in the periphery. Dying slowly, or not dying slowly, depending on whether I stayed and how long and whether the emission decreased and whether…

I stayed.

On the western hill, the signature was also retreating.

The cultivator beyond Immortal, the Hermit Master, had watched from 200 paces through the entire engagement: the combat, the trap, the death, the silence, the wilting. He had watched as beings of our magnitude watched, with full perception, full comprehension, and a distance that was discipline rather than cowardice.

He could have intervened. He could have crossed the distance, entered the fight, stopped the four cultivators, prevented it. He could have. I could have. But we didn't.

We didn't, because intervention was something beings of our magnitude did not do in the affairs of *lesser beings*. The policy was old. The wisdom behind it was older. You don't interfere. You watch. You carry the weight of watching, the lighter weight, the one that doesn't collapse the world beneath it.

He was leaving because staying was expensive.

The cost was exposure. My emission was detectable. At 200 paces, with his defenses, the danger was discomfort, since proximity to grief at my scale was uncomfortable. Like standing too close to a star. The star didn't target you. The star was the star. And you moved.

So did he. No gesture this time, no two-finger salute, no acknowledgment, no departure ritual. Just gone, his signature receding up the hill, over the ridge, into the distance.

A cultivator beyond the rank of an Immortal, retreating from a woman standing in dead grass.

Retreating from grief.

The settlement was settling. The combat was over, the qi-blasts silent, the fire at the south-west burning itself out or doused by the settlers who had followed Wei's instruction and gone south with buckets.

The walls were intact, my barriers holding, the protection signs glowing faintly, the escape routes functional, used, successful. The civilians were alive, the settlement standing.

Everything I had protected was standing.

Everything except the body in the dirt. At the center of dead grass. At the center of silence.

I did not track the two operatives in the periphery. Whatever the radius did, whether they crawled far enough, whether the exposure was lethal or only debilitating, I didn't care. They belonged to the category of things happening that were not important. The thing that was important was the body. His body.

In the world that I had built: the barriers, the signs, the routes, the walls that bore my qi and held. The world that I had protected.

Everything.

Except the one thing I should have protected and didn’t because protecting it meant opening a door. And behind the door was now a dead boy in dirt, fourteen, hands torn, core dark, eyes open to the gray sky.

The grass around him was dead.

The sky above him was indifferent.

The woman beside him was standing.
