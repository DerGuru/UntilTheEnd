We heard a sound from the undergrowth, the opposite of the anti-sound of a predator. Human sound. Pain too exhausted for screaming had settled into sustained distress. A moan. Continuous. Someone had been hurting for so long that the hurting had become background.

Wei stopped.

His body stopped mid-stride, his head turning toward the sound at once. Automatic. His wiring ran straight from the sound of pain to the act of response, with nothing in between.

This was Wei at the base layer, what he was before he was anything else: someone who heard pain and moved toward it.

He was already running.

"Wei—"

Gone, into the undergrowth. No assessment, no pause, no calculation. He reacted to someone who was hurt.

I followed, fast, scanning for threats, sweeping the qi-field for anomalies, doing the tactical assessment he hadn't.

Wei knelt at the side of a woodcutter, a man in his fifties with his leg pinned under a fallen tree. Not a large tree, but large enough that he couldn't move it. The trunk lay across his lower leg at an angle that said broken.

His face was gray and sweating with pain. His eyes were wide and desperate.

Wei was reaching for the tree.

"Hold still," he said. The command voice, the authority of someone who had been in danger and come out the other side carrying confidence like a tool.

He channeled qi into his arms. Raw amplification, strength that muscle alone couldn't give. He gripped the trunk and lifted. The tree moved. Not much, but enough.

The woodcutter dragged himself backward, gasping, a body scrambling at the chance it had waited for. His broken leg trailed at the wrong angle, and the groan that escaped was relief and agony in equal measure.

Wei let the trunk drop. It hit the ground with finality.

"Your leg." A quick field assessment. He gathered branches for a splint and tore a strip of cloth from his own shirt for binding, the compression wrap I had shown him once. He did it from memory, a boy who learned by seeing once and doing forever.

The splint wasn't perfect, with the angle off and the binding tight in one place, loose in another. But functional. Sufficient.

Wei lifted the man and supported him with his right shoulder under the man's arm, taking care of his own injuries.

They walked toward the settlement, slowly, the woodcutter leaning on Wei, who was steady and patient under a weight that should have been too much for a body that had just fought a qi-beast.

I followed behind.

*Of course you'd help.*

This boy could not not help.

Behind him I thought about the lynx, the grin, the warmth in his sternum, and the two-heartbeat breath-stop. Then the word he'd used, tired, and the word I would have used, beginning.

The thing that made him beautiful was the thing that made him vulnerable.

And the thing that made him vulnerable was the thing that would—

I didn't finish.

We walked home.
