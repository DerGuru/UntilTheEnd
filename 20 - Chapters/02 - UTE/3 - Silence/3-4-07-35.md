The night wore on and the settlement went to sleep, the deep, heavy sleep that follows relief, food, and firelight. Earned and total.

Wei slept too, his face peaceful, the peace of someone who had saved twenty-seven people, stabilized a qi-source, and been celebrated, processing all of it through unconsciousness.

I knelt beside him in the nightly arrangement, knees on the floor, my hand hovering the usual two fingers' width above his chest. The ritual had become the architecture of my nights, producing data I filed, and the data showed a disturbing trend. It was getting worse. The progression continued.

A flicker, unsteady and visible through the shirt, nothing like the steady glow of a core at rest. The core was not resting. It was still resonating from today's output.

I counted.

Five.

Five twitches in sixty heartbeats now.

Not long ago it was three. Then four. Four became irregular. And now five. The progression bent upward, accelerating, each stage faster than the last, the intervals shortening.

I withdrew my hand.

I pulled his blanket higher, automatic. The hands that had braced against the cave wall and gripped the cold tea now performed the most basic act of care, covering a sleeping body against a cold that wasn't cold.

"Fixed it," Wei mumbled. Sleep-talking. The word surfacing from whatever dream he was in: victory, probably. The cave. The node. The doing.

"You did," I said quietly, though he couldn't hear me.

"All of it." A mumble. Then nothing, deep sleep reclaiming the consciousness that had surfaced.

I went outside into the night and the stars and the garden.

His garden, the three pots, was recovering from the qi-disruption. The dock grass was still wilted, but less. The nettle-cousin began to open its sealed bloom again. The lemongrass stood resilient, vertical, correct.

All because Wei had fixed the source. He had gone into the cave, channeled his qi, and stabilized the flows.

He saved the fields, and with them the coming harvests. He saved the water, cured the headaches. He saved Auntie Huang's chickens, Lao Chen's forge-fire, the unnamed grain, the dock grass, and the nettle-cousin.

He saved everything.

The man from the memory, in the city, in the square that would be named after him, had saved everything, too.

And the saving was the cost. The core was the clock.

*Five twitches. Accelerating. The curve suggests—*

I stopped.

The calculation, the forecast, the number the curve produced when I extended it.

I drank my cold tea. The swallow served as interruption, a trick that worked because the body's involvement broke the thought-chain, and the thought-chain needed breaking.

Stars, indifferent, the same stars that had hung over the man in the city and would hang there long after the boy in the settlement. Continuity as cruelty. The universe not caring.

I was not supposed to care either. The thing I was, the thing I had been, had included something close to indifference in its design. But the design was old, and I was no longer what I had been.

I cared again, which terrified me.

The word. The admission.

I cared about the boy. His core. The five, the curve, the trajectory. The grin, the scar, the hip correction, the victory, the voice that had shifted.

You were the one who taught me what that word costs.

Caring was the problem. Caring without acting was the torture the principle imposed, and acting without his permission was the violation caring prevented. The paradox, care demands action while principle prohibits it, was the architecture of my nights: standing in a garden with cold tea, five twitches in sixty heartbeats, and the thought I wouldn't finish.

Five. In sixty.

The night held.
