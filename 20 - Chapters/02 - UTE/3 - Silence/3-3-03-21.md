The lynx's paw swept in sideways, fast with qi-enhanced muscle, the forelimb almost as heavy as Wei's torso. For a moment I remembered the boar. At the time, he'd been just a boy without any cultivation. This was different.

Wei dodged, mostly. He moved left with the reflexes he'd built through months of training. His feet found the ground and his center held.

Almost.

The claw caught his sleeve, and the fabric tore in a clean line from shoulder to elbow. No blood, but close enough that Wei must have felt the air of the passing strike.

The lynx didn't pause. Its hindquarters compressed and drove it forward into a second attack, the animal's full weight aimed at Wei's chest.

Wei rolled over his shoulder, along his spine, across his hip, and onto his feet. The combat roll he'd practiced two hundred times carried him three paces across the clearing. The lynx landed where he'd been, claws finding earth instead of boy, dirt flying.

Wei stood with qi in his hands, bright and ready.

The lynx turned, impossibly fast for something that large, its mass scarcely slowing the pivot.

It came again.

This time Wei didn't dodge. He met it. The decision that separated defense from combat: he held his ground, choosing the space where the attack would arrive over the safety of stepping aside.

His qi-filled hands caught enough of the lynx's momentum to deflect it. At fourteen, he couldn't take the full weight of a qi-enhanced predator as heavy as a grown man. He was still learning the difference between confidence and capability. This time, enough.

The lynx slid past. Its flank scraped Wei's shoulder. Brief and hot.

Wei spun and followed with a qi-strike, palm forward, directing a pulse of raw energy. He connected. The lynx skidded, recovered, and shook its head.

The lynx showed no sign of injury. It circled the clearing now, keeping its distance after the two exchanges. Its head tracked Wei, but it no longer came straight at him.

Wei breathed hard, his face red, his chest heaving. His eyes were clear, fixed on the lynx.

I stood at the edge, eight paces away, my hands closed into fists. My nails pressed crescents into my palms. I kept them closed. Restraint made muscular.

I told myself again that he was ready. He was trained. He was capable. If I intervened now, unmaking the lynx with a fraction of my capacity, I would solve this fight and settle him more firmly into dependence.

I tasted iron. I'd bitten the inside of my cheek. The body's version of the mind's restraint.

Blood on my tongue and palms. Eight paces of distance that felt like a continent.

The lynx charged.
