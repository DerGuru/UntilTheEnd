Around the clearing, the birds went silent. The stream became audible because everything louder had gone quiet. The silence was the signal.

A heavy, compressed qi-signature moved fast through the undergrowth thirty paces east. A predator, coming toward the clearing.

A rock lynx.

It came out of the undergrowth, gray-brown fur over muscle denser and wider than normal animals should have. A qi-enhanced beast, years into an unguided, organic enhancement, the natural consequence of a qi-rich environment absorbed and compounded over time.

It was wolf-height but broader, as heavy as a full-grown man, moving at a speed normal cats didn't reach. Qi-lines ran through its fur like veins of pale blue light, pulsing as its muscles gathered for the charge. The canines ran longer than anatomy suggested, revised by qi into something sharper.

Its eyes were fixed on Wei. He'd trained here for twenty-one days, sending qi through the same clearing every morning. A territorial predator could have caught his scent on the ground or felt him from past the trees. Whether it had watched him before, I couldn't tell.

Now it came straight for him.

Wei's eyes opened.

Within a heartbeat, his training-trance broke. His hands opened, his knees bent, and his weight settled low over his feet. He turned toward the sound in the undergrowth.

"Something's here," he breathed.

He'd been training unarmed, practicing the forms that didn't require steel, the hand-to-hand vocabulary that was the foundation under the blade work. The sword he'd made with Lao Chen was at home, leaning against the wall by his bed, being ornamental.

The lynx charged directly at him. It didn't circle or pause at the clearing's edge. Its paws struck the moss in a steady, accelerating rhythm, its head low as it closed the distance.

Wei set his stance low, qi flowing into his hands in raw channels. This fight demanded raw power: refinement took time, and he had none with the lynx closing at eleven paces per heartbeat.

His hands glowed gold-white with qi directed through flesh, power gathered around his fingers.

"I can do this!"

He was speaking to himself, reaching for confidence and finding it.

The lynx closed to ten paces. Eight.

"Come on," Wei said. Quieter now. The bravado burned off, leaving something harder. Something that didn't need volume. "Come on, come *on*—"

I dropped from the branch.

The three-pace fall ended in a landing that made no sound. I stood at the clearing's edge, eight paces from Wei. Close enough.

My hands were ready, my qi suppressed but present, held just under the threshold of release. I could intervene. I could cross eight paces in a fraction of a heartbeat. I could unmake this animal with a thought.

I could, but not right now. First I wanted to see how Wei handled this. Whether he could do it. Whether he would.

"Hold your ground," I said, at conversational volume across eight paces of charged air. Shouting would make him flinch, and flinching was death.

Wei didn't turn. Good. But his spine straightened by a fraction: he was not alone, and he knew it.

The lynx reached Wei.
