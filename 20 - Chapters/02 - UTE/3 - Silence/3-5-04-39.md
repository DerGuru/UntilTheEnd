It was morning and he stood in the doorway, already dressed, already decided.

"I'll train slower."

"What?" I asked.

"You said my qi is growing too fast. So I'll train slower."

He faced me, a believer in the relationship between problem and solution. Rational. Linear. Fourteen.

"That's not—"

"What?"

"So simple."

"Why not?"

His core couldn't slow down. The acceleration came from me, whatever he did with his training. The instability was structural and structural problems didn't respond to behavioral solutions.

I said nothing.

He waited, then went outside. He trained, but slower, deliberately checking the speed his movements usually gathered. He moved through the forms at half-tempo, concentrated, his face set with the determination of having identified velocity as the variable and the variable as controllable.

It wouldn't work. His training speed was not the cause. The cause was the core. The core was the clock. The clock was independent of the training.

But I watched him train slower and said nothing more, because saying more required the truth.

He finished, wiped away sweat, and turned to me.

"Better?" he asked.

"Yes," I lied.

He went back to the forms. Slower still. A boy who believed discipline was the solution to everything, and the belief was the thing that made him Wei.

I stood at the edge, watching a student in danger. Young, talented, trained by me, and now facing the consequence. The sight of a student at the forms had reliably produced memories in the past: a sudden image, a lesson, a sentence about someone else who had done the same.

Nothing came.

I searched the archive for a face, a name, an era. For someone before him with the same grin or the same symptoms. It was empty.

Wei had no parallel.

Or I had grown too close. He was too near, too present, and too much himself. I couldn't see him clearly enough to compare him.

I stood watching him train, the forms at half-speed, the deliberate restraint that wouldn't work but that he believed would.

He finished and turned to me.

"Better?"

"Yes."

The second lie. Or the same lie repeated. Your slower training is working. The solution you've identified is correct.

He went inside. To eat and rest. To be fourteen. To be slower and believing.

I stood in the silence where the memory should have been. In the empty archive.

He's different.

The sentence had been hope. It was now fact. It was now a problem.

Different. No parallel, no roadmap, no prior case to consult.

For the first time in a long time, I didn't know what would happen next.
