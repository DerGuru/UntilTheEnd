I stood up at some point, unmarked, without decision, impulse, or trigger. The body stood because lying was not sustainable.

Outside the door, the bowl had gone cold, the steam gone, the rice hardened at the surface. I stepped over it without noticing, because the bowl was irrelevant.

Past the garden, his garden, the three pots. They registered at the edge of my vision, present and green, and the edge was all I permitted. Direct was not an option.

I started walking.

I walked through the settlement. Damaged walls stood on both sides. Settlers, civilians, survivors filled their doorways and windows, watching something that should not be watched and could not be avoided, because that something was walking down their street.

They moved out of my way.

People made the small, instinctive adjustments bodies made near something wrong: stepping back, pulling children closer, turning sideways. The universal vocabulary of avoidance, which needed no language or understanding. Only proximity.

Under my feet the grass turned brown in a radius of three paces, smaller than the thirty of the discharge's aftermath but persistent. Each step left brown behind it, the trail of someone whose trail was death.

An insect dropped mid-flight inside my radius and lay on the dirt, wings still.

Leaves curled at the edges on the trees I passed, on the bushes, on the vegetation lining the path out, the slow wilting autumn produced and I was producing now.

Past the east gate. Past the open ground. Past the place.

I did not look at the place. The dirt where he had lain, marked by blood, qi-residue, the chemical evidence of a body that had discharged, fallen, lain, and been. Whatever marked it, I did not look.

I went into the forest, to the large, old trees that had been growing before this settlement existed and would continue growing after it forgot itself. Their age was their resistance. The grass died in my wake, but the trees only weakened: leaves losing rigidity, bark drying, the subtle symptoms of exposure that would heal when the exposure left.

I walked with no direction and no purpose. The body moved because the alternative was the house and the house was empty.

I wanted to get away from people. Away from the settlement. Away from everything. The walls were holding, the proof that I had protected everything. But everything was a lie. Everything excluded him.

His mother, his brother, his sister, somewhere in a village with a cracked well and dry fields, would learn. Or they wouldn't, and the absence itself would become the message.

The forest closed behind me, green, and then the brown path I left, a trail of dead grass and curled leaves that told anybody not to follow. Someone had come this way who killed what she walked through.

I walked.
