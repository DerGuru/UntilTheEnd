In the late light, Wei moved through the seventh-form integrative sequence. The most complex pattern I'd taught him: simultaneous qi-circulation through three meridian sets while maintaining physical precision through twelve transitional postures. He executed it perfectly. There was nothing to correct or adjust.

There was grace in it, movement that transcended technique and became expression. I had seen it thousands of times across lifetimes, and it still did what it always did. It was beautiful to watch.

He was ready.

It showed in the steadiness of his hands. A few months more. Maybe a year. Then he would have nothing left to learn from me that he couldn't learn faster from experience.

His hand opened at the end of the sequence, palm up. An ordinary gesture; I'd seen him make it a hundred times. This time it brought back another pair of hands, as suddenly as a smell can bring back a room you haven't entered in decades.

He was ready. Someone else had been ready too. Once.

A man. I don't remember his face accurately. Time had averaged it, merged it with others. But his hands: those I remembered. Large. Calloused from holding a sword, a brush, and a child with equal frequency.

He was the last of a long line. Others had been better-born. He had made himself through diligence, through the daily use of those hands.

He smiled when he told me, "I don't need you anymore." An honest smile. I had welcomed it.

I was relieved. He was ready. He didn't need me. I could go.

So I went.

Three months. Three months and the kingdom he'd built was ash. He'd overreached; it hadn't even taken a war. He'd used what I taught him correctly, with precision, but competence alone was not enough. He had never learned his limits.

"He got everything he needed," I said. Out loud. To no one. To the air, the fence, and the cold tea. "Everything, except—"

I didn't finish. Still. After millennia.

Wei stopped, wiped sweat from his face and grinned.

"Was that good?"

"Acceptable."

He WAS different. He was stubborn where the other had been precise. He was defiant where the other had been methodical. He adapted where the other had optimized. He survived through stubbornness and the universe's inability to kill things that refused to acknowledge the attempt.

He was different.

He had to be different.

Because if he wasn't...

I drank my cold tea. The garden was golden in the late light. The dock grass was thriving. The lemongrass was lemongrass. The blade fragment caught the sun and threw a line of reflected light across the yard, a small bright stripe on the packed earth.

Wei trained on. I watched.

Different.

Please.
