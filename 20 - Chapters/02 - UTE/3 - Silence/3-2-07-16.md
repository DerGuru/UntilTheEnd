Before sunrise, Wei woke, dressed, and stood in the doorway. He could move quietly when he chose to, though quietly was a relative term when the listener was me. This morning he let me hear him getting ready.

"I'm going to have a look."

"I know."

"Just look."

I nodded and he left.

His bare feet sounded on stone, then dirt, then faded along the path east. The sound thinned as distance accumulated between us. I followed him a good distance with my qi-senses and stopped when he left the village.

I sat.

I was good at waiting. Millennia of it. Patient was what I was, with the long, empty patience that comes when the outcome doesn't matter because nothing matters. That kind of waiting was easy.

This time I wanted something. It made every part of the day heavy.

The house was small and quiet. It contained me, with room left over where he should have been. His absence pressed against the walls.

I made tea. Properly for once: hot water, leaves, and careful attention to the steeping. I used the process as a distraction, since my focus's current target was as empty as the house. I drank it hot, and the novelty was startling. The warmth of the cup as a substitute for the warmth of a boy.

Inadequate.

I don't know how many watches had passed. Time moved at the rate that the wrong kind of waiting imposed: slow, thick, and full of anxiety.

I had things I could do: training, repairs, walking, cooking, reading, meditation, and plans. I sat there instead, with the emptiness that had filled me for years, until I met a boy in the woods being attacked by a spirit beast. He'd been collecting the wrong mushrooms and herbs, trying to help his dying father.

The teacup had a hairline crack running from the rim to the midpoint. It was shallow, harmless, a flaw that reduced value without reducing function. I held the crack, my thumb over the line where the ceramic was compromised.

I released the crack. The cup held.

The sun moved from east to west. Four watches, four and a half, five.

In the garden, the dock grass had produced a second stalk, vertical and stiff. The lemongrass was being lemongrass, and the nettle-cousin had bloomed. A small white incorrect flower, beautiful in its own right. He'd cared for it as carefully as the herb.

The blade fragment caught the afternoon light. His things. Objects he'd put there himself. Proof he'd been here, that this was his place.

I waited. He would come back. Or he wouldn't. He would stay there. He would walk through the gate to see things that were shiny and people who were impressed. The brightness would be irresistible.

The crack in the cup. I put my thumb back.

The light turned low and golden by evening, the honey-shade that preceded sunset. Then footsteps approached along the path. I knew the rhythm of his feet on dirt, the cadence that was Wei arriving.

He stood in the doorway, feet black with road dust. His face was neutral.

"I'm staying."

Two words. The two best words. I went, I saw, and I chose. My choosing is here.

I said nothing. The exhale that didn't exhale. But my shoulders dropped, a shift from coiled to settled that I hadn't authorized and that he probably noticed.

"Why?" I asked. After he'd sat and poured tea.

He grinned, delighted to have found something funny in the serious.

"Because they were boring."

We stared at each other.

The crack in the cup was still holding.

"Boring," I repeated flatly.

"Boring."

Maybe the Iron Lotus training grounds had impressed him without interesting him. Impressive and interesting were two different things; his judgment lived in the difference.

Maybe the people there didn't see him. They saw his qi, his potential, and his usefulness. They saw a resource. At fourteen, he knew the difference between being seen and being measured. Perhaps he'd noticed.

Maybe back was here. Because I was here?

He wasn't saying that, and I never asked.

The answer was the grin.

"Have some tea," I said.

He drank.

The cup held.
