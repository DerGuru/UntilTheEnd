At night, Wei lay under his blanket, already heavy with exhaustion earned honestly through training, cooking, and existing at the velocity that fourteen demanded. His body spent its days fully and then stopped fully. There was rarely anything gradual about it.

"My form was better today," he said from his blanket, eyes already closed.

"Go to sleep."

"You're going to sit there all night again."

"Sleep."

"You worry too much."

"I worry the correct amount."

"Night, Yun."

"Good night."

I sat with my back against the wall, his breathing the room's only sound. A house at night had a different silence from a road at night: contained, bounded, and intimate.

Midnight. Or near it. I knew it by the depth of the darkness, the stillness that came once every lamp in the settlement was out.

The ambient qi shifted first, a subtle fluctuation that normal perception missed and mine catalogued, because cataloguing was my function or my curse.

Then there was light.

Through his shirt.

Through the fabric that covered his chest, through the threads and the weave. Fabric opaque to sunlight, candlelight, and firelight, yet this shone through. The glow that qi produced when it exceeded the body's capacity to contain it: warm gold, like late afternoon compressed into a point at the sternum of a sleeping boy who didn't know he was a lantern.

I stood and crossed the room. Two steps and I was on his bed. I knelt at his side.

His face in the glow was peaceful. He slept through the radiance. Awake, he could control it, suppress it, becoming the daytime version of Wei, the one who dampened, compressed, and contained. That version was a performance of normalcy, increasingly expensive, paid for in tremors, nosebleeds, and other symptoms he tried to hide.

This nighttime version was what he was. Unperformed. Uncontained. A core shining at its natural strength, three stages above what was usual for his age, still growing at a rate that defied every pattern I'd ever observed.

I held my hand above his chest, a fingerbreadth clear of his shirt. The space between my palm and his sternum filled with the warm pressure of qi dense enough to feel like a substance, like water caught between liquid and light.

Beautiful.

The word that kept arriving for things that were also terrifying. There was a sunset's beauty in it: brilliance I wanted to watch, even as the light passing through his shirt made clear how little his body could contain.

I counted. Counting gave me something definite to hold on to while he slept. Numbers were manageable.

One for every twenty heartbeats. His core pulsed and shone through his shirt, stuttering at a constant interval. The instability had become rhythmic, a structural flaw repeated with every interval I counted. Each twitch pressed against my palm as distinctly as a crack beneath a finger.

Yesterday there had been two in sixty heartbeats. Today there were three.

I withdrew my hand. I pulled up his blanket and tucked it around his shoulders, the ordinary gesture of adjusting bedding. I am here, you are warm, and the covering is intact. I did it because it was the only action I could bear to take. The other action was waking him, telling him that his core was flickering, that it was worsening, and that I had seen this shape before.

I went outside.

The half-moon hung at its angle, half-lit, half-dark.

The garden. Three pots in a row. I grounded myself in the ordinary: the dock grass, the nettle-cousin, and the lemongrass. The blade fragment. The stone. Things I could touch, see, and smell. Things that were dark under the moon, with no pulse to measure.

The blade fragment caught the moonlight. A thin line of reflective silver.

Three twitches in sixty heartbeats.

That is...

I didn't finish the thought, the sentence, the calculation. I knew the end. Knowing it and acknowledging it were different acts; once I acknowledged it, I would have to do something with what was real.

Inside, the light faded. His core settled. The boy slept.

I sat outside and longed for tea. Even cold tea. I would have drunk it anyway.
