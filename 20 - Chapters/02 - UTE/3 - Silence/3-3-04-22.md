The lynx came low, a different approach. Two high attacks had been met with evasion, and low was the angle Wei hadn't defended. Qi-enhanced animals learned fast. The intelligence wasn't human, but it was sufficient.

Wei adjusted, but too late. In the fraction of a heartbeat between anticipation and reaction came the lynx's claw.

The paw caught him across the shoulder, diagonal and deep.

Tearing fabric and something wetter, skin separating under force, reached me across eight paces with the clarity of a perception designed for detail and currently producing nausea.

Blood came immediately, red, hot, and vivid.

Wei screamed, short and loud. One syllable. His body had been breached, and pain was always a surprise, even with training. The first pain in a fight always makes you flinch.

Then silence, his jaw closed, teeth together. He stopped screaming through sheer willpower.

And he didn't step back.

That was the thing with him: he didn't retreat. Not anymore. His left arm hung at his side, bleeding from shoulder to elbow, but his right arm came up with qi concentrated in the palm, brighter than before. The brightness of a body that was hurt and answered by pushing harder.

The lynx turned and closed the distance. It came low again, the strike aimed at his midsection.

Wei met it with his right palm forward, another qi-pulse, raw and unfiltered. A fourteen-year-old whose left shoulder was open and whose right hand was everything.

The strike hit the lynx square in its center.

The animal staggered. A real stagger, physical disruption, from a qi-strike powered by pain and the fuel only desperation provided.

The lynx shook. Its qi-lines flickered. For two heartbeats, maybe three, the enhancement wavered.

Wei pressed forward. Another strike with his right hand. The same hand. The same arm. He channeled everything he had, expensive and unsustainable, because it was the only option. He had no choice but to go all in.

"Stay back," he said. Through his teeth. To me. Without looking.

The lynx backed off two steps, three, its posture shifting from predatory to hesitant, a territorial animal facing a bleeding boy whose qi was too bright.

I stood eight paces away, my mouth full of blood, crescents cut into my palms by my own nails, my feet planted, holding myself still by making my body heavy.

Wei's eyes found mine.

One glance, across the clearing, through the blood, the sweat, and the torn sleeve. One word in them: No.

It crossed the channel between us, built by proximity and silence.

*I can do this.*

I reined myself in and stayed.

The cost of staying was the blood on my tongue and the crescents in my palms. Not acting was the hardest thing I could do, which was weird, because two years ago, and for centuries before that, it had been my default, my only reaction.

But that was before I got involved in the rescue of a village, with a boy too stubborn to leave me alone.

The lynx steadied. Its qi-lines re-established. It hadn't retreated far enough. Still here. Still in the fight.

Wei's left arm was dripping, not critical yet but accumulating.

He breathed and set his stance, right arm up, left arm held close.

The lynx gathered. Muscles bunching under qi-lit fur.

Wei breathed out. Slow. Ready.
