The western hill. Two hundred paces away.

A signature expanded all at once, like a door opened onto a continent of qi. Immortal-stage and above.

My perception whited out. Everything else vanished: the combat, the signatures, the barriers, Wei's core. A presence on a scale I had not encountered this century replaced them.

I turned by reflex, a stimulus that bypassed thought and arrived at muscle, at bone. All of me oriented toward it like a compass toward north.

Two hundred paces up the slope. Gray morning, gray trees. A figure, simply present as beings of that magnitude were present. Inevitable.

The Hermit Master. The mountaintop. The encounter that had lasted moments and contained centuries.

He was here.

His qi reached me, probing rather than aggressive. One Immortal-tier being assessing another. The question he asked with energy: *Are you in danger?*

He had felt Wei's core-flare, the instability, the brightness screaming across the spectrum, and he had come. The last time he'd seen me, I was alone on a mountain. Now I was at the center of a conflict. He was trying to protect me.

The irony. A being who had traveled far to protect someone who did not need protection, and the protection was the distraction.

He raised his hand, two fingers extended in salute, the same gesture as before. Friendly. Unsettling. Knowing.

My back was to the east gate. My back was to Wei. And the third knock was happening behind me.

I answered. My qi met his, brief, confirmatory. *Not in danger. Not from them.*

He adjusted the tilt and lowered his salute.

The combat continued behind me, distant, muffled, like rain heard through glass. Real but separate.

Then Wei's core. A flare unlike the combat flares I'd been tracking. This was a core under maximum stress, discharging without governor, without limiter.

I turned back.

*Of course. Right now.*

The thought arrived cold. The timing was causality. The Immortal had arrived at the third knock, my attention pulled at the exact moment Wei ran into open ground.

I reached for his signature and tried to read the settlement, the positions, the combat. Static. The Hermit's presence had burned through my perception like staring at the sun burns through vision. The afterimage lay over everything, vast, bright, blinding. Wei's core registered, and it was wrong. Nothing else came through the interference.

I was on the wall. Facing the right direction. And I was blind.

I walked the field afterward. Before the blood dried. Before anyone moved the bodies. I walked it as I had walked battlefields for centuries: reading positions, reading angles, reading the story that violence wrote in dirt and distance.

The bootprints told me where Wei had stood. The scorch marks told me where qi had discharged. Shen told me the rest, later, in fragments, between sobs, while Auntie Huang held him and I listened from outside the door. I knew Wei well enough to reconstruct every choice he made.

This is what happened while my back was turned.

Wei ran. Out through the gate. Into the open. Toward the figure. Alone, completely alone, separated from the settlers, from the walls, from every defensive position.

Wei reached the figure and knelt. Hands reaching out, the gesture of aid, the instinct Guo had identified and placed a body to trigger.

He looked down.

Clean boots.

Clean boots on a "scout" who had been crawling through dirt. Clean boots, polished. Real scouts had dirt-covered boots, bark, blood. These boots had touched the ground moments ago, and those moments had not included crawling.

It was a trap.

Wei's hand went to his sword. But the false scout was already reaching under his coat for a signal amulet. A pulse of qi, bright and directed: *here, now, he's isolated.*

Four signatures converging. From the treeline. From positions where they had waited patient and hidden for the third knock, the beacon, the boy alone in open ground.

Wei stood with his sword up and his core pulsing dim-bright-dim, his reserves mere remnants.

Four cultivators. Four angles. This was a kill-box.

And then, from behind Wei, from the gate, from the settlement, a small figure running.

Shen.

Auntie Huang's son. Twelve. Who had been pushed toward the escape route by his mother's hands. Who wasn't gone. Who had followed Wei through the gate because he followed Wei everywhere.

Fifteen paces in front of Wei. In the open ground. Between Wei and the converging signatures.

In the crossfire.

*You turn around and they're gone.*

Auntie Huang's words. At the landslide. The sentence that had passed through me once and settled somewhere deep.

The boy stood there, small and confused, his eyes on Wei, a boy who had followed a hero into a place where following was dying.

Wei saw the child and the attackers both.

Four signatures closing. Ten heartbeats.

The boy standing, frozen.

Wei's core flickering, the light at his sternum visible and bright, the countdown counting in heartbeats.
