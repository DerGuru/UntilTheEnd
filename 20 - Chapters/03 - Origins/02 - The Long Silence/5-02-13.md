I could set the years after the garden down as a list, and the list is the point, so it will run thin.

There were people. There are always people. I moved among them, and they were kind to me and cruel to me and mostly neither, and I helped some and buried some and left the rest. I know this happened, because it always happened. What I cannot do is hand them back whole, because I did not take them in whole. By then I was letting the world pass through me without catching on anything.

There was a woman by a salt marsh who— I had her a moment ago. Something about her hands and a song for gutting fish. She is gone already.

A man who took me in over a hard winter and asked for nothing but talk. I have the winter. I have the shape of his kindness. His face has closed over like the rest.

A child I pulled out of a flood channel, who— someone's child, small, screaming, and alive when I set her down. I could not tell you if she grew. I did not stay to see. There were so many children pulled from so many floods that they run together now into one wet, screaming, saved child with no face at all.

This is what the numbness did that frightens me most to tell, now that I can feel again. It did not hurt. It was not grief. It was the slow going-out of my ability to see one person as different from the next. Lives became interchangeable. I have looked a long time for a gentler word than that one, and there is none. A face was a thing that would be gone soon, so why learn its edges. Better to save the seeing for something that would last. And nothing lasted, so I stopped seeing much at all.
