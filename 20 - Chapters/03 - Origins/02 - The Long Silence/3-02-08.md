I made a bargain, though there was no one to make it with but myself.

The terms were simple. Every fire I sat at, I would leave. That was fixed. I could not change it. But I could change how much of me I brought to the fire in the first place. If I gave less, I would lose less. If I never learned the names, the faces would cost me less when they went. A smaller life, bought at a smaller price.

So I traded down. I stayed weeks instead of years. I helped with my hands and kept my history behind my teeth. When someone leaned toward me, wanting a friend, a daughter, or a wife, I felt the lean and stepped back from it before it could land. And it worked. That is the part worth understanding: it worked. Leaving stopped costing what it had cost. I walked away from good people with my chest quiet where it used to ache.

There was a settlement in a high valley, a decent place, decent people, a season of good work. I had kept my careful distance the whole time and learned no one's name on purpose. When the snow threatened and it was time to go, a woman I had worked beside every day pressed bread into my hands and looked at me as though she was sorry to see me go. I walked out over the pass. I waited for the pull, the small tearing that leaving had always cost.

It did not come. I felt almost nothing. A little tug, thin as thread, and then the clean cold air and the open road and my own steady breath.

I remember being pleased. I had finally learned to do the thing well. I had made the wound so small I could barely feel it close. It did not occur to me to wonder what else in me might have gone quiet to make that possible. It would be an age before I connected the smallness of the hurt to the smallness of everything else. That evening I noted only the success, like a snare finally set right, and walked on into the dark, well pleased with my own good sense.
