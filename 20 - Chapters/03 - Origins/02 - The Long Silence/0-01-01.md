The fire has burned down to the stage where it gives heat and almost no light. Xu Ran feeds it anyway. Three sticks, set down with more care than the task needs, because his hands want something to do while he waits.

Across the coals, the woman does not seem to notice the dark. She has been giving him the beginning these last few nights. The cave. The cold of it. The water that fell from the ceiling and kept time when there was nothing else to keep it. The people who took the girl in, and the people who drove her out. He has all of it now, set down in the small careful hand he keeps for the nights he does not want to lose a word.

She has gone quiet. He has learned what her quiet is worth, so he waits and does not fill it.

"What I didn't tell you," she says, and she is not looking at him, "is that I can't find her anymore. The girl. Mei." She says the name like a word in a language she stopped speaking a long time ago. "I remember what she did. I don't remember being her. When I reach for her I find a story, told well, by someone who was there. That is as close as I get now."

The pen is loud against the quiet. He makes himself keep it moving.

"She came before," she says. "Before the—" She stops. He has watched her stop at this word on other nights, and he has learned to let it lie. "Before the thing in me that won't let go. She had it by the end, I think. She just didn't know what it would cost, or that it would cost anything at all. Neither did I, for a long time after."

The fire settles into itself. She watches it do that, and for a breath he thinks she has forgotten he is there.

Then she looks up. "After the tribe, I left." She has not looked at him quite like this before. "That part, no one has heard. I'll give it to you first."

---

After the tribe. After the girl became a stranger to me, and I to her, I left. I walked out of everything I knew and into everything I did not. And for a while, it was the best thing that ever happened to me. The only long while I can honestly call happy.
