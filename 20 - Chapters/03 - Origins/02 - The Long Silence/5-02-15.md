There was a man she traveled with for a while after that. She would not have called him a friend; she had given the word up. But he was good company on a hard road, quick to laugh, easy with a fire, and in an earlier age she would have kept him as long as he let her.

He narrated the road to himself under his breath, naming the hills things like Big Ugly, The Other One, and That Bastard There, a low running commentary that asked nothing of her but filled the silence anyway. Once he caught her almost smiling at it. "There," he said, pleased with himself. "Knew you had one in you." She did not tell him he was the first person in a lifetime to go looking. She let him think it was his doing. It cost her nothing, it pleased him, and those were the terms she kept the world on now.

She traveled with him a summer and part of a winter. When he fell ill on a road between two towns whose names she never learned, she stayed, because staying with the dying was the one habit that had outlived everything else in her.

He died in a roadside shelter in the rain. She closed his eyes. She built him a cairn out of what stones the field gave her, because it seemed owed. And then she sat down to feel it.

I want to be exact about this, because it is the hinge the rest of my life turns on. She knew the grief was supposed to come. She had felt it over the old woman by the lake, over Lin Feiyun, over more people than a long life has days. She had felt it coming a hundred times before it broke, the pressure of it building behind the eyes. So she sat by the man's cairn in the wet and opened the place it came from.

Nothing came. Not a lessened grief. Not a buried one. An absence, clean and complete, like reaching for a color at the edge of the sky and finding the sky had none there.

She sat with the nothing a while. And here is the thing I have never been able to soften, in all the time since: the nothing did not trouble her. A wiser woman, a woman still whole, would have been frightened to feel nothing over a man who had shared his fire with her. Mei only noted the emptiness, like a tool worn smooth, with a distant, practical interest and no alarm at all.

It did not bother her that it did not bother her. That is the whole of it. That is the sentence I would carve on the marker of the girl she used to be, if I could find where that girl is buried. She got up when the rain eased. She left the man his cairn. She walked on. Her step was easy, and the ease was the worst thing about it. She did not think so at the time.
