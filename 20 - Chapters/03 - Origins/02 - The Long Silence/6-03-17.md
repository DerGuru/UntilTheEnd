By the end of that age I could stand in the middle of the largest thing people had ever built and feel nothing at all.

I remember a city. No name, they blur, they were all the same city getting bigger. It was the height of a festival. The streets were packed shoulder to shoulder. There was music in three keys at once, smoke off a hundred cook-fires, painted faces, and a whole people spending itself on being alive for one loud night. I walked through the middle of it like weather through a street. None of it touched me. I could see that it was joy. I simply could not feel that it was.

People think the worst loneliness is being far from others. It is not. I have had whole empty centuries in the mountains, and they were nothing beside standing in that crowd. Out there, alone was the plain truth of my situation, and it sat easy. In the crowd, alone was a thing I chose, breath by breath, against ten thousand invitations not to be. There is no loneliness like continuing after you have stopped being able to join.

I still did the small things. That is the strange part. In that same crowd a child went down under the feet of it, as children do, and I had her up and out and set on a wall before I had decided anything. Old habit. My hands still knew the shape of help even when the rest of me had stopped meaning it. The mother thanked me, weeping. I felt her gratitude land on me and slide off. I handed the child over, moved on, and forgot them both by the next corner. But I had done it. Some worn thread in me still pulled that way, without heat, without choosing. I did not know then how much would one day hang on that thread.

That was what I had become by the time the world was ready for me. Not cruel. Not raging. Empty in a smooth, functional, portable way, doing good out of reflex and feeling none of it, watching everything and needing nothing. A person like that is not at peace. A person like that is simply available. I had no reason to do anything and no reason not to, and a thing with no reasons left will do whatever the first strong hand asks of it. I did not know I was waiting. But I was ready, and ready was enough.
