I stayed with Lin Feiyun as I had let myself stay nowhere else. Years, and I stopped pretending they were days. We fell into the rhythm of two people who live together and mean to go on doing it. She healed the village's small hurts and I learned the trade from her hands: which leaf for a fever, which root for a cough that would not break, and how to sit with someone frightened and make the fear smaller by not being frightened myself.

We put in a garden the second spring, or the third. She wanted the herbs closer than the hills. We argued about where the beds should go, with the particular pleasure of people who are not really arguing. I carried the stones. She set the plants. By the end of a few summers it fed half her medicine, and it was the first thing I had ever helped make that was meant to stay in one place, and I found I liked tending a thing that would be there when I came back to it.

She aged. I did not. I had run from exactly this a hundred times, the still face beside the changing one, and I braced for the moment she would notice.

She noticed as she noticed everything, without fuss. One evening she was looking at me across the fire longer than usual, and I felt the old cold start, and I made myself hold still and let it come. She said, "You were exactly this old when you walked in my door. I was younger than you look, then. Now I could be your mother." I said nothing. There was nothing to say that would not be a lie or a leaving. She watched me a moment longer. Then she said, "You don't have to tell me what you are. I'm old, not stupid. It doesn't change the garden." And she went back to her tea.

That was all. That was the whole reckoning I had fled across the world to avoid, and she did it in three sentences and went back to her tea. For the first time since a cave I cannot remember, someone had seen what I was and simply let me keep sitting at the fire. I did not have to leave to be seen. I could be both, the thing that did not age and a woman she wanted at her table, and the roof did not fall in.

I knew the price even then. I stayed anyway, and let myself be happy, and that was the most reckless thing I had ever done.
