One of them, late in that stretch, I watched from close enough to touch.

I will not give it a name. It had a name, and banners for the name, and a founding story its poets swore was unlike any before it. I have forgotten the name on purpose, as I forgot most of them, because remembering would have meant believing there was a difference worth the room.

I saw it born. A hard man out of a hungry province pulled the pieces together by war, by marriage, and by killing the right cousins, and his people believed, as the young of every rising thing believe, that they were the ones who had finally gotten it right. That this order would hold. That they were the end of the story and not a chapter of it.

And I knew. Not guessed. Knew, in my tired bones, every turn the thing would take before it took it. I could have told the crowd at the first crowning how the last one would go. The founder would be wise, and then merely lucky, and then dead. The heirs would mistake the trappings of power for the thing itself. The borders would reach past what the roads could feed. Someone far from the center, in a hungry province much like the first, would begin to pull the pieces together by war, by marriage, and by killing the right cousins. I had watched the same water go around the same wheel more times than that empire had years.

You would think knowing the future would feel like power. It felt like boredom. There is no contempt quite like the contempt of watching people bleed and boast and build over something whose ending you could recite. I did not hate them for it. Hate would have been warmer than what I had. I watched a hundred thousand people pour their one short life each into a shape I knew was sand, and I felt the last warm thing in me harden over and go quiet, the last place that had still wanted to warn them, or save one of them, or care which of them won. After that empire I did not look at the next with anything at all. I had become a woman who could watch the world burn and check it off a list.
