I began to test the edges of myself. It is what anyone does with a new thing, before they know where it ends.

I climbed higher than sense allowed and hung there with my fingers burning, because the burning was interesting and the fall did not frighten me. I stayed in a river fed by snowmelt until my legs stopped answering, walked out on stumps I could not feel, and sat in the sun while they came back to me as pain. I cut myself on a blade I was sharpening and watched the edges of the wound think about it, then draw closed. None of it held. Whatever the world did to me, my body treated as a suggestion, and declined.

So I grew careless. A person cannot be careful with something they do not yet know they can lose.

It caught up with me on a scree slope above a valley I wanted to see the bottom of. The ground I trusted was resting on nothing. It went, and I went with it.

And then the world stopped.

The stones fell with me and then they did not fall. They hung in the air around me, gray and still, each one throwing its small shadow. A bird had been calling somewhere above. The call was not there. My own breath was not there. No wind, no slide, no sound at all, only the held weight of everything at once, and me inside it, waiting without knowing what for.

Then it ran on.

A stone came down where a stone should come down. My shoulder found rock, then my hip, then the whole long grinding rest of it, and I stopped at the bottom in a cloud of my own dust, breathing, with the bird calling again as though it had never left off.

I lay there while my body took itself apart and put itself back together, which took the better part of two days and hurt the entire time. When I could stand, I looked back up the slope. I could not find where I had started. Too high. Nothing should have walked away from that.

I decided I was lucky. Or that I was simply hard to break, harder than other people, like green wood that turns the wedge. I turned the thought over once, found no use in it, and set it down. Then I went to find the bottom of the valley, because I had come all that way to see it.
