Here I have to do the thing I warned you I would do. I have to hand you thousands of years in a breath, because she lived them at a distance, and to give them to you slowly would be to lie about how they felt to her. They felt like weather. So: the world got busy while she felt less and less of anything, and this is how that went.

Paths she had walked when they were game trails became roads with stones set in them. The small settled places grew. Villages like the one Lin Feiyun had lived and died in grew. They thickened, joined, and threw up walls. People who had always kept a little more than they needed began keeping a great deal more, then counting it, then building walls around the count. Some of it still reached her, in the early days of it. The first time she saw marks pressed into wet clay, and watched a man read yesterday's words off yesterday's mud, she stared like a child. She had been alive when speech was the only way to carry a thought across a night, and here was a thought outliving the mouth that made it. For a while she went from town to town learning the marks, as she had once learned the words for water, fish, and cold.

But wonder needs a short memory, and hers had gotten very long. She watched the marks go from marvel to ledger inside a few lifetimes: poems, then tallies of grain, then debts, then the names of who owed and who was owed. She watched walls go up to keep raiders out and get used to keep farmers in. And she watched the first empires rise, and found she knew every step before it took the step.

She would stand at the crowning of some hard young king and know, in her tired bones, exactly how it ended. The founder wise, then merely lucky, then dead. The heirs mistaking the trappings of power for the thing itself. The borders reaching past what the roads could feed. And somewhere out past the edge, in a hungry province much like the first, another hard young man beginning to gather the pieces by war, by marriage, and by killing the right cousins. She had watched the same water go around the same wheel more times than any of their empires had years. It was not new. It was only bigger. And knowing the ending of every rising thing did not feel like wisdom. It felt like being the only one awake in a room full of sleepers who think their dreams are the world.

I did not hate them for it. Hatred would have been warmer than what I had. I watched a hundred thousand people pour their one short life each into a shape I knew was sand. And I felt the last warm thing in me harden over and go quiet: the last part that had still wanted to warn one of them, or save one, or care which of them won. After that I stopped seeing the empires as anything but weather. I had become a woman who could stand at the edge of a burning city and check it off a list.
