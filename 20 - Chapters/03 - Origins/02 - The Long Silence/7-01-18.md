For most of my life I had a single rule I never broke: be gone before they see what you are.

I had broken cover only once, for Lin Feiyun, and she had held the secret to her grave. Every other time, at the first long look, the first child's arithmetic, the first gap opening at the fire, I left. It had kept me a rumor at most, never a fact. A strange woman who passed through once, who maybe did not age, who was surely misremembered. Nothing anyone could follow.

Then I stopped bothering.

It was not a decision. Deciding would have taken more wanting than I had. It was only that leaving had always been work, the packing, the cover story, and the long walk to the next place, and one day I no longer had the small store of care it took to do it. A village in a river valley took me in for a season. A cart went over in the road, and a wheel that should have broken me across the chest broke against me instead, and I got up out of the mud in front of a dozen people and shook it off.

The old rule said run. I looked at the faces, all of them staring, doing the arithmetic much faster than usual, and I felt for the place where the urge to leave should have been. It was empty, like all the other places. So I stayed. I finished the season. I let them watch.

They did not drive me out. It had gone past that. You cannot stone a woman who stands up from under a cart, so instead they made me into something to be talked about. The talk went out as talk goes, down the river with the boats and up the roads with the traders, and it grew as it went. A woman in the valley who does not die. By the time it had traveled a season it had my hair wrong and my face wrong, and half of it was lies. But the center of it was true, and it was the first true thing about me to outlive my leaving, because for the first time I had not left. I stood still and let the story form around me. I did not care who heard it.

That, it turned out, was exactly the mistake the world had been waiting for me to make.
