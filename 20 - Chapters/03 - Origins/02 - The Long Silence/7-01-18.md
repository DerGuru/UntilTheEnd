For most of her long life she had kept one rule above the others: be gone before they see what you are.

She had broken it once, for Lin Feiyun, and Lin Feiyun had held the secret to her grave. Every other time she had left: at the first long look, the first child's arithmetic, the first gap opening at the fire. It kept her a rumor at most, never a fact. A strange woman who passed through once. Who maybe did not age. Who was surely misremembered. Nothing anyone could follow.

Then she stopped bothering.

It was not a decision. Deciding would have taken more wanting than she had. It was only that leaving had always been work: the packing, the story, the long walk to the next place. One day she no longer had the small store of care it took to do it. She had taken a season's work in a river town. A cart went over in the road. A wheel that should have broken her across the chest broke against her instead. She stood up out of the mud in front of a dozen people and shook it off.

The old rule said run. She looked at the faces, all of them staring, doing the sum much faster than usual, and she felt for the place where the urge to leave should have been. It was empty, like all the other places. So she stayed. She finished the season. She let them watch.

They did not drive her out. It had gone past that. You cannot stone a woman who stands up from under a cart, so instead they made her into a thing to be talked about. The talk went out as talk goes, down the river with the boats and up the roads with the traders. It grew as it went. A woman in the valley who does not die. By the time it had traveled a season it had her hair wrong, her face wrong, and half of it was lies. But the center of it was true, and it was the first true thing about her to outlive her leaving, because for the first time she had not left. She had stood still and let the story form around her. She did not care who heard it.

That, it turned out, was the one mistake the world had been waiting for her to make.
