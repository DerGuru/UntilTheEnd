She came down out of the mountains, however long she had truly been up there, into a city. It was one of the nameless ones, because by then they had blurred together into the same city getting bigger. She arrived during a festival, at the height of it. The streets were packed shoulder to shoulder. Music in three keys at once, smoke off a hundred cook-fires, painted faces, and a whole people spending itself on being alive for one loud night.

She went into it on purpose. She had come down off the mountain for this, though she would not have said so: to stand in the middle of the loudest living thing she could find and see whether any of it would catch. A dance ring came wheeling down the street. A girl with a painted face grabbed her hand and pulled her in. Mei knew the steps. She had known steps like them for longer than the city had stood, and she did them right, every one. After a turn of the ring the girl let go of her hand and reached for someone else, without unkindness, as you put down a cup that turns out to be empty. Mei stepped out of the ring. It closed behind her.

Still, her hands did the small good things. That is the part I hold onto, when I am trying to be fair to what she had become. In that same crowd a child went down under the feet of it, as children do. She had it up, out, and set on a wall in three movements. Old habit. Her hands still knew how to help even when the rest of her had stopped meaning it. The mother came shoving through the crowd, snatched the child off the wall, and caught Mei's hands in both of hers. She held on, weeping, saying something over and over that the music took. Mei stood and let her. She waited for the woman to let go. When she did, Mei went on, and had forgotten them both by the next corner. But she had done it. Some worn thread in her still pulled toward it, without heat, without choosing. She did not know then how much would one day hang on that thread.

That was what she had become by the time the world got around to wanting her. Not cruel. Not raging. Empty in a smooth, functional, portable fashion, doing good out of reflex and feeling none of it, watching everything, needing nothing. A woman like that is not at peace. A woman like that is only available. She had no reason to do anything and no reason not to. A thing with no reasons left will do whatever the first strong hand asks of it. She did not know she was waiting. But she was ready, and ready was enough.
