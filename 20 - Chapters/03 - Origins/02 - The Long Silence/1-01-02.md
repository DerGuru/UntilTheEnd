I walked east, because the morning came from there and I liked walking into it. When the land bent, I bent with it. That was the whole of my method in those years, and for a long time it was enough.

I ate things to find out what they were. A white root that snapped like green wood and tasted of pepper and cold earth. The pale inside of a bird's egg, still warm from the nest. A blue berry that numbed my tongue to the root, so that for half a day I said words to myself only to feel them come out crooked. I learned which mushrooms sat quiet in the belly and which ones turned the night into a long argument with the dark. I had time to be wrong. I could eat the wrong thing, be sick against a rock until the stars moved, then wake with the taste still in my teeth, my body already deciding the matter was settled. Very little I put into it could find a reason to stay.

I came to a place where the sea reached in between two shoulders of rock, a narrows of gray water pulling hard two ways at once. On the far side the hills were a different green. That was the only reason I needed. I went in where the pull was worst, because it was the shortest line across, and the cold closed over me like a fist. The current took me and I let it. I did not drown. I remember thinking, down in the churn of it, that I was curious how this would go. Not afraid. Curious. Then my feet found stone, I climbed out into strange grass, and lay there steaming, pleased with the new green.

That was how those years went. The world was enormous. It was all mine to taste. Nothing in it could touch me for long. I did not think of myself as a woman who could not die; I had no such idea, and no word to hold it in. I only knew that whatever happened to me closed over and was gone, like water over a dropped stone.

And I had time.

That is the part I have never been able to hand to anyone whole. Time was not a thing I could run out of. It was not water in a skin, going down with every swallow. It was the ground I stood on to reach for everything else, and it did not end. So nothing I wanted was urgent. Nothing I loved was in danger of running out. I had somewhere to be only when I decided I did, and most days I decided I did not. I wanted everything the world could hand me, and I felt no hurry about any of it, because there was always more morning coming.
