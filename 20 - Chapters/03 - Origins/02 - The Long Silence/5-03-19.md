She went looking for the sea on purpose the season after the potters, and it took her most of a year to find one. She could not have told you which coast it had been, that first time, or how far it lay from anything she knew now. Any sea would do. She was not going back to a place. She was going back to a girl who had walked into the water with her clothes on and come out laughing.

On the way she ate things to find out what they were. A white root that snapped like green wood. It tasted of root. A blue berry off a low bush. She held it on her tongue a long time, waiting. Then she swallowed it and walked on.

She came over a headland one morning and found the sea.

It was gray and enormous. It went all the way to the edge of the sky, exactly as it had before. She stood on the height and waited for it to do to her what it had done the first time. The wind came up off the water and flattened her clothes against her. Gulls. Salt. A long slow roar that never quite stopped. She stood there until the sun was well beyond its zenith, and then she went down to it, because she had not come all this way to stand on a hill.

She walked in to her knees with her clothes on. The cold took hold of her shins. She noted it. She walked in to her waist, then her chest. The swell lifted her off her feet, set her down, and lifted her again. 

The whole time she waited for the laugh.

A wave she had not thought to respect knocked her flat.

It rolled her, and she let it. Then the backwash took her.

She felt the sand go out from under her in one long pull, as if the whole beach were being drawn back into the sea, and she went with it. She came up past where the waves broke, in deep water. Her years among the lake people had made a swimmer of her. She had dived for snagged traps out past the reeds until deep water was only water. She turned for the shore with the long, easy stroke she had learned on the lake.

The shore was already farther off than it should have been, and it did not come any closer. She swam harder, but it went farther away. The current had her by the whole body. It was taking her out, sideways and out, toward the dark water past the headland.

Her stroke came apart. She stopped swimming and started fighting. The water came over her face. She swallowed it, coughed, and swallowed more. Her arms burned. Her chest burned worse. A swell rose behind her, broke over her head, and drove her under. Green dark, full of sand and roaring. She did not know which way was up.

She needed air. She needed it so badly that the need was all there was. Her mouth opened on its own, and the sea came in.

The next wave found her and threw her at the rocks.

She hit the foot of the headland back first. Something in her went with a crack she heard through the water. The wave dragged her down the rock face, across a crust of shells that opened her from shoulder to hip. It pulled her back out. It threw her again. This time her head. White, then nothing, then the green again. The cold. Her hands clawing at stone they could not hold.

The third wave put her on a ledge. She got one hand into a crack and held on while the water went back without her. Then she was lying on wet rock with the sea pouring out of her mouth and nose, retching, every breath dragging over something broken in her chest.

She lay there through the rest of the tide while it mended. The ribs went first, one deep private click and then another. The back took longer. She did not scream this time. She did not have the breath to spare, and later, when she did, she found she did not need to.

When she could sit up, she was on a little shelf of shingle at the foot of the rocks. The sea had gone quiet, as though none of it had happened. There was a white spiral shell by her hand, the kind a creature has finished with.

She looked at it until the next wave took it.
