I carried the old woman's fire out of that place in a horn of bark, and I told myself it was only sensible not to waste a good coal. I had walked away from her grave with her fire in my hands and a lie already on my lips, and I did not yet know that this would become the shape of me. Taking the warm thing, and giving it a cold reason.

I found other fires. That was still easy then. A pair of hands that knew wood and fish and weather was welcome most places, and I was good company when I wanted to be. I stayed with a river people through the birth of two sets of children. And that was the mistake, staying long enough for the children to grow.

A child who has known you all her short life measures you against her own growing. She gets taller. You do not. By the time she is a woman with children of her own, she has done the arithmetic her elders are too polite to do out loud. One of them, grown, said it to my face without meaning cruelty. The old one in the young face. She said it almost fondly, naming the thing everyone had noticed and no one would say.

After that the looks changed into something careful. A gap opened around me at the fire, the width of a question no one wanted answered.

I knew the looks. I had met them before, in a life so far back it had gone soft at the edges, in a cave and a cold I could no longer feel from the inside. Back then the looks had come first, and then the hands, and then I had been driven out into the dark to die. I remembered how it had gone without remembering the girl it had happened to.

So this time I did not wait for the hands. One night, when the fire was banked and the camp was breathing in the dark, I filled my skin with water and my horn with coal and walked out of the ring of them while they slept. No argument. No goodbye to spoil. I chose the hour and the door, and that was the only power the whole business left me: to be gone before I was sent.

Outside, with the camp gone dark behind me and the cold pressing in, I understood what I had just taught myself to do, and that I would be doing it forever. This was the shape now. Find the warmth, take what it gives, and go before it turns. It was not cruelty. It was arithmetic. I would bury every fire I ever sat at, and there is only so long a person can do that in the open, in front of others, before they begin to look like the wrong kind of miracle.
