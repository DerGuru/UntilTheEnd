For a long time after, I went back to the garden.

Not to live there. I could not live in that house with her gone from it. But every few years my walking would bend, without my quite deciding it, toward that valley, and I would come over the ridge and down to the beds and find them half-wild, and I would spend a season putting them right. Pulling what had choked out the good plants. Turning the soil. Setting her plants where she had liked them. It was the one thing I still did for love and not for use. As long as I kept the garden, some part of me was still the woman who had helped make it.

I could hear her when I worked it. Not a voice from anywhere, just the memory, sharp as a splinter: her telling me I set the seedlings too deep, always too deep, burying them as if I were ashamed of them. I would catch myself setting one shallower to spite a woman forty years in the ground. Those visits kept her a little alive, and kept me a little alive with her.

The visits came further apart. I stopped noticing when the season was right for it. When I did go, I worked faster and stayed less, and one time I caught myself tending it as I would clear any overgrown path, not for her, just to have it done. I did not think much of that at the time.

Then a stretch of years went by, and one evening, a whole range and a river away from that valley, it came to me that I had not been back in a long while. A season, at least. More. The beds would be wild by now. Her plants would be choking under the weeds we used to pull together.

I noticed the thought arrive. I noticed it ask me to turn around.

And it did not matter to me at all. I stood there and waited to feel the pull toward her garden, as I had once waited on a pass to feel the pull of leaving. Nothing came. Only the evening, and the road going on, and a mild flat readiness to keep walking it.

So I did. I never saw the garden again. The part of me that had kept a garden stayed on that road behind me, and I walked on without it, and I would not feel the size of what I had left there for a very long time.
