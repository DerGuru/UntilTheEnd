The drill ground below the north wall had not burned. In the morning the lord's gifted were standing on it.

There were six of them in those days. The line had gone on finding them long after she stopped looking, in conquered towns and in its own villages. It had given them to whoever held the drill ground. They had grown up on it. They knew the stances. They had seen the smoke all night. Now they stood on the beaten earth and looked at the ash where the house had been, then at her.

Nobody gave them an order. There was nobody left to give one.

One of them was a young man with soot in the creases of his knuckles. For a breath she thought he was the baker's boy. Then she counted, for the first time in a very long time, and knew that the baker's boy had been dead for longer than this young man's grandfather had been alive.

He looked at her across the drill ground. Then he settled into the low stance, once, with his back heel lifted the width of a finger.

Somebody had taught him that. It had not been her.

He straightened up, picked up his spear, and went out through the north gate. The others went out by the other gates, one at a time, each a different way.

She went out through the south gate. She did not take the road.

For a while she was walking away from the ash. Then, at some point I have never been able to find, she was only walking. There wasn't a day when it changed. There was the hill behind her, then no hill, then a country where she could not have told you which way it lay.

Behind her the lord's lands came apart. She heard it in pieces, from people who did not know who she was. A steward held the river towns for a summer. A captain held the ford for three. The granary city shut its gates and kept them shut. Nobody rode the boundaries anymore, and the stones that marked them stayed wherever the last man to move one had left it.

You've heard me say it already, by another fire: I killed the man who was pointing me and walked away. I kept walking. Said that way, it sounds finished. Somewhere behind us the owl has started up again. Let it.

The fire has burned down to coals and one last stick. Xu Ran has filled his page to the bottom and gone on into the margin. He does not look up.

Yun watches the coals for a long time.

Then she leans forward. She takes a split stick from his pile and lays it across the coals, where it will catch. She watches until it does.

The brush stops. Xu Ran looks at the stick, then at her. He says nothing.

After a while he writes something down. She does not ask what. She waits until he has finished and dipped the brush again.

Then she tells him about the farmers.
