Everyone was asleep. Mei was not. She lay on her side, knees drawn up, her back against her mother's warmth. The fire had burned to coals — orange and black, pulsing faintly, like something breathing under ash. Around her, the People slept in the shapes they always slept in. Curled, spread, tangled together, children pressed against adults, mouths open, chests rising and falling in rhythms that almost matched.

Mei could hear all of it. The breathing. The small sounds of sleep — a twitch, a murmur, someone grinding their teeth. An owl somewhere in the trees, two notes repeated. The creek below the camp, whispering over stones.

And the hum.

It was always there now. During the day it mixed with everything else — the sounds of walking, talking, hunting, eating — and she could ignore it the way she ignored the weight of her own body. But at night, when everything else went quiet, the hum remained. Low, steady, alive. Not outside her. Inside.

Mei placed her hand on her chest.

There.

Warm. Not skin-warm. Not fire-warm. Something deeper, centered beneath her ribs, to the left of where her heart beat. Her heart was fast, small, and regular. This was slower. Wider. Like a second pulse that moved at its own pace, answering to something she could not see.

She pressed.

The warmth responded. It pushed back — gently, the way water pushes against your hand when you hold it in a stream. She pressed harder. It grew warmer. Not hot. Not painful. Just more.

She held her breath.

The warmth shifted. It moved — up, toward her collarbone, a slow trickle of heat that spread along the bone like water finding a crack in stone. She felt it in her shoulder. Then it stopped, as if waiting, and she realized she had pulled it there. Not with her hand. With something else. With wanting.

She let go of the wanting. The warmth slid back to her chest.

She did it again. Pushed the warmth toward her arm this time. It moved — slower, less certain, like an animal being coaxed from its den. Down through her shoulder, along the inside of her arm, into her wrist. Her fingers tingled. Not unpleasantly. Like blood returning to a limb that had been pressed too long against the ground.

She made a fist. The warmth collected there, pooling in her palm, and her hand felt — not different, exactly, but fuller. Heavier. As though something invisible had been poured into it.

She opened her hand. The warmth drifted back.

Mei stared at her palm in the coal-light. It looked the same. Small, dirty, the lines she knew by heart.

She did it again. Chest to arm. Arm to hand. Hand to chest. Faster this time. The warmth obeyed — not perfectly, not smoothly, but it went where she aimed it. She tried the other arm. Slower. Clumsier. Like using her left hand to throw when her right hand was the one that knew how.

She tried pushing the warmth down, toward her belly. It went easily, spreading like heat from a stone that had sat all day in the sun. She tried further — her legs, her feet. Harder. The warmth thinned, lost shape, became more feeling than presence. Her toes felt distant, then warm, then the warmth faded and she could not hold it that far.

She pulled everything back to her chest. It settled there, easy and familiar. The hum steadied.

A sound from beside her — her mother rolling over, arm reaching out in sleep, finding Mei's back and resting there. The hand was heavy and warm in the usual way. Human warm.

Mei lay still. Her heart beat. The thing beneath her heart beat slower.

She did not think: what am I? She did not think: what does this mean? Those were not questions that fit in her mind, not yet, not in words she had. She thought the way a child thinks when it discovers it can wiggle each toe separately — a small, bright surprise. A new thing her body could do.

She moved the warmth once more — chest to hand, hand to chest — and then the tiredness came, the heavy kind that pressed her eyelids down, softened the edges of the world. She let it come.

Her mother's hand on her back. The coals breathing. The owl, two notes.

She slept.
