A morning like any other morning — the People moving slowly after a night of rain, the ground soft and the air thick with the smell of wet earth. Mei had wandered from the column to dig for roots where the soil was loose near a fallen tree. The bark was slick under her fingers, and beetles scattered when she turned over the rotting wood — fast, bright-shelled, vanishing into the mulch before she could count them.

She pressed her hand flat against the earth.

There.

Something warm. Not from the sun — the sun was hidden behind low clouds and had been all morning. This came from deeper. Below the soil, below the roots, below the layer of clay and pebbles that her fingers could reach. It moved. Slowly, like something breathing, like something alive that was not an animal. A current. Pulling, faintly, in one direction.

Mei leaned forward and pressed harder. The warmth intensified. She shifted her weight, crawled a hand's breadth to the left. Weaker. Back to the right. Stronger. She followed it on her knees, palm flat, fingers spread, dragging through the mud, and the warmth led her like a trail of hot stones under the skin of the world.

She stopped where it was strongest. Dug.

The soil was dark and wet. After two hands deep it became darker, wetter. Then her fingers hit something that wasn't soil at all — a cold, smooth surface of water, seeping up through the clay. A spring. Hidden, shallow, but real.

"Koh!"

Koh looked up from wherever she had been — chewing on a blade of grass, probably, or throwing pebbles at a tree trunk. She came over and crouched beside Mei.

"Water," Mei said, and pointed at the hole she had dug.

Koh peered in. Poked the wet earth with a finger. Watched the water seep and pool.

"Good find."

"Warm. Under. Shows where."

Koh looked at her. "What warm?"

Mei took Koh's hand and pressed it flat against the ground, as her own had been. "Feel?"

Koh waited. Her face was patient, open, the face she wore when Mei showed her things that Mei found interesting and Koh did not. After a moment she shook her head.

"Cold mud."

"No, under the mud. Warm. Like — " Mei did not have a word for it. She pressed her own hand down again. It was still there. Obvious. Bright as a fire in the dark.

Koh shrugged. "You find water. Good."

She said it the way she said everything — simply, without weight. As though finding hidden water by pressing your hand on the ground was just another thing Mei did, like following ant trails or noticing which berries were bitter before you tasted them.

Mei looked at her hand. At the wet earth. At Koh's hand, which had felt nothing.

She did not think about this for long. Koh was already up, already moving. One of the women had seen the water hole and was calling others over. Someone was digging it wider. The People would drink here, fill the skins. That was good. Mei had helped. The warm had helped. It was like finding a trail or reading the sky before rain — just something she could do.

She wiped her hands on her thighs and followed Koh.

But something stayed with her — a small thing, light as a seed caught in hair. Koh had pressed her hand to the same earth. And Koh had felt nothing.

Mei looked at her own palms. They looked the same as anyone's palms. Small, dirty, lined.

The warm was still there. Under the ground, under her feet, moving in its slow way like water that nobody could see. She could feel it if she paid attention — a faint, steady presence, like sun on the back of your neck even with your eyes closed.

She closed her hand and walked on.
