The spear went in. Mei felt it enter — through skin, through muscle, through the space between ribs where the warm lived. The point was flint, cold and sharp. It went deep. The pain was not pain anymore. It was a sound. A single, clear note, like a stone dropped into still water.

She looked up. The young man holding the spear stared down at her. His hands were shaking. His face was the face of someone doing a thing he had been told to do — set, pale, afraid. She knew him. He had been a child when she left. Small. Quiet. He had watched her from behind his mother's leg.

He was not small anymore.

Blood came. She felt it — warm, wet, spreading under her back. The warm in her chest flickered. Dimmed. The note that was the pain grew louder. The world began to darken at the edges. Her body was doing the thing that bodies do when something essential has been opened. It was shutting down.

*Not now.*

It came from deep within her. The place below the warm, below the bone, below the blood that was leaving her. The place where the shape of her — the fundamental, irreducible pattern of what she was — lived. 

And that place said no.

Something paused.

The air went still. The kind of still that happens when the world notices something. The fire-crackle, the breathing of the men around her, the distant sound of the river — all of it dropped, for one heartbeat, to silence. As though the world had drawn a breath and held it.

The young man pulled the spear out.

It came free with a wet sound. The blood on the tip was bright. Mei's chest should have been open, should have been a ruin of torn flesh and broken bone.

The wound closed.

Not slowly. This was immediate. The edges of the wound drew together like a mouth closing. Skin sealed over the gap. Blood stopped. The red that had been spreading from her ceased, and the wet warmth that had been leaving her reversed — pulled back, drawn inward, as though it had never left.

Mei breathed.

Her chest was whole. She could feel it — the warm, brighter than it had ever been, filling the space where the spear had been, filling everything. Even. Complete. As though the wound had never existed. 

The young man stared at the place where the spear had been. His mouth was open. The spear in his hands was still wet with her blood.

He thrust again.

Harder this time — both hands, his weight behind it, aimed at the same spot. The flint tip hit her chest.

And shattered.

The sound was small and final — a crack, like a branch underfoot. The spearhead broke into three pieces that fell to the ground like dead leaves. The wooden shaft splintered in his hands. Mei felt the impact — a pressure, dull and distant, the way you feel rain through a roof. Nothing more.

Her skin was unmarked. Unharmed. As though the agreement between sharp things and soft things had been renegotiated, and her body was no longer party to the old terms.

Silence.

Seven people. Standing, kneeling, holding her down. All of them still. All of them staring at the broken spear on the ground and the girl beneath them whose chest was whole and whose eyes were open and whose face held an no expression that was neither triumph nor anger or fear.

Mei did not know what her face held. She did not know what was in her. She knew only that she was alive. That she should not be. That something in her had refused the alternative with a certainty that left no room for doubt.

A woman screamed. The sound broke the silence like a rock breaks water.

They ran.

All of them. At once. The hands holding Mei released. The weight on her back vanished. Bodies scrambled, stumbled, fled. Away from her, away from the ground where she lay, away from the broken spear and the blood that was on the earth but not on her. They ran like animals run from fire — blind, total, without thought or direction.

In ten heartbeats, Mei was alone.

She lay on the ground. The sky above was blue, clear, and very far away. The warm pulsed in her chest — slow, vast, the biggest it had ever been. Bigger than the cave. Bigger than the mountain. As though the thing that had happened had opened a door she had not known was there, and the warm had poured through it. She was more than she had been a moment ago.

She sat up. Looked at her chest. The skin was smooth. No scar. No mark. Not even a bruise where the spear had shattered.

She touched the spot with her fingers. It felt warm.

Around her, the camp was empty. Fires still burning. Shelters standing. Tools on the ground where they had been dropped. The People had left everything. They had taken nothing but their fear.

Mei stood. Her legs held. Her body worked. The blood on the ground — her blood, that was too far away to have been drawn in again — was already darkening in the sun.

She looked at the direction they had run. All of them. Her mother among them. Running from her.

She stood in the empty camp and breathed. The warm breathed with her. The world was quiet. She was alive, and she did not understand any of it.

*Not now.*

The words sat in her chest like the warm — permanent, foundational, part of what she was.

She picked up a spear from the ground. One of theirs, dropped in the flight. It was a good spear — better than the ones she remembered. Someone had gotten better at making them.

She held it in her hand and looked at the trail the People had left — trampled grass, dropped things, the wide, panicked path of twenty-odd people running from something they could not kill.

She followed.
