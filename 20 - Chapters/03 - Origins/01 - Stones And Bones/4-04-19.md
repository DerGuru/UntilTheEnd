The warm flowed through her — always moving, in and out, a current she rode but could not stop. Following her breath. When she stopped reaching, it drifted back to the cave, returning to its source. More left than stayed. Always more leaving.

She tried holding her breath.

It worked — for a moment. The warm pooled in her chest, dense and still, and she thought: yes. This. But then her lungs burned, and her chest heaved, and the breath came anyway — and with it the warm rushed out, all of it, more than before, as though punishing her for trying to trap it.

She tried again. Held longer this time. Her face grew hot. Her vision darkened at the edges. The warm stayed — bright, full, hers — and then the reflex broke her, violent with a cough. She gasped and it was gone.

She tried to imagine holding it while breathing out — exhaling the air but keeping the warm inside. But the warm followed the breath. It did not know the difference between air and itself. When the air left, the warm left with it.

She could not hold her breath forever, and she could not separate the warm from the breathing. That was not the way.

She tried imagining it staying — the way she had imagined the ribbons. Seeing the warm sitting still in the place behind her ribs. Not leaving. Just resting there. It worked, a little. While she held the picture, some remained. But the image took everything she had — jaw clenched, forehead tight, her whole mind straining to keep the picture clear. The moment hunger or tiredness blurred her thoughts, most of it slipped away like smoke through fingers.

She tried to seal herself. To close every place the warm leaked from — her skin, her breath, the spaces between her ribs. She imagined her body as stone, as sealed hide, as something without gaps. Nothing happened. She could not make herself airtight. The warm seeped out through a thousand invisible cracks and she could not find any of them.

Days passed. She sat in the cave, reaching the warm in and watching it leave, reaching it in and watching it leave, and the frustration grew until she could not sit still anymore.

She went outside.

The morning was cold. The valley below the mountain was full of fog — white, heavy, motionless, filling the low ground between the ridges. She stood on the slope and watched. The fog did not rise. Did not move. The sun was not high enough to burn it away. It simply lay there, held by the shape of the land.

But the valley was not sealed. There were gaps between the hills — low places where the fog could have drifted out. And some of it did, at the edges, thin wisps pulling away into the wind. But not fast enough to empty the valley. More settled from above than escaped at the sides. The valley filled because it lost less than it gained.

She went back inside.

She sat. Cross-legged. Hands on knees. She breathed in and reached, and the warm came — ribbons condensing, flowing into the place behind her ribs. She breathed out, and most of it left. But she was not trying to hold it anymore. She was not trying to seal herself. She was thinking about the valley.

She imagined the place behind her ribs as a valley. Low walls. A small opening — narrow, not closed. The warm could leave through it. But slowly. Slower than it came in.

She breathed in. The warm flowed down into her valley. She breathed out. Some left — rising, thinning, escaping through the narrow opening. But not all. Not as much as before. The walls were there. Low, imperfect, but there.

She breathed in. More came.

She breathed out. Less left.

It was not much. A whisper of difference. But she was gaining faster than she was losing.

She lost it after a few breaths — the image wavered, the walls thinned, and the warm leaked out in a rush. But she had felt it. The accumulation. The slow net gain.

She tried again. And again. For days — losing the image, rebuilding it, holding it a little longer each time. Some days the walls crumbled immediately. Some days they held for an hour before fading. She went out for food, came back, tried again. Went out, came back, tried again.

Slowly, the valley inside her grew deeper. The walls grew higher — not because she forced them, but because the warm itself, pooling there day after day, pressed the shape deeper. The more it filled, the less wanted to leave. The way fog grows heavier as it thickens, pressing itself down, too dense to rise.

But it still left. Slowly, thinly, constantly — wisps escaping over the walls, through the gap, thinning while she slept. Every morning she was a little less full than the night before. Every day she had to reach again, fill again, rebuild what the night had taken.

She went outside in frustration. For the need to move her legs and think about something else. But her feet carried her toward the berry bushes anyway, lokking for some berries, she could eat. She pushed through the thicket, hands parting branches, eyes searching the low growth. 

The wind picked up — gusting through the canopy, tearing loose leaves from their stems. They swirled around her head, brown and dry, spinning past. She blinked them away. Then a gust and a branch whipped across her face. The leaf at its tip slapped flat over her nose, broad and damp. She could not breathe. She snorted a sharp push of air through her nostrils and the leaf peeled back, the branch swinging away.

She stood still.

She breathed in. The air pulled toward her. Breathed out. It pushed away. If the leaf had been there when she breathed out, it would have blown off. But when she had tried to breathe in, the air pulled it flat against the opening and sealed it.

Something settled in her mind. Not a thought. A shape.

She went back inside.

She sat. Reached. The warm came. She did not try to hold it. Did not try to seal herself. She thought about the leaf — something pressed against an opening from the inside. Not by her. By what was already there.

She pushed a small amount of the warm toward the place where it always escaped — the narrow passage, the crack, the exit. Not pushing it OUT. Pushing it AGAINST the exit, like a leaf pressed against a hole by the current behind it. The warm sat there — thin, barely a wisp — leaning against the opening from within.

She breathed out. The air left. The warm did not. It pressed against the passage and held. Not because she clenched. Because it was in the way of itself.

She breathed in. More warm came through — slipping past the wisp that guarded the exit, because the flow was inward now. The wisp stayed where it was.

She breathed out. The wisp pressed tighter. Nothing left.

She breathed in. More came. The wisp grew thicker — fed from within, pressed against the exit by the growing pressure behind it. The fuller she got, the firmer it held. The seal was not a wall — it was the warm itself, leaning on its own door.

She sat with this for a long time. Breathing in, the warm accumulating. Breathing out, the wisp holding. Sometimes it thinned, and a thread escaped. But it was better than anything before.

She imagined a hide over the entrance of a shelter. Hung on the outside, the wind blows it open — useless. Hung on the inside, the warm air presses it closed. The more warmth, the more it seals.

She had been hanging the hide on the wrong side.

She slept that night and woke with the warm still there. Not all of it — the wisp had thinned in her sleep, and some had seeped out. But most remained. More than ever before. And when she reached again in the morning, the wisp rebuilt itself in moments — the warm knew where to go now. It remembered the shape.

Days passed. The wisp grew into something thicker — not a wisp anymore, a layer. A skin over the opening, fed from within, pressing outward. It held while she slept. It held while she ate. It held while she went outside and searched for food. The warm inside her grew denser, and the denser it grew, the tighter the seal pressed, and the tighter the seal pressed, the denser it grew.

She was filling.
