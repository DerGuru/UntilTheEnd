He was smaller than she remembered. She had been eleven when she left — a child, looking up at everything. She grown up. He was still bigger thant her, but not as much. And he was broader. Heavier. The years had thickened him — his neck, his shoulders, the trunk of his body. He stood at the entrance with the hunters at his sides, the spear in his hands. Twice her weight.

Mei approached. Casually. Like someone walking home.

He threw the spear when she was ten paces away.

It was a good throw — hard, fast, aimed at her center. Mei's hand came up. Sweeping upward, catching the shaft from below with then back of her hand. The spear left his trajectory and went flying up, spinning, tumbling end over end into the sky above the basin. Gone.

Mei kept walking. The big man staring at her.

Then he charged. He came down the slope like a rockslide, head low, shoulders forward, roaring, running towards her. He hit Mei with everything he had — two hundred pounds of bone, muscle, and the fury of a man who had never lost and could not accept that he was about to.

Mei planted her feet and took it.

The impact drove her back. Her feet cut grooves in the loose rock. But she did not fall. The warm was there — everywhere, in every part of her, dense, steady, and absolute. His hands found her shoulders. His fingers dug in. He pushed. Mei did not move.

He hit her. Fist to the ribs. Again — shoulder, chest, the side of her head. Each blow landed with the full weight of a man who had killed with his hands before. Mei did not flinch. The warm answered each impact before she could think — hardening where the fist fell, absorbing, dispersing. His knuckles split on her collarbone. Blood ran down his fingers. He did not notice.

He grabbed her waist. Bent his knees. Tried to lift her, bring her out of balance and down. Mei felt the warm sink. Down through her legs, into her feet, into the stone beneath them. It held. She was part of the ground now, connected, rooted like a tree — *anchored*. He strained. His face went red. The veins in his neck stood out like ropes. She did not rise.

He screamed a raw, animalistic sound, the noise of something breaking inside a man who has never been denied. He hit her again. Wild now. Fists, elbows, his forehead — cracking against her temple, her jaw, her chest. Uncontrolled. Each blow harder and less aimed than the last, the attacks of a hunter replaced by the last stand's flailing of prey. He grabbed her arm, twisted and tried to throw her sideways. Mei's feet stayed. He clawed at her face. His nails left no mark.

He was slowing. The rage burning itself out. His arms loosening. His breath tearing in his chest.

She looked at his face.

It was close — close enough to see the pores in his skin, the grey in his beard, the veins in his eyes. Close enough to see the moment when fury turned to something else. When the man behind the rage understood what was standing in front of him.

She hit him.

One blow. Open palm. Center of the chest. The warm came with it — all of it, everything she had gathered, concentrated into a single point and released. The force left her hand, entered him and kept going.

His chest caved. His back burst. His body left the ground. He hit the far wall of the basin. The sound was wet and final. He stayed where he landed — broken, open, the shape of a man who was no longer a man.

Mei stood in the silence. Her hand still raised. Palm still open.

She looked at the People.

They were pressed against the back wall — women, children, the remaining hunters. All of them staring. All of them still. None of them moved. None of them spoke.

One by one, the hunters slowly and carefully put down their weapons on the ground. Their hands now empty and heads down. The gesture was older than language: I yield.

Nobody else moved. Not the women, not the children, not Koh, not Mei's mother. None of them looked at her. None of them spoke.

Mei stood in the basin with blood on her hand, the warm in her chest, and what was left of the biggest man in the world against the far wall.

Mei looked at the People. The warm told her everything. They were terrified. They had never seen anything like this. They had never seen anything like her. They did not understand what she was, or how she had done what she had done. But she had stopped.

She nodded at the hunters, who had surrendered.

"Pick up.", she said, pointing at their spears. "Follow."

Her voice was calm, steady, and low. The warm carried it to them — the sound of a command. The hunters obeyed. They picked up their spears and followed her out of the basin. 

The rest of the group followed.
